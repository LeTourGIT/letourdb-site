📝 Session Summary

The ASX 200 eased 0.33% to 8,787.0 and the All Ords fell 0.48% to 8,988.3 — a risk-off session imported wholesale from Wall Street's Mag-7 rout. This was not broad-based selling: it was a tech-and-resources de-rate against a defensive bid. Decliners swamped advancers 146 to 45 on the XJO (a brutal 3.2:1), yet Financials still closed green — the single clearest signal that capital didn't leave the market, it rotated within it.

Information Technology (XIJ −4.04%) was the epicentre, a direct read-through from Alphabet's −5% overnight on AI-talent defections. Technology One (TNE −7.10%, ASX200) led domestic software lower, and WiseTech extended its slide on the founder-probe overhang. When the world's AI bellwether de-rates on a talent and valuation story, the ASX's high-multiple software names have no insulation — and today they got none.

Materials (XMJ −1.38%, 5day −6.06%) was the second engine of the decline, but for a different reason: the lithium complex is unwinding. Chinese lithium carbonate futures fell sharply on speculation that CATL's Jianxiawo mine could restart in 2H26 — the mirror image of May's lithium re-rating. Iluka (ILU −10.82%) was the session's institutional casualty, and tellingly it fell despite announcing a binding multi-year take-or-pay magnet rare-earth oxide supply deal with a global automaker (≈10% of planned production from 2028). That is a classic sell-the-news in a tape with no appetite for critical-minerals risk — Alpha HPA (A4N −10.43%) and a string of silver and gold juniors confirmed the rout.

The defensive counter-trade is where the real story lives. Energy (XEJ −0.69%) was held back by Brent −2.75% overnight as a US–Iran 60-day peace roadmap drains the Hormuz risk premium — a disinflationary positive for the RBA but a revenue headwind for Woodside and Santos. With the RBA having held rates last week, the domestic rate narrative now pivots to next week's May CPI and labour data; globally, the more pressing signal is the US 2-year at 4.23% (highest since Feb-25) reviving Fed hike bets and pressuring every rate-sensitive name on the board (Centuria CNI −6.57%).

AUD/USD slipped 0.57% to 0.6961 — a quiet but helpful offset. A softer Aussie cushions the USD-denominated revenue of BHP, RIO and FMG (≈0.7% revenue uplift per 1¢ AUD fall), partially blunting the commodity-price drag on the miners. The bounce-back question for tomorrow is simple: with ES/NQ futures still red at the close, does the defensive rotation hold the 50MA, or does a second leg of the Mag-7 unwind drag the index through support?
