AI capex shock

The Nasdaq's 2.6% AI-capex shock — worst day since June, led by Meta's 7% fall after it raised its AI spending range — had almost nowhere to land on the ASX, which has no mega-cap AI complex. The read-through came through rates instead: the US 10yr fell 11bp and dragged the AU 10yr down 10bp to 5.164%, the exact relief that lifted banks and insurers today. The only direct AI casualty is the data-centre chain (XIJ -0.92%, NXT 5day -41.38%, SKS clarifying its NEXTDC exposure at 14:54). Futures are flat at report time (ES 0.00%, NQ +0.14%) — no follow-through selling. Brent near US$113–114 is the number that matters for Australia: oil-led inflation fears broke gold and keep the RBA hike live.

Session Summary

The XJO's +0.02% close at 8,795.2 is the most misleading print of the month. Underneath it ran a 6.4-point sector spread — Consumer Discretionary +2.57% and Financials +2.21% against Materials -3.85% — and a 2.5-point gap between large caps and small caps. Capital did not leave the market; it left the resource tape and moved into bank and retail balance sheets.

Resources led — downward. Gold slid to a three-month low of US$4,003 as higher oil revived inflation and rate fears, and the gold miners' September quarterlies landed straight into the rout: OBM -16.54% (08:16, price sensitive), BC8 -15.83% (08:17), SRL -13.68% (09:06), ALK -10.88% (08:23) and NEM -4.93% after its Q3 release (08:21). The margin cushion that excused cost blowouts at ~US$4,280 a month ago is gone at US$4,000. The majors added weight — BHP -3.64%, RIO -2.68% (despite its $1.9b Kangwinan bauxite commitment), FMG -2.06%, S32 -5.28% — with iron ore soft at US$90.73 (20day -6.6%). Lithium (PLS -8.07%, IGO -7.38%, LTR -7.25%) fell with no company news at all: de-risking, not information. XMJ 5day -7.69% and 20day -11.12% make this a trend liquidation.

The money went to the banks and the shopping centre. CBA +4.35% to $164, NAB +3.25% and WBC +3.11% all printed new 20day highs, riding the 10bp drop in the AU 10yr and the view that a November hike widens NIM faster than it lifts bad debts. Retail ran harder: TPW +33.42% on its AGM strategy update (08:19, price sensitive), NCK +5.15%, WES +3.76%, HVN +3.22%. Rallying the rate-sensitive consumer 11 days before a likely hike says the hike is priced and the consumer is holding up — EVT's "strong Q1" and ABB's trading update back that read.

ZIP -29.47% was the institutional casualty: its 1Q FY27 update (08:22, price sensitive) disclosed a US fraud incident that hit volumes and new-customer growth and cost $10.1m in cash opex (per ABC); Citi flagged onboarding controls. A control failure is a multiple problem, not a one-quarter problem.

Breadth is split down the middle: the XJO logged 59 new 20day highs and 59 new 20day lows, and 40 constituents fell more than 4% against 3 that rose 4%. That is a rotation market, not a risk-off market — but small caps are paying for it. Next week: Q3 CPI (the RBA decider), Champion Iron results 29/10, six trading halts to resolve, and the post-close PMV annual report plus IMM's ESMO data as Monday catalysts.
