Session Summary

The ASX 200 surrendered 1.99% to 8,614.4 in a session where every one of the 11 sectors closed red and only 12 of 200 constituents rose — a 93.9% downside day by issues, and roughly $50bn wiped from market value. Wednesday's +0.92% rally was fully erased by late morning. The driver was rates, not equities: US 10yr and 30yr yields pushed to 5.29% and 5.63% overnight, and the local 3yr climbed to 4.98%, two sessions after the RBA's fourth hike of 2026 lifted the cash rate to 4.60%. The market spent Tuesday and Wednesday hoping the hike was the last; today it accepted that nothing in the yield structure had changed.

Energy (XEJ -3.03%) was the worst sector even with WTI up 2.27% to US$91.16 and Brent holding above US$100 — a rare decoupling that says the sector traded balance-sheet risk, not the oil price. Yancoal (YAL -6.98%) lodged completion of its US$1.85bn Kestrel acquisition at 08:47 [PS], with up to US$550m of contingent payments if met-coal exceeds US$225/t — the market priced the leverage, not the asset. Materials (XMJ -1.89%) was led lower by lithium and rare earths: Liontown (LTR -15.05%, no same-day announcement) extended its post-FID slide after Macquarie cut its target to $1.20, and Lynas (LYC -8.60%) fell after lodging its $968m all-scrip bid for Meteoric pre-open (08:39 [PS]), inheriting a US$498m Caldeira capex bill. Firmer oil is also the wrong direction for the RBA's inflation fight.

Financials (XFJ -2.13%) carried two idiosyncratic hits on top of the rate trade. Suncorp (SUN -7.36%) gave back Wednesday's +4.8% takeover pop after its 08:55 [PS] response to an ASX price query confirmed it is not in discussions with Tokio Marine and has received no offer — a speculative premium removed in one print. Helia (HLI -7.33%) fell without an announcement; as a lenders-mortgage insurer it is the purest listed expression of housing credit on a day ABC flagged 'the biggest property downturn in 40 years'. Healthcare (XHJ -2.49%) was dragged by Cochlear (COH -6.63%), which has no same-day announcement but carries Tuesday's class-action overhang.

The rare bright spots were stock-specific. Data3 (DTL +13.68%, ASX200, new 20day high) lodged its 1H FY27 trading update at 12:00 [PS], guiding PBT up more than 40% — ~20% above consensus — and ran as high as +23% intraday. The Lynas bid (MEI +47.06%) lit up the Brazilian rare-earths complex: Viridis (VMM +14.67%) and St George (SGQ +3.23%) were bid as the next logical targets with no announcements of their own. Manuka (MKR +3.45%) reported first gold revenues from Wonawinta (09:33 [PS]). Codan (CDA +2.52%), the top ASX200 name in the RS table, held its Electronic Equipment leadership — the same AI-hardware theme Micron reinforced overnight.

Structurally the damage is now broad: XJO 20day lows (46) outnumber highs (6) almost 8:1, only 23% of XJO stocks are above their 20MA and 50MA, and T2108 has fallen to 20.0% from 31.5% in one session — capitulation-grade breadth. October is historically the 'bear-market killer', but a bounce needs confirmation by a 90% up-day or a reclaim of 8,700. Forward catalysts: five trading halts lodged this morning (VR8, OD6, SRN, WEC, P1E) awaiting resolution; post-close, Mayfield Childcare (MFD) received a shareholder request for a meeting (17:15 [PS]); US payrolls Friday; China Golden Week (Dalian shut).
