Session Summary

Tuesday 1 September wore a quiet headline (XJO -0.10% to 9,066.7) over a sharp rotation. The catalyst was rates, not equities: a heavy Australian government bond sell-off — the press called it 'once-in-a-generation' — backed yields up and detonated the rate-duration corners of the market.

The clearest expression was a fund-manager rout. Pinnacle (PNI) -10.1%, MA Financial (MAF) -5.5%, Regal (RPL) -4.3% and credit fund Metrics (MXT) -5.0% all fell hard, yet Financials as a sector slipped only -0.37%. This was not a broad bank de-rating — it was a targeted hit on asset-gatherers and credit vehicles whose fee earnings and NAVs are most exposed to a yield shock. AMP (+3.4%, RS rank 36) holding up while the pure fundies were routed confirms the distinction.

The second leg was long-duration growth and housing. With '93% of capital-city suburbs' recording price declines and banks openly forecasting further hikes, REA -4.2%, Car Group -4.2% and Nextdc -4.2% dragged Consumer Discretionary (-1.81%) and IT lower. It was a textbook bond-up / duration-down day — and it mirrored the US overnight almost exactly, where mega-cap growth and credit names fell on the same yield back-up.

The tape's other half was firmly risk-on in resources and cyclicals. Oil spiked (Brent above US$91) on renewed US-Iran strikes, lifting Energy (XEJ +1.18%) to sole sector leadership. Metallurgical coal (CRN +8.9%, SMR +6.6%), high-purity alumina (A4N +17.1% on its FY26 result) and lithium (LTR +6.5% on a Centenario brine earn-in) powered a spec/small-cap resources rally — precisely why XSO (+0.41%) outran the large-cap indices. Smart Money confirmed the reflation tilt: Homebuilding vaulted to rank 1, with Industrial Machinery, Auto Parts, Steel, Coal and Gas Utilities all surging.

The internals carry a warning the flat close hides: new 20day lows outnumbered new 20day highs on every one of the four indices (XJO 8 vs 35; XAO 36 vs 73). Advance/decline was near-even (XJO 105/90). This is a market splitting hard along one factor line — own the yield-up winners, avoid the yield-up losers — rather than trending as a whole. The RBA (29 September, cash rate 4.35%, market ~44% hike-priced) is the event that resolves it.
