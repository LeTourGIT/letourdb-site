Session Summary

A quiet +0.30% XJO grind to 8,757.8 flattered a session that was really about two forces: an AI-led US tailwind and a hardening RBA hike bet. IT (XIJ +2.67%) led every sector, importing the overnight Nasdaq record, while Utilities (−2.03%) and Energy (−1.16%) — the rate- and oil-sensitive ends — dragged.

The tape's real energy was in single names. GL1 (Global Lithium) +49.62% on a binding Scheme Implementation Deed with Titan is the headline — a takeover, not a lithium-price move — and it topped the [H]-flagged mining RS cohort. WTM +31.40% on a genuine gold discovery (best intercept to date) and IEL +20.67%, a pure short-covering bounce off a −67% yearly collapse with no ASX announcement, were the other standouts. TGN −15.58% is the mirror image: its $50M funding is dilution, not a win.

Underneath, the RS and Smart Money tables tell a precious-metals-plus-defence story. Gold dominates the institutional RS leaders (BNZ [H], SBM, TCG, SX2 [H]) alongside uranium (VR8, BOE); Aerospace & Defence is a Smart Money surge (rank 34→6 over nine days), corroborated by EOS +8.22% and the ESK defence-deal filing. An insurance cluster (Life & Health, Multiline, P&C) is quietly rotating in — defensive capital positioning ahead of the decision.

Company news skewed small-cap resources: a wave of FY26 annual reports (30 June year-end lodgement deadline), heavy gold/silver/copper drilling results, and a cluster of six trading halts (AXL, PR2, NXN, A1M, 3DA, USL) — each a pending catalyst. M&A was live (CSC selling Cozamin, GSS/MAP merger terms, MAT's early $55M AngloGold option, MXI's lapsed Ares bid). Post-close flow was routine annual reports, leaving no fresh catalyst into Wednesday.
