Session Summary

The ASX 200 fell 1.03% to 8,819.4, its worst session since June, erasing about $32 billion after being down as much as $50 billion intraday. It was the third straight decline and leaves the index roughly 5.7% below its early-August record of 9,296. Crucially, this was a top-down macro event, not a stock story — every one of the 11 sectors closed in the red.

The catalyst was entirely offshore. Brent settled above US$101 for the first time since July after the US destroyed five Iranian tankers and Iran pledged to intensify attacks, reviving a stagflation narrative. At the same time the US 10-year yield hit its highest since 2023 (~4.85%) and the Australian 3-year broke out to levels last seen in 2011. Higher oil plus higher yields is the textbook headwind for long-duration equities — and it showed, with IT (-1.74%) the worst sector.

Materials (XMJ -1.63%) was the second-heaviest drag: BHP fell 2.9% and RIO 3.0% despite COMEX copper printing a record US$6.86/lb and iron ore holding ~US$99/t. That divergence is the tell — the repricing of equity risk premium overwhelmed a genuine commodity tailwind, which marks this as a discount-rate event rather than a demand-destruction one. The lone bright spot in resources was gold equities: SBM +16.44% on agreeing to sell its Simberi interest to Lingbao, FML +9.17% on a maiden dividend, and OBM +4.93% on first ore at Waihi.

The defensive fingerprints were unmistakable. Utilities (-0.33%) and Comm. Services (-0.13%) were the least-damaged sectors while growth was sold hard. Smart Money rotation confirmed the flow: Electric Utilities entrenched at rank 1, with the entire energy/coal complex plus REITs and insurance surging up the table — capital hiding in real-asset and inflation-hedged cashflows, mirroring Wall Street where energy was the only green S&P 500 sector.

The read-through into month-end: with the RBA decision on 29 September (NAB tipping a 25bp hike to 4.60%, markets ~55% hold) and the deputy governor already flagging 'more needs to be done on inflation', an oil-driven inflation scare is the worst possible pre-meeting backdrop. Watch the XJO 200-day MA (~8,816) — a decisive break opens the path toward the 8,700 area.
