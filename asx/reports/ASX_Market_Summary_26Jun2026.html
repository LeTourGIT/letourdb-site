📝 Session Summary

Friday 26 June was a defensive grind that ended marginally green — XJO +0.18% to 8,764.2, XAO +0.14% to 8,964.2 — but the headline masks one of the year's cleaner risk-off rotations underneath. The global chip rout (KOSPI -10%, SoftBank -13%, SK Hynix -10%, Micron-led US weakness) dragged ASX Information Technology (XIJ -1.17%) and Health Care (XHJ -1.30%) to the bottom of the board, while capital that must stay invested funnelled into Utilities (XUJ +1.01%) and Consumer Staples (XSJ +0.81%). When the two defensive anchors lead and the two growth-sensitive sectors trail, that is textbook capital preservation, not conviction buying.

The session's defining stock story was the continued lithium / battery-metals wipeout. Pilbara Minerals (PLS -6.32%, ASX50), Liontown (LTR -5.68%, ASX200), Elevra Lithium (ELV -8.94%, ASX200), Core Lithium (CXO -7.27%) and Patriot Battery Metals (PMT -5.83%) all fell hard, extending five-day losses of 14–19%. The catalyst is offshore: speculation that CATL's giant Jianxiawo mine could restart has reignited oversupply fears, with Chinese lithium carbonate (~CNY169,000/t) recovering off a two-month low but still far below the mid-May peak. This is a structural supply story, not a one-day flush — position accordingly.

Against that, gold provided the offset. After bullion slid below US$4,000 overnight to a seven-month low, it bounced +0.62% to ~US$4,043, and the ASX gold complex caught a strong bid — Solstice Minerals (SLS +29.07%) topped the board on a new 20day high, with Kingsgate (KCN +4.46%) and others firm. That gold strength plus resilient iron-ore majors (iron ore ~US$105/t, its highest since January) is why Materials (XMJ +0.81%) printed green despite the lithium carnage — a reminder that "resources" is not one trade.

The Smart Money rotation reinforced the domestic-cyclical and defensive tilt: Airlines surged to the #1 industry rank (from #30 nine sessions ago), with Home Furnishings Retailers (#2) and Department Stores (#3) confirming a consumer-discretionary flow that the retail-spending data has been feeding all week. Notably, Semiconductors rocketed to #8 (from #72) — the local market beginning to price a contrarian dip-buy in the very theme being routed globally, worth watching but early.

The RBA sits in the background on a hawkish hold after three hikes since January; May headline CPI eased to 4.0% (from 4.2%) but core re-accelerated to 3.6%, and Deputy Governor Hauser has stressed inflation remains "too high." June meeting minutes are due next week — the next domestic catalyst. With oil back at pre-war levels, the inflation impulse is softening, but the bank's tone keeps rate-sensitive sectors on a short leash. Real test: can XJO hold 8,700 through the chip-rout resolution.
