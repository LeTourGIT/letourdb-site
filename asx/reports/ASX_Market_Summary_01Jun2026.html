Session Summary

Monday 1 June opened the new month almost exactly where Friday left it — the XJO closing 8,729.4, a fraction of a point lower — but the flat tape masked the most concentrated single-sector move in weeks. Information Technology (XIJ +5.43%) did virtually all of the lifting, tracking Friday's record Nasdaq close and the Dell-led AI-hardware rally on Wall Street. WiseTech (WTC) was the standout, with support stepping back in around its AI-transformation/CargoWise pivot narrative after a brutal twelve months; Xero and NextDC participated. This was bargain-hunting in the most beaten-down corner of the index, not a broad risk-on surge.

Underneath IT, the session was defensive-to-soft. Healthcare (XHJ −1.68%) was the worst sector, with CSL (−2.3%) still carrying its impairment/guidance overhang and dragging the complex. Real Estate (−0.76%) and Financials (−0.33%) drifted, the banks mixed in a narrow band. Materials (+0.49%) was a quiet positive: BHP went ex-dividend (−0.9% on the day, $1.03 fully franked) yet RIO bucked the ex-div rule (+1.2–1.7% after going ex $3.60), and the gold miners were the genuine strength — Evolution (+4.3%) and Northern Star (+3.4%) firmed as bullion held near record levels.

The lithium/critical-minerals cohort had a strong day inside an otherwise muted Materials sector — IGO (+4.7%), Pilbara (+4.33%) and Liontown (+3.31%) all advanced, consistent with lithium carbonate's continued recovery (NE Asia ~US$21/kg, +17% in May). That keeps the institutional lithium re-rating thesis alive even as the broad miners tread soft on a −4.15% iron ore print.

The most important macro development isn't on the screen at all: WTI is down ~16% over 20 days as the Iran/Hormuz risk premium drains out, and Australia's monthly inflation indicator just slipped 0.3% month-on-month — its first decline since February. The RBA's May hike leaned explicitly on Middle-East fuel costs; both of those props are now reversing into the 16 June meeting. Set against that, the structural caution is real: only ~39% of XJO names sit above their 200-day MA, XAO breadth was actually negative on the day (226 advancers vs 231 decliners), and every index remains below its 200MA. A narrow, tech-driven flat session is not the same as a healthy market — it's a market leaning on a handful of large names while it waits for Q1 GDP and trade data later this week.
