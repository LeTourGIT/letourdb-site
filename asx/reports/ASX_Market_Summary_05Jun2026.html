📝 Session Summary — 05/06/26

Friday 5 June delivered the ASX's most decisive internal rotation of the June quarter: a flat headline (XJO −0.06% to 8,625.1) that masked two markets moving in completely opposite directions with genuine conviction. Healthcare (XHJ +3.54%) led all sectors — the first meaningful institutional re-engagement with a sector down 44.82% over one year — driven by Pro Medicus (+10.8%+) extending its 1 June US contract momentum, with WiseTech Global (+7.9%), Xero (+7.5%), and Life360 (+13.3%) completing a technology-healthcare SaaS complex that collectively re-rated international-revenue growth stocks as the dominant ASX trade.

The other side was equally unambiguous. Materials (XMJ −2.31%) and Energy (XEJ −1.11%) both fell hard as iron ore's five-day decline of 6.49% to $101.96 signalled the Chinese steel demand thesis is softening at exactly the moment BHP/RIO/FMG trade at elevated valuations relative to spot commodity prices. Financials (XFJ −0.89%) remained under structural pressure from the FWC's 4.75% wage decision — a direct margin compression signal for bank-adjacent retail and hospitality, with the added sting of an August RBA hike now in multiple bank base cases.

The session's internal architecture confirms the rotation's authenticity: eight ASX200/300 names made new 20day highs on a flat index day — the breadcrumb trail of institutional accumulation beneath headline noise. MP1 (Megaport) at RS 98.51 making 20day highs while delivering +15.19% is the institutional story in a single data point. The XSO vs large-cap regime divergence (Neutral Bull vs Neutral Bear) is the week's most important structural flag — watch for confirmation or reversal around the 16 June RBA meeting.

📰 Key Headlines — 05/06/26
⚠️ FWC / RBA — August Hike Back in Play
The Fair Work Commission's 4.75% minimum wage increase (2 June, effective 1 July), benefiting ~2.9M workers, came in above expectations and directly contradicts the RBA's inflation reduction mandate. Citi analysts flagged the decision keeps an August hike firmly in play. Cash rate at 4.35% has already unwound all of 2025's 75bp easing. Next meeting 16 June: CBA/ANZ forecast pause; NAB forecasts August +25bp; Westpac forecasts peak 4.85%. April CPI of 4.2% (down from 4.6% March) provides the RBA room to pause June without abandoning its mandate — but August remains structurally live.
💊 Healthcare Re-Rate — Early Signal or False Start?
XHJ +3.54% was the session's dominant move — the healthcare sector's single best day in months after a brutal −44.82% one-year drawdown. Pro Medicus (+10.8%+) extending momentum from 1 June's A$44M US contract wins (Allegheny Health Network 5yr renewal + TidalHealth 7yr deal). WTC +7.9%, XRO +7.5%, Life360 +13.3% swept international-revenue SaaS names higher. Eight ASX200/300 constituents made new 20day highs on a flat index — institutional accumulation is the read. One session does not establish a trend; watch whether T2108 in XHJ component names recovers above 50% and whether RS leadership sustains across multiple sessions.
🇺🇸 DJI All-Time High 51,562 — But the Wrong Kind
The Dow's record was a value-rotation story (UnitedHealth +5.2%, Goldman +4.9%, JPMorgan +3.3%), not a broad tech rally. AVGO fell more than 12% on a disappointing Q3 AI chip revenue guidance — the first major US semiconductor earnings miss of the AI cycle. NDX −0.53%. The bifurcation between AI chip hardware (fading) and AI software monetisation (accelerating) is now confirmed across US and ASX markets simultaneously. May NFP (~85K consensus) due Friday — the Fed's next input on whether US disinflation is achieved at the cost of labour market deterioration.
⛏️ Materials Selloff — Iron Ore Breaking Support
XMJ −2.31% as iron ore slid to $101.96 (5day −6.49%, 20day −8.03%). BHP, RIO, and FMG face a compounding headwind: commodity price decline at current AUD/USD levels directly compresses USD-denominated revenue in AUD terms. A break below $100/t would be a material re-rating catalyst for ASX resources earnings season. Notably, Coal diverged — WHC, NHC, and YAL held Smart Money rotation #3 positioning despite the broader materials selloff, confirming the coal sub-sector thesis remains intact on thermal coal Hormuz premium dynamics.
⚡ Geopolitical — Fragile Ceasefire, Ongoing Iran Tensions
The Israel-Lebanon ceasefire announced by the US remains conditional on Hezbollah acceptance (not yet confirmed as of Friday). US-Iran exchanges of strikes are ongoing despite Trump's assertion that negotiations "could be completed this weekend." EIA data showed a sixth consecutive week of US crude inventory draws — confirming the Hormuz-driven supply premium has a structural floor regardless of diplomatic progress. ASX Aerospace & Defense Smart Money surge (#9 from #76) reflects institutional pricing of a prolonged geopolitical risk premium, not a short-term ceasefire trade.
