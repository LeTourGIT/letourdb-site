📝 Session Summary

Tuesday delivered the purest expression of a reporting-season tape: a headline index that barely moved (XJO 9,070, −3.2 points) sitting on top of violent single-stock dispersion. The ASX 200 fell just short of snapping a week-long losing streak — six straight sessions of grind-lower — but the story is entirely beneath the surface. Five of eleven sectors closed green, yet decliners swamped advancers 134 to 58 on the XJO. A flat close was manufactured by a small cluster of mega-cap earnings winners; the median stock had a poor day.

Health Care (XHJ +7.81%) was the engine. CSL +17.25% on its FY2026 result was the largest single contributor to the index, joined by Pro Medicus +11.88% and Cochlear +7.58% — a clean sweep of the healthcare majors beating and re-rating on the same day. That is not sector rotation; it is a coordinated fundamental repricing of Australia's healthcare franchise, and it shows up simultaneously in the Smart Money table (Healthcare Facilities surged 57 ranks to #10, Medical Equipment to #9, Biotech to #12).

The session's second headline was corporate, not operational. Reliance Worldwide +24.65% after disclosing a fourth, and now formalised, takeover approach from Brookfield — an A$4.1bn bid at A$4.75/share, with a signed process deed and a four-week exclusivity window. The Board pulled its final dividend and RWC's own FY26 numbers (adjusted EBITDA −12.8% on tariffs and higher copper costs) were softer than the tape implied — the move is a control-premium re-rate, not an earnings beat. It is the standout [ASX200] event of the day.

The offset came from rates and resources. Financials (XFJ −1.10%) was the heaviest large-cap drag, an echo of Monday's NAB −4.7% slump on negative-gearing repeal risk and BEN −5.29% on results — but the deeper driver is global: the US 30-year Treasury yield hit a 19-year high, and long-duration, rate-sensitive Australian sectors (banks, REITs, staples) sold in sympathy. Materials (XMJ +0.15%) was a wash: BHP +2.65% post-result versus SGM −11.04%, MAH −8.59% and a −7.31% give-back in Liontown as lithium's leadership passed decisively to gold.

Gold is the new Smart Money #1 (from rank 25 nine sessions ago), and the ASX200/300 relative-strength board is now gold-saturated — VAU, CMM, GMD, RRL all inside the institutional top twelve. With bullion at ~US$4,415/oz and global long yields breaking out, the rotation into precious metals as a duration hedge is the cleanest cross-market theme on the board. The RBA sits on hold at 4.35% (next decision 29 September), so the domestic rate signal is quiet — the pressure on rate-sensitives is being imported through the US long end.
