📝 Session Summary

Monday 17 August was an earnings-driven large-cap sell-down rather than a broad de-risking. The XJO eased −0.46% to 9,073.2 and the All Ords −0.37% to 9,279.0, but the Small Ords finished +0.49% — a clean capitalisation divergence that mirrored Friday's US session, where a −0.6% retail-sales miss rotated money into the Russell 2000 (+0.51%) over mega-caps.

JB Hi-Fi (JBH −12.31%) was the story of the day. The FY26 result itself was solid; a cautious outlook on discretionary spending did the damage, and it dragged the entire Consumer Discretionary sector (XDJ −3.00%, worst on the board) with it — SUL −6.64% and PMV −4.71% fell in sympathy ahead of their own results. Lendlease (LLC −11.15%), IRESS (IRE −11.63%) and Aurizon (AZJ −10.34%, which fell despite winning a major Queensland coal-haulage contract) completed a brutal large-cap results slate.

Financials (XFJ −1.76%) were the heaviest index drag by weight. NAB −4.62% on a soft Q3 trading update set the tone and SUN −5.08% followed. With the banks and discretionary both down, the large-cap benchmark had little chance of holding green regardless of what resources did.

And resources did plenty. Materials (XMJ +1.76%) topped the board on a broad gold-miner rally — EVN +5.15%, GMD +4.46%, CMM +4.30%, VAU +3.72% — with copper firm (+1.82% on the LME). Gold jumped to Smart Money rank 2 (from 34 nine days ago) and six of the ten ASX200/300 RS leaders are now gold names: a precious-metals re-rating with genuine breadth. Healthcare's index was red (−0.85%) but masked huge single-stock wins — ACL +14.89% and HLS +13.75% on results, MSB +10.86% on a Phase 3 trial milestone.

Structurally the large-cap uptrend is intact: the XJO holds +1.91% above its 50MA, T2108 sits at 66.5%, and 20-day highs (22) still beat lows (17). Today reads as earnings dispersion inside an uptrend. The real test is tomorrow — BHP, CSL, Cochlear, Pro Medicus, Challenger and Sims all report, opening the mega-cap results wave.
