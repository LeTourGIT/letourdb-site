Session Summary

Monday 14 September closed fractionally green — XJO +0.10% to 8,749.90, XAO +0.04% to 8,923.90 — but the headline flatters a soft session. Wall Street surged on Friday and SPI futures pointed the ASX ~0.2% higher, yet the index gave back an early +0.31% to finish barely positive as heavyweights BHP, RIO, NAB, Goodman and Fortescue all dragged. This was a narrow, defensive tape, not a broad advance.

The rotation was textbook late-cycle risk-off. Health Care (+1.52%), Consumer Staples (+0.61%), Financials (+0.43%), Energy (+0.38%) and Utilities (+0.33%) led; Materials (-0.55%) and IT (-1.14%) lagged. UBS framed it precisely — equities are selling off as yields rise, the inverse pattern that forms when inflation, not deflation, is the dominant fear. With the Fed ~80–90% to hike on 16 September and the US 10yr closing on 5%, rate-sensitive growth is being repriced in real time.

Health Care led on a clean catalyst: Telix (TLX +4.28%) won FDA approval for its brain-cancer imaging drug Pixclara — a genuine institutional re-rating, not flow. Corporate activity was the other bright spot, with FleetPartners (FPR +12.11%) on revised non-binding takeover offers and Cleanaway (CWY +4.67%) on the EQT Infrastructure proposal. Materials was the drag: iron ore has slipped below US$100/t (~$98) and the sector is now down roughly 6% over three sessions.

Beneath the surface the tape was heavy. Uranium was hit hard (PDN -6.91%, DYL -8.83%, both extending 5-day losses beyond -18%), IT growth names de-rated (MP1 -8.30%), and Core Lithium (CXO +13.04%) spiked to a two-year high on no news — short-covering rather than fundamentals, with lithium carbonate still soft. Breadth told the real story: XJO advancers 93 versus decliners 94, and 40 new 20-day lows against just 4 new highs.

The read into the back half of September: this is an oversold market with no confirmed bid, sitting under a synchronized central-bank tightening (Fed 16 Sep, RBA 29 Sep). The defensives, energy and gold leadership is where the money is hiding, and the takeover cluster (FPR, CWY) shows corporates still see value — but until breadth turns and yields stabilise, rallies are to be sold rather than chased.
