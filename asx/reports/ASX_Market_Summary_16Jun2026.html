Four index closes inside a 5-basis-point band is about as flat as a session gets — but it masks an intraday round-trip. ASX 200 futures opened down 1.10%, the cash index traded as low as -0.90% by mid-morning, then fully recovered into the close. The RBA hold removed one source of uncertainty at 2:30pm; the bigger driver was the market re-pricing how much of the overnight Wall Street rally was "real" versus an oil-relief bounce that doesn't fully translate to AUD-denominated, ASX-listed resource earnings.
Session Summary

Tuesday's session was a study in contrasts: the headline indices closed within a hair of unchanged, yet underneath sat one of the more violent sector and industry rotations of the year. The catalyst was external — Trump's weekend announcement of a "complete" US-Iran deal to end hostilities and reopen the Strait of Hormuz triggered a Wall Street rally (Nasdaq +3.07%, its best day since March) and sent oil into a multi-day collapse (WTI -22.33% over 20 days per the delayed commodity feed). The RBA's 2:30pm hold at 4.35% was, by contrast, a non-event — every major bank had pencilled in exactly this outcome, so the index-level reaction was negligible. The real story was the unwind of the war-risk premium that had been embedded across markets for weeks.

That unwind showed up most clearly in the Smart Money industry rotation table, where capital rotated almost wholesale out of defense, coal, and critical-minerals exposure and into insurance, consumer staples retail, employment services, and construction supplies — a textbook "peace dividend" trade. Aerospace & Defense collapsed from #3 to #76 in the 10day rotation table in just nine sessions, a direct mirror of the US "Defense Stocks Are in a Bear Market" narrative running through MarketWatch overnight. Energy (XEJ +1.10%) was, paradoxically, the day's best-performing ASX sector even as the underlying commodity it's named for fell — a value/mean-reversion bounce off a three-month low rather than a fundamentals re-rating, since the sector is still down -4.43% over five days.

At the stock level, the standout was Southern Cross Electrical Engineering (SXE +19.90%), which resumed from a trading halt with a combination of new contract wins, an earnings upgrade, and a $150m institutional placement — the headline item being a Letter of Authorisation from Multiplex to begin electrical and communications works on the first stage of NEXTDC's S4 data centre in Sydney's Horsley Park. That's a direct, ASX-specific data centre/hyperscaler-capex thread, the same theme that has powered NXT for over a year. On the losing side, the critical-minerals complex took the hardest hit from the de-escalation trade: Liontown (LTR -8.48%), Core Lithium (CXO -8.33%) and IperionX (IPX -13.79%) all fell sharply as the urgency behind non-China critical-minerals diversification eases with Hormuz reopening, while Karoon Energy (KAR -11.56%) wore the most direct oil-price hit of any ASX-listed name today. Gold miners bucked the resources weakness entirely — Dateline Resources (DTR +15.38%), Predictive Discovery (PDI +8.09%) and Catalyst Metals (CYL +7.10%) all featured in the top 15 gainers, a flight-to-quality pocket inside an otherwise de-risking resources trade.

Breadth told its own quieter story: the All Ordinaries actually saw MORE decliners than advancers (233 vs 203) despite closing positive — a classic cap-weighting divergence where a handful of large names carried the index while the median stock lagged. The ASX 200's breadth was healthier (104 advancers vs 84 decliners, T2108 at a comfortable 64.5%), but only 44.5% of XJO constituents sit above their 200-day MA, underscoring that today's calm headline number sits on top of still-uneven structural footing. Consumer Discretionary (XDJ -1.15%) was the session's clear underperformer, snapping a six-day winning streak as the sector gave back some of its recent run.

Looking ahead, today's calm belies a busy stretch: the Fed delivers its own decision tomorrow (17 June, >98% priced for a hold), domestic CPI lands a week from Wednesday (24 June) and will be the first real test of whether the oil-price relief is feeding through to headline inflation, and the ASX 200's quarterly rebalance takes effect on 22 June — only six sessions away — with the index continuing its multi-quarter tilt toward resources and "sovereignty-critical" names at the expense of consumer discretionary and technology constituents. China's May retail sales posting their first outright drop in over three years is a fresh demand-side headline worth tracking against that backdrop, even though Materials (XMJ -0.19%) shrugged it off today.
