Session Summary

Friday 4 September was a shallow risk-off drift at the index level (XJO -0.16% to 9,005.9) that masked a sharp rotation underneath. The heavyweight resources and energy blocks dragged — Materials -0.80% (BHP -2.40%, 5day -7.5%) and Energy -1.22% (ALD -6.01%, VEA -3.65%) — while a US-led technology bid pulled Info Technology +1.00% and Communication Services +0.98% to the top of the board.

The overnight cue was strong: SPX +1.06%, Nasdaq +1.4%, Dow +1.18% (its best day since 4 August) after Fed Governor Waller flagged ‘disinflation’ and eased US hike bets. But that relief is US-specific. Australia is travelling the opposite direction on rates — NAB is now the first big-four bank to call a 29 September RBA hike to 4.60% — so the tailwind lifted ASX tech but not the rate-sensitive or resources complexes, leaving the index fractionally red.

Stock-level, the story was M&A and gold. Stanmore (SMR +3.06%, [H] 20day high) agreed to acquire Moranbah South, and Loyal Metals (LLM) advanced its scheme of arrangement to implementation — corporate action was the session’s spine on an otherwise quiet macro tape. FRS +18.31% was the standout, confirmed by a 14:52 price-sensitive filing completing the Edna May gold acquisition — an evidenced catalyst, not momentum.

Gold miners continue to own the relative-strength table (VAU, RSG [H], GMD, PRU [H], OBM) even as spot gold paused -0.31%; the RS regime is unambiguously gold-led at both the institutional and speculative cap tiers. The clearest large-cap loser was Nine Entertainment (NEC -8.50%, ASX200), tied to an 08:35 price-sensitive share-acquisition disclosure.

Breadth was deceptively constructive — XJO advancers led decliners 124:65 and small-caps ran 2.66:1 — but only 37.5% of XJO names sit above their 20MA, so the advance was broad yet shallow. The read: a gold and energy pause, plus building RBA hike anxiety 25 days out, are capping an otherwise firm tape.
