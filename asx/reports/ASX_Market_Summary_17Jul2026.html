Session Summary

Friday 17/07/2026 closed lower — XJO −0.50% to 8,796.70, XAO −0.64% — but the headline understates a violent internal rotation. Seven of eleven sectors actually finished higher; the index was dragged down almost entirely by Materials (XMJ −2.91%), which closed at its lowest since 7 April, right on its 200-day moving average.

The miners were the story. BHP (−2.3% to −2.6%) fell after hitting FY26 output and cost targets but guiding FY27 production below consensus across copper, WAIO and BMA — a soft forward read that overshadowed a clean FY26. Add industrial action threatening the Port Hedland iron-ore terminal and a firmer-US-yield backdrop pressuring gold and base metals, and the whole complex de-rated: RIO −2.7%, Northern Star −3.6%, Evolution −3.8%. Mining names accounted for effectively all of the top ASX 200 laggards.

Underneath sat a textbook risk-off rotation into defensives and yield: Utilities (XUJ +1.77%, APA +2.29%), Energy (XEJ +1.66%, WDS +3.29% on the Hormuz oil premium), Consumer Staples (XSJ +1.06%, COL +2.88%) and Comm. Services (XTJ +1.62%, TLS +2.65%). AMP +6.32% — a new 20-day high with RS 96.17 — was the single standout: the top ASX200 mover and a genuine institutional-grade breakout on a broadly red tape.

The small-cap and speculative tiers took the real pain. XSO −1.95%, and the Emerging Companies index is now at its lowest since 24 March, down 8.3% over nine sessions. The losers board was a wall of critical-minerals and micro-cap resources (lithium: EUR, LRV, GLN, EQR; rare-earth/tin/gold developers) plus a few blown-up tech/biotech names (WBT −10.4%, 4DX −15.6%, MSB −11.9%). This is risk being pulled from the leveraged end of the market — not a broad financial de-rating. The ASX20 held together while the tail was cut.
