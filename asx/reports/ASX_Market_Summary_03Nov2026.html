📝 Session Summary

Melbourne Cup day delivered the RBA's fifth hike of 2026: +25bp to 4.85%, the highest cash rate since November 2008. The XJO still closed +0.34% at 8,886.8, recovering Monday's -0.58%. It opened about 0.6% higher, gave the gain back into 2:30pm and briefly dipped red on the decision. It then rallied once the details landed: a 6-3 split, a statement with no tightening bias, and a Governor emphasising lags. That is a peak-rate signal, and the tape priced it.

The tell is which sectors led. Real Estate (XRE +2.88%) topped the board with LLC +5.5%, SGP +4.0%, SCG +3.7% and GPT +3.6%. Consumer Discretionary (+1.87%) extended Monday's pre-positioning, with TPW +7.0%, HVN +5.8% and NCK +5.6%. The sectors that should suffer from higher rates outperformed the ones that shouldn't, which is the market calling the top of the cycle.

Energy (XEJ -3.45%) was the casualty after WTI settled -7.36% on US-Iran talks hopes. WDS fell -3.5%, ALD -5.0%, VEA -6.6%, YAL -6.4% and NHC -5.4%, and uranium (NXG, DYL) went with the complex. The mirror image was the airlines: VGN +8.9% and QAN +6.4% (a 20day high), as jet fuel is their largest variable cost.

Breadth confirmed the large-cap tape but not the small-cap one. XJO advancers led 123/71, new 20day highs rose to 48 (from 38) and lows fell to 12 (from 20). XJO stocks above their 200MA improved to 41.5% from 37.0%, but the XAO (34.1%) and XSO (32.9%) show two-thirds of the broad market still in long-term downtrends.

The forward look is crowded. US midterms run tonight with the VIX at 21. NAB reports Thursday and MQG Friday. Avenira (AEV) entered a late trading halt at 15:25. Oil remains the master variable: fresh US strike reports on Iranian targets capped the intraday rally, and WTI is only +0.33% at report time.
