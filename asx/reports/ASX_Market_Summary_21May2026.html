This is the single most consequential piece of domestic macro data since the March CPI print drove rates expectations in the opposite direction three weeks ago.

The market's "bad news is good news" interpretation was textbook rate-sensitive logic — and the sector ordering told the story precisely. Financials surged +1.45%, the same sector that led declines on every prior RBA anxiety session. REITs +2.21% were the second-strongest sector. When REITs and Financials rally simultaneously, the market is explicitly pricing out imminent rate action. Both sectors face structural headwinds at 4.35%+ and have been the largest underperformers all year. One weak jobs print does not end the RBA's tightening bias — inflation at 4.6% remains 160bp above the target ceiling — but it removes the immediate trigger pressure and leaves the two 2026 hikes potentially representing the cycle peak.

WTI crude -5.66% overnight to sub-$100 provided the second structural pillar. Iran deal proximity removes the energy inflation premium that has been embedded in RBA models since February when the Hormuz blockade began. Sub-$100 WTI structurally changes the RBA's inputs for its June review — energy costs have been the most persistent driver of above-band CPI and the primary justification for continued tightening. With oil retreating and unemployment rising, the June meeting is now almost certainly a hold.
