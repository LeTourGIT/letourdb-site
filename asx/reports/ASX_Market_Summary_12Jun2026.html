Wall Street logged its best session since the 8 April ceasefire announcement after Trump called off planned strikes on Iran and signalled a draft peace deal could land within days — the S&P 500 +1.75%, Nasdaq +2.54%, and the Russell 2000 led with +3.02% as small-caps led the risk-on rotation. Crucially for the ASX, the de-escalation hit oil hard: WTI -4.68% and Brent -6.1% overnight, while a broad commodity-ETF bounce saw Strategic Metals +9.2%, Copper Miners +7.4%, Lithium & Battery Tech +5.5%, Gold Miners +5.3% and Steel +4.6% — this is the direct transmission mechanism for today's resources-led ASX surge.

Note the ES/NQ futures are pulling back slightly (-0.36% / -0.80%) from the overnight cash close at the time of this report — some of the euphoria is fading into the US afternoon, a mild caution flag for how durable today's ASX strength proves into next week. ⚠️ RBA Watch — Meeting Tuesday 16 June (4 days away)

Current Cash Rate: 4.35% after three hikes in 2026 (Feb, Mar, May; cumulative +75bp, fully unwinding 2025's easing). A Reuters poll of 45 economists is near-unanimous for a hold at 4.35% on 16 June (42 of 45), with a rate hike priced at under 2% and a cut effectively priced at zero.

The real story is the messaging, not the decision. Macquarie's equity strategists argue markets are under-pricing the chance the RBA keeps a further hike "on the table" — their internal probability split is hawkish-hold 55%, balanced-hold 22.5%, dovish-hold 12.5%, hike 7.5%. Globally, 10 of 11 developed-market central banks are now priced for further tightening (the ECB delivered its first hike since 2023 overnight), which constrains the RBA's room to sound dovish without risking AUD weakness and imported inflation.

Looking past Tuesday: of 44 economists surveyed on the end-September rate, 26 still see 4.35% (i.e. done), while 18 see 4.60% or higher (Westpac is the most hawkish outlier, flagging a possible 4.85% peak). NAB has now dropped its prior call for an August hike and argues the next move is more likely down — but with no timing conviction. Rate-sensitive sectors (REITs, consumer discretionary, banks) rallied hard today regardless (XRE +1.82%, XDJ +2.05%, XFJ +1.61%) — today's move is a US-led risk rotation, not an RBA pre-positioning trade, but a hawkish tone on Tuesday could quickly reverse the real estate and discretionary gains. Resources and energy names with USD-denominated revenues remain comparatively insulated from the domestic rate cycle.

📝 Session Summary

Materials (XMJ +4.06%) drove the ASX 200's sharpest single-session gain in months, lifting the benchmark +1.98% to 8,804.0 and erasing Thursday's slide to a four-month low. The catalyst was unambiguous: Trump's overnight decision to call off planned strikes on Iran and signal an imminent peace deal sparked a broad Wall Street risk-on bounce (S&P 500 +1.75%, Nasdaq +2.54%, Russell 2000 +3.02%), and crucially collapsed the oil-driven inflation premium that has been weighing on risk assets all week (WTI -4.68%, Brent -6.1% overnight). The flow-through into ASX resources was immediate and broad-based: Liontown (LTR) +9.82%, Pilbara Minerals (PLS) +9.76%, Elevra Lithium (ELV) +12.49% [H], Develop Global (DVP) +10.37% [H] and Minerals 260 (MI6) +14.67% all surged as the overnight Lithium & Battery Tech (+5.5%) and Copper Miners (+7.4%) ETF moves found their ASX equivalents.

Gold told a more nuanced story. Bullion itself was little-changed to slightly lower in AUD/USD terms (Gold -0.64% per the data feed, and the overnight wrap flagged gold at a six-month low as SpaceX's record $75bn IPO drains liquidity from defensive assets) — yet ASX gold miners were sharply two-way: Genesis Minerals (GMD) +10.83%, Pantoro (PNR) +9.57% and Black Cat Syndicate (BC8) +9.77% rode the broader risk-on Gold Miners ETF bounce (+5.3% overnight), while Dateline Resources (DTR) -11.54% and Forrestania Resources (FRS) -5% reversed hard. This is a sector being driven by overnight momentum and stock-specific newsflow rather than the gold price itself — a useful reminder not to read gold-miner strength as a pure bullion signal today.

Breadth confirmed the rally's width: XJO advancers led decliners 160 to 36 (a 4.4:1 ratio), with 126 of 200 constituents above their 20MA and 59 stocks notching new 20day highs against just 4 new 20day lows. Across the broader All Ordinaries, advancers beat decliners 345 to 110 (3.1:1). Financials (XFJ +1.61%), Real Estate (XRE +1.82%) and Consumer Discretionary (XDJ +2.05%) all participated strongly — rate-sensitive sectors rallying into next Tuesday's RBA decision, which is itself a touch counter-intuitive given the meeting is still live, but is best read as these sectors simply being high-beta participants in a broad global risk-on day rather than an RBA-specific repricing.

The only soft spots were Energy (XEJ -0.46%) and Communication Services (XTJ -0.29%) — Energy's underperformance is the direct mirror of the overnight oil collapse, while Viva Energy (VEA) -3.43% was among today's worst ASX200 performers, a name whose refining margins are squeezed by the same oil-price air-pocket. Smart Money's 10day rotation table tells a different story to today's tape: the top-ranked industries by 10day capital flow are still domestically-defensive — Multiline Insurance & Brokers (#1), Specialty Chemicals (#2) and Distillers & Wineries (#4) — suggesting today's resources/tech-led bounce is a sharp one-day catch-up trade layered on top of a medium-term rotation that has, until now, been quietly favouring insurance, consumer staples and domestic services names (Coles and Woolworths' five-day gains of +8.8% each in the heatmap reflect this).

Gold/commodity divergence: spot gold (yfinance, -0.64% to $4,193.70) sits near a six-month low per overnight commentary, even though the broad commodity-ETF complex (Gold Miners +5.3%, Silver Miners +6.5%, Strategic Metals +9.2%) surged overnight on the same Iran de-escalation news that hit oil. Lithium carbonate (web search, not yet in 7d feed): global benchmark prices sit around US$24,000–26,000/t (≈$24/kg), still up enormously from the ~$13,400/t low in December but showing a softer weekly trend through early June per SMM data.
