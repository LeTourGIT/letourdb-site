Session Summary

Thursday 2 July closed the ASX 200 fractionally higher (+0.02% to 8,724.5), but the headline flatters a defensive, negatively-skewed session. Beneath the surface, decliners beat advancers 108 to 85 on the XJO and new 20day lows (17) outnumbered new highs (6) — distribution dressed up as calm, not genuine balance.

The global driver was the semiconductor rout: Samsung and SK Hynix fell ~9% and SoftBank −12% in Asia as AI-infrastructure cost concerns spread from Wall Street. That flowed straight into ASX growth/tech — NXT −5.87%, MP1 −4.91%, WBT soft — and IT (XIJ) was among the weakest sectors at −1.14%. Utilities (XUJ −3.58%, ORG −5.42%) and Consumer Discretionary (XDJ −2.17%; WES −4.03%, ARB −4.37%, APE −4.29%) were worse still, the latter pressured by accelerating housing-downturn headlines.

The other side of the rotation was unambiguous: gold and precious-metals miners led the gainers board — MEK +19.57%, Ora Banda +8.33%, Black Cat +6.98%, Catalyst +6.24%, Asara +12.50% — with gold itself holding ~US$4,084 after its late-June dip below US$4,000. Medical-device and biotech spec names also surged (IMR +12.47% [H], EBR +15.79%, AVH +15.38%, Dimerix +16.28%), and Financials (XFJ +1.17%) was the only clearly green sector. Money left AI/semis and rate-sensitive growth and bought gold, healthcare and banks.

Materials (XMJ −0.02%) looked flat but was a split tape — gold up, iron-ore majors leaking with SGX iron ore near US$100/t on soft Chinese steel demand. AUD/USD at 0.6895 (−0.01%) was a non-event, removing the usual miner-revenue cross-current. With the RBA on hold at 4.35% and its next decision not until 30 July, there is no near-term domestic catalyst to arrest the growth-to-defensive rotation — the offshore chip tape is in control.
