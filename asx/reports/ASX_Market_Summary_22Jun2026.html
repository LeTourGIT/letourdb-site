📝 Session Summary

Monday delivered a textbook "index says nothing, internals say plenty" session. The XJO eased 0.14% to 8,816.1 and the All Ords 0.18% to 9,031.2 — cosmetic declines that masked a decisive rotation. Despite a strongly positive US lead (Nasdaq +1.91% Friday, futures higher again Monday), two domestic blocks dragged the tape: Information Technology and Materials.

The session's defining event was WiseTech (WTC −18.4% to ~A$30.08), which cratered to multi-year lows after media reports that the Australian Federal Police are investigating Executive Chairman and founder Richard White over serious allegations relating to a former employee. For a stock already carrying the ASX's largest governance discount — a 2024 CEO exit, a trading halt, an insider-trading probe and a 2,000-job AI restructuring — this is pure key-person risk repricing. WTC alone drove the IT index (XIJ) down 4.20%, by far the worst sector. The underlying CargoWise franchise (1H NPAT A$114.5m, guidance reaffirmed) is intact; this is a sentiment and trust event, not an earnings event.

Materials (XMJ −0.61%, −4.93% over 5 days) was the second anchor, with iron ore stuck near US$101/t (−8% over the month) and lithium range-bound as Australian mine restarts (MinRes Bald Hill, Core Lithium Finniss) add supply. Note this is a sharp regime change from early May's lithium-led "resources rally" — the resources complex is now a drag, not a leader, and the AUD's slide to ~0.70 (from a 0.72 four-year high six weeks ago) is the partial offset cushioning USD-denominated miner revenues.

Where the money went is the real story. Consumer Discretionary (+0.56%), Financials (+0.50%), Industrials (+0.48%) and Utilities (+0.32%) led — the domestic, rate-sensitive, cyclical end of the market. Gold miners were the standout in the gainer board (BNZ +11%, OBM +6.6%, KCN +4.6%, with VAU/RRL strong) on a firm gold price (~$4,216) and a defensive-rotation bid. Construction names (SXE +5.0%, FBU +4.9%, SRG, PRN) confirmed the infrastructure/housing-fix theme that also dominates the Smart Money table.

The macro enabler is the RBA's 16 June hold at 4.35% — the first pause after three consecutive 2026 hikes — with NAB now flagging the next move is more likely down, and the US-Iran peace roadmap reopening the Strait of Hormuz and easing the oil-inflation impulse that justified the hikes. A market that believes rates have peaked rotates exactly this way: out of crowded megacap growth, into beaten-up domestic cyclicals, financials, insurers and small-caps. XSO's Bullish regime is that thesis in price.

📰 Key Headlines

WiseTech — governance shock
WTC fell ~14–18% to multi-year lows (~A$30) after reports the AFP is investigating Executive Chairman Richard White over serious allegations. The CargoWise business and FY guidance are intact; this is key-person/governance repricing on a stock already carrying the ASX's largest governance discount. Single-handedly drove IT (XIJ) −4.20%.
RBA on hold — reflation enabler
The RBA held at 4.35% on 16 June (unanimous), its first pause after three 2026 hikes. NAB now sees the next move as more likely down; markets price roughly even odds of one more hike. Next meeting 10–11 August. A market that believes rates have peaked is rotating into rate-sensitive domestic cyclicals — visible across today's heatmap and Smart Money table.
US-Iran roadmap · Hormuz reopens
A US-Iran peace roadmap and resumed Strait of Hormuz shipping have pulled oil lower (WTI ~$75), unwinding the energy-inflation impulse that justified the RBA's hikes. The clearest medium-term disinflationary signal for Australian rates — and a tailwind for rate-peak positioning.
China trade curbs · iron ore soft
China imposed trade curbs on dozens of US firms in retaliation for a Pentagon blacklist, and iron ore sits near US$101/t (−8% MoM). Together they keep the BHP/RIO/FMG complex on the back foot — the Materials drag is as much a China-demand story as a price story.
Gold miners bid · small-caps lead
With gold firm near $4,216, ASX gold names (BNZ, OBM, KCN, VAU, RRL) led the gainer board, and the Small Ords (XSO) printed a full Bullish regime. The defensive-gold plus domestic-cyclical combination is the signature of a market rotating away from crowded megacap growth.
No ASX earnings reporters this session (non-reporting window). Forward watch: AU June-qtr CPI is the key print before the RBA's 10–11 Aug meeting; US Micron earnings + inflation data this week.
