Session Summary

The ASX 200 fell 137.8 points (-1.58%) to 8,592.6, its seventh straight loss, with every one of the 11 sectors closing red. The trigger was offshore: Wall Street's 2% slide on Anthropic's listing day turned the world's largest IPO into a liquidity drain on the rest of the AI complex, and Asia followed (Nikkei -4.8%, SoftBank -16%). The All Ords lost 1.63% and the Small Ords 2.71% -- the further down the cap curve, the harder the selling.

Information Technology (XIJ -4.70%) took the full force. NextDC (NXT -7.11%) is the session's most telling print: it lodged a FY27 GUIDANCE UPGRADE at 08:24 and still fell 7% -- when good news is sold this hard, positioning, not fundamentals, is driving price. Megaport (MP1 -12.89%), Xero (XRO -5.65%), WiseTech (WTC -3.37%) and Macquarie Technology (MAQ -3.52%) completed a de-rating of anything priced off AI/data-centre demand. None of MP1, XRO or WTC had an announcement -- this was pure sector flow.

Materials (XMJ -2.15%) extended a brutal week to -12.32% over 5 days -- the worst sector drawdown on the board. The damage is in gold and copper, not iron ore: the Global X Physical Gold ETF (GOLD -5.29%) caught down to the overnight bullion slump and gold miners fell for an eighth straight day (NST -3.73%, EVN -4.78%, NEM -5.58%), while copper-exposed names and mining services (PRN -11.23%) remained under pressure. Yet the iron ore and lithium end held: FMG +2.13%, PLS +2.34%, ELV +2.10%, RIO flat -- iron ore steady near US$91/t keeps the bulk miners as the relative safe harbour inside the resources complex.

Domestic macro is compounding the offshore shock. Thursday's labour data (43,500 jobs lost; unemployment 4.9%) has the ABC carrying three separate recession-warning headlines, yet with the cash rate at 4.85% and inflation still above band, the market is not getting the usual 'bad news = rate cuts' cushion. Banks slipped (WBC -2.23%, NAB -1.31%, CBA -1.00%) and consumer credit was punished -- Zip (ZIP -9.41%) is the purest expression of rising unemployment meeting high rates. Only Utilities (XUJ -0.53%) and Staples (XSJ -0.84%) held under -1%.

Breadth confirms capitulation-grade selling: 19 advancers against 175 decliners in the ASX 200 (9.8% up), zero stocks up 4% versus 29 down 4%, 71 new 20day lows against 3 highs, and T2108 cut from 50.2% to 39.2% in one session. Readings this lopsided often mark the late stage of a flush rather than the start of one -- but a durable low needs a catalyst, and the next two are Friday night's US session (ES flat after hours) and Tuesday's October CPI.

Australia lost 43,500 jobs in October against expectations of a gain, and the ABC ran three separate recession-warning stories off the print. With the RBA at 4.85% after five hikes this year, the market faces the worst mix for equities: a weakening economy that the central bank cannot yet cut into. Consumer credit (ZIP) and discretionary are first in line.

🏢 Anthropic lists in record IPO -- and the AI trade falls anyway

The most duplicated story in the feed (ABC Business, ABC World, CNBC, MarketWatch): ANT priced at US$83, opened at US$111.50 and closed +17%, yet the Dow fell ~1,100 points. Australians largely missed the allocation. Locally the listing worked as a funding drain on AI-adjacent stocks -- NXT, MP1 and MAQ were sold to make room.

🇺🇸 Nvidia has shed US$611bn in six sessions; SoftBank -16%

Nvidia's slide, California's subpoena over China chip diversion and AMD's short-report drop put semiconductors at the centre of the sell-off, and SoftBank's 16% plunge carried it through Asia. ASX semis (WBT -8.02%, SLX -7.89%) followed.

🏢 Xi declares 'complete turnaround' in China-Australia relations

After the APEC meeting (Trump, Xi and Putin issued a joint declaration), Xi's comments are a medium-term positive for Australian exporters, but iron ore near US$91/t and falling Chinese steel output say demand has not yet followed the diplomacy.

🏢 Rio Tinto pledges $370m to Bougainville landowners

A step toward resolving the Panguna legacy, which matters for Rio's licence to operate in the region. RIO closed flat (+0.04%) while Materials fell 2.15% -- the bulk miners remain the defensive end of the resources trade.
