Session Summary

Thursday 9 July delivered a deceptive session. The XJO fell just -0.26% to 8,762.5, yet eight of eleven sectors closed green. The index was dragged down entirely by the two sectors that dominate its capitalisation — Materials (XMJ -1.48%) and Financials (XFJ -0.15%), together roughly 40% of the benchmark. This is a cap-weight illusion, not a broad sell-off. Breadth confirms the ambiguity rather than resolving it: 90 advancers against 103 decliners (0.87:1), two stocks up more than 4% against three down more than 4%. Nobody was in a hurry to sell. Nobody bought.

The Materials story is the whole story. Iron ore is pinned near a one-year low after Chinese port inventories reached a record ~160Mt, and the China Mineral Resources Group / Fortescue standoff over Super Special Fines has escalated from negotiation into an open pricing war — mills have been ordered to take delivery before 15 July or see the product blacklisted. S32 -3.54% is the clean read. More telling, the gold complex sold off hard (PNR -10.18%, CYL -4.97%, BNZ -3.56%) while spot gold rose +1.10% to $4,116.80. When producers fall on a rising metal, the market is pricing operational and cost risk, not commodity risk. Layer in China's June data — consumer prices weakening while producer inflation hit a near-four-year high — and you have the worst configuration imaginable for Australian resource exporters: Chinese factory-gate prices rising while consumer demand stalls. XMJ is -4.21% over five sessions.

Energy (XEJ +1.67%) led, for the plainest reason available: the Iran ceasefire collapsed. US strikes resumed, Iran says it struck US bases, and crude spiked roughly 6% overnight before easing back today (WTI $73.25 -1.39%, Brent $77.71 -1.47%). NHC +5.45% and TBN +6.82% captured the war premium. But the Smart Money table proves this is not a one-day trade: Oil & Gas E&P has surged from rank #71 to #3 in nine sessions, Oil & Gas Refining #67 → #5, Integrated Oil & Gas #69 → #6. Three energy sub-industries in the top six. Institutional capital rotated into energy before the ceasefire broke. The headline confirmed the flow; it did not cause it.

Real Estate (XRE -1.11%) was the second-worst sector and the tell on rates. SGP -3.67% and CHC -3.28% fell because the FOMC minutes showed members arguing for further hikes on energy-driven inflation. The RBA sits at 4.35% after three consecutive 2026 hikes (February, March, May) and held on 30 June; the next meeting is 10–11 August, a month away. There is no immediate event risk — but a renewed oil shock is the single variable that reopens the tightening door, and every REIT and consumer name is now trading the oil price by proxy.

Beneath the index, the domestic housing cyclical is quietly the strongest thing on the board. Homebuilding is Smart Money #1 with a 14.20% 10day ROC and has held the top rank for four straight sessions — entrenchment, not a spike. JIN +10.46%, FBU +7.55% (Construction Supplies), TPW +5.19% and ADH +4.42% (Home Furnishings), TEA +4.59% and MGH +4.55% (Construction & Engineering). Record-high Australian rents and a genuine supply crunch are being expressed through builders and home retailers rather than through the banks. That is a durable, ASX-specific theme with no US equivalent — and it is compounding while the index goes nowhere.
