📝 Session Summary

Wednesday 24 June was a tale of two markets colliding. Overnight, Wall Street suffered a sharp memory-chip rout (Nasdaq -2.21%, Micron -13%, KOSPI -10%) that should, on any normal cross-market read, have dragged the ASX lower. Instead the XJO closed +0.24% at 8,808.4 — snapping a four-day losing streak — because two local forces overrode the offshore tape: a violent tech short-squeeze and the ASX's structural lack of mega-cap chip exposure.

Information Technology (XIJ +5.21%) delivered the session's most dramatic — and most puzzling — move. WiseTech Global (WTC +14.26% to $32.86) staged a violent reversal from its preceding two-session ~22% governance-driven collapse, despite no material new announcement. The rebound looked far more like short-covering and a delayed read on WTC's statement that it was not party to any legal proceedings than any genuine resolution of the AFP-probe overhang. Xero (XRO +8.17%), tethered to WTC and helped by a positive Citi note, rode the same wave. This is the "quality growth repair" theme — paying up for recurring-revenue software — but built today on flows, not fundamentals.

The market's calm reaction to the CPI print — Financials +0.27%, Real Estate +0.64%, both green — signals it is pricing the August RBA meeting as a coin-flip rather than a hike, helped by the mechanical fuel-excise buffer on headline.

The flip side of the tech squeeze was carnage in the resource and gold complex. Materials (XMJ -0.63%) and Energy (XEJ -1.04%) were the only two red sectors — a direct expression of the Hormuz-reopening commodity unwind: Brent slid below US$76 to its lowest since the war began, iron ore sits near US$100.5/t on soft Chinese demand, gold eased to ~US$4,076. Gold miners were the day's worst cohort — Predictive Discovery (PDI -11.23%), Bellevue (BGL -6.03%), Alkane (ALK -5.61%), Ora Banda (OBM -5.28%) — alongside Beach Energy (BPT -8.51%). The rotation is clean: out of inflation-hedge real assets, into growth and duration as the oil-shock impulse fades.

Breadth confirmed the bounce was narrow-but-real: XJO advancers led decliners 102/89 (1.15:1) — a win, but hardly the broad 3:1 thrust of a conviction rally. The takeaway: a structurally-intact market staged a tech-led, short-covering bounce on a mixed-inflation day, with the August RBA meeting still the circuit-breaker ahead.
