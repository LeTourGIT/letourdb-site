All four indices carry the Excel regime label "Bearish": the MA(10) < MA(20) < MA(50) stack is fully aligned to the downside, the most damaged state on the decode scale. But there is a first crack. XJO and XTO closed back above their 10day and 20day MAs today ('Above 10/20 Day MA: Yes') while the MAs themselves are still stacked bearish. Price reclaiming the short MAs before they turn is the usual first step of a repair, but it fails more often than it works until the 50MA (~8,936) is retaken. XSO is the outlier: below every MA, 15day ROC -3.83 and RSI 37.4, so the small-cap complex has not participated in the repair at all.

No regime divergence, since all four are Bearish. There is a magnitude divergence, though. XTO (ASX 100) is 0.77% below its 200MA with a positive 15day ROC, while XSO (Small Ords) is 7.19% below it. Large caps are holding the line while small caps (heavy in lithium, uranium and explorers) absorb the damage. Breadth rotating back into small caps would be the tell for a durable low.

RBA: the next meeting is 03/11/2026, 25 days away, so there is no RBA Watch this week. ASX rate futures price roughly a 27% chance of a November hike (rbaratewatch.com). 📝 Session Summary

The ASX 200 recovered most of Thursday's loss, adding 0.64% (+55.7 points) to 8,716.6, and the rally was broad: 140 advancers to 48 decliners (2.9:1), with 10 of 11 sectors green. What matters is who led. Technology (XIJ +2.23%), Consumer Discretionary (+2.16%), Real Estate (+1.84%) and Utilities (+1.75%) carried the index, while Materials finished flat. That is a domestic-growth and yield rotation, and it came on a night when the Nasdaq fell 1.25%. The ASX took its cue from the US equal-weight index (+0.60%) and the 5bp drop in the US 10-year, not from Nvidia.

Resources split three ways. Gold producers rallied with the metal at US$4,219 (DPM +6.23% on Q3 production, WAF +5.43%, NEM +2.71%, EVN +2.26%). Energy-transition metals were liquidated: lithium carbonate futures fell 4% to their lowest since December 2025 (PLS -5.41%, SRL -11.05%, CXO -7.55%, VUL -6.89%), and uranium fell hard with no company news (BOE -9.93%, BMN -7.37%, DYL -7.11%, PDN -3.67%). Iron ore near US$91 weighed on FMG (-2.85%). The XMJ's flat close averages three very different trades.

The stock-level message is about expectations. EOS opened +15% (and traded as high as +22%) on a A$700m counter-drone contract, then closed -4.25%. That is a bearish outside reversal on a genuinely large contract, and the conditions (including a £74m bank guarantee) gave holders a reason to sell into the spike. GYG reported comps of +7.1% (vs +4.0% a year ago) and extended its buyback, yet closed -2.25%; the stock had already run +5% in 5 days and sits in the hottest 60day industry. MGH (-6.65%, -32% over 5 days) was halted, then released with a Firmus update after the AI data-centre group withdrew its A$43.7bn-valuation float.

Structurally, nothing has been repaired yet. All four indices still print a Bearish regime with 5day and 20day trends Down. Only 26.5% of XJO stocks are above their 50MA and 37% above their 200MA, and T2108 is just 30%. New 20day lows (27) still outnumber new highs (17), even on a 2.9:1 up day. XJO has reclaimed its 10day and 20day MAs but sits 2.46% below the 50MA (~8,936) and 1.25% below the 200MA (~8,827). 8,827 is the first real test, and a close above it would be the first higher-timeframe repair signal. Small caps are the weak link: XSO is 7.19% below its 200MA with an RSI of 37.

Into next week: the MGH/Firmus fallout, BOE's September quarterly (call notice lodged after the close), the gold quarterlies arriving early, and BOQ results on 15/10. With Brent at US$103 and the RBA on 03/11 (~27% hike odds), the rate-sensitive leadership needs yields to cooperate. A rebound in the US 10-year is the main risk to today's rotation.
