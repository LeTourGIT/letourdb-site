📝 Session Summary — 29/05/2026

Materials (XMJ +2.89%) led large sectors for the second consecutive week, with SGM (Sims, +3.59%) hitting a 20day high and MIN (Mineral Resources, +3.76%) recovering from Thursday's broad market selloff on news of the completed US$765M POSCO lithium transaction that accelerates balance sheet deleveraging. The critical structural read for the week: April CPI at 4.2% (vs 4.6% prior) essentially closed the door on a June RBA hike, and the ASX responded accordingly — XRE +1.84%, XDJ +1.59%, XFJ +1.24% all bounced hard as rate-sensitive sectors unwound their RBA-anxiety discounts. However, the fuel-excise unwind in July creates an inevitable mechanical CPI uptick regardless of underlying trends, and the trimmed mean rising to 3.4% (highest since late 2024) keeps the August-hike scenario live. This week's CPI-driven relief is real but tactically bounded — not the all-clear for rate-sensitive sectors to reclaim structural leadership.
Breadth was genuinely strong: XJO 160 advancers vs 31 decliners (5.2:1) is not a low-quality bounce driven by a handful of large-caps — it's broad enough to suggest real accumulation. 📰 Key Headlines
🇮🇷 Geopolitics / Oil
US and Iran tentatively agreed to a 60-day ceasefire extension, with Iran reportedly clearing all Hormuz mines within 30 days under the MOU framework. WTI fell to ~$87 in Asian Friday trade — down sharply from ~$97 Thursday. Vice President Vance cautioned the deal remains unconfirmed at the presidential level. Westpac's terminal rate forecast of 4.85% (June + August hikes) depends partly on sustained elevated oil prices — a sustained WTI sub-$90 scenario challenges that call.
📊 Australia CPI / RBA
Australia's April CPI printed 4.2% (from 4.6%), below the 4.4% consensus — driven by fuel excise reduction. June hike probability collapsed to near zero (<5% via swaps). However, trimmed mean CPI rose to 3.4% (highest since late 2024), underscoring that core inflation is not cooperating. The fuel excise cut reverses in July, creating a mechanical CPI uptick. With current cash rate at 4.35% (three hikes delivered in Feb/Mar/May 2026) the RBA is in a pause window — but Westpac maintains June + August hike forecasts to 4.85% given the Hormuz-driven inflation persistence in the pipeline.

📉 Household Spending / Macro
Australian household spending fell for the first time in four months in April 2026, confirming the RBA's three-hike cycle is flowing through to the real economy. Unemployment has risen to 4.5%. Q1 capital expenditure data was solid (plant & machinery up), but this is a lagging indicator. The combination of falling household spending + rising unemployment + housing at +6.3% CPI creates the classic RBA bind: hiking further risks stagflation; pausing risks inflation entrenchment. The June pause is the path of least resistance — but market confidence in this call depends entirely on oil not re-accelerating.
