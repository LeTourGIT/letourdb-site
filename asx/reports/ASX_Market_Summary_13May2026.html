📝 SESSION SUMMARY — 13/05/2026

Wednesday's session was a tale of two markets waging war inside the one index. The XJO shed 40.3 points (-0.47%) to 8,630.4 — a headline that obscures the most dramatic intra-index divergence since February: Financials (XFJ -4.01%) suffered their worst single-day decline in months as CBA's brutal quarterly update — a $316 million impairment charge and profits missing consensus by 2% — collided directly with Tuesday night's 2026-27 Federal Budget announcement scrapping negative gearing for established properties and replacing the CGT discount with indexation. The combination is structurally corrosive for bank revenues: mortgage lending growth slows, investor property demand falls, and bad debt provisions climb simultaneously. CBA alone erased nearly $10 billion in market capitalisation.

Against that, Materials (XMJ +1.97%) and Consumer Discretionary (XDJ +2.94%) provided structural offsets. BHP closed at a fresh all-time high of $61.52, and Sandfire Resources (SFR +4.5%) moved sharply higher as COMEX copper futures hit a new record above US$6.45/lb on the data centre electrification demand thesis — copper is increasingly being priced as an AI infrastructure input, not just a construction commodity. Aristocrat Leisure (ALL +12%) dominated the discretionary rally, reporting normalised EBITA of $1.12 billion (+6.2%) and a $1 billion expansion of its buyback to $2.5 billion — an unambiguous management confidence signal in an environment where most companies are quietly pulling guidance.

The budget's negative gearing and CGT reform impact is multi-layered. Limiting negative gearing to new-build residential properties from July 2027 is expected to reduce house prices ~3% (CBA modelling), softening the residential loan book growth that underpins bank earnings. But the flipside — implicit support for new construction activity — is arguably positive for the Construction & Engineering cluster (NWH, SXE, GNP) that sits at Smart Money #4 for the 10th consecutive session. The budget also introduced a $1,000 instant tax deduction for workers and the Working Australians Tax Offset — these are inflationary stimuli that complicate the RBA's already deteriorating inflation picture.

The AUD sits at 0.7239 (7d data, 12/5), slightly off its recent highs but still in territory that compresses USD-denominated mining revenue. Every cent of AUD appreciation costs BHP, RIO, and FMG approximately 0.7% in translated revenue — at 0.72, the currency drag is meaningful but not yet enough to cap the mining rally driven by physical commodity price strength.

Breadth was split: XJO Adv/Dec 108/83 (1.3:1) — positive but not convincingly so, with the bank weight artificially depressing the headline number. More instructive: 7 stocks gained 4%+ vs just 3 that lost 4%+, and new 20day Highs (18) comfortably exceeded new Lows (data n/a) — the internals are less bearish than the index suggests.
