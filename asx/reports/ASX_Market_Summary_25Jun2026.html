📝 Session Summary

Thursday 25 June was a textbook risk-off rotation, not a broad capitulation. The ASX 200 slipped 0.68% to 8,748.7, but capital didn't leave the market — it stampeded out of resources, energy and gold and into defensives. Healthcare (XHJ +2.56%) and Consumer Discretionary (XDJ +2.11%) led every sector while Materials (−2.28%) and Energy (−2.48%) were dumped. When the two largest cyclical sectors are red and defensives are green, the market is repricing the growth/rate outlook, not fleeing equities.

The defining shock was JDO (Judo Capital) −40.39% to A$0.91 — a near-A$800m wipe-out. The SME lender slashed FY26 profit-before-tax guidance to A$163–169m (from A$180–190m) after three specific loan exposures deteriorated, lifting expected cost of risk to A$116–122m and pushing 90-days-past-due/impaired loans toward 3% of gross loans. This is a single-name credit event, not a systemic bank read — the Big 4 are far better provisioned — but it is a pointed signal that the lagged bite of 4.35% cash rates is now surfacing in business loan books. It dragged Financials (XFJ −1.22%) red despite IAG +4.32% propping up the insurers.

The commodity complex took a triple hit: gold futures slipped below US$4,000/oz (the 7d gold ETF −2.22%), gutting gold miners (PDI −13.25%, SMI −10.38%, PNR −7.84%); iron ore sits near US$100.5/t under Simandou's supply ramp and softer Chinese steel demand; and lithium remains in oversupply (China carbonate ~CNY 157–169k/t) with Bald Hill and Finniss restarts adding tonnes (ELV −8.77%, GLN −7.69%, INR −9.68%). WOR −9.70% was the large-cap industrials casualty as the oil-services premium evaporated.

The bright spots were genuinely fundamental. EIQ +30.24% surged after Pro Medicus agreed to an initial A$10m (up to A$20m) convertible-note investment and to become a reseller of EchoIQ's EchoSolv cardiac-AI platform — blue-chip validation of a micro-cap medtech ahead of a pending US FDA decision. GYG +8.89%, LOV +5.05%, MYR +5.17% and LLC +8.93% show the consumer/REIT bid has legs: with NAB economists now flagging the RBA's next move as a cut rather than a hike, the rate-pain trade for spending- and duration-sensitive names is unwinding. The week's macro shifted — stronger-than-feared May jobs data trimmed near-term cut bets even as underlying inflation ran hottest since 2024, a mixed bag that keeps the RBA firmly on hold.

The structural caution stands: breadth was poor (XAO advancers/decliners 175/284; only 42.5% of the All Ords above their 50MA) and the "Bullish" labels mask a sub-MA tape. The offset — Micron's after-hours beat flipping US futures green, plus oil's collapse easing the inflation pulse — is constructive for Friday. The real test: can XJO hold 8,700 into the back half of the week.
