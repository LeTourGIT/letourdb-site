SESSION SUMMARY

Monday 27 July delivered the ASX 200's best session in six weeks, +1.39% to 8,894.0, as the weekend decision by the US and Iran to pause missile strikes gapped oil sharply lower and unwound the war-risk premium that had hung over markets for a fortnight. Brent fell below US$92 and WTI sat at US$83.69 (-1.78%). Cheaper crude is a direct inflation reliever for Australia, and the whole rate-sensitive complex responded. Breadth was genuine: 166 advancers to 28 decliners on the XJO, a 5.9:1 thrust.

The paradox of the session is that Energy (XEJ -2.99%) was the only red sector — the exact mirror of the bullish macro. Santos fell 3.64% even as it announced its first Barossa condensate cargo; Whitehaven (-3.61%), Karoon (-7.82%) and the coal names all sold as the oil premium bled out. This is textbook: the catalyst that lifts the index is the same one that sinks its energy sub-index. Trade the mechanism, not the tape.

Materials (XMJ +2.42%) did the heavy lifting, and it was gold-led, not iron-ore-led. Capricorn Metals +15.13% (Mt Gibson PFS, reserves lifted to 5.2Moz), Bellevue +9.09%, Ora Banda +6.67%, with bullion above US$4,100. On top of that, M&A fired: Evolution Mining's agreed takeover of Carnaby Resources sent CNB +57.29%. The gold complex — not the BHP/RIO iron-ore majors — is where the institutional relative strength now sits.

Information Technology (+4.52%) topped the board, but read it carefully: this was Wisetech +7%, Megaport and BrainChip — domestic software and data-centre names, plus 1414 Degrees +14.29% on a 1GW data-centre HoA. It is not the semiconductor trade that just sank the US Nasdaq (SOX -4.3% Friday); the two markets price AI infrastructure through different vehicles. Financials (+1.19%) quietly firmed with all Big-4 banks at fresh 20day highs and Perpetual fielding a revised takeover bid.

The caveat sits in the small-cap tape: XSO is Bearish, 8.1% below its 200MA, and today's leadership was concentrated in large-cap quality. A single risk-on session on an external oil catalyst is not a small-cap re-rating. It was also a heavy late-July quarterly-reporting Monday — dozens of Appendix 4C/5B filings and a cluster of trading halts across small-cap resources.
