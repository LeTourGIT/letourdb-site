📝 Session Summary — 15/05/26

Friday 15 May closed essentially flat for the XJO (-0.09% to 8,630.8), but the session's surface calm masked a significant rotation under the hood. Technology surged +3.20% — the ASX's best sector by a wide margin — while Materials gave back -2.85% in a healthy consolidation of the lithium-led April rally. The week's defining macro event was not the XJO price action but the diplomatic read from Beijing: Trump and Xi agreeing that the Strait of Hormuz must remain a free waterway is the most consequential development for Australian inflation and RBA policy since the Iran war began in late February.

WBT (Weebit Nano) +11.84% was the standout institutional story: SPP allotment day at $4.05 (from the $102M total raise), with the Texas Instruments ReRAM licensing deal providing the fundamental anchor for what is genuinely a semiconductor IP commercialisation story at a critical inflection. Cisco's +13.4% overnight on AI-driven network hardware acceleration drove the sector bid — and WBT is the ASX's most direct play on AI memory infrastructure. Smart Money Semiconductors printing a 10day ROC of 22.67% (rank #1, up from #30 nine days ago) confirms this is not a single-stock event.

The financial sector's +1.00% gain on the day of a flat XJO is the counter-intuitive signal worth dissecting. Post the 5 May hike to 4.35%, banks face mixed arithmetic: NIM benefits from higher rates, but mortgage stress and potential bad debt provisions are the shadow. The Financials bid today reflects markets repricing the probability of a June hike lower — if Hormuz reopens and oil falls, the RBA's inflationary justification weakens materially. The financials are pricing the peak, not the next hike.

The XSO 5day trend flipping UP while XJO remains Down is the week's most important structural signal. Small Ords are above their 50MA (+0.29%), the only index to achieve this. In the prior recovery from the March lows, XSO led XJO by approximately four sessions. The precedent is not a guarantee — but the probability of follow-through is elevated if Hormuz diplomacy holds and the June CPI data confirms the oil-inflation peak.
