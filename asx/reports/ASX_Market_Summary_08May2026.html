SESSION SUMMARY — 08/05/2026

Friday's session delivered the ASX 200's biggest single-day fall in seven weeks — a −1.51% decline to 8,744.4 — reversing Thursday's constructive +0.96% rally and most of the week's gains in a single afternoon. The proximate catalyst was twofold: an overnight Wall Street retreat (SPX −0.38%) driven by Hormuz skirmish resumption, and the continuing digestion of Tuesday's RBA hike, which has left rate-sensitive sectors structurally impaired. Tehran's explicit statement that the Strait of Hormuz reopening plan was "unrealistic" pierced the peace-deal optimism that had built through Monday–Thursday, reigniting oil risk premium and squeezing the financials and consumer sectors that had briefly recovered.

The breadth collapse was the defining feature: XJO Advancers/Decliners came in at 35/158, a starkly one-sided 0.22:1 ratio versus Thursday's 136/55. Every sector finished in the red. Financials (−2.25%) and Real Estate (−2.23%) were the worst performers — the most direct expression of the RBA's decision still percolating through mortgage-linked balance sheets. The Big 4 banks shed between 1.2% and 3.8% according to market reports. The March trade balance data added to the bearish cocktail, printing the first deficit since December 2017 as imports hit records and exports fell — a direct consequence of the energy-shock import bill from elevated oil.

Earnings-day stories created pockets of resilience but could not stem the tide. SKS Technologies (+6.49%) was the session's standout ASX200/300-tier name, securing a $22m Buildcorp contract and reporting a tender pipeline surging 120% since February to $1.25bn, with data centre tenders now accounting for $1bn. This directly validates the ASX IT Services Smart Money rotation. QBE Insurance reiterated FY26 guidance with premium growth ahead of targets and catastrophe costs well below allowance. REA Group's Q3 result missed revenue and EBITDA expectations but April listing volumes (+19% YoY) and lowered cost guidance provided forward comfort. MQG full-year results were due and the 20D High sheet confirms new high territory — the financials sector weakness did not extend to Macquarie.

The week's net result is still constructive in context: the XJO is up approximately 0.5% for the week, its first positive week in over a month. But the Friday reversal illustrates the fragility of the recovery. The RBA's hike has embedded a dual-risk regime: persistent inflation (March CPI 4.6%, likely to remain elevated given oil above $100 Brent) with slowing demand. Westpac's forecast of two further hikes (June + August to 4.85%) would put the cash rate at 2011 peak levels. Until Hormuz de-escalates or the trade data stabilises, the XJO faces asymmetric downside on any Iran headline.
