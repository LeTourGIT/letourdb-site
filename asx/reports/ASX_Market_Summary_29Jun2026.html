Session Summary

Monday 29 June opened the week with the ASX 200 +0.68% to 8,823.4 and the All Ords +0.70% to 9,026.9 — a narrow, catalyst-led advance rather than a broad risk-on day. Healthcare and Technology did the heavy lifting while Real Estate and Utilities fell on mechanical ex-dividend, not any fundamental de-rating.

Neuren Pharmaceuticals (NEU) +36.07% to A$16.60 was the defining move — a European regulatory approval for trofinetide (DAYBUE) re-rated the stock and pulled Healthcare (XHJ +2.12%) higher. This is a binary fundamental de-risking, not technical momentum, opening an ex-North-America commercial pathway Neuren had retained. Clarity (CU6 +15.70%), 4DMedical (4DX +10.34%) and Pro Medicus (PME +4.54%, new 20day high) rode the coattails.

Technology (XIJ +4.04%) was the top sector outright — Wisetech (+7.19%), Life360 (+11.64%, new high), Nuix (+7.11%). The cross-market read matters: this came despite a soft US tech tape (Nasdaq −0.24% Friday on an AI/chip selloff and a reported OpenAI IPO delay), so ASX tech is trading its own SaaS/logistics-software thesis rather than importing US semiconductor sentiment.

The losers' board is dominated by ex-dividend names: APA (−5.20%), Transurban (−4.42%), Dexus (−4.17%), Growthpoint (−5.63%), Digico (−5.06%), Centuria Office (−4.30%), Region (−3.64%) all traded ex-div Monday — price adjustments, not selling. The genuine weakness sits in beaten-up small-cap resources: Core Lithium (−3.92% / 5d −18.33%), Minerals 260 (−4.12%) and Dateline (−3.57% / 5d −22.86%), confirming XSO's structural softness.

The structural read: XTO sits above all four MAs while XSO sits below all four. The index is being carried by mega-caps and a handful of catalyst stocks; XAO T2108 is just 44.6% and only 169 of ~490 All-Ords names are above their 200MA. The 'Bullish' regime is real on short-term MAs but breadth doesn't yet confirm it. With the RBA on hold (next 28–29 July) and the Hormuz premium unwinding, the macro backdrop is benign — the test from here is breadth, not macro.
