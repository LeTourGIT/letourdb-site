This was not a risk-off session in the US — it was a duration repricing, and that distinction is what determined which ASX sectors worked today. Brent rose 4.2% to US$95 on an 11th consecutive night of US strikes on Iran, which pushed the 10-year yield up 11bp over three sessions and lifted July Fed hike odds to roughly 34% from 10% a week ago. The 30-year settled at 5.15% and has now held above 5% for twelve straight sessions, the longest run since 2007.

Alphabet beat on revenue (+24% to US$119.8B) with Cloud up 82% and still fell 3.1% after hours — because it lifted FY26 capex guidance to US$195–205B from US$180–190B. Tesla beat revenue by 7% and missed adjusted EPS by 35% with auto margin ex-credits down 310bp. The message is that the market has switched from rewarding AI capex to penalising it. That read-through landed directly on ASX software.

Signal for Friday: ES and NQ futures were both still negative at the ASX close (-0.21% / -0.31%). There is no overnight relief priced in for long-duration names.

Session Summary

The ASX 200 closed +0.18% at 8,839.0, a one-month high — and a hollow one. The index traded up 1.17% intraday and surrendered roughly two-thirds of that gain into the close. Advancers led decliners by just 98 to 92, a ratio of 1.07:1 on an up day, and six ASX 200 constituents fell more than 4% against only four that rose more than 4%. A genuine +0.18% session does not look like this underneath.

The 11:30am labour force release set the entire tone. Employment rose 76,300 against a consensus of 15,000 — a five-fold beat — with full-time up 29,300 and part-time up 47,000. The unemployment rate held at 4.4% only because participation jumped 0.3 percentage points to 67.0%; Westpac notes that at the second decimal the rate actually edged from 4.37% to 4.43%. For the RBA this removes the one data point that could have justified a comfortable hold on 11 August. The cash rate sits at 4.35% after three hikes in 2026, and with Q2 CPI landing on 29 July, that print is now the binary event for Australian rates. Westpac already forecasts an August increase; CBA, NAB and ANZ expect a hold through the remainder of the year. That disagreement is what the bond market is trying to resolve.

The transmission into equities was textbook duration repricing. Info Technology (XIJ −3.32%) was the worst sector by more than 1.8 percentage points, with WiseTech −6.97%, Life360 −5.45% and Xero −5.01% — none of them on company-specific news. Healthcare (XHJ −1.43%) fell for a fifth consecutive session, led by Pro Medicus −6.53%, now down 16.35% over five days; at its multiple, PME is the purest long-duration expression on the exchange and is behaving accordingly. Consumer Discretionary (XDJ −1.52%) made it three straight, squeezed simultaneously by higher petrol prices and a hawkish rate repricing.

The other side of the ledger was energy and resources. Materials (XMJ +1.37%) posted a third consecutive gain following a roughly 15% multi-week drawdown, led by James Hardie +6.14% after flagging that first-quarter results would exceed prior guidance on stronger Siding & Trim sales. Energy (XEJ +0.98%) is the more structurally significant move: up 6.66% over five sessions, with Brent now 34% higher since 1 July as US forces struck Iranian targets for an eleventh consecutive night and Trump threatened to destroy an Iranian bridge or power plant for each ship fired upon in the Strait of Hormuz. Paladin Energy +11.61% carried the uranium complex along with it, helped by Bell Potter retaining a buy rating with a $14.80 target after FY27 guidance.

Two idiosyncratic moves dominated the tape and both need careful handling. OFX Group +69.47% entered a transaction process deed with UK fintech Equals for an all-cash A$1.00 per share takeover — a 108% premium to its undisturbed 4 February close of $0.480, valuing equity at $247m and enterprise value at $233m on 9.2x FY26 EV/EBITDA. The board intends to recommend unanimously, but the deal is conditional on Equals securing debt funding; that financing condition, not the price, is the risk. Generation Development Group +37.13% on FY26 group funds under management rising 36% matters beyond its own move — it is single-handedly responsible for Insurance Funds ranking first in the Smart Money table, which is a data artifact rather than a rotation signal.

Five green, six red on a +0.18% index day. The split is not defensive versus cyclical — it is short-duration versus long-duration. Materials, Energy, Utilities, Real Estate and Financials all have near-term or inflation-linked cash flows and all rose. Info Technology, Consumer Discretionary, Healthcare and Communication Services are terminal-value heavy and all fell. That is a single macro variable, the discount rate, sorting eleven sectors cleanly.

Two things deserve attention. First, the 10day and 60day picture: Energy's +6.66% five-day gain is the largest structural move on the board and is not yet reflected in the 1day ranking, while Healthcare's −5.15% over five sessions marks a completed round-trip of the 23% rally from the 3-June low to 7-July. Second, Real Estate holding +0.53% through a five-fold jobs beat is the anomaly. REITs are the most rate-sensitive structure on the exchange and they should not be rallying into a hawkish repricing. Either the market believes CPI cools on 29 July, or this is positioning that breaks first.
