Session Summary

Monday delivered a quiet index drift — XJO −0.18% to 9076, XAO −0.25% to 9271 — that masked violent single-name dispersion, the signature of FY26 August full-year reporting season colliding with a sharp overnight gold selloff. Beneath the flat headline this was a defensive rotation: capital left gold, miners and technology and moved into banks and staples.

The driver was offshore. Gold fell 2.88% Friday after Fed Chair Warsh’s Jackson Hole keynote read hawkish — US rate-hike bets jumped, the 10-year yield and the USD firmed. That repriced the entire ASX gold complex: Materials (XMJ −2.02%) was the worst sector, with GGP −6.40%, BNZ −9.24%, PDI −6.26% and LRV −9.96% all sold. The cruel irony is that gold miners are the market’s institutional leadership — 8 of the top-10 ASX200/300 relative-strength names are gold — so today the strongest cohort took the hardest hit.

Not all of Materials was red. Coal and lithium diverged higher. Coronado (CRN) +25.00% led the entire market with no company announcement — a coal-sector move (WHC, NHC, YAL and CRN all at 20-day highs; Coal is Smart Money rank 5). Lithium names were bid too — GLN +9.72%, CXO +4.11%, LTR +2.51% (FY26 results) — as carbonate holds +29% YTD on CATL restart delays. Treat CRN’s +25% as unconfirmed until a catalyst surfaces.

Financials (XFJ +1.14%) was the only clean leader — the mirror image of the gold trade, banks absorbing the rotation out of rate-sensitive and momentum names. Reporting-season winners and losers set the tails: BLS +15.22% (FY26 results + FY27 guidance), KCN +4.71% (Appendix 4E + a maiden 10c dividend, the one gold name up), PXA +9.43% (post-result re-rate); against NVX −12.00% (H1 miss), SLX −13.76% (US-semi read-through) and Star Entertainment’s A$307m FY26 loss reviving survival doubts.

Breadth was negative across all four indices — XJO advancers/decliners 85/111, and new 20-day lows (21) outnumbered new highs (11). The quiet index print flatters a session where sellers clearly had the upper hand internally. The real test now is whether banks-led defence can hold the index up while the gold leadership repairs, or whether the negative internals win through into September.
