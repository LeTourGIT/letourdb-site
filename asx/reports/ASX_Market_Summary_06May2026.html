📝 SESSION SUMMARY

Wednesday 6 May delivered the market's second straight advance (+1.30% to 8,793.6) on one of the most consequential domestic sessions of the year. The RBA's 8-1 vote to raise the cash rate by 25bp to 4.35% landed as widely expected, and with that uncertainty resolved, the market exhaled. Risk-on sentiment arrived from two directions simultaneously: the post-decision relief trade and a powerful US overnight backdrop — SPX at record highs, WTI -3.55%, and Hormuz ceasefire signals compressing the energy risk premium.

Materials (XMJ +2.48%) again led all sectors with Liontown (LTR +6.28%) extending its extraordinary April re-rating into a consecutive institutional confirmation. The genuine surprise was Financials (XFJ +2.39%) — the sector most directly exposed to the RBA decision turned into the second-best performer. This is the "sell the rumour, buy the fact" trade executed cleanly: the banks had been pricing hike anxiety for weeks and, with certainty delivered and language hinting at a possible pause, traders covered shorts and rotated in. CBA +2.96% and ANZ +3.12% led. Westpac was the drag after its half-year earnings ($3.4B, broadly in-line) were met with NIM compression concerns — confirming that the structural banking thesis depends on how long 4.35% holds, not just that it arrived.

The WTI -3.55% overnight was arguably more important for the RBA's next move than today's decision itself. Sub-$103 oil materially reduces the fuel-cost inflation component that Bullock named as the primary second-round risk. If Brent consolidates below $110, the probability of a genuine June pause rises from the current ~20% market pricing. Meanwhile the AUD at 0.7238 (+0.77%) confirms risk-on and rate-differential attractiveness post-hike — but at these levels the miner revenue compression intensifies: today's 0.77% AUD move alone generates approximately a 0.54% aggregate revenue headwind for BHP/RIO/FMG in AUD terms.

Critically, XJO is now above its 50MA (+0.27%) for the first time since the losing streak — a technical milestone that shifts the near-term posture. The 200MA sits at approximately 8,803 (IG data). A close above 8,803 would clear the structural damage and reopen the path toward 9,000. Real test: sustain 8,793+ through the June 16 RBA meeting without a fresh oil-price or geopolitical shock.
