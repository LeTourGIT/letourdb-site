⚠️RBA Watch — Meeting 10-11 August

Current cash rate4.35%

Consensus  HOLD at 4.35% — all four majors (CBA, NAB, ANZ, Westpac) now forecast no change.

CPI  June-quarter CPI (30 July): headline 3.8%, trimmed mean 3.6% — softer than expected.

Three hikes in Feb/Mar/May took the cash rate from 3.60% to 4.35%. The soft June-quarter CPI removed the immediate case for a fourth, flipping the consensus to a hold; Westpac, previously the hike outlier, has moved across. This is a Statement-on-Monetary-Policy meeting, so the updated forecasts and Governor Bullock's tone — she has kept the door open to hiking 'if required' — matter more than the decision itself. Finder's survey still has 55% of economists expecting at least one more hike in 2026, so a hawkish hold is the risk case.

Rate-sensitive sectors firmed into the decision on the hold consensus — Real Estate (XRE +0.79%) and Consumer Discretionary (XDJ +0.74%) both green, and the consumer-cyclical Smart Money rotation reads partly as a bet that a hold clears the path for the household. Resources and energy — USD-revenue, offshore-demand names — are largely insulated either way.

📝Session Summary

The XJO printed a fresh intraday all-time high and closed +0.90% at 9,227.8 in a session imported almost wholesale from Wall Street, where the S&P 500 (+1.79%) and Dow (above 54,000 for the first time) set their own records on an AI-led rebound. Materials (XMJ +3.56%) and Information Technology (XIJ +2.51%) led; Energy (XEJ -2.22%) was the lone heavy drag.

Crucially, the Materials leadership was gold, not iron ore. Gold +1.18% and silver +1.65%, together with the Diggers & Dealers forum in Kalgoorlie, drove a clean sweep of gold miners up the movers board — St Barbara +10.31%, Meeka +14.13%, Rox +13.70%, Southern Cross Gold +13.23%, Santana +11.76%. Iron ore, the traditional BHP/RIO/FMG driver, sits soft near US$94/t and below US$100. The single standout was Neuren (NEU +16.28%) on a record DAYBUE quarter and CY26 royalty upgrade — a genuine fundamental re-rating that carried Health Care (XHJ +1.70%). Adisyn +14.29% on a graphene milestone and SciDev's data-centre contract wins kept the IT theme live alongside the Nasdaq's overnight surge.

Energy was the mirror image of the risk-on tape. US officials signalling the Strait of Hormuz is ‘free and open’ and an Iran deal ‘close’ repriced the oil risk premium lower, softening Brent toward ~US$79 and dragging Woodside -3.55%, Santos -2.19%, Karoon -2.87% and Beach -2.73% (the latter reporting FY results tomorrow). Utilities (XUJ -1.62%) fell with the broader bond-proxy unwind on a risk-on session.

Breadth confirmed the advance rather than flagging a narrow melt-up: XJO advancers beat decliners 145 to 46 (3.15:1), 24 stocks rose more than 4% with none down 4%, and new 20-day highs swamped lows 76 to 1. The single caution sits beneath the surface — only 105 of 200 XJO constituents (53%) are above their 200-day average, and the Small Ords index is still Bearish. This is a record set by the top of the market, with the RBA decision on 11 August the next real test of whether it holds.
