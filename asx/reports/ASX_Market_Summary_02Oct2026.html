SESSION SUMMARY

The ASX 200 added 67.7 points (+0.79%) to 8,682.1, recovering 39% of Thursday's 174.9-point (-1.99%) rout and leaving the index +0.20% for the week. The trigger was the bond market, not equities: the US 10-year yield reversed from 5.344% to 5.24% overnight and the policy-sensitive Australian 3-year has rolled over to 4.922%. Thursday's sell-off was a discount-rate shock, so Friday's relief went straight to the sector with the longest duration.

Information Technology (XIJ +4.45%) led by a wide margin and is +7.34% over 5 days, its best week since August, but the context is a rebound from a six-month low in a sector still down 40.63% over 12 months. The quality of the leadership is better than a pure short squeeze: Megaport +10.32% and DroneShield +6.41% carry contract wins from earlier this week, Data#3 +7.76% extended to a second all-time high after Thursday's 1H27 trading update (profit before tax up more than 40%), and Accenture's 18% surge in the US gave IT services a global read-through. The beaten-up names with no news (WiseTech +6.67%, Life360 +7.03%, Xero +4.59%) are the short-covering component; the stocks with catalysts are the part worth trusting.

Two sectors refused the bounce, and both carry a message. Real Estate (XRE -1.65%) fell on a day yields eased, which says the problem is no longer the discount rate but the asset: Goodman -2.56%, Vicinity -2.58%, Scentre -1.76% and Lendlease -2.94% (MSG North sale update, 09:42, price sensitive) traded against headlines calling the biggest property downturn in 40 years, and Homebuilding sits last of 73 industries in the rotation table. Healthcare (XHJ -1.10%) was pure flow: Cochlear -3.80% (5day -11.88%), Pro Medicus -2.92% and CSL -1.62% had no announcements, so this is institutional selling of expensive defensives to fund the growth rebound.

Resources were split by commodity. BHP +1.58% and Rio +1.20% (Macquarie upgrade to Outperform) carried Materials (XMJ +1.14%) and Energy (XEJ +1.48%) followed Brent above US$100, but the prior leaders were sold into strength: DPM Metals -8.41% on the Bosnian Constitutional Court decision (08:16, price sensitive), Benz Mining -8.38%, EQ Resources -10.11% and Macmahon -9.91%. Relative-strength leaders falling 8 to 10% on an up day is distribution. Banks did the index-level lifting (NAB +1.61%, Westpac +1.24%, CBA +1.16%) as CBA chief Matt Comyn said RBA hikes are probably finished after Tuesday's move to 4.60%, the highest cash rate since 2011.

Structure has not changed. The regime is Bearish on all four indices, only 24.5% of the ASX 200 trades above its 40day average (T2108) and 36.5% above the 200day, and new 20day lows (29) still outnumber highs (9) by three to one on a day the index rose. The forward catalysts are macro rather than corporate: US payrolls tonight will decide whether yields retest Thursday's highs, and the post-close announcement slate is thin, with Hammer Metals' scheme booklet (16:49) the only price-sensitive corporate item for Monday. The level that matters is Thursday's 8,614.4 close: hold it and the bounce can extend toward the 10day average, lose it and Thursday was the start of the next leg rather than a flush.
