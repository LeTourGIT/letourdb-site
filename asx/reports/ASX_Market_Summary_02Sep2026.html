📝 Session Summary

Wednesday 2 September was an imported risk-off session. The ASX 200 fell −0.97% to 8,978.4, tracking a global bond rout that drove developed-market yields to multi-decade highs and a fresh US-Iran escalation that pushed Brent above US$95. In origin this was Sydney wearing Wall Street's Tuesday (S&P −0.71%, Nasdaq −1.03%) — with a distinctly local twist.

That twist was the Q2 GDP print: +2.1% year-on-year, beating expectations and reviving the rate-hike thread into the 29 September RBA. The combination is corrosive for long-duration equity — higher global yields plus a domestic growth beat that hardens the case for another RBA move. Information Technology (XIJ −3.35%) was the clearest expression: SLX −8.57% and ELS −8.72% as discount rates rose across the board.

Materials (XMJ −3.08%) was the heaviest single drag and the day's real damage — broad within the sector. Uranium (LOT −9.62%, BMN −9.34%), copper (CSC −8.89%, FFM −7.69%), lithium (CXO −9.21%) and a gold-miner pullback all hit at once. Critically, gold's structural RS leadership did NOT break: 7 of the top-10 institutional RS names remain gold miners (VAU, GMD, RSG, ALK, RRL, PRU). Today was profit-taking inside an intact uptrend, not a top. EQR −9.41% even fell on RECORD A$55m revenue — a classic sell-the-news tell.

The green was defensive and idiosyncratic. Consumer Staples (+0.81%) and Financials (+0.25%, the only cyclical green) held, carried by IAG +5.46% and CGF +5.16% — insurers and diversified financials that read higher-for-longer as a tailwind. GNC +11.02% (a fresh 20-day high) and SHV +6.17% gave agriculture a genuine bid, while KAO +151.7% on a copper discovery led an otherwise thin speculative board.

Breadth is the warning. XJO advancers/decliners ran 50/143 (1:2.9), with 42 new 20-day lows against just 7 highs and only 45% of XJO names above their 200MA. The medium-term structure holds for now, but small-caps sit below both key moving averages and the tape has clearly rolled short-term. The real test: whether ~8,900 holds as US yields and oil settle, or whether this is the first leg of a deeper mean-reversion into the RBA.
