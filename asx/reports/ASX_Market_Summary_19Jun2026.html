Session Summary

The ASX 200 fell −0.92% to 8,828.70 and the All Ords −0.87% to 9,047.30, but the headline number badly understates what happened beneath the surface. This was a single-sector earthquake: Materials (XMJ) collapsed −4.03% while ten of eleven sectors finished within ±1.3% of flat and five were green. Strip out the miners and this was a quietly constructive, rotational session.

The catalyst was offshore and overnight. The signing of the US–Iran 14-point memorandum removed a large geopolitical risk premium, and Kevin Warsh's debut as Fed Chair delivered a hawkish hold — a dot plot tilting toward a 2026 hike and a firmer USD (the dollar touched its highest in over a year). That combination is kryptonite for precious metals: gold −3.78% to ~US$4,175 and silver −6.25% overnight. ASX gold and silver miners were marked down hard at the open and never recovered.

The casualty list is unambiguous — the bottom of the market was wall-to-wall gold, silver, uranium and lithium names: DPM −7.92%, Meeka −8.70%, Brightstar −7.14% and L1 Gold Fund −8.08% (gold); Unico Silver −7.69%; Deep Yellow −9.88% and Alligator Energy −29.52% (uranium); Wildcat −10.32% (lithium). Alcoa (AAI −6.88%) extended the base-metals weakness. Iron ore, meanwhile, is quietly soft at ~US$101/t (−8.1% over 20 days), giving BHP/RIO no offset.

Against that, capital rotated cleanly into defensives and domestic cyclicals. Health Care (XHJ +3.51%) led the board on CSL +7.62% — a sharp bounce in a sector still down ~40% over twelve months. Consumer Staples (+1.27%) followed on a2 Milk +9.82%. Smaller-cap winners told the same story: Audinate +27.43%, 4DMedical +17.62%, Electro Optic Systems +14.13% (defence), IDP Education +6.67% and Life360 +6.19%. The Smart Money table corroborates it emphatically — Multiline Insurance surged to #1 and the top 16 is dominated by Consumer Cyclicals, Financials and a biotech surge.

The tell is breadth versus the tape: XJO advancers trailed decliners 84 to 111 (0.76:1), yet new 20-day highs (33) crushed new lows (9) and 65% of XJO names held above their 20MA. That is the fingerprint of an index dragged by a handful of heavyweight miners while the median stock holds firm. With the RBA having held at 4.35% on 16 June (citing inflation still too high), the macro overhang is steady rather than acute — the real question is whether the gold complex stabilises or whether a crowded, overbought metals trade has more unwinding to do.
