⚠️ RBA Watch — Decision Day (HOLD 4.35%)

The RBA left the cash rate at 4.35% at its 2:30pm AEST decision today — a near-unanimous call (all four majors, 37/37 Reuters economists, ~97% market pricing). The surprise was in the guidance, not the level: the Board dropped its two-sided risk language and now frames inflation risks as tilted to the upside, explicitly citing the Hormuz-driven energy shock, and does not see inflation back in band until late 2027. June-quarter trimmed mean at 3.6% (headline 4.0%) gave cover to hold after three hikes Feb–May took the rate from 3.60% to 4.35%.

The hold was a non-event; the hawkish forecast track is the signal. By dropping two-sided risk language, the Board is telling markets 4.35% may be a floor, not a peak, if oil stays bid. Rate-sensitive names stayed heavy — ARF -12.5%, XRE -0.14%, XFJ -0.35% — while USD-earning resources and energy are structurally insulated. The quarterly Statement on Monetary Policy carries more weight than the decision itself.

📝 Session Summary

Tuesday 11 August 2026 was an RBA decision day that resolved exactly as priced — a hold at 4.35% — leaving the tape free to trade the two things that actually moved: oil and earnings. The XJO added +0.19% to 9,250.6, holding record territory, but the modest headline masks unusually sharp single-stock dispersion.

Energy (XEJ +3.91%) led every sector as the Hormuz risk premium kept Brent near US$88 and Santos loaded its first Alaskan Pikka cargo. This is the same force the RBA named as its chief upside-inflation risk — the macro story and the sector leadership are one and the same. Health Care (+2.15%) took a defensive-plus-biotech bid, while Materials (+0.51%) stayed green on lithium and gold rather than the iron-ore majors, with the bulk price soft near US$94 into BHP's 18 August result.

FY26 reporting season set the losers' board. Life360 (360 -19.44%) posted record Q2 numbers and was still dumped — a textbook high-multiple "sell-the-news" — while SGH -10.27% and Amotiv -13.50% (the latter compounded by a CEO succession) show zero tolerance for any large-cap miss. The gainers rewarded genuine beats: Austal (ASB +17.45%, exiting a trading halt) and Chrysos (C79 +17.21% on its FY26 result).

Beneath the large caps, the speculative book is a one-way critical-minerals bet. Lake Resources (LKE +26.67%) and Core Lithium (CXO +11.48%, on a real BP33 drill hit) confirm the lithium re-rating has broadened well beyond the majors, and gold/silver spec names rode the same safe-haven-plus-scarcity theme.
