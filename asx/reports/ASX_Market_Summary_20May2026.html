📝 Session Summary — Wednesday 20/05/2026

Wednesday delivered the clearest evidence yet that the RBA minutes regime change is embedding in market pricing. The ASX 200 shed 1.26% to 8,496.6 — its lowest close since early April — as the release of the May board minutes at 11:30am AEST removed any residual ambiguity about the rate path. Eight of nine board members backed the May hike as essential, underlying inflation is now projected above 3% through mid-2027, and critically, the board flagged explicit concern that inflation expectations could become de-anchored. That phrase is the RBA's equivalent of a red alert — it signals the board is prepared to hike again if needed, even as CBA and others maintain an on-hold base case for June.

The only bright spot in an otherwise uniform decline was Technology One (TNE +7.86%), which staged a sharp reversal after Tuesday's initial sell-off on H1 results. The market reconsidered the numbers overnight: strong ARR growth and SaaS+ adoption momentum justified the premium. TNE's recovery held XIJ from a deeper decline, but it was a one-stock story — the broader IT sector was flat and the underlying bearish regime is not challenged by a single name. Alcoa (AAI +2.95%) was the other top performer — an aluminium-linked read on energy cost improvements if WTI consolidates below $110.

Materials (XMJ -2.12%) was the session's hardest-hit sector — a sharp reversal from the 10day ROC leadership we documented on 01/05. BHP, RIO, and MIN were all lower as iron ore futures pulled back. This is the most important sector signal of today: the May minutes explicitly project global supply chain disruption from Hormuz will raise inflation in "globally traded goods," and that headwind suppresses risk premium for resource names that need China demand AND benign USD to outperform. The XSO small-cap index suffering a -2.12% decline — worse than the large-cap XJO — confirms that institutional rotation is actively selling the speculative and mid-tier resource complex.

Financials (XFJ -1.11%) deserve separate context: the Big 4 banks face a genuinely complex setup. The May minutes note that mortgage stress indicators are rising, even as higher rates technically expand net interest margins. The market today is pricing the bad-debt provision cycle over the NIM expansion benefit — a rational response to a central bank explicitly warning that household spending may deteriorate faster than expected. With CBA data showing signs of pullback in travel and selected discretionary, the provision risk is becoming consensus.
