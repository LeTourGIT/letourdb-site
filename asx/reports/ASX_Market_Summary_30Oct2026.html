⚠️ RBA Watch — Decision Tuesday 03/11/2026

  • Cash rate 4.60% after four hikes in 2026 (latest +25bp on 29/09, unanimous). Decision Tuesday 03/11/2026, 2:30pm AEDT -- Melbourne Cup day -- with a fresh Statement on Monetary Policy.
  • Market pricing: ~90% for +25bp to 4.85% (ABC markets blog, 30/10). All four majors now forecast a hike after CBA and NAB moved off "hold" this week.
  • The trigger: Q3 trimmed mean +1.0% q/q, 3.7% y/y -- above the RBA's own 0.8% August forecast. Headline CPI 4.3% y/y (September), fuel-led.
  • The counterweight: unemployment 4.7% after a 39,300 September job loss; HSBC's Paul Bloxham now calls recession a coin toss -- the ABC Business lead today.

A fully priced hike is not the risk -- the Statement on Monetary Policy is. A hawkish SMP that keeps 5.10% alive hits the rate-sensitive REITs and consumer names that Smart Money has just rotated INTO (four REIT industries in the top 15). A "one-and-done" signal would validate that rotation. Resources and energy (USD revenues) are insulated either way; banks get a NIM tailwind but carry the arrears risk if recession odds keep climbing.

Session Summary

The headline +0.18% hides a sharp intraday fade. The ASX 200 ran to roughly 8,986 mid-morning (+1.1%, a seven-week high per Trading Economics) on strong domestic trading updates, then gave back about 80% of the gain to close at 8,908.1. The regime engine still upgraded XJO to Neutral Bull and the index holds above both its 50MA and 200MA -- but a day that peaks at 10:30 and bleeds into a Friday close ahead of an RBA hike is distribution, not accumulation. Note the base: the week still delivered a 5day Up trend on every large-cap index.

Domestic earnings dispersion was the session's real content. Reece (REH +8.44%) delivered Q1 FY27 sales of A$2.5B (+4.3%) at 09:41 and led the ASX 200; Endeavour (EDV +4.31%) cleared the bar with its AGM trading update at 09:24. Coles (COL -7.67%) went the other way: Q1 comparable growth of 2.7% and a warning that first-half supermarket earnings will be flat as price investment, competitor promotions and fuel costs bite -- RBC flagged an implied ~6% 1H EBIT downgrade and market-share loss. Staples (XSJ -0.97%) was the casualty, while Discretionary (XDJ +2.73%) led all sectors on a relief rebound in JBH (+6.21%) and LOV (+4.26%) after Thursday's run of soft retail updates.

Underneath, the commodity-and-tech complex was liquidated. Chinese lithium carbonate futures fell 8.4% after the ASX closed Thursday and slipped another 1.3% today; the local lithium and critical-minerals cohort followed -- VUL -12.55%, IPX -12.24%, SRL -11.61%, WA1 -9.03%, BRE -7.64% -- and uranium names were dumped alongside (DYL -8.92%, BOE -7.59%). Catalyst Metals (CYL -24.76%) was the ASX 200's second-worst stock after a September-quarter production miss and an FY27 guidance cut. Materials (XMJ -0.87%) gave back part of a +4.06% week. Info Tech (XIJ -1.34%) imported Wall Street's AI-disruption sell-off, with ELS (-21.3%), WBT (-8.1%) and APX (-21.3%) the casualties.

Breadth confirms the two-speed tape. XTO advancers beat decliners 68/32 with 72% of the ASX 100 above its 40day MA, and XJO new 20day highs doubled from 27 to 56 -- almost all banks, insurers and REITs (CBA, NAB, WBC, ANZ, MQG, IAG, SCG, GPT, MGR, DXS). Yet the XSO had 68 advancers against 79 decliners and 15 stocks down more than 4%, and only 38% of the ASX 200 trades above its 200MA. Leadership is narrow, defensive-financial and rate-sensitive -- an awkward mix to carry into a near-certain hike.

Next week is loaded. Westpac reports Monday 02/11, the RBA decides Tuesday 03/11 (~90% priced for 4.85%), NAB reports 05/11 and Macquarie 06/11 -- the banks that powered this week's 20day highs face their own results inside four sessions. China's official PMI lands Saturday and sets the tone for iron ore (flat at US$90.75). After the close, PointsBet lodged its H1 FY27 result (16:18) and 85 further filings hit the tape post-16:00 -- none are in today's prices.
