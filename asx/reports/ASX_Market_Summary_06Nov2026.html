📝 Session Summary

The ASX 200 shed 44.3 points (-0.49%) to 9,020.8, giving back roughly a fifth of Thursday's +2.6% post-midterm surge. In the context of a 4.4% S&P 500 fall and a 6.36% Nasdaq 100 collapse, that is a remarkable act of relative strength. The index held above 9,000 at the close after opening at 8,962 — buyers defended the round number and the XJO regime stays Bullish (10MA > 20MA > 50MA) with price 2.31% above the 50MA.

The damage was concentrated in Materials (XMJ -2.51%), which accounted for most of the index loss. BHP -2.34%, RIO -2.03%, FMG -2.00% and MIN -3.36% fell while iron ore was flat at US$93.12/t — so this was not a China-steel story. It was a copper and critical-minerals de-rating: IGO -5.88%, LYC -4.94%, ILU -3.73%, VUL -9.72%, IPX -8.68%, DVP -10.13%. The market is treating anything with a data-centre/electrification demand thesis as US AI beta, and selling it accordingly.

The offset came from the classic defensive trio. Consumer Staples (XSJ +1.41%) led on COL +1.80% and WOW +1.75% (a new 20day high), Financials (XFJ +0.55%) rode NAB +2.67% and QBE +3.32% [H], and Utilities edged up. That is the textbook rotation when US Treasuries rally on a growth scare — bond proxies and balance-sheet quality bid, cyclicals sold. Macquarie (-3.69%) was the exception in financials: its 1H27 result landed alongside an APRA review of how it sets capital for its non-bank businesses.

Breadth tells a weaker story than the index. XJO advancers/decliners were 54/134 (0.40:1) after Thursday's 168/29, and the XSO (Small Ords) fell 2.23% — more than four times the XJO loss — with only 21 of 147 stocks rising. The XSO regime is Bearish with volatility flagged 'Caution' and price 7.69% below its 200MA — a sharp divergence from the large-cap Bullish regime. The rally is being carried by mega-cap defensives and banks, not by the broad market.

Stock-specific news did produce the session's biggest gains. Mayne Pharma +13.65% on its $55.5m International divestment, Nine Entertainment +11.64% on a A$180m cost-out target, and Amcor +4.67% after its 10-Q. On the other side, News Corp -9.29% after its 10-Q/8-K and James Hardie -6.22% even though it reaffirmed guidance and lifted its FCF target — the same reaction as its September investor day, when reaffirmation without an earnings upgrade was sold. The RBA's 03/11 hike to 4.85% is now flowing to mortgage rates, and the next RBA meeting is 08/12. Monday brings ANZ's FY26 result and Dyno Nobel.
