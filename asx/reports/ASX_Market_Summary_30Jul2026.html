Session Summary

Thursday 30 July delivered an orderly -0.78% pullback to 8,967.7, the ASX absorbing an ugly Wall Street session after the US struck Iran and the Fed held rates with three dissenting hike votes. The calm was earned: US futures had already stabilised by the ASX close, so the local tape repriced without panic. But beneath a benign index number, breadth was poor — 36 advancers to 160 decliners on the XJO, 16 stocks down more than 4% versus just one up.

Materials (XMJ -1.59%) was the clear laggard, and the internals matter more than the headline: this was small-cap mining and uranium carnage — LOT -26.09%, LTR -10.86%, ARU -7.89%, RSG -7.22% — swamping green prints from the liquid large-caps RIO +1.83%, MIN +3.90% and PLS +2.68%. That divergence is the tell. With iron ore drifting toward one-year lows (~US$98/t on the Simandou ramp and China property weakness), the big miners did not rise on fundamentals; they rose on defensive, oversold rotation into liquidity, amplified by the late-July quarterly reporting window (352 announcements, 293 price-sensitive).

The two green sectors told the macro story cleanly. Info Tech (XIJ +0.91%) tracked the offshore capex read-through (Microsoft +8%, Samsung's AI-chip beat), and Energy (XEJ +0.32%) rode the Hormuz crude spike — VEA +2.20% [H] and Ampol's "Resilient Supply Chain Underpins Earnings Growth" note (09:12, price-sensitive) are the ASX's direct refiner-margin windfall, echoing the ABC report of Ampol margins jumping 250% on the Strait closure.

The loudest capital-flow signal came from Smart Money: Insurance Funds rocketed to #1 from #79 nine sessions ago (ROC +17.24) — a genuine institutional surge with GDG the listed ASX proxy. Oil & Gas Refining sits entrenched at #2, confirming the energy-margin theme is a flow, not a one-day headline. Consumer names bucked the tape idiosyncratically: DMP +9.08%, SGR +8.00% and GYG +2.64% [H], with Restaurants & Bars ranking #3 in Smart Money.
