Session Summary

Wednesday's +0.34% lift to 8,823 was a narrow, resources-led grind rather than a broad advance. Only three of eleven sectors closed green — Materials (+2.30%), Energy (+1.67%) and a barely-positive Financials (+0.19%) — while the other eight, led by Healthcare (-1.93%), Real Estate (-1.46%) and Consumer Discretionary (-1.44%), were sold. XJO advancers beat decliners just 100 to 94: a green index masking a split tape.

The engine was the commodity complex. Copper leapt ~3% overnight on tightening Chinese supply, gold bounced 1.7%, and Brent settled +3.1% on the Hormuz standstill — a trifecta that drove the majors: South32 +3.5% (FY26 volumes +15%), BHP +3.0%, RIO +2.5%, Fortescue +2.1%. This is the XMJ >1.5% resources signal, and it's institutional in weight, not just speculative juniors.

The juniors levered the theme — gold (TCG +11.2% [H], KCN +7.6%, OBM +7.3%), silver (SS1 +9.8%, USL +7.8%) and uranium (CXU +15.4%). But uranium was bipolar: Paladin +6.7% on a Q4 beat and FY27 guidance ~11% ahead faded to +2.7% as brokers cut targets, and Peninsula cratered -26% after withdrawing CY2026 guidance.

The other side was defensive and growth destruction. Healthcare was gutted (CSL -2.8%, COH -4.9%, PME -3.7%, TLX -2.9%) and tech tracked the global chip wobble (WBT -7.3%, XRO -2.2%). Lynas was the standout single loser, off ~8% intraday on a Q4 NdPr miss (15% below consensus on Mt Weld ore quality), HRE costs blowing out to ~$294m.

Net: a commodity-driven tape riding the Hormuz oil premium, China copper tightness and gold's safe-haven bid — powerful at the top end, thin underneath. Sustaining above 8,800 needs the resource bid to broaden or the defensives to stop bleeding. Thursday's labour-force data is the circuit-breaker.
