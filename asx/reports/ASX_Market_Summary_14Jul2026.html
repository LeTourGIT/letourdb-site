Session Summary

Tuesday 14/07/2026 was a quiet index masking a loud rotation. XJO finished dead flat at 8,808.5 and the All Ords a hair lower at 9,001.3, yet beneath the surface capital moved hard along a single fault line: the Strait of Hormuz.

Trump's weekend move to reinstate the Iranian blockade and impose a 20% cargo toll on Hormuz traffic sent Brent up double-digits and drove a clean energy bid. Energy (XEJ +1.98%) led all eleven sectors — Santos, Woodside and Karoon (KAR +3.81%, 5day +10.7%) the large-cap expressions, Stanmore (SMR +8.13%, coal) the standout. The Smart Money rotation table confirms this is institutional rather than noise: Oil & Gas Refining surged to #1 (from #49 nine days ago), Exploration to #3, Integrated Oil & Gas to #5, and Coal to #10 — an entire complex re-rating in unison.

The mirror image was the rate-sensitive and uranium complex. Real Estate (XRE -1.62%) was the worst sector as bond yields backed up on revived inflation fear — the oil spike directly threatens the CPI path and pushes any RBA-cut hope further out. The ASX nuclear-fuel names were sold hard and independently of oil: Paladin -5.99%, Deep Yellow -5.38%, Bannerman -4.00%, Silex -9.52%, Alligator -7.55%. Kingsgate (KCN -16.09%, gold) was the single worst large mover.

Breadth tells the cautionary tale the index hides. XJO advancers trailed decliners 90/103, the All Ords a weak 184/253, and new 20day lows outnumbered highs at every index (XJO 22 v 13, XAO 64 v 25). Only 35% of XJO stocks sit above their 20MA and 40.5% above their 200MA. This was a flat tape carried by a handful of energy heavyweights, not a broad advance.

On rates: the RBA held at 4.35% in June (three hikes already in 2026) and next meets 10-11 August, outside the immediate window — no rate event today. But the oil shock matters precisely because it reloads the inflation risk that has kept the Bank hawkish. Westpac still flags August/September hikes; CBA, NAB and ANZ see a hold. A sustained oil bid is the swing factor for that call.
