📝 Session Summary

Wednesday 17 June saw the ASX 200 add +0.54% to 8,966.3, shrugging off the RBA's hawkish hold and instead trading the single most important number for Australian markets right now: oil. With Brent and WTI sliding toward 3-month lows on the Strait of Hormuz reopening path, the session was a textbook oil-relief rotation — money out of energy, into the rate-sensitive and growth cohorts that benefit from lower input costs.

The dispersion was stark. Energy (XEJ -2.26%) was the clear worst sector as the oil complex unwound: Karoon (KAR -13.37%, ASX200) was the heaviest fall, with Woodside (WDS -3.6%, ASX20) and Yancoal (YAL -3.78%) confirming a coordinated energy de-rating rather than a single-name event. Utilities (XUJ -1.68%) compounded the bond-proxy pain — a hawkish hold keeps the long end elevated, and APA -2.98% led the drag.

Against that, Information Technology (XIJ +2.03%) led every sector on the global chip/AI tailwind — Japanese chip-export data hit a 3-year high, Intel advanced its leading-edge node, and KOSPI ran +1.8% overnight. The ASX expression was broad: Megaport (MP1) sits #8 on the all-market RS board, Weebit Nano (WBT +4.57%) and Dicker Data (DDR) carried the institutional semis/hardware read, and SiteMinder (SDR +10.1%, ASX200) was a standout software mover.

The resource tape split by commodity. Gold equities ripped — Resolute (RSG +8.6%, ASX200), Pantoro (PNR +8.16%, ASX200) and a long tail of juniors — while uranium was the velocity trade of the day: Cauldron (CXU +34.3%), Alligator (AGE +28%) and Lotus (LOT +13.79%, ASX300) drove Uranium to #13 in Smart Money from #80 just five sessions ago. Lithium lagged within materials (ELV -6.87%, EUR -4.71%) and Novonix (NVX -25%) was the session's worst performer. Net, Materials (XMJ +1.15%, +9.64% over 5 days) remains the structural leadership trade, with iron ore steady around US$102/t underpinning BHP/RIO/FMG.

Breadth validated the advance: XJO advancers led decliners 142:52 (2.7:1), with 59 new 20-day highs against just 10 lows and T2108 at a healthy 67%. The small-cap board was broader still (XSO 114:32, 20 stocks +4%). This was participation, not a narrow index push.
