📝 Session Summary

Tuesday 26 May delivered a -0.40% decline on the XJO (8,657.8) against a session with no US overnight lead — Memorial Day kept Wall Street dark — and driven entirely by the Hormuz oil whipsaw and domestic corporate news. The session had two competing narratives that ran in real time: peace-deal euphoria from Monday's -6.9% WTI collapse, versus the sharp reversal triggered by fresh US military strikes in southern Iran mid-session.

The macro structure is now fully Bearish across all three large-cap indices. Regime has deteriorated from the Neutral Bear readings of early May — a reminder that the 01 May bounce (+0.74%) that snapped the eight-session losing streak did not repair the underlying MA structure. Bearish regime means MA(10) < MA(20) < MA(50) fully aligned. The five-day trend has turned Up, providing a short-term bounce cushion, but this is textbook bear-market-rally geometry rather than a structural reversal.

The session's most consequential domestic event was ASX Limited (ASX.ax) crashing -11.83% to $51.84. The exchange operator raised FY27 capex guidance to $180–200M (from $160–180M), citing technology cost inflation and new product development, while simultaneously flagging higher expense growth. In a 4.35% rate environment with market participants scrutinising every margin signal, a double-negative (capex up + costs up) warranted severe repricing. The meta-signal is uncomfortable: if even the exchange infrastructure running the Australian market cannot contain tech cost inflation, the pressure is systemic, not company-specific.

Goodman Group (GMG) provided the institutional anchor. The Q3 update reaffirmed FY26 EPS growth of ≥9%, with a targeted work-in-progress of $18B by June 2026 and a data centre pipeline of ~6GW — a 10–11 year development runway at the current 400–500MW annual production rate. Morgan Stanley had flagged scope for a guidance upgrade; the lack of one (reaffirm at 9% vs consensus 10%) created mild near-term pressure. However, GMG CEO Greg Goodman's commentary on cost lock-ins — securing long-lead capex items early to achieve certainty on 2028 delivery dates — is the mature capital allocation response that distinguishes GMG from infrastructure peers. NRW Holdings (NWH) subsidiary Fredon added $69M to a Victorian data centre project, taking total site value to ~$155M, further reinforcing the ASX construction sector's data-centre revenue thread.

Coal #16 (from #70, 9 days): A counterintuitive 54-rank surge alongside Renewables surging to #1. WHC (Whitehaven) and NHC (New Hope) are the ASX instruments. This is not a contradiction — it is the energy transition playbook playing out at two different time horizons simultaneously.
