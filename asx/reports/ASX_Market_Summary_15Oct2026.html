📝 Session Summary

Thursday was a rotation day that the headline number hides. The ASX 200 slipped just 0.09% to 8,719.7, but under the surface two sectors fell more than 3% while three rose more than 2%. Materials (XMJ −3.95%) was the worst sector. BHP −4.70%, RIO −4.85%, S32 −5.70%, MIN −4.85% and the critical-minerals group (LYC −6.57%, PLS −6.31%, IGO −5.44%) were all sold, with iron ore flat at US$90.74 and copper only −0.30%. This was de-risking of global-growth and AI-capex exposure, not a commodity-price event. ABC headlined it directly: "BHP dives as AI stocks fall out of favour". BHP's plan to cut iron-ore jobs adds a cost-defence angle.

Information Technology (XIJ −3.41%) took the US CPI shock head on. NXT −7.42%, MP1 −10.06% and XRO −3.04% fell, and the AI-infrastructure chain was de-rated even on good news. SHAPE fell 6.26% on the day it announced an $800m data-centre contract, and DigiCo slipped 2.91% despite a 71MW SYD1 deal. When a market sells the contract wins, the capex theme is being repriced as a whole. In Smart Money, Electronic Equipment & Parts fell from rank 1 to rank 70 in nine sessions and IT Services & Consulting from rank 5 to rank 73, among the sharpest exits on the board.

The money moved into domestic defensives and banks. Consumer Staples (XSJ +3.47%) led: WOW +4.27%, COL +2.71%, A2M +3.65% and TWE +4.58% (AGM trading update, 08:40). WOW, COL, MTS, EDV and TWE all printed 20day highs. Financials (XFJ +2.19%) followed: CBA +3.29%, NAB +3.01%, WBC +2.94%, and BOQ +5.86% on its FY26 result (08:31, price sensitive). The insurers (QBE +2.74%, IAG, SUN, MPL all +2%+) are pulling Property & Casualty Insurance up the rotation table (rank 56 → 5). A 4.7% unemployment rate lowers November hike risk, which favours banks over cyclicals. US banks did the opposite (Bank of America −4.8% overnight), so this is a domestic, ASX-specific bid.

Breadth shows the real damage. XJO advancers/decliners were 89/106, but the All Ords ran 143/328 (a 1:2.3 ratio) with 109 stocks down 4% or more. New 20day lows (211) far outnumbered highs (37). Small Ordinaries fell 2.19% to 3,120.4, a fourth straight loss and −4.9% across those four sessions, and only 18% of XSO stocks are above their 40day MA. XTO (top 100) rose 0.06% while XSO fell 2.19%. When the largest stocks hold up the index while the rest of the market falls, the move is usually not sustained. A real bottom needs small caps to stop falling, not just banks to rally.

The quarterly reporting window is open. Section 2b carried 183 announcements, dominated by small-cap resources 4C/5B and drill results, plus four trading halts (ALB, EMA, CMG, PNN) that are now forward catalysts. INA fell 11.57% after a trading pause and an update on a further revised non-binding offer, a sign the bid is in doubt. The one post-close item is Latitude (LFS, 17:20, price sensitive) announcing a hybrid security suspension and removal, a niche item for credit holders. Tomorrow's open has to absorb whether US futures hold their gains and whether Materials finds support near its 5day low.
