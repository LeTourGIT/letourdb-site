📝 SESSION SUMMARY
Tuesday 12 May delivered the XJO's 15th decline in 19 sessions — a -0.36% fall to 8,670.7 that touched a five-week intraday low of 8,619 before partial recovery. Three headwinds converged: Federal Budget-night anxiety over proposed negative gearing and capital gains tax reform, ASIC's formal investigation of DroneShield dragging the IT sector to a four-week low, and Trump's weekend rejection of Iran's peace terms keeping Hormuz supply anxiety alive and WTI above $100.

Against that, Materials (XMJ +2.43%) was the clear structural winner. NEM +4.32%, RIO +3.09%, Alcoa +3.81%, BHP +2.50%, and MIN +1.26% delivered a commodity complex session that directly mirrors Hormuz geopolitical risk-premium and the rare earths dimension of the Trump-Xi summit. LTR extended its run with another +5.28% — the lithium institutional RS complex remains intact.

The DroneShield (DRO) -14.76% governance shock deserves its own framing. This is not a sector story — it is a reminder that names which rerate on defence-tech sentiment rather than audited recurring revenues carry acute single-stock governance risk. The ASIC probe into ~$67–70M of director share sales post-botched contract announcement in November is material. Until investigation outcomes are known, DRO carries elevated counterparty risk regardless of the underlying defence demand narrative.

Tomorrow's CBA Q3 trading update is the session's true risk-overhanging event. CBA is the ASX's single largest constituent; its guidance will set the tone for the entire banking sector's second half. The double-whammy of post-Budget negative gearing reform pressure AND the 4.35% rate environment makes tomorrow a genuine binary risk for the XFJ sector.
