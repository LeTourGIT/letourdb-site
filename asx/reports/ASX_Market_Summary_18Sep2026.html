📝 Session Summary

Friday 18 September was a lesson in why the ASX cannot simply borrow US risk appetite. Wall Street staged a sharp post-Fed-hike relief rally overnight — SPX +1.14%, NDX +1.73%, VIX -12.8% — yet the XJO finished dead flat at 8,731.2 (-0.01%). The ceiling sits 11 days away: the RBA meets 29 September with roughly 82% of a +25bp hike to 4.60% now priced. A domestic central bank about to tighten — freshly reinforced by the Fed's 16 September move to 4.00% and the Bank of Japan's hike to a 31-year high — is a hard cap on any relief bounce.

Materials (XMJ +1.60%) was the only sector to lead with conviction, and its engine was precious metals. Gold in record territory (~US$4,434, +0.85%) drove a wall of ASX gold re-rates: PDI +12.36% [ASX200], and the entire institutional relative-strength leaderboard — SBM [H], TCG, SX2, ALK [H] — is now gold. Rare earths supplied the day's cleanest catalyst: ARU +11.76% [ASX300] on a binding offtake extension lodged 09:23 (price-sensitive), which dragged BRE +13.65% and DVP +11.84% along with it.

Beneath the resources bid, the rate-sensitive complex bled. Real Estate (XRE -1.34%) was the worst sector — the purest expression of the RBA overhang — alongside Consumer Staples (-1.04%), Energy (-1.10%, on lower oil) and Financials (-0.59%). AFG -7.37% [ASX300], a mortgage aggregator, was rate-sensitivity in its rawest form. The session's disaster was GEM -17.39% [ASX300]: G8 Education gapped to a multi-year low on a weak trading update and lowered attendance guidance, atop an existing impairment and occupancy overhang.

Smart Money rotation reinforced the split. Construction & Engineering surged 53 ranks (9day rank 57 → 4) — the fastest velocity move in the table — and it is corroborated stock-for-stock by TEA +13.70%, MGH +12.72% and GNP +12.49%, with Macmahon's (MAH) acquisition of Aspect Engineering feeding the same infrastructure-build theme. The energy complex was the other rotation leader (Oil & Gas Refining #1, +36), a crack-spread story that bid refiners even as crude fell.

Breadth confirms this was resources-narrow, not broad-based. XJO advancers/decliners were only 107/89, new 20day lows (23) outnumbered highs (8) almost 3:1, T2108 sits at 32.5%, and just 41% of XJO names hold their 200MA. Strip out gold and the tape is heavy. The real test is whether the resource bid can broaden — or whether the 29 September decision simply confirms the downtrend the moving averages already describe.
