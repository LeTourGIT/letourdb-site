📝 Session Summary

Thursday 28 May gave back Wednesday's short-covering bounce with compound interest. XJO fell 124.8 points (−1.43%) to close at 8,592.9, with every dimension of the sell-off structurally meaningful: the index broke back below 8,600, confirmed the full Bearish regime across all four indices, and produced an ADV/DEC ratio of 38:155 — the most lopsided distribution since the May-20 capitulation session. An ADV/DEC below 1:4 is not profit-taking. It is re-distribution.

The immediate catalyst was the Iran deal whiplash: Iranian state TV's publication of a purported draft US-Iran interim peace agreement sent WTI down over 5% intraday to $87.90, briefly providing some relief to inflation expectations. The White House's rapid denial reversed the move, with WTI bouncing back to settle -2.7%. For the ASX, this matters on multiple levels simultaneously: lower oil is disinflationary (positive for rate expectations), but "fake deal then denial" is precisely the kind of geopolitical volatility that forces risk managers to reduce gross exposure — hence the broad selling across all eleven sectors.

Materials (XMJ −2.43%) led all sectors lower — the biggest single-sector decline in four weeks. BHP, RIO, and the major miners sold off on a combination of iron ore hovering near $109 (delayed data) and a softer AUD/USD (0.7137) providing only marginal revenue relief. The irony: AUD softening is a partial positive for miners' USD-denominated revenues (~0.7% revenue benefit per 1 cent AUD fall), but it's a weak offset when the macro risk-off narrative is dominant. Financials (XFJ −1.64%) continued their structural underperformance — the Big 4 remain caught between the RBA's approaching decision and deteriorating credit sentiment as elevated rates feed through to mortgage stress indicators.

The only green sectors were Consumer Staples (+0.25%) and Consumer Discretionary (+0.15%) — both effectively flat, representing rotation into perceived defensives rather than genuine buying conviction. Energy (XEJ −0.29%) outperforming on a day when its primary commodity fell 2.7% is itself a signal: the sector has so thoroughly priced Hormuz geopolitical risk premium that even a significant oil decline couldn't crack it materially.

Breadth statistics confirm systemic distribution: XJO 200MA participation fell to 35% (70/200 stocks), T2108 at 43%, and 20day Lows (27) outpacing 20day Highs (14) by nearly 2:1. The speculative tier is deteriorating faster than blue-chips — XSO QTR-25% stocks (45) now outnumber QTR+25% stocks (33). That relative deterioration in small-caps vs large-caps is classic late-cycle behaviour as institutional risk management trims speculative positions first.

📰 Key Headlines
💥Iran Deal Whiplash — White House Calls Draft "A Complete Fabrication"
Iranian state TV published a purported draft US-Iran interim peace agreement. WTI fell over 5% intraday to $87.90 before the White House immediately denied the report as fabricated. WTI settled -2.7%. Post-ASX-close, Iran rejected Washington's terms, citing demands over Strait of Hormuz control and nuclear preservation. The binary risk event remains unresolved: a confirmed deal removes ~$10-15/barrel oil risk premium; a confirmed deal collapse pushes WTI back toward $100+. Until resolution, ASX energy and rate-sensitive sectors remain in a permanently uncertain state.

NUF hit a 20day High [H] flag — institutional accumulation confirmed alongside the Smart Money Agricultural Chemicals rotation from #55 to #3 in nine sessions.

ASX medium-term beneficiary: NXT (Nextdc) as Australia's primary data centre operator in the hyperscaler supply chain. This was constructive but arrived too late to influence Thursday's ASX session.
📊XJO Bearish Regime Confirmed — Wednesday's Bounce Was a Dead-Cat
The ASX 200 has now confirmed the full Bearish regime (MA10 < MA20 < MA50) across XJO, XAO, and XTO. Only XSO remains in "Neutral Bear."
