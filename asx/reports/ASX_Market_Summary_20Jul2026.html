Session Summary

Monday 20 July was a headline-flat session (XJO -0.06% to 8,791.3) that masked a violent rotation. A single macro force set the tape: overnight US strikes near the Strait of Hormuz pushed oil and bond yields higher and drained risk appetite from the market's most globally-exposed sectors. Energy (XEJ +1.80%) was the sole strong performer; anything with US-tech or precious-metals beta was sold.

Information Technology (XIJ -1.55%) was the worst sector — an offshore import. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell ~20% from its June record into a bear market on China's Moonshot Kimi K3 release and TSMC capex-cost fears. Weebit Nano (WBT [ASX300]) -7.36% and BrainChip (BRN) -7.14% were the local casualties; ASX semiconductor-adjacent names have no shelter from a global chip de-rating.

Materials (XMJ -0.34%) saw a base- and precious-metals wipeout: gold -0.45%, silver -0.97%, copper -0.21%, with local gold miners off ~1.9% and Liontown (LTR [ASX100]) -4.14% and Mineral Resources (MIN [ASX100]) -3.86% leading lithium/diversified miners lower. The offset was energy: Yancoal (YAL [ASX200]) +5.97%, Deep Yellow (DYL [ASX200], uranium) +6.10% and South32 (S32 [ASX50]) +4.62% rode the commodity-premium bid. Star Entertainment (SGR) +9.09% was a stock-specific standout on casino news.

The defensive complexion was textbook risk-off: capital that left tech and gold parked in banks (XFJ -0.08%, near flat) and consumer staples (XSJ +0.43%). CBA's commodities desk read the oil move as the market pricing Hormuz staying open — a premium, not a closure — which is why the index held rather than panicked. But the large-cap/small-cap divergence is the warning: the XJO's Bullish MA stack is doing the heavy lifting while XSO breaks down and only 41% of XJO stocks hold their 200MA.
