The overnight session was a study in late-session volatility orchestrated by a single macro variable: the US–Iran relationship. Oil spiked 4% intraday on a hawkish uranium edict from Iranian Supreme Leader Khamenei, then violently reversed into a -2.5% close on WTI after ILNA reported a full ceasefire agreement — covering freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz — had been reached via Pakistani mediation. The Dow hit a record close on this development; the S&P and Nasdaq eked small gains. For the ASX, the key read is the WTI sub-$100 signal: if the Hormuz reopening holds, the RBA's case for a fourth hike in 2026 collapses materially. Energy importers (airlines, consumer, REITs) re-rate positively; resources/energy names lose one tailwind but gain on improved AUD via risk-on. Copper's +10% May rally — entirely driven by China restocking and tight supply, independent of Middle East — is a genuine structural buy signal for BHP, RIO, and OZ Minerals. ASX SPI futures pointed to a +0.5% open and the session delivered exactly that.

📝 Session Summary — 22/05/2026

Friday's session delivered the ASX 200's second consecutive advance, building on Thursday's +1.47% surge driven by April's unemployment shock. The XJO added +0.41% to 8,657.0 — a five-session high — with Materials leading (+1.27%) as copper's structural breakout extended and energy names caught a bid from the oil reversal. The session was bookended by two macro catalysts: the overnight US–Iran ceasefire report (which sent oil down -2.5% and equities up) and the domestic confirmation that April unemployment hit 4.5%, the highest since November 2021, which fundamentally alters the RBA's forward guidance calculus.

Materials (XMJ +1.27%) again provided structural support. The copper price narrative — up ~10% since May 1 on China restocking and supply discipline unrelated to Hormuz — is the cleanest and most durable of the current commodity moves. BHP and RIO were both in the green, adding institutional weight to what could otherwise be dismissed as speculative positioning. ELV (Elevra Lithium) +12.36% was the session's most dramatic institutional move, breaking to a 20day high and printing RS 96.94 — lithium's re-rating continues methodically and is now confirmed across the institutional tier (ELV, IGO +1.54%, LTR +3.11%, PLS +2.92%). ELV's gain above LTR's (+3.11%) suggests fresh capital rotating into earlier-stage lithium names.

GYG (Guzman y Gomez) +9.57% to $19.81 was the session's consumer story — exiting the US market (Chicago restaurants closed, ~$30-40m one-off P&L impact mostly non-cash) and lifting FY26 Australian guidance. In a higher-rate environment, the discipline of capital allocation away from a loss-making expansion into a profitable domestic franchise is exactly what the market rewards. Short interest remains elevated at 13.82%, making this a partial short squeeze as well as a genuine strategic re-rating. STO (Santos) +1.35% printed a new 20day high — the integrated oil and gas thesis (Hormuz exposure + potential peace dividend) continues to attract Smart Money (ranked #6 in 10day rotation).

The session's weakest sector was Communications (XTJ -1.86%) — domestic telcos face a double headwind: consumers under rate pressure reduce discretionary spending including mobile upgrades/streaming, and the sector's duration characteristics make it vulnerable in an elevated rate environment even if the RBA is now leaning toward pause. Utilities (XUJ -1.09%) and Real Estate (XRE -0.93%) also underperformed, though the framing here is crucial: these sectors are not deteriorating further — they're consolidating after Thursday's partial recovery on the unemployment pivot. If the Hormuz peace deal materialises, both sectors will stage a structural re-rate on the prospective rate trajectory.
