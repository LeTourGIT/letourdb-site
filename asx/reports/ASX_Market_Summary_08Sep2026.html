📝 Session Summary

The ASX 200 fell 1.00% to 8,920.8 — its lowest close in six weeks — as a domestic confidence shock collided with hardening rate-hike expectations. The Westpac-Melbourne Institute consumer sentiment index dropped 5.2% to 84.4, NAB business confidence slid to -8, and Westpac became the last of the big four banks to forecast an RBA hike, now tipping +25bp to 4.60% in November (NAB tips September). With the 29 September meeting 21 days away and market-implied September odds near 54%, the tape repriced duration aggressively.

This was a textbook defensive rotation, not a broad capitulation. Only three sectors held green — Utilities (+0.59%), Health Care (+0.45%) and Energy (+0.22%): the two rate-agnostic defensives plus the oil-leveraged name. The damage concentrated exactly where duration and rate-sensitivity live — Consumer Discretionary (-1.88%), IT (-1.76%), Financials (-1.63%) and Real Estate (-1.47%). Banks did the heavy lifting to the downside (ANZ -2.9%, CBA -1.8%, NAB -1.7%, Westpac -1.5%), the direct expression of hike plus mortgage-stress fears.

Stock-level catalysts cut against the gloom. SRL (Sunrise Energy Metals) +21.19% was the standout on its FY26 results and final dividend. AEL (Amplitude Energy) +12.31% intersected high-quality gas at Juliet-1, and CXO (Core Lithium) +6.06% produced first spodumene concentrate at the restarted Finniss operation — a genuine lithium milestone. Gold names (TCG +7.23%, plus RS leaders RSG, VAU, GMD) provided the Materials cushion that kept XMJ to just -0.57% even as BSL fell 5.84% on the escalating US-Canada steel-tariff war.

Oil is the macro wildcard. WTI rose 2.85% to US$94 and Brent hit a six-week high near US$98 after fresh strikes on Saudi Aramco facilities and unresolved Strait of Hormuz tensions. Higher fuel feeds straight into Australian headline CPI — the precise channel that hardens the RBA's hand — so an oil spike is unambiguously hawkish for Sydney regardless of its risk-on gloss elsewhere. Housing stress deepened in parallel, with property developer Bathla's near-collapse compounding the banks' bad-debt overhang and REIT weakness.

Beneath the index, the resilience of small-caps stands out: XSO fell just 0.33% versus XJO's 1.00%, having largely side-stepped the bank-heavy selloff — though it remains the only index below its 200-day average. Breadth was decisively negative (XJO advancers/decliners 59/134, T2108 at 41%, 27 new 20-day lows against 5 highs), and the structural test is now whether 8,900 holds into the 29 September RBA decision. The post-close EchoIQ FDA update (16:53) is one to carry into tomorrow.
