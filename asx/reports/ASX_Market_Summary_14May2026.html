📝 SESSION SUMMARY — 14/05/2026

Thursday 14 May was a session of extreme bifurcation, not a genuine recovery. The XJO's nominal gain of +0.12% to 8,640.7 was entirely constructed by two isolated catalyst-driven moves — Megaport (MP1) +27.7% on a $254 million AI infrastructure contract win (three agreements with two US tech hyperscalers powering GPU, CPU and storage workloads), and Aristocrat Leisure (ALL) +13.3% on first-half profit above expectations with a $1 billion buyback expansion. Remove those two names and the session closes in the red. The breadth data confirms this starkly: 80 advancers vs 110 decliners — a 0.73:1 advance-decline ratio on a technically positive session — the lowest ratio of the past week.

The session's dominant macro narrative is the intersection of three compounding headwinds. First, Federal Budget fallout (day 2) — Tuesday night's Budget introduced the most significant property tax changes in two decades: negative gearing restricted to new builds from 1 July 2027, and the 50% CGT discount replaced by indexation plus a 30% minimum tax. Financials (XFJ +1.02%) recovered in price but the structural read for CBA and the Big 4 is unambiguously negative — residential mortgages represent 45–50% of bank assets, and the combined negative gearing/CGT shock will compress investor demand for established dwellings, threatening collateral values and bad debt provisions over the medium cycle. CBA's trading update this week was described as triggering a "$30 billion wipeout." Second, the RBA's third consecutive hike (delivered 5 May at 4.35%) remains fully embedded — the 8–1 vote and Governor Bullock's language signal a pause but not an all-clear. Third, US PPI +6% YoY overnight drove 10-year Treasuries to 2026 highs and 20Y/30Y above 5%, validating the structurally higher-rates regime that underpins all of these domestic headwinds.

Consumer Discretionary (XDJ +2.9%) surge is the most interesting sector read: the Budget's housing construction narrative — negative gearing available only on new builds — has lit a direct fuse under renovation and appliance retailers. Breville (BRG +4.2%), JB Hi-Fi (JBH +2.0%), Light & Wonder (LNW +4.9%) all participated. This is the trade: Budget policy has inadvertently created a new-build construction boom thesis, redirecting investment capital from established property into new supply. RWC and REH from the Smart Money Construction Supplies theme that appeared in the 1 May report should be on watchlists. Gold sub-index (XGD +0.9%) is also worth flagging — the inverse relationship between WTI and gold is reasserting after weeks of correlation; WTI -0.85% on Hormuz peace optimism eased inflation expectations and reduced gold's opportunity cost.

The structural picture for the XJO remains challenged. The 200-day MA at ~8,804 has been the oscillation axis since the April 7 rebound from 8,262, and the index remains pinned 1.89% below it. Range support at 8,620–8,600 is less than 0.5% away. The XSO's slight negative session on a day the XJO managed marginal green reinforces that the small-cap speculative layer is softening. Until Hormuz clarity materialises and the June RBA meeting confirms hold, the base case is continued range-bound churn between 8,600 and 9,030.
