📝 Session Summary

Thursday 18 June closed lower, the ASX 200 easing −0.62% to 8,911.1 in a textbook risk-off session — but the index print flatters a tape that was broadly red beneath the surface. Advancers were buried by decliners 56 to 133 on the XJO and 137 to 310 across the All Ords, yet large-cap defensive ballast (Consumer Staples +0.73%, Health Care +0.35%) kept the headline loss shallow. This is a market rotating defensively, not capitulating.

The catalyst was offshore and monetary: Kevin Warsh's debut as Fed chair landed hawkish, and the resulting lift in the US yield path repriced the most rate-sensitive corners of the ASX. Information Technology (XIJ −1.36%) was the weakest major sector and Real Estate (XRE −1.15%) close behind — both the predictable victims of a "higher-for-longer" global read. The local ABC wrap framed the day bluntly: the ASX fell as a US rate rise this year looks increasingly likely.

The commodity complex was the other story. The signed US-Iran peace deal — covering the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz — combined with an IEA forecast of a 2027 supply glut to knock WTI to roughly US$74 (Energy XEJ −1.16% on the day, −8.21% over five sessions). At the same time bullion consolidated (gold −0.31%), and the gold-mining cohort was dumped wholesale: Ora Banda (OBM, ASX200) −7.53%, Pantoro (PNR, ASX200) −6.56%, and a long tail of gold/silver specs (SVL −13.79%, AEE −13.91%) filled the losers board. Materials (XMJ −1.27%) was the worst major sector — but note its +7.93% five-day gain; this was profit-taking after a strong run, not a thesis breaking.

The clear winner was uranium, which bucked the entire tape on the back of the US nuclear-fuel supply-chain narrative reinforced by the Iran deal: Cauldron (CXU) +33.33%, Peninsula (PEN) +19.32% and Atomic Eagle (AEU) +12.5% led the gainers. This is the speculative tier expressing a structural Western-enrichment theme with maximum leverage — real signal, but to be read through the [Spec] liquidity filter.

Macro backdrop: the RBA held at 4.35% on Tuesday (unanimous), pausing after three consecutive 2026 hikes, with April CPI cooling to 4.2% from 4.6%. Bullock explicitly kept the door open to further tightening, so the hold is a pause, not a pivot — and Financials' +3.42% five-day gain shows banks are still treated as the net beneficiary of the higher-rate plateau (NIM tailwind). The falling oil price is the quiet game-changer: if WTI holds sub-$80, the inflation case that drove the third hike weakens materially into the 10–11 August meeting.
