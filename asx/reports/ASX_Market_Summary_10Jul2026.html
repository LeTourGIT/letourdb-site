The overnight tape was a risk-ON tape wearing a risk-OFF headline. The US and Iran exchanged fresh strikes, Tehran targeted US military sites across the Gulf — and the Nasdaq still added 1.3%. That is the single most important read for ASX traders today: the market has decided Hormuz is a headline risk, not a supply risk, because laden tankers keep transiting. WTI fell 1.9% and Brent 2.1% into a live shooting war. Falling crude during escalation is the market pricing a ceiling on it.

For Australia the read is two-sided. Cheaper crude is the RBA's friend — it removes the imported-inflation channel that underwrote three hikes this year, and is why roughly 75% hold is priced for 11 August. The AI-memory bid (SK Hynix's US listing 7x oversubscribed, Micron +5.2%, SanDisk +7.6%) maps to WBT (+7.11%) and the data-centre complex, not to XIJ's mega-caps. Live futures are flat-to-soft (ES -0.04%, NQ -0.16%) at the 5pm Sydney read: no follow-through signal into the weekend.

Session Summary

Friday 10 July closed the week with XJO +0.50% to 8,806.0, and the headline number is the least interesting thing about it. The session was a violent sector rotation dressed up as a quiet half-percent advance: Materials +2.32% against Health Care -1.96% — a 4.3-point spread between best and worst sector on a day the index moved 50 basis points. Only three of eleven sectors closed green. Capital did not enter this market today. It moved across it.

Materials is the mandated lead, and the composition of the bounce matters far more than its size. XMJ +2.32% arrives after roughly a 14% drawdown from 17 June, but this was not an iron ore rally — iron ore CFR China 62% sits at $98.57/t, -0.29%, stuck in a $95-105 band on Chinese port inventory build. Leadership came from uranium (CXU +27.78%, SLX +9.40%, DYL +7.43%, BMN +7.25%, BOE +6.72%), copper (CSC +6.45%, MLX +7.69%) and gold (BC8 +5.56%). LME copper +2.04% and zinc +2.64% gave base metals a hard bid, and UBS's note on the pending US Section 232 copper tariff decision — arguing any US import moderation is only a modest headwind — gave the copper names permission to run. BHP and RIO appear nowhere in today's top fifteen. This is a second-tier resources bounce with first-tier index consequences, and XMJ remains -4.41% over 5days. Tradeable, not yet investable.

Health Care -1.96% is the funding source and the cleanest mean-reversion trade on the board. XHJ rallied roughly 25% from 3 June and is still +11.68% over 20days even after today. PME -6.34% (and -6.00% over 5days) is the marquee unwind — a stock at RS 93.29 with ASX100 constituency, so this is institutional profit-taking, not retail capitulation. TLX -3.96% and 4DX -2.46% confirm that the sector, not the stock, is being sold.

The quietest and most important line in the entire dashboard is the 20day High list: CBA, NAB, WBC, ANZ, MQG, CGF, ASX, NHF, REA, EDV. That is the whole banking and financial-infrastructure complex printing fresh 20day highs on a day XFJ moved just +0.55%. Banks do not make new highs quietly by accident. With ~75% hold priced for the 11 August RBA meeting and three hikes already delivered in 2026 (cash rate 4.35%), the market is beginning to price the end of the tightening cycle. The first expression of that thesis is always the banks, then REITs (XRE +0.58%), then Homebuilding — which sits at Smart Money rank #3 and has been entrenched in the top three for two weeks.

Breadth says be careful. XJO advancers beat decliners 112 to 78 (1.44:1) — respectable, not emphatic. But only 37.2% of XJO constituents sit above their 20day MA, 41.7% above their 200day MA, and quarterly -25% names (34) still outnumber +25% names (26). On the All Ords the damage is starker: 163 stocks above their 200MA against 325 below, barely one in three. A market where two-thirds of stocks are in long-term downtrends while the index sits 0.29% above its own 200MA is an index held up by a handful of names.
