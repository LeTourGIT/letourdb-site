📝 Session Summary

The ASX 200 rose 155.8 points (+1.82%) to 8,726.4, snapping an eight-session losing streak that had taken 6.4% off the index. The trigger was offshore: Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO 11 days after he resigned, and Wall Street had its best session since April 2025. Ten of 11 sectors closed higher; Information Technology (XIJ +2.97%) led and Energy (XEJ -0.18%) was the only decliner.

Resources did real work (XMJ +1.72%), but the composition matters. Gold miners led — RSG +4.39%, WAF +3.59%, BGL +3.37%, NST +2.99% — and FFM added +7.23% on a price-sensitive regional discovery release (08:27). The iron ore majors lagged: BHP +1.35%, RIO +0.69%, FMG +1.94%. With iron ore at ~US$90.67/t near four-year lows, the majors are a China-demand story that an AI bounce does not fix. XMJ is still -3.44% on 5day and -6.75% on 20day.

Breadth was the standout. The ASX 200 had 180 advancers and 16 decliners (11.3:1), the All Ords 395/51, and new 20day lows collapsed from 73 to 10. That is a breadth-thrust day. The trend gauges barely moved, though: only 32% of ASX 200 stocks are above their 20MA, 39.5% above the 200MA, and T2108 is 47%. A one-day thrust inside damaged structure needs a second day before it means anything.

Leadership came from the most-beaten groups — IT (5day -4.07%), Discretionary and REITs — which is the classic relief-rally signature. The Big 4 rose 1.7–1.9% to end a five-session financials slide. The biggest moves had stock-specific catalysts: SHV +17.07% on FY26 results, RWC +11.92% after major shareholders backed its scheme, and LOV +10.58% on its AGM trading update. TUA +32.35% had no ASX announcement in the feed, so treat its cause as unconfirmed.

Forward look: US futures are flat, so Wednesday opens without a fresh lead. RYM and WEB report on 25/11, FPH and TWR on 26/11, and PTX is in a trading halt. Nothing was lodged after the close. The test is whether XJO holds 8,700 and reclaims the 8,812–8,834 moving-average band before the RBA meets on 07–08/12.
