Session Summary

Friday 24 July was a breadth failure dressed as a modest index decline. The XJO fell just 0.75% to 8,772.3, but the internals were the worst of the month: 38 advancers against 149 decliners (0.26:1), 21 stocks down more than 4% against a single stock up more than 4%, and 38 new 20day lows against 13 new highs. In the broader All Ordinaries it was 86 advancers to 353 decliners. T2108 fell to 41% and only 35% of XJO constituents now sit above their 200day moving average. A −0.75% index print with those internals means the losses landed precisely where index weight is not — and that the top 100 is doing all the load-bearing.

Two forces did the damage and they arrived from opposite directions. Overnight, Alphabet fell about 6.9% and Tesla about 14% after guiding capital expenditure toward US$205bn, taking the Nasdaq Composite −2.15% and the S&P 500 −1.21%. Alphabet beat — it was punished for spending, not for missing. That is a discount-rate judgement rather than an earnings judgement, and it transmitted mechanically into Info Tech XIJ −3.98% (the worst sector by a clear point), Comm Services XTJ −1.52% and Consumer Discretionary XDJ −1.06%. Appen −8.38% and Brainchip −7.69% were the sharpest local expressions of the same repricing.

The second force was domestic and arguably the more durable of the two. Thursday's employment print delivered 76,000 jobs against a 15,000 expectation, pushing Australian 10year yields toward 5% and repricing August RBA hike odds from near-zero to roughly 36%. The RBA does not meet until 10–11 August, so this is pure anticipation risk with twelve sessions to run and no circuit breaker. The rate-sensitive complex wore it: Real Estate XRE −1.22% with Centuria Capital [ASX200] −8.49% the worst of it, and ALS [ASX50] −7.59% — a stock with no Section 2b announcement whatsoever, which means a pure multiple de-rating on a long-duration mining-services earnings stream rather than any company event.

Where capital actually went is the constructive part of the tape and it was highly discriminating. Energy XEJ +0.93% led on Brent pushing above US$100 after Houthi-claimed strikes on two Saudi tankers in the Red Sea, with Karoon Energy [ASX200] +10.84% the standout — and again no company announcement behind it, making it clean commodity beta rather than a stock story. Financials XFJ +0.88% rose on exactly the same yield curve that broke the REITs, with Westpac, NAB and Bendigo all printing new 20day highs. Utilities +0.80% and Consumer Staples +0.40% completed a textbook defensive rotation. Four green sectors, and every one of them is either a yield beneficiary or a defensive: that is a market repricing rates up and growth down simultaneously, not one selling indiscriminately.

Materials XMJ −2.84% deserves separate treatment because both legs of the Australian resource trade broke at once. Gold fell 2.4% overnight to about US$4,048/oz as US 10year Treasury yields hit a 52-week high of 4.67%, while iron ore remains pinned near US$98/t with Dalian futures breaking below 1,000 yuan on Chinese steel production controls. Turaco Gold −8.80%, Dateline −10.71% and Chalice −8.47% carried the precious and critical-minerals damage, while Liontown [ASX100] −7.42% extended the lithium unwind to −10.90% over five sessions on a day it held a quarterly webcast (14:43) — context, not catalyst. There is no commodity tailwind available to BHP, RIO or FMG at these levels.

The forward look is unusually loaded. Two trading halts remain unresolved — Greenvale Energy (09:43) and American Tungsten & Antimony (11:31) — each pending a material announcement. Radiopharm was suspended at 09:14 and reinstated at 17:21 having completed a A$12.5M placement plus a A$6M share purchase plan, ClearView determined a special dividend at 16:30 alongside a second court hearing notice, and Energy Action lodged FY26 preliminary unaudited results at 16:34. None of those four post-close items is in today's close. Rio Tinto reports on 29 July and Champion Iron on 30 July, into an iron ore price that is offering them nothing.
