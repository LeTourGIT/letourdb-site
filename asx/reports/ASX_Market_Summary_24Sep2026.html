SESSION SUMMARY

The ASX 200 fell -0.72% to 8,702 in a session defined by the global bond market rather than anything Australian. SPI futures had implied a ~1.2% gap down after the US 10Y printed a 19-year high; the index duly broke below 8,650 intraday before buyers halved the loss into the close. That intraday recovery matters — it came without a catalyst, which says dip-buyers still exist — but the close parks XJO only 30 points above last week's 8,672.5 low, the lowest finish since June.

Domestically, August unemployment jumped to 4.6%, a five-year high, but the composition blunted any dovish read: employment rose 39,500, all of it part-time, with participation up to 67.1%. Crucially, Bullock had pre-framed 4.5–5.0% as the desired range, so markets held ~95% pricing for a hike to 4.60% on Tuesday. The rate-sensitive leg took the hit: Real Estate (XRE -1.93%) was the worst sector, Financials (XFJ -0.83%) lost WBC -1.81%, NAB -1.32%, ANZ -1.06%, and consumer lender ZIP (-11.38%, no announcement) was punished as the market priced rising unemployment and higher funding costs at the same time.

Materials (XMJ -1.46%) was the heaviest index-point drag. BHP (-1.69%) suspended operations at Escondida — the world's largest copper mine — after a worker fatality during maintenance, with no restart timeline. The paradox for traders: an Escondida outage is supportive for the copper price but negative for BHP volumes. Lithium was routed without company-specific news (CXO -15.48%, LTR -7.14%, PLS -5.74%, VUL -5.05%; CXO's only 2b item was its annual report), and gold miners (NST -2.32%, EVN -1.65%, KCN -5.17%) followed spot gold's -1.7% slide as real yields rose. The AUD's -1.07% fall on 23/09 (7d, delayed) is a partial offset: ~+0.7% AUD revenue per cent for BHP/RIO/FMG.

Energy (XEJ +1.17%) was the only sector with conviction — WDS +1.54%, STO +1.79%, ALD +1.66% — as WTI rose +1.18% to US$92.38 and Brent settled near US$103 on the US diesel-export-ban threat. Refiners (ALD, VEA) now sit at rank 4 in Smart Money. The irony is that the energy bid is the same force feeding the RBA's second-round-inflation worry. Stock-specific results still worked: PMV +7.08% on its FY26 Appendix 4E (08:26) and SOL +6.20% on FY26 (08:10), which single-handedly lifted Holding Companies to rank 2 in Smart Money.

Breadth confirms the damage is broad, not just index-weighted: XJO Adv/Dec 65/121, 20-day lows outnumbered highs 49 to 10, T2108 is 27.5% and only 30.5% of XJO stocks hold above their 20MA. Forward catalysts: the Trump–Xi summit (US–China trade truce extended two months) for critical-minerals names, post-close items from Peninsula Energy (impairment) and Reef Casino Trust (bidder's statement), and above all the RBA on Tuesday. Real test: hold 8,672 on a closing basis into the decision.
