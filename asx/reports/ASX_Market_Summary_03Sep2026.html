Session Summary

The ASX 200 added +0.46% to 9,020.1, with the All Ords, ASX 100 and Small Ords all clustered within a whisker (+0.41% to +0.45%). It looked like a broad green session — and by advancer count it was, 125 to 66 on the XJO. But the internals tell a narrower story: new 20day lows (21) still outnumbered new 20day highs (6), only 37% of XJO names sit above their 20MA, and T2108 is a soft 45%. Large-caps did the lifting; the median stock did not.

Financials (XFJ +0.86%) led the tape — the fingerprint of the day's macro catalyst. Australia's Q2 GDP printed stronger than expected, and with July trimmed-mean CPI stuck at 3.6%, the market has nudged the 29 September RBA hike probability back to ~54%. Higher-for-longer supports bank net interest margins, so banks and insurers were bid; Property & Casualty Insurance is simultaneously surging +38 ranks in Smart Money. The mirror image was Info Tech (XIJ −0.78%, worst 5day at −3.98%) and Energy (XEJ −1.24%, the outright laggard) as WTI eased overnight.

The day's dominant single-stock move was mechanical, not fundamental. Corporate Travel Management (CTD −85.56% to A$2.32) resumed trading after a year-long suspension tied to a UK accounting and customer-overcharging scandal (>A$250m; former UK CEO dismissed). Against its last pre-suspension price near A$16, the open was a brutal repricing — but it is a relisting event and says nothing about broad-market health, so it should be excluded from any breadth read. Regis Healthcare (REG −26.99%) is the more meaningful red flag: the government's AN-ACC aged-care pricing announcement (lodged 09:12, price-sensitive) is a genuine funding shock for listed aged-care operators.

Beneath the sector print, the real rotation is thematic. Gold miners own the RS leaderboard — VAU, GMD, RSG, SBM, ALK, RRL and PRU are seven of the top institutional names — while lithium (LTR −4.03%, ELV −5.40%) is being sold as spodumene and lithium-carbonate soften (−2.2% on the day). Uranium provided the cyclical spice (DYL +8.44%, BMN +7.79%). Smart Money confirms a reflation tilt: Industrial Machinery, Homebuilding, Steel, Ground Freight and Auto Parts are all climbing the rankings — a domestic-cyclical bid that squares neatly with a hotter GDP number.

Net: a constructive but unconvincing session. The right way to hold it is 'large-caps bounce on a rate-driven Financials bid, with breadth still not confirming and leadership quietly rotating from lithium into gold.' The 29 September RBA decision, not this bounce, is the next real test.
