04 Session Summary

Tuesday delivered a textbook rotation session dressed as a pullback. The tape printed red across three of four majors — SPX -0.32% to 7,728.20, NDX -0.33% to 29,525.48, Dow -0.34% — yet the internals told the opposite story: SPX advancers led decliners 274 to 224 and Russell 2000 closed +0.32% with breadth of 1,159 to 758. The headline decline was manufactured almost entirely by the cap-weighted top of the market, not by deteriorating participation. This is the cleanest positive breadth divergence in weeks.

The regime read underlines the split. The Excel carries NDX in Neutral Bear with Elevated volatility and a 20-day trend that has rolled to Down, even as SPX, Dow and Russell all hold Bullish/Normal. Anomalous on a day this quiet — flagged, and preserved as the Excel reports it — but directionally it fits: leadership is leaving mega-cap growth. GOOGL -3.84%, DELL -3.69%, Datadog -5.37%, Adobe -3.39% and AppLovin -5.99% (the S&P's worst) all bled, while capital found alternative-asset managers (KKR +6.88%, Apollo +6.26%, Blackstone +3.89%), oil refiners (Marathon +5.03%, Phillips 66 +4.10%) and semi-equipment (KLA +4.01%, Teradyne +3.96%).

The macro engine behind it is the collapse of the June US-Iran Hormuz MOU. With the Strait closed to compliant traffic, the US naval blockade reimposed, and Trump shifting to economic pressure over strikes, WTI added +1.63% to $83.46 and Brent +1.73% — the EIA lifted its oil-price forecasts on the renewed constraint. Energy (XLE +1.25%) and Utilities (XLU +1.16%) topped the sector table; oil refiners (CRAK +2.27%), homebuilders (ITB +2.14%) and uranium (URA +1.78%) led the ETF board even though nothing breached ±3%. This is the exact inverse of the early-May peace-proposal session that sent crude below $102 — the geopolitical trade has flipped from fade to offense.

Nowhere is that clearer than Smart Money, which has become a hard-asset monolith: nine of the top eleven industries are mining, metals, uranium or energy. Mining Support Services sits entrenched at rank 1 — up from rank 129 nine sessions ago — with Uranium, Aluminium, Integrated Mining, Oil & Gas Drilling and Semiconductor Equipment all flashing SURGE. Australian rare-earth miners soared on a US DoD investment. Gold slipped -1.13% intraday and silver -1.70%, but the flow into the ground beneath them — not the metal price on the day — is the structural signal.

After the bell, the AI-infrastructure cash cycle reasserted itself: CoreWeave +10.65% and Super Micro +10.00% both roughly doubled revenue (preliminary AH), Nebius +4.54% extended the neocloud read, and Riot signed an Anthropic compute deal as CME launched AI-compute futures. The paradox of the session: AI-infrastructure suppliers are compounding even as AI-spend skeptics (Oracle -3.69%) get sold. Everything now hinges on Wednesday's 8:30am CPI — consensus 3.4% headline / 2.5% core — with a soft print extending the value/small-cap bid and an oil-driven upside surprise validating the Hormuz inflation impulse.
