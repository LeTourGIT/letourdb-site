📝 Session Summary

Monday was a textbook leadership-rotation session. The Dow closed +0.51% and small caps +0.62% while the Nasdaq 100 fell -0.32% — the mirror image of the tape traders have been conditioned to expect all cycle. The catalyst was a weekend WSJ report that NVIDIA is in talks to provide a ~$250B financing backstop for OpenAI's 10-gigawatt Ohio data-center project (total cost >$500B). Markets read the structure as "circular financing" — the chip vendor guaranteeing its own customer's debt — and re-rated the entire AI-infrastructure complex: NVDA -4.99%, AMD -5.17%, SOXX -2.05%. Michael Burry publicly expanded shorts against NVDA, MU, SOXX and CAT into the news.

The second semi blow was idiosyncratic but aligned: SanDisk -11.02% on the day's largest turnover ($26.8B) after China's ChangXin (CXMT) completed an $8.6B memory-chip IPO that surged 466% on debut — a direct competitive-share threat to Western memory. WDC -4.21% and the semi-equipment cohort (LRCX -4.46%, AMAT -3.61%, TER -4.33%, AMKR -6.54%) plus optical (LITE -6.69%, COHR -3.92%) fell in sympathy. When the market's most crowded long is the funding source AND the geopolitical loser simultaneously, capital rotates rather than leaves — and it did, decisively.

Where it rotated is the real signal. Enterprise software was the primary beneficiary — IGV +3.33% with WDAY +9.01%, ADSK +7.70%, NOW +6.86%, CRM +6.07%, PLTR +7.00%, TEAM +10.35% — repriced as the asset-light way to own AI without the capex or circular-financing risk now attached to hardware. Consumer discretionary joined (XLY +1.31%, retail XRT +2.91%, online IBUY +4.28%, travel AWAY +3.88%, gaming ESPO +3.57%; DASH +6.63%, CVNA +5.92%, RDDT +6.22%). Digital assets were the cleanest risk-on tell: Ethereum ETHA +4.77% and EETH +4.67% topped the ETF board, BMNR +13.49% and MSTR +7.61% surged — CNBC framed it as "crypto rallying on rotation FROM AI infrastructure."

With VIX up a trivial +0.54%, this was orderly reallocation, not fear. The bid even reached defensives (staples XLP +1.46% led all sectors), painting a barbell: safety AND high-beta non-AI risk simultaneously. Underpinning everything, oil collapsed — WTI -3.90% to $81.89, Brent -5.64% — as Iran reportedly agreed to suspend attacks while the US pause holds, evaporating the Strait of Hormuz premium that drove crude ~40% higher earlier this quarter. Falling energy costs are a disinflationary tailwind into this week's Fed meeting (hold expected) — quietly the most market-friendly macro development of the session.
