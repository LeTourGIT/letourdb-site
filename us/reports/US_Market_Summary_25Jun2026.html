04

Session Summary

One catalyst, two technologies

Thursday was a single-catalyst session that fractured technology along the one fault line that matters in 2026: who SELLS memory versus who BUYS it. Micron reported the largest quarter in its history — fiscal Q3 revenue more than quadrupled YoY to $41.46B, gross margin held an historically absurd 81%, and Q4 guidance landed near $50B versus a ~$44B Street estimate. CEO Mehrotra confirmed all of calendar-2026 HBM output is sold under contract, with tightness extending beyond 2026 and new fabs not delivering meaningful bits until fiscal 2028. MU closed +15.74%. This is no longer a cyclical bounce; it is a structural shortage economy.

The second-order consequence was the day's real story. If memory is sold out and contract DRAM prices are up 90–95% q/q, then every company that CONSUMES memory faces margin compression — and Apple proved it in real time, raising prices across its entire Mac / iPad / HomePod / Vision Pro lineup on "unsustainable" memory costs. AAPL fell -6.12%, its worst day since April 2025, erasing ~$275B of market cap. The mechanism is exact: the same shortage minting Micron's 81% margins is now a tax on the hardware OEMs (Dell -5.67%, HPE -4.16%).

The memory tax also detonated the semiconductor-EQUIPMENT complex — the picks-and-shovels beneficiary of the $58B+ memory-fab capex arms race: AMAT +13.42%, TER +10.48%, KLAC +7.62%, LRCX +7.21%, ONTO +6.83%. SanDisk (SNDK +21.97%) led the entire S&P 500 as the purest NAND/storage pricing-power play. The nuance: ON Semi fell -7.21% after hours — analog/auto silicon is NOT in the memory trade and got sold as capital concentrated into the memory-levered names. This is surgical rotation, not a blanket "chips up" day.

Beneath the tech civil war, the market broadened impressively. Industrials led all sectors (XLI +2.17%) on CAT +6.29%; healthcare and materials were green; Russell 2000 closed +0.71% with 990 advancers. The weak spots formed a clean three-bucket short — high-multiple software (PLTR -5.49%, NOW -4.56%), consumer discretionary (TJX -6.04%, ROST -5.89%), and the entire crypto-beta complex (MSTR -9.35%, COIN -5.06%, IBIT -3%) as speculative leverage was bled off. Late, after crude dipped below $70, Iran's IRGC struck the Singapore-flagged Ever Lovely off Oman — oil reversed to close higher (WTI +3.06%). De-escalation remains the base case; the tail risk is not dead, and any dip toward $70 is being defended.
