04Session Summary

Thursday was a regime event, not a pullback. Democrats' sweep of both chambers of Congress turned the AI data-center buildout from an economic certainty into a political variable, and the market priced it immediately: NDX -6.36%, SPX -4.36%, the Dow down 1,833 points, and both the Nasdaq Composite and Russell 2000 closed in correction. Wednesday's post-election relief rally — the best session in more than a year — was fully erased in a single day.

The selling was surgical by theme even if it was broad by count. SOXX -12.15% and XSD -11.23% led all 82 ETFs lower; XLK -8.00% is an EXTREME sector move. The 16d loser list reads like an AI-capex org chart: memory (MU -17.46% on $50.1B turnover, SNDK -22.26%, WDC -17.35%), networking/optical (AAOI -20.89%, LITE -18.68%, CRDO -17.71%, MTSI -18.35%, ALAB -19.62%), compute (AMD -17.26%, MRVL -16.43%), AI hosting (IREN -16.95%) and AI power (BE -16.41%). The Magnificent Seven shed more than $1 trillion of market cap, NVDA alone over $440 billion.

The critical tell is what happened to the beats. AMD posted a record quarter with data-center revenue nearly doubling and traded +7.7% after hours on Tuesday — it closed today at -17%. ANET posted its fastest growth since IPO and raised guidance for the fourth time this year — -10.82%. When the best fundamentals in the sector are sold hardest, the market is compressing the multiple on future capex, not on current earnings. This is a positioning unwind of the year's most crowded trade, and the Nasdaq-100 erased roughly $2.3 trillion in value doing it.

Capital did not leave equities entirely — it rotated. Only 4 of 82 ETFs closed green (ESPO, GDX, GLD, GLTR): gaming and gold. Within the 16d universe, the gainers were AI-monetising software with no capex burden (APP +31.04%, EPAM +20.63%, U +14.93%, TTWO +2.13%), plus vol beneficiaries (CBOE +2.28%) and healthcare distributors (CAH, BDX). Smart Money confirms the pivot: 7 of the top 16 industries are Consumer Cyclical, while Electrical Components (rank 9 to 81 in two days) and Construction & Engineering (rank 23 to 87) — the physical data-center build — were dumped.

Oil added a second shock: WTI +5.24% to $91.20 and Brent +6.05% to $99.25 as the US issued fresh sanctions on Iranian oil shipments to China — yet XLE fell -2.98% and OIH -5.03%. Energy equities being sold into a crude spike is the signature of forced de-grossing: funds sell what they own, not what they want to. Retail traders posted their largest single-day equity selling since the 2020 COVID crash.

Breadth paradox worth respecting: SPX printed 73 advancers vs 427 decliners (1:5.8) with 101 stocks down 4%+, yet 62.4% of SPX members remain above their 50MA while the index itself sits 4.53% below it. That gap is the cap-weight distortion running in reverse — the generals are falling faster than the army. It means the median stock still has structure to defend; the AI cohort does not.
