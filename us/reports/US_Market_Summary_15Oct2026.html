04Session Summary

Thursday was a rotation day: a broad rally sat on top of a flat index. 377 of 500 S&P 500 members advanced (3.1:1), and the Russell 2000 printed 1,467/470 only 24 hours after a 339/1,599 washout. Yet the Nasdaq 100 closed -0.05% and the S&P 500 rose just +0.33%. The cap-weighted tail absorbed the rally: NVDA -1.84% on $27.5B of turnover, AMD -1.50%, AVGO -0.55% and TSLA -1.29%, while DELL -6.80% ($7.7B) and HPE -7.10% led the AI-hardware liquidation.

The macro chain ran backwards from the textbook. PPI came in hotter than expected and MarketWatch flagged that a Fed hike now looks more likely. Instead of rising, the 10-year yield dived, because bond buyers prioritised the 'tech wreck' over the inflation print, with CoreWeave credit default swaps at distress levels. When duration rallies through a hot inflation print, the bond market is pricing a growth or credit accident. That is the most important signal in this report.

Falling yields drove the rest of the tape. Housing caught the first bid: ITB +4.03%, XHB +3.13%, HD +4.31%, LOW +3.94%. Home Improvement Retailers jumped from Smart Money rank 90 to rank 2 in three sessions. Mortgage-linked credit bureaus followed (TRU +10.73%, EFX +7.25%), as did regional banks (KRE +1.77%). Q3 bank prints supplied the fundamental floor (BLK +4.38%, BNY +3.23%, WFC +2.29%), and with UNH +2.79% they carried the Dow +1.08% and upgraded the DJI regime from Bearish to Neutral Bear.

Inside technology the split was surgical. Application software re-rated higher (NOW +6.14%, INTU +6.36%, APP +6.52%, FICO +6.99%, ADSK +6.07%), while the capex layer was sold (AMAT -3.72%, LRCX -3.30%, SMTC -10.01%, ANET -5.04%). Semiconductor Equipment & Testing dropped 63 ranks to 136. Notably, CRWV +5.94% and MU +1.87% bounced in equity even as credit stayed stressed. Capital is not leaving AI; it is leaving AI capex, which fits Julius Baer's 'anti-AI' framing.

Energy is the anomaly to respect. WTI rose +2.33% to $96.20 and Brent to $105.78 on attacks against Saudi Aramco facilities, yet XLE fell -1.58%, WMB -4.92% and the tankers lost 6% or more. Energy equities were the crowded 60day trade, and today they treated higher crude as a demand tax rather than an earnings upgrade. Gold miners took the geopolitical bid instead: GDX +4.34%, SLV +3.39%.
