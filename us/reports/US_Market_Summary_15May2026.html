📝 Session Summary

Friday delivered a broad institutional risk-off flush — all four indices closed lower, VIX +6.78% to 18.43, and Treasury yields hit their highest levels in a year as two structural macro events collided: Kevin Warsh officially assumed the Fed chair on the same day Trump publicly declared he is "losing patience" with Iran following his Beijing summit with Xi. WTI surged +4.14% to $108.99. The 10-year yield spiked to 4.595% — its highest since early 2025 — while the 30-year breached 5.127%, a level not seen since 2007.

The macro read: Thursday's ATH at SPX 7,501.24 was the cycle's reward for optimism about Trump-Xi progress on Iran. Friday's selloff was the market adjusting when that optimism failed to materialize as a concrete resolution. Trump confirmed Xi would like Hormuz reopened and agreed not to supply Iran with military equipment — but that is geopolitical alignment on direction, not a ceasefire deal. Energy markets, properly, did not accept "aligned intentions" as a resolution premium. WTI breaking higher negates the April 30 peace proposal bounce and forces the market back into the Iran-inflation-yields chain: oil up → CPI up → rate hike odds up → growth multiples down.

The Warsh transition adds a policy uncertainty layer that markets had not fully priced. CME FedWatch rate-hike probability climbed from 1% a month ago to ~45% for somewhere in 2026. CPI already at a three-year high; PPI running hotter. The 30Y at 5.127% is not noise — bond vigilantes are repricing the Warsh era in real time. The metals rout was the cleanest expression: gold down 3.19%, silver down 8.57%, gold miners GDX down 7.03%. Gold falling while oil rises is a rate-hike signal, not a fear signal — confirming the primary market fear is inflation persistence, not geopolitical escalation per se.

The session's sole structural positive was solar energy. SEDG +22.93% and ENPH +10.16% against a -1.24% SPX represents the widest positive sector-vs-index divergence of the week. Section 25D residential solar tax credit expiration in 2027 is pulling forward installation demand, creating an earnings acceleration story independent of the Iran narrative. Renewable Fuels #1 and Renewable Energy Equipment #6 in Smart Money confirm the capital flow is institutional and multi-week — not one-day momentum. The energy transition is being accumulated as a hedge against fossil-fuel inflation, and that is a structural re-rating in progress.
