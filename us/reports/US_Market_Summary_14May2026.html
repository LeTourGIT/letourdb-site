📝 Session Summary

Thursday delivered its defining catalyst before the open: Cisco's blowout Q3 — record revenue $15.84B, EPS $1.06 vs $1.04 est, and a Q4 guide over $1 billion above street consensus — triggered a 13.41% surge on 189% of average volume and catalysed a structural re-rating of the entire AI networking layer. IYZ (telecom infrastructure) +3.68% and CIBR (cybersecurity) +3.31% were the only ETFs above +3%, the session's two thematic pillars. This is qualitatively different from the 01/05 software surge: today's driver is hardware and physical networking infrastructure, completing a vertical stack — chip → networking → security → software → services.

The Trump-Xi Beijing summit ran simultaneously, producing a joint Hormuz statement (must remain open, China will not arm Iran, interest in purchasing US oil). Markets read this correctly: directional signal without a resolution mechanism. WTI held above $100; energy stocks rose +0.76%, pricing the optionality of resolution, not the resolution itself. The most structurally significant commodity move was REMX -6.03% — rare earths unwinding a China-supply-disruption premium as US-China cooperation language reduced geopolitical risk for critical minerals.

The macro context remains unfriendly to rate cuts. CPI 3.8% YoY (released Tuesday, highest since May 2023), PPI +1.4% MoM (released Wednesday, largest since March 2022), and retail sales +0.5% (released Thursday, healthy) together paint a "resilient economy + re-accelerating inflation" picture that locks in the Fed's higher-for-longer stance. Kevin Warsh replaces Powell on Friday — no accommodation expected.

Applied Materials AH results (EPS $3.51 vs $2.71 est, a 31% beat on record revenue $7.91B) are the evening's most structurally significant datapoint. A 31% earnings beat in semi equipment does not happen by accident — it reflects multi-quarter capex commitments already locked in by semiconductor foundries globally. The AI equipment cycle is not peaking. It is accelerating.
