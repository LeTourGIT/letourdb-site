Session Summary

Two shocks landed on the same session and reinforced each other. Houthi forces struck two Saudi tankers in the Red Sea after declaring a maritime blockade; Trump threatened major military punishment on Iran and told Axios he was considering a massive attack. Brent settled +7% at $100.69 and WTI +6% at $92.19, both back to pre-ceasefire levels. The structural point that matters is that Bab el-Mandeb was the alternative route while Hormuz traffic sits near a standstill — with both chokepoints impaired there is no reroute left, so the market priced this as an inflation event rather than an energy-sector opportunity.

That distinction defines the whole session. The 10year yield topped 4.70%, its highest since January 2025, the 2year cleared 4.35%, and fed funds futures moved to roughly an 82% probability of a September hike. A discount-rate shock of that magnitude lands hardest on the longest-duration assets in the market — which is precisely where the earnings news hit.

Alphabet beat on every line: revenue $119.8B, Cloud +82% to $24.8B, Search +17%. The stock fell 7.13% because management lifted 2026 capex guidance to $195–205B and free cash flow swung to roughly -$5.8B. Tesla beat on revenue at $28.24B but missed EPS at $0.33 against about $0.53, with operating margin collapsing to 1.4% from 4.1% and capex up 142% year over year — its first cash burn in two years — closing -14.52% at an 11-month low. The mechanism is identical in both names and it is not an earnings problem: the market has stopped treating AI capex as an automatic growth signal and started underwriting it as cash burn against a rising discount rate. Every hyperscaler reporting into August now faces a bar where the beat must arrive with a spending ceiling.

Where the money went is more instructive than where it left. ITA +3.08% was the day's only ETF above +3%, powered by three defence primes reporting into an escalating conflict: LMT +10.54%, RTX +7.33%, LHX +5.09%. Health care ran a completely separate bid — TMO +8.71%, DGX +8.61%, DHR +7.46%, MEDP +14.71% — and extended after hours with THC +15.57% and EW +6.48%. Both destinations share one property: near-term realised cash flow with no AI capex line to defend. That is the organising principle of this rotation, and it is a cleaner read than any sector label.

Then Intel reported. Revenue $16.13B against $14.42B expected, adjusted EPS $0.42 against $0.21, the fastest revenue growth since 2011 on a 59% jump in Data Center and AI, with Q3 guidance above consensus; the stock rose 8.73% after hours. Note that SOXX -0.77% held up far better than IGV -2.16% during the session — the tape was already separating hardware with realised AI revenue from software still spending toward it, and Intel's print validates that split. Friday's semiconductor open is the confirmation test.

The key signal is gold falling. A 7% oil move on tanker strikes and a threatened US attack should be unambiguously gold-positive. Instead gold fell 1.85% and silver 3.53%. That inversion only occurs when real yields dominate the safe-haven bid, and a 10year at 4.70% with an 82% September hike probability is exactly that condition. Today was a rates event that happened to be triggered by oil — not a geopolitical risk event. The AUD holding 0.6990 while the metals complex sold off is a quiet vote that FX markets do not expect the move to persist. Crypto behaved as high-beta equity rather than as a hedge: IBIT -1.85% is orderly, but MSTR -6.38% and BMNR -6.48% show where the positioning stress actually sits.

Key Headlines

AI CAPEX
Alphabet and Tesla erase roughly $400B in combined value on spending, not results
Alphabet posted revenue of $119.8B with Cloud up 82% and beat across the board, then fell 7.13% after lifting 2026 capex guidance to $195-205B and reporting negative free cash flow of about -$5.8B. Tesla beat on revenue at $28.24B but missed badly on EPS ($0.33 vs ~$0.53), with operating margin collapsing to 1.4% from 4.1% and capex up 142% year over year. The mechanism is identical in both: the market has stopped treating AI capex as an automatic growth signal and started underwriting it as cash burn. Every hyperscaler reporting into August now faces a raised bar where the beat must come with a spending ceiling.
GEOPOLITICAL
Brent settles above $100 for the first time since May after Red Sea tanker strikes
Houthi forces struck two Saudi tankers in the Red Sea after declaring a maritime blockade, and Trump threatened major military punishment against Iran, later telling Axios he was considering a massive attack. Brent closed +7% at $100.69 and WTI +6% at $92.19, both at levels last seen before last month's tentative peace deal. The critical structural point is that Bab el-Mandeb was the alternative route while Hormuz traffic remains near a standstill — with both chokepoints impaired there is no rerouting option left, which is why the equity market treated this as an inflation event rather than an energy-sector opportunity.
RATES
10year at 4.70%, highest since January 2025 — September hike odds jump to about 82%
The oil move fed straight into the inflation expectation channel: the 10year yield topped 4.70% and the 2year cleared 4.35%, with fed funds futures now pricing better than an 80% chance of a September hike. This is the reason today's selloff hit long-duration growth hardest while XLI and XLV rose. A market repricing from cuts to hikes changes the discount rate under every AI-capex story simultaneously — it is the same shock that made Alphabet's spending look expensive.
SEMICONDUCTORS
Intel beats hard after the close — revenue $16.13B, EPS $0.42, stock +8.73% AH
Intel delivered its fastest revenue growth since 2011 on a 59% surge in Data Center and AI, roughly doubling the EPS estimate, and guided Q3 above consensus. SOXX -0.77% held up notably better than IGV -2.16% during the session, and the Intel print validates that divergence: hardware with realised AI revenue is being separated from software and platforms still spending toward it. Friday's semiconductor tape is the confirmation test.
BREADTH

ITA +3.08% was the day's only ETF above +3%, with LMT +10.54%, RTX +7.33% and LHX +5.09% all on earnings into an escalating conflict. Health care ran a parallel bid (TMO +8.71%, DGX +8.61%, DHR +7.46%, MEDP +14.71%). Two sectors working does not offset a market where two-thirds of small caps declined.
