Session Summary

macro → sector → stock

Friday closed the week with a deceptively quiet index tape hiding a violent rotation beneath the surface. The S&P slipped just 0.25% to 7,711 and the Dow was essentially unchanged (-0.02%), yet the Russell 2000 dropped 1.39% and the real action was in the hard-asset complex — a coordinated liquidation of gold, silver, uranium and the miners that pulled thirteen ETFs below -3% and left zero ETFs above +3%. All four indices sit in a Neutral Bull regime, a one-notch downgrade from the Bullish reading earlier in the week.

The catalyst was Jackson Hole. Chair Warsh's "tough talk on inflation" convinced bond traders he is serious about holding — or hinting at hiking — into September, and Treasury yields rose in response. That single move rippled straight through the risk surface: gold futures -2.75% ($4,504), silver -3.56%, GDX -3.90%, and the uranium complex cratered (URNM -7.19%, URA -5.79% — both EXTREME). Rising real yields are kryptonite for zero-carry inflation hedges, and Morgan Stanley's public questioning of gold's portfolio role added fuel. This was the great unwind of the geopolitical-hedge trade that has run all year.

Technology was the worst sector (-1.55%). NVIDIA reported and the tape could not hold it — down 4.57% with a near-flat after-hours print — while Marvell was pole-axed -10.28% on AI-accelerator disappointment. The chip rebound failed, dragging SOXX -3.20% and XSD -3.68%. Crucially, the second-derivative AI-infrastructure names were punished hardest: the crypto/bitcoin-miner cohort (IREN -12.53%, APLD -7.65%, MSTR -7.34%, COIN -6.33%, BMNR -7.14%) and quantum/optical (IONQ -7.68%, LITE -6.39%, AAOI -6.22%) all bled. When yields rise, the longest-duration, lowest-cashflow AI proxies are sold first.

The offset — and the reason the index only fell a quarter percent — was an enterprise-software earnings bid. Elastic detonated +19.31%, with Workday +5.76%, ServiceNow +4.54% and Atlassian +2.58% confirming a cohort move. Consumer risk-on held too: Amazon +3.97%, Nike +3.02%, DraftKings +4.29% lifted Consumer Discretionary +1.15% and Comm Services +1.42% to the only green large-sector prints. Capital is rotating out of rate-sensitive hard assets and AI-hardware and into cash-generative software and defensive-growth healthcare — visible directly in the RS leaderboard, now dominated by biotech and pharma.

Breadth quietly deteriorated. The SPX printed 227 advancers vs 272 decliners — mild — but new 20-day lows (61) outnumbered new 20-day highs (31) for the first time in weeks, and the Russell's internals were ugly: 699/1,200 adv/dec with 160 stocks down more than 4%. With VIX pinned at a year-to-date low of 14.43, the equity market is calm on the surface while its rate-sensitive underbelly is being repriced — a divergence worth watching into the September FOMC.
