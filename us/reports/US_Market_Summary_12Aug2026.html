Session Summary

Wednesday was a macro-and-earnings double unlock. Two independent catalysts — a benign July inflation print and a blockbuster AI-infrastructure earnings cluster — drove a hardware-led advance that lifted SPX, NDX and RUT while the Dow idled. The signature of the session was not the index move (SPX +0.26%); it was what led and what bled beneath the surface.

Macro first. July CPI landed exactly on consensus: headline +0.1% month-on-month and +3.4% year-on-year (down from June's 3.5%), core +0.2% and +2.5%. Crucially, gasoline fell for a second straight month, softening the very energy channel that drove this year's inflation spike. The read-through is about the Fed's direction of risk: three officials dissented at the last meeting in favour of a rate hike, not a cut. An in-line print with cooling gas takes the hike scenario off the near-term table and lets the Fed hold in September — Treasury yields barely moved. For high-beta tech, removing hike risk is oxygen, and the market inhaled it.

Then the earnings. CoreWeave +19.28% detonated the AI-infrastructure thesis: revenue doubled year-on-year to $2.58B, a smaller-than-feared loss, and a revenue backlog near $104B (+246% YoY) with management raising full-year guidance to $12.4–13.2B. Super Micro +19.02% confirmed the demand signal from the hardware side with record AI-server orders and a raised Q1 forecast. Lumentum +13.63% and Coherent +8.24% carried it into optical; Ciena +11.49% and Arista +6.39% into networking; Dell +9.87% and HPE +8.11% into servers; Credo +8.26%, Teradyne +6.11% and Entegris +7.24% into the chip supply chain. The crucial point: the bid spread across chips, memory, networking, optical and cloud — far healthier breadth than a single-stock Nvidia move — and it lands eleven trading days before Nvidia reports (26 Aug). SOXX +2.32% and XSD +1.90% confirm the re-rate is supply-chain-wide.

But every dollar has a source. The mirror image of the hardware surge was a software bleed: AppLovin -4.68%, Workday -3.30%, Twilio -3.65%, UiPath -2.93%, monday.com -1.74%, and mega-cap online names Meta -3.38% and Uber -4.05%. The tape is executing a clean intra-AI rotation — out of the application/software layer, into the infrastructure/hardware layer. When capacity is "essentially sold out" (CoreWeave's words), the market pays for the pick-and-shovel owners and de-rates the software that has to prove monetisation next. This is a positioning shift, not a growth scare — note RUT +0.61% and 1,151 advancers.

Geopolitics remained the slow-burn backdrop. The Iran conflict is in its sixth month with the Strait of Hormuz still effectively closed (~1% of pre-war transit), Houthi attacks continuing in the Red Sea and on Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery, and the US SPR at multi-decade lows. And yet WTI fell 1.96% to $82.60 and Brent -1.79% to $88.37. That divergence — closed strait, falling crude — is the market moving from panic to pricing-in. The war premium is now baked in rather than re-escalating daily, which is exactly why gasoline eased and helped the CPI. Energy sat this one out (XLE +0.16%) even as the hard-asset complex beneath it stayed firmly bid.
