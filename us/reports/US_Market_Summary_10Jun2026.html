📝 Session Summary

Wednesday delivered a triple shock that unravelled April's optimism in a single session. First: CPI printed at a 3-year high, confirming that Iran-driven energy inflation has migrated fully into the broader price complex — Trump's "I love the inflation" framing directly undercuts Fed rate-cut expectations and introduces stagflation-adjacent pricing. Second: Trump abandoned peace-signal framing entirely, threatening more Iran strikes. Every market that had been pricing Hormuz resolution — gold, semis, construction, industrials — violently reversed. Third: Oracle reported earnings, beat on revenue, then announced plans to raise another $20 billion in debt for AI infrastructure. The equity market sold the leverage appetite. ORCL -5.97% AH.

The sector rotation was surgical and brutal. Industrials (XLI -3.38%) was the worst SPDR ETF — CAT -6.4%, Quanta -5.93%, Comfort Systems -6.12%, Eaton -6.54%. The Construction & Engineering cluster that had dominated Smart Money rankings through May is being systematically liquidated. Semiconductors followed hard: SOXX -3.67%, QCOM -6.92%, ON -5.84%, TER -5.86%. SMCI -27.98% on $5.5B turnover is the AI poster-child unwind of the cycle — 5day -38.27% represents institutional exit velocity, not normal profit-taking.

The defensive repositioning is equally transparent. Consumer Staples (XLP +1.65%) and Energy (XLE +1.50%) were the session's only SPDR winners. Gainers were dominated by food retail (CASY +20.29%), energy (DVN +5.74%, COP +2.68%), telecom (TMUS +3.39%, VZ +2.56%), and tobacco (PM +2.50%) — pure inflation-protection rotations. CME +3.07% and HOOD +3.09% confirm the ultimate hedge: volatility-monetising platforms benefit when everyone else is losing. The Smart Money rotation into Marine Port Services (#1, from #139), Fishing & Farming (#2, from #118), and Food Retail (#13, from #113) tells the story precisely — capital is repositioning for a prolonged Hormuz blockade, not a ceasefire.

One signal stands apart: RUT (-1.10%) materially outperformed SPX (-1.62%) and NDX (-1.98%). Small-cap breadth (883/969) was far healthier than SPX (173/326 — a 1:2 negative ratio). This is a megacap tech unwind, not a systemic dislocation. Gold's -2.74% collapse to $4,092 (first bear market since 2022) is paradoxically rational: Trump's offensive Iran posture + CPI shock = dollar strength + rate hike repricing = gold headwind. The flight-to-safety bid has rotated from gold to crude — an unstable configuration that resolves sharply in either direction.
