Session Summary

Wednesday was a regime-repricing day, driven top-down by geopolitics. The US Energy Secretary declared that Washington has ended Iran's ability to close the Strait of Hormuz, Trump said Iran assured the US there would be no tolls on transit, and tankers moved through the strait as US crude briefly dipped below $70. The war premium that has defined 2026 markets since the February–March Hormuz closure was repriced out in a single session: WTI -3.6% to $69.81 and Brent -4.2% — both back to pre-conflict levels.

That collapse rippled straight into rates: the 10-year Treasury yield fell below 4.5% as the oil-driven inflation impulse faded. Lower yields lit a fire under the rate-sensitive complex — homebuilders exploded (ITB +6.15%, XHB +5.62%, PHM +7.24%, DHI +6.68%), helped by KB Home's +16.65% earnings beat, while airlines (JETS +4.17%, AAL +8.05%, UAL +7.40%) caught a double tailwind from cheaper jet fuel and a softer rate path. Travel (BKNG +7.29%, EXPE +6.97%) and broad retail (HD, TGT, XRT) joined.

The mirror image was a violent safe-haven and war-trade unwind, and the Street pinned it on Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish posture as much as on Hormuz. With rate-cut odds fading and the geopolitical bid gone simultaneously, non-yielding hedges were hit together: silver -6.4% (SLV -7.09%), gold -1.6% (GLD -2.75%), copper (COPX -4.76%), gold/silver miners (GDX -3.95%, SIL -3.77%), and the entire digital-asset block — Bitcoin back under $60,000, its lowest since October 2024 (IBIT -4.08%, ETHA -4.79%). The crypto-proxy equities were carnage: MSTR -9.35%, COIN -5.10%, HOOD -5.87%, CRCL -6.21%. Space/defence names tied to risk-on speculation (RKLB -10.21%, ASTS -6.67%) and alt-asset managers (BX -5.90%, APO -6.13%) rounded out the losers.

Underneath all of it ran the day's second story: memory. Chips sold off through the regular session as traders de-risked into Micron's after-hours print — then Micron quadrupled revenue, posted $24.46 EPS against $1.68 a year ago, and ripped +14.84% AH, dragging SNDK, WDC, STX, QCOM and LRCX up double digits with it. The intraday weakness was positioning, not a thesis break. The Fed also cleared all big banks in its stress test (capacity to absorb $708B in losses), a quiet structural positive lost in the noise.
