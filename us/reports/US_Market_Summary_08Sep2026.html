Session Summary

Tuesday was a rotation day masquerading as a down day. Headline losses were modest (SPX -0.58%, NDX -0.12%) but beneath them capital moved violently along one axis: out of long-duration (software, healthcare, biotech, high-multiple growth) and into hard, physical, cash-flowing assets — energy, commodities, and the picks-and-shovels of the AI datacenter buildout. The catalyst was macro: a fresh escalation in the Strait of Hormuz (US forces struck three Iranian oil tankers after missiles targeted Navy warships; a Saudi facility was hit; Iran seized an unmanned sub) sent WTI +3.0% to $94 and Brent back toward $100. That pushed the 10yr yield above 4.8%, and rising real yields are kryptonite for anything valued on distant cash flows.

The single cleanest micro-story — and the one to build a watchlist around — is fibre optics. Corning (GLW +7.56%) announced a multi-year fibre-optic supply agreement with Verizon, and the read-through rippled instantly through the optical complex: Lumentum LITE +11.04%, Coherent COHR +7.10%, Ciena CIEN +6.32%, and Applied Optoelectronics AAOI +5.7%. This is not a one-name event — it is the market re-pricing the entire optical-interconnect and fibre-buildout chain as an AI/datacenter-bandwidth capex theme with a fresh, named, blue-chip customer commitment behind it. Watch whether LITE/COHR/CIEN hold these gains: CIEN did it on a still-negative 5day (-10.8%), which is exactly the kind of trend-reversal footprint that precedes an RS-list entry.

The other leadership cluster was AI physical infrastructure more broadly — power and hardware. Bloom Energy (BE +9.63%, 5day +34%) leads the Heavy Electrical Equipment industry that Smart Money has dragged from rank 122 to 3 in nine days; datacenter-electrical names (Vertiv, Eaton) drove Electrical Components from rank 67 to 14 in a single day. On the compute side, Intel +9.05%, HPE +7.75%, Dell, SanDisk and the memory complex all ripped — consistent with CNBC's "memory stocks pressing their summer highs." CoreWeave +11.72% and the crypto-miner-to-AI-host names (WULF +8.2%, APLD +7.4%, IREN +5.0%) round out the datacenter-capex trade.

What got sold tells the same story in reverse. Software was the epicentre of the exodus: ServiceNow -4.99%, ServiceTitan -18.7% AH, UiPath -7.8%, Atlassian -6.9%, Adobe -3.5%, Salesforce -3.9%, MongoDB -3.5%, Zscaler -4.6% — a wholesale de-rate of application SaaS as duration got repriced. Healthcare was the worst sector (XLV -2.52%) in a genuine rout: Amgen -10.1%, Stryker -8.8%, Boston Scientific -5.9%, ISRG -4.5%, Lilly -2.2%. Travel/leisure (BKNG -6.7%, EXPE -7.9%, DASH -5.3%) and financials (XLF -1.38%) completed the risk-off picture.

Breadth confirms the caution. SPX advancers 145 vs 354 decliners on a -0.58% tape, with 114 new 20-day lows against just 18 new highs — the internal damage is far worse than the index print. Russell breadth was uglier still (590/1329, 219 new lows). T2108 dropped to 36.4% and only 27% of SPX names sit above their 20-day MA. Yet ~60% remain above the 200-day across all four indices: the structural bull is bending, not breaking. This is the kind of narrow, rotational, rates-driven pullback that resolves either through an oil peak (bullish reversal) or a yield break higher (deeper correction) — the next 3–5 sessions of crude and the 10yr are the whole ballgame.
