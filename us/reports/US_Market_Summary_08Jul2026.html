Session Summary

This was a geopolitics-first, risk-off session — the mirror image of 1 May's peace signal. Trump declared the Iran ceasefire “over” from the NATO summit in Ankara after CENTCOM struck 80+ Iranian targets and 60+ IRGC fast-boats, retaliating for attacks on three Hormuz tankers (Saudi and Qatari vessels hit). Washington reimposed the Iran oil sanctions it had lifted under June's Islamabad Memorandum. Same chokepoint, opposite vector.

Oil repriced the entire tape. WTI +3.32% to $74.66 and Brent +4.08% dragged the 10year yield to 4.57%, re-underwriting the inflation path. That single rate move is the transmission belt for the day's worst damage: homebuilders were the ugliest cohort (ITB -3.97%, XHB -3.08%, both ~-8% over 5day) and consumer-credit lenders cracked with no company catalyst — Synchrony -9.61%, Capital One -5.39%, Citizens -4.49%. Trace the chain cleanly: Hormuz strike → oil +3-4% → 10yr 4.57% → housing and credit gutted.

The offsetting bid was narrow and mechanical. Energy was the only sector clearly working (XLE +1.76%; VLO +6.26%, MPC +5.39%, PSX +5.02%), and OIH +3.15% was the lone ETF above +3%. Technology (+1.24%) was green only because a mega-cap chip bounce (ANET +8.76%, SMCI +7.31%, AKAM +10.67%) and a crypto-miner squeeze (WULF +12.8%, IREN +8.01%) papered over broad weakness — NDX closed up just +0.27% on 40 advancers vs 62 decliners. Not leadership; a handful of index-weight names masking a distribution day.

Breadth is the tell: SPX advancers 112 vs decliners 388 (1:3.5) on a -0.28% day; RUT 601/1375. Only 16 SPX new 20day highs vs 25 lows, and across the entire RS Top 20 only one name (PENG) carries an [H] flag. Participation has collapsed even as the headline indices hold — a narrowing, top-heavy market one catalyst from a broader repricing.

Precious metals offered no shelter, which is itself the signal. Gold -1.15% and Silver -3.48% fell on the day of the shock because the dominant force was the yield spike (a real-rate, stronger-dollar impulse), not a flight to safety. When risk assets and gold sell while yields rise, the market is pricing an inflation/rate problem, not a growth panic — the crucial distinction for positioning into next week.
