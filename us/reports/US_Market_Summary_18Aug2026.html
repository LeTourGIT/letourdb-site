Session Summary

A rotation, not a rout — the AI-infra trade meets the bond market

Tuesday's headline indices masked a wholesale repricing of the AI-infrastructure complex — optical, memory, semis, AI power — while capital rotated into pharma, energy and staples. The SOX semiconductor gauge fell ~5.5% and NDX −1.68% dragged its regime to Neutral Bull, yet SPX only lost 0.69% and the Dow 0.22%. That divergence defines the day.

The proximate catalyst was Fabrinet: a record quarter re-read as "good but decelerating" after a 25% run-up, sending FN −19.4% and dragging AAOI −15.2%, COHR −12.8%, LITE −9.9%, CIEN −8.9%. Fabrinet lit the fuse; the macro caught it.

That macro was the bond market. Long-end Treasury yields extended a surge to multi-year highs — the 30-year pushing to roughly its highest since 2007 — on persistent inflation risk, heavy government borrowing, and, pointedly, the debt issuance funding the AI-capex boom itself. That last link makes the rotation coherent: the same flood of AI-related debt that lifts term premia is the force repricing AI-infrastructure equities. A weekend data point — Anthropic disclosing ARR near $65B, below the $80B-plus some bulls had floated — added a fundamental question mark just as NVDA looms next week.

The unwind was broad within its lane: memory (SNDK −9.0%, echoing "Micron peers falling"), AI power (BE −10.0%, TLN −11.0%, APLD −8.6%), AI cloud (CRWV −12.1%), semi equipment (TER −8.8%). Eleven ETFs breached −3% — every one a semi, miner, uranium or construction fund — and zero breached +3%. The only extreme-move flag was XSD −5.75%. This is the cleanest single-day de-rating of the AI-infra trade this cycle.

Where did the money go? Into defensives and real assets. Pharma led the S&P wholesale — LLY +3.6%, JNJ +3.3%, ABBV +3.4%, GILD +3.3%. Energy (XLE +1.76%) was bid on a 20-year TRGP–ExxonMobil midstream deal (TRGP +7.1% to a record) plus the prolonged-Hormuz-blockade premium, even as crude slipped (WTI −0.94% to $84.45). Staples (MNST +4.1%) and defensive software (INTU +4.4%, ADBE +3.6%) rounded it out. Tellingly, gold and silver did NOT play defence: rising real yields sent Gold −1.4%, Silver −3.1%, GDX −3.2% — a tell that the dominant driver was the yield surge, not a classic fear bid.
