📝 Session Summary

Tuesday belonged to Jensen Huang. Four words at Computex in Taipei — "the next trillion-dollar company" — directed at Marvell CEO Matt Murphy, sent MRVL surging 32.52%, its largest single-day gain in company history. This was institutional confirmation, not discovery: NVIDIA's $2B strategic investment in Marvell and Marvell's tracking greater than $10B custom chip revenue by FY2029 had been accumulating in the RS list for weeks, with the stock at RS 98.49 before Computex opened. The endorsement was the re-rating trigger the market needed to price the connectivity layer of AI infrastructure at hyperscaler valuations.

HPE's earnings (reported BMO, following Monday's AH release) provided the day's second structural catalyst: $10.68B revenue (+40% YoY, a $900M beat), EPS $0.79 vs $0.53 consensus, AI server orders doubling, cumulative AI bookings of $16.4B, and full-year EPS guidance raised by a full dollar. Management stated HPE is now two years ahead of its own long-term financial plan. The ProLiant DL394 Gen12 — built on NVIDIA's Vera CPU, purpose-designed for agentic AI workloads — confirms HPE is now a platform, not a hardware box-mover. Two AI infrastructure hardware prints in successive weeks (Dell, then HPE) are forcing institutional re-weighting of the AI stack above GPU makers themselves. Computer Hardware now ranks #3 in Smart Money rotation.

Alphabet's $80B equity raise — the largest ECM transaction in history — was the session's counterweight. The structure matters: $10B direct to Berkshire Hathaway (the most significant non-tech AI capital commitment of the cycle), $30B in underwritten offerings, and $40B via at-the-market sales. The ATM mechanism creates systematic dilution as shares are sold incrementally into the secondary market — that is what hit GOOGL -4%, not the thesis. Capex guiding $180-190B for 2026 and rising in 2027 signals the hyperscaler build-out is equity-financing its own moonshot. Berkshire's entry validates the AI platform; the ATM creates a near-term overhang worth monitoring.

Geopolitics re-escalated. Lebanon fighting intensified through Tuesday; Iran suspended US talks after ceasefire conditions were debated. WTI bounced +1.57% to $97.47, giving back some of May's ceasefire-driven decline. The oil market is now a binary volatility machine: Hormuz resolution compresses WTI to $87-90; re-escalation pushes back toward $110+. Cryptocurrency simultaneously sold off (IBIT -6.03%, ETHA -4.83%) while semiconductor ETFs surged +6%. This is not coincidence — it's institutional capital expressing a risk-factor preference: physical silicon over speculative digital assets.

Breadth improved meaningfully. SPX printed 257 advancers vs 242 decliners — essentially balanced, a structural upgrade from Monday's 207/293 negative print at ATH. RUT's 979/872 A/D with +0.90% gain is the cleanest breadth signal of the session. NDX remains split 44/56 — semiconductor weight concentration continues to drive index-level performance above what breadth alone would suggest. After-hours, PANW delivered a Q3 beat-and-raise (+~10% AH) confirming AI-driven cybersecurity demand is accelerating.
