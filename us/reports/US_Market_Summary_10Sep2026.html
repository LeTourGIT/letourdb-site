04Session Summary

Thursday was a textbook "negative risk trinity" session: crude spiking, Treasury yields surging to multiyear highs, and equities sliding in unison. WTI ripped +8.16% to $103.94 (Brent +7.90%) as OPEC+ held October output unchanged and Trump warned Iran "not to get cute" over activity at the Pickaxe Mountain nuclear site — the Hormuz premium reasserting itself just as the market had begun to discount it. Simultaneously, a hotter-than-expected wholesale (PPI) inflation print drove the 10year yield toward 5%, and a Treasury buyback operation failed to calm the long end. Oil-push inflation plus a hawkish yield backup is the exact cocktail that compresses equity multiples.

The cleanest expression of the day was in the commodity complex, which split violently. Energy sub-industries dominated the Smart Money rotation — six oil-linked groups in the top 15 — while the entire metals and miners complex was liquidated: every one of the nine ETFs that closed below -3% was a metal or miner (COPX -6.99%, SLV -5.30%, REMX -5.13%, uranium -4%, silver miners -3.9%, gold miners -3.5%). Silver futures fell -5.69% and gold -2.11% as the real-yield spike knocked the props out from under the precious-metals trade that had been a 2026 workhorse. Capital did not leave the resource sector — it rotated hard from metals into hydrocarbons.

Within equities, the selling concentrated in the AI-hardware and semiconductor complex — the market's leadership for the entire cycle. INTC -5.57% ($9.6bn turnover), MU -4.90% ($25.0bn — the day's heaviest tape), LRCX -5.65%, ALAB -5.33%, plus AI-infrastructure names CRWV -6.13%, VRT -5.61%, DELL -5.35%, HPE -6.25% and LITE -5.39%. CNBC's framing — "AI is losing its stranglehold on the market" and "chip stock rebound fails" — captures the psychology: after months of AI carrying the indices, a yield shock forces a re-rate of the most richly-valued, longest-duration equity cohort first.

The offsetting bid was defensive and idiosyncratic, not a genuine risk appetite: staples and tobacco (PM +2.19%) held, managed healthcare firmed (ELV +4.95%), autos rallied on their own catalysts (F +3.20%, GM +2.82%), and Apple stood out at +3.56% on $22.8bn of turnover — the "Phones & Handheld Devices" industry vaulting to Smart Money rank 9 from rank 136 nine days ago, an institutional rotation into the one megacap perceived as insulated from the capex-cost anxiety hitting the rest of tech. Skyworks +9.79%, an Apple supplier, was the single best large-cap gainer. But breadth told the real story — and it was ugly.
