04Session Summary

Three shocks in one tape — and the index barely flinched. That resilience is the signal. Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO with immediate effect; CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO. NVDA opened -14%, was bought hard, and closed -4.34% at $166.19 on $123B of turnover. At the same time Trump said the US doesn't need a deal with Iran, sending Brent +4.91% and WTI +4.02%, and the bond market — reopening after Veterans Day — pushed the 10-year back above 5% (5.107%) as traders lock in a December hike. SPX recovered a near-1% early loss to finish -0.35%.

The key-person discount didn't leave AI — it rotated inside it. AMD +5.86% (5day +44.73%), AVGO +5.54% and ANET +5.06% were three of the top four 16d gainers, GOOGL +1.79% joined them on TPUs, and Cerebras added +16.62% after hours on a Microsoft deal. NVDA is -8.49% over 5day while SOXX is +17.51% — a 26-point gap that re-prices NVDA's governance, not AI capex. What did sell was the financed layer: CRWV -6.86% (NVDA owns ~13%), NBIS -16.84%, SMCI -11.30%, IREN -6.53%. With PIMCO noting AI borrowers now drive nearly half of long-dated IG issuance, a 5.1% 10-year hits the debt-funded builders before it touches the cash-rich chip designers.

Rates did the real damage underneath. SPX Adv/Dec was 123/375 and RUT 433/1,492, with 230 Russell names down 4%+ and new 20day lows (172) outrunning highs (103). Equal-weight RSP -0.87% vs IVV -0.30% quantifies the cap-weight cushion at roughly 57bp — the index print hid a broad, rate-driven de-risking.

Oil drew the sector map. XLE +1.51% was the only meaningful green sector (VLO +3.09%, MPC +2.48%, XOM +1.84%), while airlines paid the fuel bill (JETS -3.81%, UAL -6.02%, AAL -5.60%, DAL -5.22%). Higher real yields and a firmer dollar crushed non-yielding assets: gold -2.48%, GDX -3.95%, SIL -3.96%, URNM -5.02%, IBIT -4.45%. Fourteen ETFs closed below -3% and none above +3%.

Smart Money confirms the posture. The rotation table is led by Paper Products, Non-Alcoholic Beverages, Personal Products, Reinsurance and Brewers, while Electrical Components — the AI-power trade — fell 85 ranks in three sessions. That is stagflation positioning: pricing power and inelastic demand over duration. It doesn't reverse until the 10-year stops making new highs.
