📝 Session Summary

Thursday delivered the market's first meaningful cooling after an extraordinary seven-day run that saw the S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 print successive ATHs. The session was defined by a three-way collision: semiconductor profit-taking after weeks of vertical accumulation, Iran's declaration that the U.S. had violated the ceasefire by striking Qeshm Port, and a $2.2 billion combined earnings beat from Datadog (+30% AH) and Cloudflare (+15% AH) that is poised to reignite the software/cloud AI thesis at Friday's open.

The semi pullback was entirely predictable given the sector's technical condition. Micron, Broadcom, and AMD (the latter up 19% on Wednesday) each gave back session gains as NDX RSI-15 hit 78.16 — the first extreme overbought reading of the 2026 cycle. XSD -1.53% was the tell: capital was rotating out of semi equipment and fabless leaders into cybersecurity (CIBR +4.23%), cloud software (WCLD +3.03%), and select reopening names (JETS +1.28%). This is the same accumulation-layer rotation visible in the RS table — equipment sliding, software ascending.

The Iran ceasefire violation is the session's geopolitical wildcard. Tehran claimed U.S. strikes on Qeshm Port broke the month-long truce. WTI initially plunged below $92 intraday on peace deal optimism before recovering sharply to close $94.81 (-0.28%), dragging energy equity underperformance wider (XLE -1.84%) even as the commodity itself barely moved. The whipsaw in crude is the market telling you it does not yet believe this is a binary peace-or-war moment — Goldman's 4-6 month normalisation timeline is still the base case. Critically, the U.S. Trade Court ruling striking down Trump's 10% global tariff received almost no session airtime. Watch for that to surface in Monday pricing once traders process its second-order implications for industrials, materials, and retail.

SPX breadth was again negative — 172 advancers vs 327 decliners — but the index closed down only -0.38% because mega-cap tech provided a cushion (AAPL touching an ATH at $290.33 intraday, Intel hitting all-time highs back to its 1971 IPO). The Smart Money Software industry jumped from rank #66 to #6 in a single day — a 60-rank institutional surge that preceded DDOG's and NET's AH prints by hours. This is not coincidence: institutional positioning in the cloud/software layer was underway before the earnings arrived, confirming structural capital allocation rather than reactive news trading.
