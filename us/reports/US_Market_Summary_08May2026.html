📝 Session Summary

Friday delivered the week's most structurally significant catalyst: Apple and Intel have reached a preliminary agreement for Intel to manufacture some chips powering Apple devices, per the Wall Street Journal. This is not a product launch — it is a geopolitical and industrial strategy event. The US government, now Intel's largest shareholder after the CHIPS Act deal with CEO Lip-Bu Tan, played a direct role. President Trump personally lobbied Tim Cook at the White House. The deal simultaneously validates Intel's foundry revival, diversifies Apple's TSMC dependence, and anchors advanced semiconductor manufacturing on US soil. INTC +13.96%, AAPL +2.05%.

The companion macro catalyst was April NFP: 115K jobs added vs a 62K estimate — nearly double consensus — and the second consecutive positive monthly print, a combination unseen in nearly a year. Unemployment held at 4.3%. The strong beat reinforces hawkish Fed rhetoric but markets read it as "growth justifies premium" given the Hormuz war backdrop. Computer Hardware's Smart Money ROC shot to #1 at +15.32% — the Apple/Intel direct beneficiary chain — while Semiconductors dropped from #1 to #3, signalling capital moving UP the value stack from fabrication toward design and hardware integration.

The semiconductor complex dominated the session. XSD +5.80% and SOXX +5.67% are the only ±3% ETF flags. AMD +11.44% on its own Rackspace AI cloud MOU for regulated industry workloads, MU +15.49%, MXL +21.20% (RS #1). RXT +55.97% was the day's most explosive single move. The Apple/Intel deal is a direct order pipeline event for Intel's 18A process node — activating the entire equipment and testing supply chain above it.

The Iran situation deteriorated with US forces firing on two more Iranian tankers. The May 1 peace proposal has not progressed. WTI rebounded to $98.87 (+0.50%), but energy equities (XLE -0.45%) failed to participate. The market is maintaining a structural resolution scenario that could violently reprice if ceasefire talks collapse entirely.
