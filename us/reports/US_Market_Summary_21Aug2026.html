Session Summary

Friday's tape was a textbook monetary-debasement rotation. The macro trigger sits in the bond market: Treasury Secretary Bessent's increasingly activist debt-management push is stoking inflation worries even as it aims to calm yields, and Ray Dalio publicly framed the move as a signal a debt crisis is drawing closer — explicitly recommending gold and bitcoin. The market traded exactly that script: hard assets and crypto bid together, the dollar softer (AUD/USD +0.80%), and rate-sensitive defensives sold.

Metals and miners were the dominant institutional story. Uranium miners URNM +7.66%, rare earths REMX +6.69% (MP Materials +9.10%), copper COPX +5.18% (Freeport +7.64%) and broad metals XME +4.05% drove Materials XLB to the top sector at +2.14%. Nine ETFs closed above +3% and not one below -3% — every one a hard-asset or capital-markets vehicle. This is a full cyclical-commodity repricing spanning precious and industrial metals, not a lone gold hedge.

Crypto ran in lockstep. IBIT +5.51% (5day +22.59%) and ETHA +3.70% (5day +28.63%) capped a ferocious week; the equity proxies led the market — Robinhood +13.70%, Coinbase +8.20%, Strategy +6.10%, BitMine +5.84%. Smart Money confirmed it: Blockchain & Cryptocurrency surged to rank 9 from rank 128 three sessions ago. Gold, silver, uranium, copper and bitcoin rising as one set is the signature of a debasement trade, not a growth trade.

The mirror image was liquidation of the electric-utility complex and the AI-power derivative trade. Utilities XLU -2.28% was the only red sector, and the losers list was a wall of grid names — AEP -3.79%, XEL -3.06%, SO -2.72%, DUK -2.31%, VST -1.96% — alongside data-center/power plays Quanta -3.49%, CoreWeave -2.13% and crypto-miner TeraWulf -4.92%. Capital rotated out of the second-derivative "AI needs power" theme and into direct hard assets and crypto.

Semiconductors stayed the drag that pinned the Nasdaq: Marvell -5.57% led the losers, with Intel -2.24%, Western Digital -2.05% and Teradyne -1.93% following as the chip-rebound attempt failed. That single-sector weakness explains the entire NDX-vs-Dow spread and the Neutral Bull regime. Retail earnings were a quiet positive — Target +4.54%, Ross +4.39%, BJ's +5.61%, Estée Lauder +6.02%, Deere +4.27%, homebuilder Hovnanian +11.24% — the consumer is not the weak link.
