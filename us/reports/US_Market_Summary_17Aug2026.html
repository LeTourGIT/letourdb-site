Session Summary

A Rotation, Not a Rout

Monday was risk-off on a dual macro shock. Oil re-priced sharply higher — WTI +3.27% to $84.93, Brent +2.68% — as Iran tensions re-escalated: Trump refused to extend the ceasefire and threatened Oman, Iran rebuffed his Strait of Hormuz claim, and a ship strike was reported. Simultaneously the 30-year Treasury yield topped 5.31%, a 19-year high. Higher oil (an inflation impulse) plus a higher long-end yield (a discount-rate shock) is the textbook combination that compresses long-duration equity multiples — exactly what the tape delivered.

The defining feature was internal rotation, not the index move. "Chip stock rebound fails" captures it: semiconductors and memory/hardware led every gainer board (SMTC +9.88%, SNDK +8.88%, CRDO +8.82%, COHR +7.79%, TER +5.81%, AMAT +5.55%, MRVL +5.54%, WDC +5.35%) on Nvidia's re-awakening — yet NDX still closed -0.17%. Their fuel came straight out of software: NOW -5.08%, MDB -4.70%, TWLO -4.03%, ADBE -3.78%, and the IGV software ETF -2.01%. Capital rotated from the application layer down into silicon/hardware.

Breadth was broadly weak (SPX 131/366, RUT 658/1268) but coherent with the down tape — not a cap-weight divergence. The index fell WITH its internals; it was not propped up by a handful of megacaps. Where did money go? Energy (XLE +1.08%) was the only green sector; miners (GDX +2.13%), shipping (BOAT +2.69%, top ETF) and Bitcoin (IBIT +2.20%) firmed even as VIX jumped +6.60% — a rotation with a fear overlay, not a wholesale de-risk. LHX -4.61% was a CEO-ouster shock, not a defense-sector read.
