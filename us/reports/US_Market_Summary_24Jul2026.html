Session Summary

Friday was a textbook leadership rotation the headline indices masked. SPX closed +0.05% (flat), the Dow rose +0.46% to record-area 51,947, yet the Nasdaq 100 fell -1.15% into a Bearish regime. The single force behind the split was the semiconductor complex: Intel's -7.89% post-earnings collapse anchored a chip wreck that took SOXX -4.40% and XSD -5.55% — the only two ETFs below -3%, and XSD's print trips the EXTREME (±5%) threshold.

But underneath the mega-cap drag, the tape was healthy. SPX advancers led decliners 360 to 140 — a decisively positive 2.6:1 ratio on a flat index, the exact inverse of a distribution day. T2108 at 61.4% and 55.2% of SPX names above their 20-day MA confirm broad participation; only the largest AI-hardware names dragged the cap-weighted print.

The rotation had a clear destination. The "sell chips, buy software" trade reappeared in force — IGV software +1.01% against SOXX -4.40% is a ~5.4-point one-day spread, led by ServiceNow +7.44%, Adobe +6.10% and VeriSign +7.04%. Software monetises AI as a margin expander with a fraction of the capex intensity, precisely as the bond market starts questioning hyperscaler capex payback — anxiety Alphabet's capex-raise crystallised.

Capital that left semis rotated into value, cyclicals and defensives, not out of the market. Real Estate (+2.22%) and Materials (+1.93%) led the SPDRs; the telco trio (VZ/TMUS/T, all +5%+) is the defensive signature. Digital Realty +11.01% is the nuance: AI-infrastructure demand remains intact, now expressed through the power-and-real-estate layer rather than silicon. Energy is the macro backdrop — WTI slipped today but holds a +9.96% five-day gain as Middle East risk keeps a war premium embedded, powering four of the top 16 Smart Money industries.
