📝 Session Summary

Friday delivered the most consequential single-session repricing of 2026 for technology investors. Two independent catalysts converged with destructive precision: Broadcom's Q2 earnings exposed the gap between AI revenue expectations and reality, and the May NFP print of +172K — more than double the consensus of 80K — killed the Fed rate-cut narrative for the rest of the year, sending the 10-year Treasury yield to 4.55%. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) fell 10.44% in a single session. XLK -6.66%, NDX -4.77%, VIX +39.68% to 21.51. This is not a correction whisper — this is an institutional repricing event.

The Broadcom mechanism is critical to understand. AVGO Q2 results were operationally strong: $22.2B revenue (+48% YoY), AI semiconductor revenue $10.8B (+143% YoY), Non-GAAP EPS $2.44 vs est. $2.32. But CEO Hock Tan did not raise the company's full-year AI semiconductor guidance above the $100B target previously communicated. In a market where the semiconductor complex was priced for $120B+ AI outcomes, the refusal to raise the ceiling triggered a full-scale re-rating. AVGO fell ~13%. The XPU custom chip model — the structural justification for the Marvell (-16.74%), ARM (-12.84%), and AMD (-10.86%) premium — came under direct institutional scrutiny. Tan confirmed a shift to "chips only" rather than complete integrated AI systems, repricing the entire custom silicon supply chain in that single sentence.

The NFP data added a second layer of pressure. May payrolls +172K (consensus +80K) — the largest upside surprise since January 2025 — with unemployment unchanged at 4.3% and average hourly earnings at +3.4% YoY. The 2-year Treasury yield hit 4.17%, its highest since February 2025. Markets now price ~85% probability of a Fed rate hike by year-end. This is the most hawkish single-session repricing of 2026. Higher discount rates are the mathematical enemy of high-multiple growth stocks — and after a May in which NDX added 15.3%, the combined Broadcom + NFP shock is a textbook mean-reversion catalyst.

The rotation into defensives was textbook and immediate. XLP +1.71%, XLU +0.93%, XLV +0.61%, XLF +0.21%. The sole ETF above +3% was IAK (insurance) at +3.19% — itself a direct beneficiary of higher-yield environments. COO +8.58%, KMB +4.84%, PGR +4.42%, PG/CL +4.09% represent institutional capital rotating into quality defensive names with pricing power and dividend yield now competitive against government bonds. 28 ETFs below -3%, 22 below -5% — this is cross-asset deleveraging, not sector rotation.

The structural bull regime is intact: all four indices above both moving averages, RSI reset, Bullish regime maintained. But a -4.77% NDX single-session with VIX +39% leaves nothing ambiguous. June 9 open is the critical test: institutional buy-the-dip or acceleration into deeper correction.
