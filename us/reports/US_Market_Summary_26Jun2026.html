📝 Session Summary

Friday closed the week with a headline shrug — the S&P 500 dipped just 0.05% — that completely masked one of the most violent sector rotations of 2026. Beneath a flat index, the semiconductor complex was routed: SOXX -5.64%, XSD -4.30%, with ON -23.7%, WDC -13.2%, SanDisk -10.5%, and a wall of -7% to -9% prints across Qualcomm, Texas Instruments, MPWR, ADI, Teradyne and Flex. Micron — fresh off a blockbuster, record-margin quarter the night before — fell 6.7%, the cleanest "sell the news" signal the tape can produce.

The trigger was financing, not fundamentals. Oracle logged its worst week since the 2001 dot-com bust as investors fixated on its ~$130B debt load, 162% capex surge and negative $24B free cash flow — the poster child for a broader anxiety about who actually funds the $725B+ that hyperscalers have earmarked for 2026 AI data centers. Reports that OpenAI may delay its IPO into next year, struggling to secure demand at a $1T valuation, poured fuel on the fire. The selloff went global: SK Hynix -8%, Samsung -5%, Advantest -10%, SMIC -7%.

Crucially, the money rotated rather than left. SPX breadth was positive — 322 advancers vs 177 decliners — a rare configuration where the cap-weighted index is dragged down by its largest (tech) constituents while the average stock rises. Capital found three homes: software (IGV +4.06%, NOW/SNOW/WDAY/DDOG all +8-10%, fading the "AI eats SaaS" fear), healthcare (XLV +3.03%, MRNA +12.6%, LLY +7.1%, pharma IHE +3.32%), and rate-sensitive defensives (Utilities, Real Estate, Staples all green).

The macro backdrop hardened the rotation. Minneapolis Fed's Kashkari flipped his 2026 dot from one cut to one hike, citing Hormuz-driven energy and fertilizer cost-push with inflation now above 4%. A hawkish Warsh Fed lifts the discount rate on exactly the long-duration, capex-front-loaded AI-hardware names being sold. Meanwhile oil fell (WTI -0.50%, USO -2.86%) despite fresh US strikes on Iran — shipping is rebounding through the Strait of Hormuz and the market has moved from "peak fear" to "pricing in," draining the geopolitical risk premium that had supported energy.

Bottom line: this is a concentrated de-rating of AI-hardware duration, not a market break. Small-caps (RUT +0.07%) and the Dow held; T2108 sits at a healthy 65.4%; VIX actually fell to 18.41. But NDX has flipped to Neutral Bull with negative 15-day momentum — the leadership that carried the tape all year is the leadership now under repair.
