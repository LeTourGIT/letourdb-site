📝 Session Summary

Friday was a textbook relief rally. The catalyst chain ran geopolitics → volatility → tech: Iran halted strikes on Israel, draining the acute war-risk premium, VIX collapsed −11.21% to 15.84, and the chip comeback that had failed the prior session finally held. Every major index closed green — but the internals say relief, not re-acceleration.

Leadership was unambiguous: AI-infrastructure hardware. Servers and storage led the entire market — HPE +12.44%, DELL +11.98% (RBC initiation; +356% over 1year), HPQ +8.40%, SMCI +7.28%, NTAP +8.54% — with the semiconductor complex right behind (ON +8.51%, SWKS +5.14%, ADI +4.85%) and networking confirming (ANET +5.61%, CIEN +4.48%, CSCO +4.37%). Equal-weight semis (XSD +3.08%) out-ran cap-weight (SOXX +1.86%), telling you the rebound was broad across the chip supply chain, not a mega-cap-only bid. Datacenter power completed the theme — Bloom Energy +6.68%, Quanta +5.15%, Comfort Systems +6.29% — the same 'electrons for AI' trade Smart Money is flagging (Heavy Electrical Equipment rotated to rank 5 from rank 129 nine days ago).

The oil tape is the session's most important dislocation. WTI fell −3.13% to $99.99 (first close below $100) and Brent −3.77% — even as the supply news turned materially worse: Saudi Arabia shut its East-West crude pipeline after drone attacks staged from Iraq, the Houthis are advancing on another shipping route, and the IEA warned of a widening supply gap with refining 'stretched to the limit.' Price fell anyway because Iran's de-escalation drained the war premium faster than the new supply threats could re-price. But under the surface, capital is not buying the all-clear: five energy sub-industries sit in the Smart Money top 14, and both the 10day and 60day hot lists are energy/refiner dominated. Spot price down, positioning up = accumulation into a supply risk the tape is currently ignoring.

Beneath the green screen, breadth is the caution flag. SPX advancers led 335 to 165 (a healthy 2:1) with zero stocks down 4%+, but only 24.8% of SPX names sit above their 20MA, 38.6% above the 50MA, and new 20day lows (81) still crushed new highs (19). Small caps are worse: RUT logged 347 new 20day lows against 95 highs and stayed Neutral Bear. T2108 sits at 33.6% (SPX) / 34.3% (RUT) — below the neutral 50 line. The bounce is real and daily participation was positive, but the structural repair has not begun.

The overhang is the Fed. Headlines now treat a rate hike next week as a 'done deal' on hot inflation data — Jefferies' Fisher says the Fed has 'no choice,' and Cramer is drawing 2018 parallels. That is the binary the market must clear, and it bounced into the meeting, not away from it.
