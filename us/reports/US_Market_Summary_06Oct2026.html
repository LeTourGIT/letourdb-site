04

Session Summary

macro → sector → stock

The S&P 500 closed above 7,800 for the first time, +0.58% to 7,818.93, finally clearing the 13/08/2026 high of 7,798.99 after 37 sessions of repair. The Nasdaq 100 made it six straight gains at 31,224.69. The permission slip came from the bond market: the 10-year yield eased roughly 4bps to 5.27% and the 2-year to 4.79%, extending the relief that began when a soft September payrolls print (+29k against roughly 90k expected, per press reports) cooled bets on a follow-up to the Fed's 16 September hike. Trace the chain: softer labour data → front-end yields ease → duration-sensitive sectors bounce (XLU +2.98%, XLRE +1.06%, SPLV +0.90%) → the index prints a record with the VIX down 3.29% to 15.01.

The session's defining trade was power, not chips. Google signed a 20-year agreement with Constellation Energy for 890 MW of new nuclear capacity, delivered through uprates at existing units in Illinois, Pennsylvania and New Jersey. CEG +12.25%, TLN +12.43%, VST +10.77%, OKLO +7.17%, with URNM +4.97% and URA +4.01% the only two ETFs above +3%. The significance is the contract structure rather than the megawatts: a hyperscaler PPA converts merchant exposure to volatile power prices into two decades of contracted revenue, which is a re-rating from commodity generator to infrastructure annuity. Vistra and Talen are not parties, so their gains are pure read-through that the next contract is theirs. Independent Power Producers jumped from rank 55 to rank 7 in the Smart Money table in one session. Keep the context honest: XLU is still -5.27% over 20day, so this is a violent reversal from an oversold base, not yet a trend.

Inside technology the tape split along a clear line. Marvell's analyst day put the AI interconnect market at roughly $65B growing 60-70% a year through 2030, and Nokia's chief executive told CNBC customers would build data centres twice as fast if capacity allowed, naming memory and energy, not optics, as the constraints. Optical and networking names ran: CIEN +13.85% (5day +25.00%), FN +7.78%, ALAB +7.58%, AAOI +7.07%, MTSI +6.81%, GLW +6.02%, MRVL +5.81%, CSCO +4.54%. Against that, semiconductor equipment was sold (KLAC -4.54%, LRCX -3.44%, TER -3.20%, AMAT -2.21%) and storage was hit again on reports that Toshiba will expand hard-drive output (WDC -6.93%, SNDK -2.56%). The tell is SOXX -0.01% against equal-weight XSD +1.09%: a flat index hiding heavy dispersion. Capital is leaving the layers where new supply is being announced and moving to the layers that are still the bottleneck. That is a mid-cycle rotation inside the AI build-out, not an exit from it.

The cost of the rotation landed in healthcare. XLV (-0.17%) was the only S&P sector lower, but that understates it: HLTH -6.39% was the session's only ETF below -3% and qualifies as ⚠ EXTREME, IBB fell 2.34%, and the laggard list is dominated by AI-diagnostics and genomics names: TEM -13.85%, GH -8.83%, MRNA -7.75%, ILMN -6.86%, NTRA -5.87%, with tools names TMO -2.98% and DHR -2.53% dragged along. No single catalyst surfaced in the press; the coverage that exists describes profit-taking after a multi-month run. That matters because this group is the RS list: 14 of the RS Top 20 are Healthcare and 11 of those fell, several by double digits. When the most crowded momentum cohort is sold on a record day for the index, it is distribution funding the next leadership, and the 20day High sheet shows where it went: cybersecurity and AI networking.

Breadth was healthy on the day and alarming in structure. SPX advancers beat decliners 337/162, and new 20day highs outnumbered lows 72 to 19 after Monday's 40 to 77. But only 29.4% of S&P 500 stocks sit above their 50MA and T2108 is 29.2%. Across the 275 record closes in the workbook's history back to October 2018, the lowest 50MA participation previously seen on a record day was 43.4%. This is the narrowest record close in the dataset by a wide margin. The mechanism is visible in the style ETFs: over 20day, QQQ is +5.75% and IWF +5.02% while equal-weight RSP is -2.03% and RPV -4.17%. The Dow remains 5.2% below its August record and the Russell 2000 7.8% below, both in a Bearish regime; RUT fell 0.59% with 844 advancers against 1078 decliners. A 10-year at 5.27% is the wedge: megacap AI earnings can outrun the discount rate, leveraged small caps cannot.

The constructive read is that breadth is turning up from a washed-out base rather than rolling over from a high one. T2108 was 19.0% just four sessions ago and has climbed every day since. Readings below 30% are where durable lows usually form, so if yields keep easing the catch-up trade in equal weight and rate-sensitives has room. The risk case is equally clear: an index at a record with under a third of its members above the 50MA has no cushion if Wednesday's FOMC minutes or the 14/10/2026 CPI re-ignite hike pricing. Crude is the other swing factor, with spot WTI near $96 and Brent futures above $100 as the Hormuz and Yemen situations stay unresolved.
