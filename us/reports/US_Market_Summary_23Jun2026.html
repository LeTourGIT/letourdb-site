📝 Session Summary

Tuesday was a single-theme session: the memory-chip complex broke and dragged every silicon-adjacent name with it. The catalyst was offshore and structural. Korea's KOSPI closed ~10% lower after tripping circuit breakers twice as regulators warned leveraged chip ETFs had become dangerously overheated; SK Hynix and Samsung each fell >12%, and a 2x SK Hynix product collapsed -23.8%. Oppenheimer framed that ETF as equivalent, scaled to its home market, to a $750B US single-stock leveraged product — when it unwinds, the whole tape feels it. The forced-selling wave crossed into every US memory and semi name.

Micron was the epicentre: MU -13.18%, with SanDisk -13.64% alongside. The cruel timing: Micron reports earnings after Wednesday's close, so Tuesday's collapse is pre-print positioning into a leveraged unwind. The most important catalyst for the entire memory cycle lands into an already fragile tape — a binary setup.

The breadth picture is the session's most important non-obvious signal. SPX fell -1.44% yet advancers beat decliners 284 to 215. This is reverse cap-weight distortion: a handful of mega-cap semiconductors (XLK -4.14%) wrote the index headline while the median S&P name rose. T2108 held a healthy 56.8%. Beneath the red screen, this was a rotation day far more than a liquidation day — capital didn't leave, it relocated.

And it relocated into defensives with conviction. Staples XLP +1.87% and Healthcare XLV +1.41% led all sectors; the gainer board reads like a bond-proxy roll-call — IBM +5.04%, Merck +3.57%, J&J +3.37%, Philip Morris +3.19%, Colgate +3.11%, Verizon +3.02%. When IBM and Merck lead a -3% Nasdaq day, institutions are de-risking growth into cash-flow durability, not exiting equities.

The deleveraging bled well beyond chips: silver -2.69% (SLV -5.40%), GDX -4.64%, copper (COPX -6.37%), rare earths (REMX -5.62%). Liquidation across uncorrelated hard assets is the signature of margin-driven, not thesis-driven, selling. Crucially, oil did not spike despite a fresh CNBC headline on Iran declaring Hormuz "closed" — WTI slipped -0.79% to $73.06, confirming the post-April-ceasefire energy premium is fully unwound.
