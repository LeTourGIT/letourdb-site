Fed minutes, 15 to 16 September meeting

  • The 25bp hike was unanimous — no dissenters.
  • Most participants judged another increase would likely be appropriate by year-end; no signal on 28/10 versus 09/12.
  • Several participants viewed policy as not restrictive or only mildly restrictive.
  • Chair Warsh framed September as removing a dose of accommodation; decisions remain data-dependent.
  • A Fed consumer survey put one-year inflation expectations at the highest since May 2023.

October hike odds fell from 51% to about 20% in a week while the 10-year rose to a 24-year high. The front end is relaxing as the long end sells: investors are not afraid of the next 25bp, they are demanding more to hold duration. Equities that are long-duration by financing (small caps, REITs, builders, debt-funded AI capacity) take the hit. Equities that are long-duration by cash flow but self-funded (megacap tech) do not. That is the whole tape.

VIX 15.08 (+0.47%; 5day -7.71%) barely registered a 24-year high in yields. Index-level hedging demand is absent because the index itself is not falling — the risk is being expressed in breadth and in small caps, where VIX does not look.

Session Summary

The bond market set the tape. The 10-year Treasury yield printed 5.36% intraday — its highest since April 2002 — and the 30-year reached 5.73%, a 24-year high, before a well-bid $39B 10-year auction (5.300%, 2.77x cover) pulled the 10-year back to about 5.28%. That auction is why the S&P 500 finished -0.22% at 7,801.77 rather than at its -0.71% session low. The 2pm Fed minutes then confirmed what the long end had already priced: September's hike was unanimous, most participants see another as likely appropriate by year-end, and several judge policy not restrictive or only mildly so.

⚠ BREADTH DIVERGENCE — the index print hides the damage. SPX advancers to decliners ran 136/361 (1:2.65) one session after a record close. Equal-weight RSP fell -0.81% against the cap-weight -0.22%: a 59bp cushion supplied by AAPL +0.91%, AMZN +1.42%, GOOGL +0.81% and a $1.2T Micron +4.06%. The mechanism is balance-sheet duration — cash-rich megacaps with no refinancing need are being treated as the defensive asset when the risk-free rate is 5.3%. Only 28.4% of SPX members sit above their 50day MA and T2108 is 26.4%: readings normally seen near correction lows, not 0.2% under an all-time high.

Everything that borrows to grow was sold. Russell 2000 -1.31% to 2,793.20 (regime Bearish, RSI-15 34.93) now sits just +0.24% above its 200day MA near 2,786 — the line that separates a rate scare from a small-cap trend break. Caterpillar -5.75% was the largest single drag on the price-weighted Dow: long yields raise equipment-finance costs, and an FTC and USDA request for information on farm-equipment distribution that named Deere (-3.80%) added regulatory risk. The same factor ran through housing (ITB -2.59%, XHB -2.55%), REITs (XLRE -1.29%), regional banks (KRE -1.68%) and debt-funded AI capacity (IREN -6.27%, APLD -6.04%).

Rotation, not liquidation. Health Care (XLV +1.03%) was the only green sector — Eli Lilly +2.70%, Amgen +2.60%, Moderna +4.81% — and it was large-cap pharma specifically: PPH +1.22% against IBB +0.12%, while 14 of the Top 20 relative-strength names, mostly speculative biotech, fell. The second bid was power and storage: NRG +4.84%, Vistra +3.88% (5day +20.51%), Everpure +3.71%, Super Micro +3.41%, NetApp +3.20%. Six of the seven names on the 20day-high sheet are hardware, storage or networking. The AI trade has migrated from chip designers to whoever stores the data and sells the electrons.

Hard assets were liquidated, not rotated into. A Fed that is still hiking, with the dollar at its highest since April 2025, removes gold's short-run reason to exist: GLD -1.67% (20day -6.81%), SLV -2.94%, GDX -3.13%, SIL -3.54%, COPX -3.13%. Uranium (URNM -4.83%, URA -4.47%) and ether (ETHA -4.58%) completed a 0-versus-10 scorecard on the ±3% ETF screen. Crude eased (WTI -1.41% to $88.94) but Brent held $100.95. The $12 Brent-to-WTI spread is a freight story — a Gulf Coast-to-China supertanker was chartered for $76M, ten times the pre-war rate — and Smart Money is following it into tanker and refining equities.

What matters next: Thursday's $22B 30-year auction is the direct test of whether the long end has found a buyer. September CPI lands 14/10 with futures pricing about 20% for a 28/10 hike and about 70% by 09/12. VIX at 15.08 says nobody is paying for protection with the 10-year at a 24-year high. That is either confidence in megacap earnings (Q3 S&P 500 growth is expected near 30%) or complacency. Breadth will tell you first.
