📝Session Summary

Friday delivered a broadening session: the Dow broke to a fresh ATH while the Russell 2000 led all four indices at +0.89%, signalling rotation into cyclicals, small-caps, and value — precisely the sectors that had lagged April's mega-cap tech concentration. SPX breadth was healthy at 352 advancers vs 147 decliners (2.4:1 positive), and the Russell showed 1,008 advancers vs 841 — a meaningful breadth expansion versus the narrow 172/326 Adv/Dec ratio of 01/05/26. This advance is architecturally different: broad-based, not AAPL-dependent.

The session's defining move was Dell +15% on no earnings release. The trigger was the confluence of Dell Technologies World 2026 (Las Vegas, 19-21 May), multiple analyst upgrades (Evercore ISI, JPMorgan, BofA all raising price targets), and a strong HPE precedent confirming AI infrastructure demand. HPE +9% and SMCI +5% surged in sympathy, confirming an industry-wide re-rating of AI infrastructure hardware. The AI capex supercycle now has three simultaneous confirmation pillars: GPU (NVDA's $81.6B Q1), networking (CIBR +2.59%, 5day +6.62%), and servers (DELL, HPE, SMCI). The cycle is explicitly post-monopoly-phase.

Thursday's quantum catalyst — the Department of Commerce $2B grant programme with equity-stake provisions for RGTI, QBTS, QUBT, IBM, INFQ and four others — extended its momentum into Friday. The government's decision to take minority equity stakes is structurally significant: federal validation of quantum computing as a national-security technology, aligning sovereign capital with shareholder upside. QUBT's Q1 2026 revenue of $3.7M (from $39K a year ago) confirms commercialisation is accelerating, even if from a very small base. Thin floats and extreme ADRs make these names high-risk individual positions.

The geopolitical backdrop showed incremental movement. Secretary Rubio at NATO Stockholm acknowledged "slight progress" and "good signs" in Iran talks. Qatar has joined Pakistan and Oman as active mediators. Iran is reviewing the US 15-point proposal. The sticking points remain structurally unchanged: Iran's enriched uranium stockpile and Hormuz tolls. WTI's near-flat response (+0.15%, $100.35) reflects this — the market is pricing reduced tail risk, not pricing a resolution. WTI remains trapped below $102, which Goldman views as the Hormuz-normalisation threshold.
