📝 Session Summary

Wednesday was a session defined by two converging macro catalysts — Micron's $1 trillion milestone completing the memory supercycle re-rating thesis, and Iran's reported framework committing to restore Hormuz traffic within 30 days of a deal. Those forces produced the sharpest intra-market divergence of the week: the Dow printed a fresh ATH at 50,644 as value/industrials absorbed the peace dividend, while NDX slipped -0.11% as chip names consolidated following MU's +19% explosion on Tuesday.

The Iran framework is the macro event of the week. Iranian state television reported Tehran committed to restoring Hormuz traffic to pre-war levels within one month of a deal. WTI crashed to $88.53 intraday before the White House denied a formal MOU, with Trump at Cabinet stating: "The strait is going to be open to everybody — it's international waters, nobody's going to control it." The gap between what Iran is offering (managed traffic with Oman cooperation, implying transit fees) and what the US will accept (uncontrolled free passage) remains the final mile. WTI settled at $92.35 — down 5.41% but well off the lows. Every dollar below $95 that WTI sustains removes inflationary pressure from the Fed's terminal rate calculus — a second-order equity positive that the bond market will price faster than equities.

Micron's $1 trillion milestone (Tuesday's session) is the structural context for everything happening in the semiconductor RS list. UBS nearly tripled their price target to $1,625, citing new long-term memory supply agreements with partially fixed pricing — a structural shift converting MU from cyclical commodity chip maker to strategic AI infrastructure supplier with contracted cash flow visibility. SNDK received a Barclays upgrade with a near-doubled PT to $2,300. TSMC reportedly plans 15% price hikes on 3nm chips in H2 2026. Three data points form one thesis: the AI memory supercycle is extending contracts, repricing at the supply level, and pulling the entire substrate complex higher. SOXX's -1.07% session is consolidation within a structural uptrend, not a regime change.

After-hours, the baton passed emphatically to software. Snowflake's +29% on a $6B AWS Graviton deal is the single most important enterprise signal of the week — hyperscalers are committing multi-year capital to AI data infrastructure platforms, not just GPU hardware. That re-rates the entire cloud data sector. Contrast with Salesforce's flat/negative AH (organic growth stripped of Informatica in the high single digits) and Zscaler's -20.78% (two senior sales departures mid-guidance cycle). Enterprise software is bifurcating cleanly: AI beneficiaries (SNOW) vs. AI threatened (CRM, ZS). Goldman's SPX target raise to 8,000 provides the macro anchoring — the gap between current levels and the new target remains meaningful.
