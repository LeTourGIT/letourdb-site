📝 Session Summary

Monday was the day the market stopped buying the AI spenders and started buying the AI suppliers. The trigger was talent, not earnings: Alphabet sank ~5% — its worst session in over a year — after a second marquee researcher (Nobel laureate John Jumper, days behind transformer co-author Noam Shazeer) defected to a rival lab. The exits crystallised a fear that has been building since Alphabet flagged ~$190B of 2026 AI capex and an $80B+ equity raise: that the hyperscalers are pouring capital into a buildout whose returns — and now whose talent — are in question.

The market's response was surgically clean. Sell the capex payers: GOOGL -4.99%, AMZN -4.75%, ORCL -5.00%, AVGO -4.67%, PLTR -6.98%, NFLX -5.82%. Buy the capex receivers: MU +6.82%, SMCI +15.66%, INTC +5.19%, LRCX +5.27%, ON +8.16%, ALAB +5.42%, plus the optical/electrical picks-and-shovels COHR +9.22%, GLW +7.65%, CIEN +7.50%, VRT +7.48%. DA Davidson framed the mechanism precisely: investors are selling the companies spending on AI compute and buying the ones taking that money. Micron — up ~6% today and reporting Wednesday — sits at the dead centre of the trade, fresh off a strategic compute pact announcement.

This is why XLK closed green (+0.37%) while XLC was the worst sector (-2.37%): Alphabet, Netflix and Fox live in Communication Services; the semis that rallied live in Technology. The sector tape isn't contradictory — it's the rotation rendered in GICS. Industrials (+0.49%) caught the same updraft through the physical AI build: Vertiv (cooling/power) +7.48%, Quanta (grid/construction) +5.40%. The AI trade didn't break today; it re-allocated from the model layer to the metal layer.

The Smart Money board confirms it without ambiguity: Semiconductor Equipment & Testing is the #1 industry (from #51 nine days ago), Semiconductors surged to #7 from #84 in a single day, and Computer Hardware sits #6. The RS Top 20 is a semiconductor roll-call — MXL, AEHR, MRVL, PENG, VSH, MU — with software (BAND, RTB) clinging to the bottom rungs. Equipment → fabless → memory: the accumulation is moving through the supply chain, not out of it.

The geopolitical overlay was, for once, a sideshow. Iran declared the Strait of Hormuz "closed again" over the weekend citing Lebanon ceasefire violations, but commercial traffic kept moving, CENTCOM disputed the closure, and the Switzerland talks produced a 60-day "road map" plus a Hormuz coordination mechanism. The market read posturing over rupture: WTI fell -2.23% to $74, below the symbolic line, even as Hormuz headlines screamed. Energy equities (XLE +0.54%) held on a shipping-disruption premium that crude itself refused to pay.
