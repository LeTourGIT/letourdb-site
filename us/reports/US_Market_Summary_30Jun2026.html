Session Summary

The final session of Q2 belonged entirely to semiconductors. Equal-weight XSD +5.24% LED cap-weight SOXX +4.30% — the tell that this was broad supply-chain accumulation, not a single mega-cap melt-up: AMD +7.68% (Wells Fargo PT to $615), KLAC +8.38%, ONTO +7.68%, MRVL +7.25%, CRDO +10.69%, SNDK +10.89%. It caps the chip sector's best quarter ever — roughly $2 trillion of combined value added across Micron, Intel and AMD.

The quarter's cruel irony: NVDA mostly sat it out, the chip-stock loser of Q2 while equal-weight semis, semi-equipment, memory and AI-CPU carried leadership. Capital rotated down the supply chain and across to AMD — a leadership-broadening healthier for the cycle than an NVDA-only tape, even as it punishes index concentration.

The second story is a wholesale rotation within the AI trade. As chips ripped, the AI-power/infrastructure layer was dumped: data-center REITs DLR −5.77% and EQIX −3.94% were the worst large-caps (XLRE −1.98%, worst sector), and the nuclear/power pair CEG −4.22% / TLN −3.78% sold in lockstep (XLU −1.48%). The market is repricing "AI needs power and real estate" lower while bidding the silicon itself. Michael Burry's disclosed CAT short is the same trade expressed short.

Crypto-fintech was the day's outright casualty: CRCL (Circle) −17.55%, MSTR −6.20%, COIN −3.60% as IBIT −2.40% and ETHA −2.53% — a coordinated de-risking of the crypto complex even as equity risk-on raged. Yield-sensitive telecom (T −5.13%, VZ −3.99%, TMUS −3.59%) and staples (MDLZ −3.84%) were hit as June jobs data lifted Treasury yields.

Macro turned constructive: oil posted its largest quarterly drop in six years, Brent its worst month since March 2020, as Iran de-escalated — US-Iran talks move to Qatar Tuesday and a peace framework reportedly grants UN access to nuclear sites. The lone hawkish note: Cleveland Fed's Hammack warned AI could fuel inflation and that rate hikes may be necessary — the new tail-risk for a market pricing AI as pure productivity tailwind.
