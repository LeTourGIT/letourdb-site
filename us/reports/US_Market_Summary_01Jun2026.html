Six OEMs ship it this fall. He also confirmed the Vera data-center CPU is in full production.

Session Summary

June opened on one dominant catalyst. At Computex, Nvidia unveiled the RTX Spark (N1X) — its first PC superchip — sending NVDA ~+6%, ARM +15.73% and MSFT +3%, while gutting the incumbents it threatens: INTC -4%, AMD -5%, QCOM -8.78%. The read-through rippled across the whole AI stack: software (IGV +5.94%) and cybersecurity (CIBR +5.74%) caught the platform bid, and Computer Hardware ripped on the OEM halo (DELL +10.75%, HPE +9.20%).

That is why the tape looks the way it does. The catalyst was structurally bullish but narrow: XLK +2.48% was the only sector up meaningfully, and everything outside the AI trade — and energy — was sold. SPX printed a record on just 207 advancers vs 293 decliners; the Dow's internals were 8 up / 22 down. Evercore's Julian Emanuel notes MU, NVDA and GOOGL alone account for 40%+ of the YTD revision to 2026 S&P EPS; Jamie Dimon (Reagan forum, 29/05) called the tape "exuberant" with risks underpriced.

The second engine was energy — the only non-tech green sector (XLE +1.79%, XOP +2.37%). The macro story has fully inverted since 01/05: the Iran "peace proposal" collapsed into active conflict. The Strait of Hormuz has been de-facto closed since the war began 28/02; US forces struck Iranian radar/drone sites, Tehran fired missiles at Kuwait and drones at the Strait. WTI rallied ~$5 to ~$92 (Brent ~$95). Trump "cited a rapid pace" in talks yet said he's "not worried about oil prices if Iran blocks Hormuz." Oil-up + tech-up + everything-else-down is a barbell, not a broad advance.

Macro reinforced the rotation. ISM Manufacturing 54.0 with prices still hot at 82.1 — growth-resilient and inflationary — pushed yields up and safe-havens down: gold -1.6%, gold miners GDX -3.14%, silver SLV -0.97%. Rate-sensitives followed: Utilities XLU -2.97% (worst sector), Real Estate XLRE -1.64%, Staples XLP -1.06%. A clean chain: strong ISM + sticky prices → yields up → defensives and gold out, AI growth and geopolitically-bid energy in.
