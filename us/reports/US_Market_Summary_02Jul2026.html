Session Summary

The Great Rotation went from simmer to boil. The Dow tacked on 594.83 points (+1.14%) to a record 52,900.07 while the Nasdaq-100 fell −1.61% and the S&P 500 finished dead flat — the widest Dow-vs-Nasdaq spread of the cycle. This is not a market breakdown; it is a violent internal reallocation. SPX Advancers/Decliners ran positive at 355/144 even as the cap-weighted index printed flat — the exact inverse of a breadth divergence. The median stock rose; the megacap-semi complex dragged the headline.

The driver was a second consecutive day of semiconductor liquidation. SOXX −5.57% and XSD −6.74% led lower, with Teradyne −13.6%, KLA −11.5%, Onto −12.5%, Lam −10.2% and Entegris −11.2% — the wafer-fab-equipment and metrology layer taking the deepest cuts. Memory collapsed alongside (SanDisk −14.1%, WDC −9.9%; the DRAM ETF is pacing −15% on the week). The mechanism is duration: soft June payrolls pulled forward Fed-easing bets, but the more powerful effect is a re-rating of the AI trade — cash flows three-to-five years out get discounted hardest when the capex cycle's durability is questioned.

The cash went straight into near-term earners. Health Care XLV +2.63% was the dominant sector (MRNA +10%, VRTX +6%, ISRG +5.9%, MDT +5%), with Utilities +2.21%, Staples +2.03% and the value/dividend complex all bid. Precious metals ripped — GDX +4.48%, SIL +3.9%, gold $4,133 +1.25% — as a hard-asset hedge, and defense primes surged in unison (AVAV +10.7%, NOC +5.6%, HONA +8.7%). The one big-tech survivor was Apple +4.84% on $23B turnover — a mega-cap ex-semi safe haven.

Smart Money confirms the flow: Home Furnishings Retailers, Real Estate Services (60→2 over nine days), P&C Insurance (33→3), Healthcare Facilities, Discount Stores, Managed Healthcare and Fintech (91→7 surge) now top the rotation table. Every one is domestic, value-tilted or rate-sensitive, and not a single semiconductor industry sits in the top 16. Crypto diverged risk-on (ETHA +5.63%, IBIT +2.62%), oil firmed (+1.1%) as Hormuz traffic rebuilt, and Tesla −7% on record deliveries was the day's marquee sell-the-news.
