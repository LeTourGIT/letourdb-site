Session Summary

Tuesday delivered the third S&P 500 all-time high in five sessions, but with meaningfully improved breadth versus Friday's anomalous narrow close. The session belonged unambiguously to semiconductors: XSD +4.45% was the day's dominant ETF, driven by MU +11% (HBM sold out through 2026, market cap crossing $700B), INTC +13% (April ATH momentum extending), and SNDK +12%. Defense Secretary Hegseth confirmed the ceasefire "certainly holds" while framing the Strait as a global burden-sharing issue — language that signals a U.S. negotiating posture rather than unilateral enforcement. WTI -3.9% to $102.27 is the second consecutive -3%+ crude session, deflating the geopolitical risk premium faster than Goldman's 4-6 month physical normalisation timeline. XLE +0.10% while crude fell hard is the clearest evidence that energy equities are now pricing earnings power and dividends, not crude as the primary variable. Smart Money confirms the pivot: Oil & Gas Transportation surged from #123 to #6 in nine days — institutional capital is positioning for Hormuz infrastructure resolution (tankers, pipelines) ahead of physical confirmation.

The after-hours earnings cluster defines Wednesday's setup. AMD AH +5% on $10.25B Q1 revenue (beat $9.89B), $11.2B Q2 guide (beat $10.8B), and Data Center +57% to $5.8B provides the broadest AI silicon validation of the week. This de-risks the NDX's AI concentration thesis; two credible players at the system level is healthier than one. Pinterest's +15-18% proves the exception: a Q2 guide beat converted immediately into equity appreciation because it contained genuine forward re-rating content.
