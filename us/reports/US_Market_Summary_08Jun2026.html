📝 Session Summary
⚠ BREADTH DIVERGENCE — SPX +0.30% on 180 Adv / 319 Dec — cap-weight distortion, not broad recovery

Monday's session was a semiconductor rehabilitation story. Three sessions after the chip complex suffered its worst day since April 2025, the sector mounted a coordinated reversal: INTC +11.19%, MU +9.87%, MRVL +9.63%, KLAC +9.27%, ALAB +9.23%, AMAT +8.64%. SOXX gained +5.87% and XSD +4.33% — both broad semiconductor ETFs clearing the institutional +3% conviction threshold simultaneously — confirming re-entry capital, not speculative short-covering. The catalyst: Iran ceasing strikes on Israel over the weekend removed the geopolitical tail risk that compressed chip appetite since Thursday's NFP shock, sending VIX -12% to 18.92.

The breadth picture remains structurally challenged. SPX closed +0.30% on 180 advancers vs 319 decliners — a 1.77:1 negative ratio on an up day. Five of eleven SPDR sector ETFs were negative. The average SPX constituent finished red. This is cap-weight distortion at scale: technology's megacap semi names are doing the mathematical heavy lifting while the market below them has not recovered from Thursday's damage. SPX T2108 at 48.4% (below the critical 50% line) and NDX above 20MA at only 45% confirm the structural repair is incomplete.

Software bifurcation was the session's underbelly. TWLO -5.95%, AKAM -4.99%, CIEN -4.41%, ADBE -2.57% — enterprise software and IT services continued Thursday's selling even as infrastructure chips rebounded hard. The market is rotating within tech: silicon and hardware in, software and services out. Smart Money's Software rank collapse from #10 to #52 in five sessions is the institutional confirmation: the May 1st WCLD/XSW surge has been definitively reversed in the post-NFP, no-rate-cut, higher-for-longer regime.

INTC +11.19% — approximately $54B of market cap added in one session — demands context beyond a simple short-cover. Intel pre-market on Tuesday was +11.93% (data file), meaning institutional accumulation continued overnight. This is an AI datacenter re-rating driven by x86 server demand and foundry capacity positioning. At $554B market cap, INTC's move is now the foundational statement of the semiconductor cycle: it is not narrowing, it is broadening toward the historically lagging incumbents.
