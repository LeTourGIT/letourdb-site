SESSION SUMMARY

This was a textbook geopolitical risk-off session — the near-perfect mirror of the earlier Iran peace-proposal trade. US forces struck Iran and Tehran retaliated, raising the risk of a wider war. The reaction was mechanical: WTI crude +4.53% to $90.68, Brent +6.97% to $95.17, the US 10-year Treasury yield spiked, and VIX jumped +9.52% to 16.34 — just shy of the +10% elevated-volatility threshold. Every major index closed red and, crucially, breadth confirmed the weakness rather than diverging from it.

The rotation was the cleanest risk-off signature you will see. Trace the chain: oil shock → inflation/rate impulse → yield spike → multiple compression on long-duration equities. Energy (XLE +1.27%) and the defensives — Utilities, Health Care, Staples — were the only green sectors. Everything high-multiple or high-beta was sold: Technology -1.53%, Consumer Discretionary -1.72%, Industrials -1.37%. The semiconductor bounce that had been building simply could not hold against a rising-rate, rising-oil backdrop.

The single most important cross-asset tell was gold. On a genuine geopolitical risk-off day, gold FELL -2.49% to $4,375 and silver dropped -4.08% — the opposite of the textbook safe-haven bid. This is a rate-and-dollar story: the same yield spike that compressed tech multiples raised the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding metal, and Morgan Stanley publicly questioned gold's portfolio role into the move. The miners bore the brunt — GDX -3.90%, SIL -3.74%, COPX -3.90%. When the haven asset sells with the risk assets, the driver is liquidity and rates, not fear alone.

Beneath the index prints, RS momentum leadership swung violently OUT of semiconductors and software and INTO biotech and healthcare — roughly 12 of the RS Top 20 are now biotech/pharma/med-tech names. Simultaneously, Smart Money is tilting into hard assets: Oil & Gas E&P, Drilling and Refining are all climbing the rotation ranks alongside Coal, Agricultural Chemicals and a Steel surge — the classic inflation-beneficiary basket an oil shock pulls into favour.
