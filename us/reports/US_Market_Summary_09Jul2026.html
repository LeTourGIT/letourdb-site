Session Summary

Thursday was a geopolitical repricing session that resolved in favour of risk. Wednesday had priced an escalating Hormuz conflict: Trump declared the Iran ceasefire over, WTI settled +4.4% at $73.52, NYSE decliners beat advancers 3.5-to-1, and the SOX had drawn down 16% from its intraday high. Thursday reversed the commodity leg of that trade without reversing the conflict. WTI fell −3.35% to $71.79 and Brent −3.60% to $76.03 as traders concluded that strikes on roughly 90 Iranian air-defence and missile sites do not, by themselves, close the strait. Oil has stopped trading the headline and started trading the tanker.

The consequence was mechanical. Falling crude relieves the second-round inflation channel that had pushed CME rate-hike-by-year-end odds toward 87% after Wednesday's FOMC minutes revealed a genuinely split committee. Yields eased, the VIX collapsed −6.27% to 15.84, and long-duration growth was repriced upward. But the capital did not flow to the hyperscalers. It flowed to silicon.

This was a semiconductor session, and more precisely a semiconductor-versus-megacap session. XSD +3.80% and SOXX +3.50% led every equity ETF on the board while XLK managed only +2.18% and the Magnificent Seven complex closed flat-to-lower. Micron's plan to lift US fab spending toward $250B and SK Hynix's US listing running more than seven times oversubscribed gave the market two independent, non-narrative confirmations that AI demand is being met with physical capacity commitments rather than slideware. LRCX +6.01%, ONTO +8.77% and AMD +5.67% show the bid extending from memory through equipment into logic; HPE +9.94%, SNDK +7.59% and LITE +11.13% extend it into hardware and optical. Capex is the trade; the compute-consumers are the funding source.

Underneath, the day's most instructive event had nothing to do with oil or chips. Ionis fell −23.90% — its worst session since March 2021 — after the Phase 3 CARDIO-TTRansform trial of eplontersen missed its primary endpoint in ATTR-CM. BridgeBio rose +15.12% on $1.83B of turnover, the largest gainer in the S&P/R1000 universe. This is a clean, zero-sum transfer of a multibillion-dollar cardiomyopathy franchise from one balance sheet to another, and it explains why Biotechnology & Medical Research holds Smart Money rank #16 with ROC +5.71% while the biotech ETFs (IBB +0.51%) barely moved. The money is not buying the theme; it is buying the winner.

Breadth was broad and genuine. SPX advancers 299 vs decliners 201, RUT 1,341 vs 621 with 87 Russell names up more than 4%, T2108 at 63.6% on SPX and 64.4% on RUT. The one dissenter is the Dow, where decliners (17) beat advancers (13) despite a green close — PEP −3.26%, COST −4.21%, MCK −3.16% and CL −2.18% dragging the staples-and-healthcare block. That divergence is not a warning. It is the rotation itself, visible in the only index cap-weighted toward the sectors being sold.

The discipline check: eleven of the fifteen largest gainers are still negative over five sessions — SNDK −8.56%, LRCX −9.74%, AAOI −12.08%, ONTO −9.77%. XSD is +3.80% today and −4.45% on the week. This is a war-scare short-cover repairing a drawdown, not a breakout initiating a leg. Only BBIO (+21.00% 5day) and HPE (+11.74% 5day) carry genuine 5day trend.
