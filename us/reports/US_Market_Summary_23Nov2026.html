04Session Summary

Monday was a governance-driven short squeeze of historic scale. Nvidia's board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as President and CEO eleven days after his 12/11 resignation — and six days after the chair said it did not intend to. CNBC reports the White House sealed the deal. NVDA rose +31.48% on $129.2B of turnover, which MarketWatch calls the biggest one-day gain in its history, and the single largest idiosyncratic risk in the AI complex was repriced out in one print. SPX +4.61% to 7,237.17 and NDX +6.71% to 26,746.10 had their best session since April 2025; VIX fell 25.83% to 26.62.

The rally was broad as well as large: SPX Adv/Dec 416/83 (5.0:1), RUT 1,678/251 (6.7:1), NDX 90/10, and 196 of 236 large-turnover names up with a median +2.91%. Yet cap-weight still did the heavy lifting — IVV +4.58% vs RSP +2.59% is a 1.99pt concentration gap, and IWF +7.97% vs RPV +1.12% is a 6.85pt growth-over-value spread. The money went where last week's pain was: SOXX +13.63%, XSD +12.08%, MU +19.04% on $35.4B, and the neocloud/hosting names (CRWV +22.32%, IREN +23.86%, APLD +20.28%) that carried 5day losses into the day.

But the regime did not flip. All four indices remain Bearish with 5day and 20day trends Down; SPX sits 1.56% under its 200MA and 5.26% under its 50MA, NDX 4.99% under its 200MA. SPX new 20day lows (34) outnumbered highs (2) 17:1 on a +4.6% day, and only 31.2% of SPX is above its 20MA. That is what a relief rally from RSI-15 sub-30 looks like. A trend change needs the 200MA reclaimed and 20MA participation above 50% — neither is close.

Rates are the binding constraint. Williams indicated higher rates are likely 'over time' and the 2-year yield jumped, which is why duration-sensitive sectors trailed (XLRE +1.04%, XLU +1.48%, XLP −0.09%) and why Breakout Capital's Ruchir Sharma frames rising rates as the AI trade's main risk. Deutsche Bank's note that the USD is increasingly reliant on equity inflows ties the two together: an AI drawdown is now also a dollar risk.

Geopolitics was a tailwind via oil. Houthis claimed attacks on a Saudi refinery and Saudi Arabia/Bahrain reported Iran-linked strikes, yet WTI fell 4.12% to $80.03 because Tehran said it is open to talks — the market is pricing diplomacy over escalation. XLE (−1.06%) was the worst sector while JETS +5.16% and ULCC +11.86% (new 20day high, RS rank 11) collected the dividend.

Smart Money's 10day board remains a defensive bunker: only 7 of 137 industries carry a positive 10day ROC and the top 16 is staples, utilities and insurance. Monday's leaders came from the bottom half — Semiconductors sit at rank 78. The test for the next 3–5 sessions is whether semis climb into the top 30; until then this is a squeeze inside a downtrend, not new leadership.
