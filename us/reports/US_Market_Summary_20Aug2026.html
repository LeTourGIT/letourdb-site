📝 Session Summary

Thursday was a bond-market story wearing an equity costume. On Wednesday, Treasury Secretary Bessent announced he would at least double the size of long-dated buyback operations (cap $2B→≥$4B, running Sept 9–Nov 4), and the 30-year yield obediently fell ~10bps to ~5.18% while stocks rallied. By Thursday the entire move had unwound: the 30Y rebounded to ~5.25% — back to where it sat before the announcement and near a 19-year high — and the Dow shed roughly 700 points (-1.32%). The market's verdict was blunt: the buybacks don't even begin until Sept 9, they rearrange the maturity schedule rather than pay down debt, and total public debt just crossed $40 trillion. JPMorgan called the intervention a measure that "belies the underlying structural challenges."

The rotation underneath was textbook fiscal-debasement positioning. Every clearly-green pocket was a hard asset: Energy (XLE +0.27%) on WTI +2.3%, gold miners (AngloGold +6.1%) as bullion held $4,575, and crypto in a class of its own — Bitcoin (IBIT +6.24%) and Ether (ETHA +10.5%, EETH +10.6%) were the day's only ETFs above +3%, and Blockchain & Cryptocurrency surged +117 ranks in the Smart Money model. When investors doubt the government's ability to control its own borrowing costs, they buy the assets that can't be printed. That chain — fiscal fear → yield spike → hard-asset bid — ran through every section today.

The losers were the mirror image: anything priced for perfection at a high multiple. Walmart beat on EPS and raised guidance yet fell 9.2% — a ~40x retailer whose US comps (+2.6%) missed, meeting a tape with no appetite to pay premium multiples while the discount rate is rising. High-growth software (CrowdStrike -5.6%, MongoDB -4.6%, Cloudflare -4.4%) and long-duration healthcare (Intuitive -5.8%, Boston Scientific -5.1%) took the same medicine. Defense was a separate, cleaner signal: ITA -3.5% and PPA -3.2% led ETF decliners with AXON -4.9% and RKLB -3.8%, as Ukraine ceasefire diplomacy (Trump envoys in Cairo, Zelenskyy facing wartime-election pressure) repriced the war premium out of the group.

Moderna was the day's spectacle — down 23.6% after Wednesday's record +177% on the first-ever Phase 3 mRNA cancer-vaccine win (intismeran + Merck's Keytruda, melanoma). That's a valuation reset and short-squeeze unwind (12.5% short float), not a broken thesis, and critically it is fuelling the broader biotech bid: 9 of the top 20 RS names are now biotech/healthcare, a genuine leadership rotation away from the semis-and-software complex that led in the spring. Deere (+6.9%) offered the one constructive earnings note, with management calling 2026 the bottom of the farm-equipment downcycle.
