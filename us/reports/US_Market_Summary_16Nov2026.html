Session Summary

This was a de-grossing session, not a correction of fundamentals. Three forces hit an already fragile tape at once. Nvidia fell another 4.1% into Tuesday's after-close report, its first under interim CEO Colette Kress after Jensen Huang's 12/11 resignation. A reported US move to close the remote/cloud-access loophole for advanced AI chips took the chip complex down harder still (SOXX -5.41%, XSD -5.15%). And the VIX added 18.70% to 22.91 as the street paid up for event insurance before the biggest earnings print of the quarter. The S&P 500 posted its third straight loss and the Dow shed 739 points.

The damage was concentrated in the AI-hardware stack, and the turnover column shows it was institutional rather than retail. MU fell 7.62% on $19.5B turnover and SNDK 8.58% on $10.2B. Together that is roughly $30B of memory stock changing hands in a single session, the signature of funds cutting exposure ahead of an earnings event they cannot hedge cheaply. Optical (LITE -8.48%, CIEN -8.19%), connectivity (ALAB -8.79%, CRDO -8.21%, MRVL -7.27%) and AI-power (BE -7.66%) all moved together. When every layer of a theme sells off at once on one headline, the market is pricing a lower long-run multiple for the whole capex chain, not just a quarter of risk for Nvidia. The policy curb hits the incremental buyer (Asian neocloud capacity serving Chinese demand), and that is exactly the marginal growth the bull case relied on.

The cap-weight distortion runs in reverse today: the index is weaker than its own constituents. SPX fell 1.91% while the equal-weight RSP fell only 1.01%, a 90bp gap that is entirely mega-cap growth. Pure Value (RPV -0.53%) beat Pure Growth (RPG -3.28%) by 275bp. 65.0% of S&P 500 stocks still sit above their 50day MA even though the index itself is 3.30% below it. That is the classic profile of a leadership unwind: the generals are being shot while the infantry holds. It is not a bear market in the average stock, at least not yet in large caps.

Small caps are a different story, and that is the real warning. RUT's regime flipped from Neutral Bear to Bearish today, its first Bearish print in the last six sessions. Only 37.0% of Russell 2000 names are above their 20day MA, and new 20day lows outnumber new highs 331 to 60 (5.5:1). 687 names are down 25%+ for the quarter against 368 up 25%+. With the 10-year yield having pushed through 5% at last week's auction, the most rate-sensitive, credit-dependent cohort is losing its bid even before the NVDA print. A Russell breakdown alongside a mega-cap unwind removes both ends of the barbell that has carried this market.

The rotation destination is unambiguous: staples, managed care and utilities. In the sector heatmap only XLP (+0.27%) and XLU (+0.18%) finished green. Consumer non-Cyclicals is the ONLY economic sector with a positive 10day ROC (+0.15%) and the ONLY one with a positive 60day ROC (+4.19%). Smart Money shows Household Products (rank 80 to 17 in three days) and Multiline Utilities (64 to 15) surging. TSN (+8.72%, earnings) and managed care (CNC +4.91%, ELV +3.86%, HUM +2.50%) led the $500M-turnover gainers. Only 52 of 217 institutionally traded names finished positive (24%).

Cross-asset, this looks like a liquidity squeeze rather than a flight to safety. Gold (GLD -0.71%, 5day -7.01%), silver (SLV -3.94%, 5day -11.94%), Bitcoin (IBIT -5.52%) and Ether (ETHA -6.57%) all fell alongside equities, while AUD/USD is -2.74% over 5days. When the traditional hedges sell off with risk assets, positions are being liquidated to raise cash and margin, not reallocated. WTI fell 3.29% to $85.43 even though Iran struck a US base in Bahrain. Oil's failure to rally on a direct attack says demand fears and de-grossing currently outweigh supply risk.
