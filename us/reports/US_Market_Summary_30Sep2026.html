04Session SummaryRates shock · AI demand · Quarter-end

The final session of Q3 was a bond-market story wearing an equity costume. A cooler-than-expected core PCE print lifted futures and pushed SPX roughly half a percent higher by mid-morning, pricing out some of the October hike risk that followed September's Fed move. It did not hold. The 10-year finished near 5.30% — within touching distance of its highest level since 2007 — and the 30-year closed around 5.64%, still pinned at its highest since 2002. A last-hour reversal (quarter-end rebalancing into a long-end rout) erased the gains: SPX -0.25% to 7,651.54, Dow -0.86%, RUT -0.39%, while NDX alone held +0.23%.

The regime split is now explicit in the Excel: SPX and NDX remain Bullish, but RUT and DJI are both Bearish with 5day and 20day trends Down. SPX sits just +0.05% above its 50MA with RSI-15 at 48.47 — the index is balanced on a knife-edge while the average stock has already broken. Only 21.0% of SPX members are above their 50MA and T2108 is 19.0%, a washout-grade reading that historically marks either capitulation lows or the early phase of an index catch-down. VIX at 16.34 (+1.87%) is not pricing either outcome — option markets are anchored to the megacap tape, not to the constituent damage underneath.

The mechanism is duration. With real yields grinding higher, every long-duration or bond-proxy cash flow is being re-discounted: Staples XLP -1.53% was the worst sector, Healthcare XLV -1.35%, Industrials XLI -1.27%, Financials XLF -1.13%, Real Estate XLRE -1.04% and mortgage REITs (REM -2.57%) the weakest ETF on the board. Technology XLK +0.64% was the only green sector — and within tech the bid was software (IGV +1.20%, CIBR +1.10%), not chips (SOXX +0.21%, XSD -0.07%). Twelve of the top fifteen $500M-turnover gainers were Technology-sector names: SNPS, PATH, HPE, INTC, ACN, MDB, NOW, ADBE, RBLX, INTU, SNOW, ABNB.

The AI-power complex is where the rate shock is biting hardest. BE -4.90% on $4.1B turnover, CEG -3.99%, VRT -2.83% and GLW -3.12%, and Smart Money's Heavy Electrical Equipment industry has collapsed from rank 1 nine days ago to rank 83 — a textbook EXIT. These are project-financed, capex-heavy businesses whose valuations assume cheap long-dated capital; a 5.6% 30-year breaks that assumption. JBL -10.03% on its print is the same lesson in manufacturing form: in a 5.3% 10-year world, 'beat but not enough' is punished like a miss.

Against that backdrop, the AI demand signal itself strengthened. Micron's FQ4 blew through the top of its own guide (revenue $54.23B vs $50.0B ± $1.0B; non-GAAP EPS $33.42 vs $31.00 ± $1.00) and guided FQ1-27 to $61.5B — while its balance sheet now carries $12.9B of noncurrent customer contract liabilities, i.e. customers pre-paying to lock memory supply. HPE booked the first commercial AMD Helios order ($1.2B, Vultr), and Google launched its Gemini 4 flagship as Anthropic's prospectus disclosures put hard numbers on the Google-Anthropic economic loop. The AI thesis is intact at the demand layer; what the market is repricing is the cost of capital used to finance it.

Setup for October: breadth is at washout levels (T2108 19%, RUT 482 new 20day lows vs 96 highs, RUT RSI-15 30.59) while the cap-weighted indices are not. That asymmetry resolves one of two ways — a yield reversal that sparks a violent breadth thrust in beaten-down cyclicals and small caps, or a megacap catch-down that finally takes SPX through its 50MA. The single variable to watch is the 30-year: stabilisation below 5.6% favours the thrust; a clean break above it favours the catch-down.
