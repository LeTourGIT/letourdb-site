Session Summary

Q3 opened with a violent, single-theme unwind: the semiconductor complex — Q2's biggest winner — was liquidated hard. SOXX -6.41% and XSD -4.43% led, with Micron (MU -10.57%) giving back the least of a stock that more than tripled last quarter. Textbook calendar-turn profit-taking: books closed on the year's best trade, capital rotated out on day one. The losers list is near-pure silicon — MU, KLAC -11.77%, TER -11.68%, ALAB -10.80%, AMAT -9.97%, LRCX -9.71%, INTC -9.03%, MRVL -8.67% — plus AI-infrastructure adjacents CoreWeave -13.92% and Corning -13.62%. Michael Burry disclosed a short on the Dow's hottest AI name.

Crucially the money did not leave — it rotated. Software (IGV +3.02%), broker-dealers (IAI +3.35%; HOOD +8.35%, COIN +8.93%, IBKR +7.13%), healthcare providers (IHF +3.18%, ELV +7.62%) and internet (OGIG +3.39%) caught the outflow. META +8.81% was the mega-cap standout after confirming a cloud-business launch — monetising the $700B+ hyperscalers have poured into AI this year and easing the biggest overhang on the stock. RDDT +13.93% and APP +9.58% show the application layer, not the chip layer, is where the bid landed: the classic late-cycle handoff from those who BUILD AI infrastructure to those who USE it.

The macro backdrop turned hostile to high-multiple silicon. New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, at the ECB's Sintra forum, said flatly that "prices are too high" and framed inflation as his primary objective; Cleveland's Hammack warned AI could FUEL inflation and force hikes. The 10-year yield rose to 4.46% — mechanically compressing the long-duration cash flows that justify semiconductor multiples. ADP printed a light 98k, throwing Thursday's June payrolls into sharp relief.

Energy kept falling: WTI -2.42% to $68.12, Brent -2.80% — its steepest month since March 2020. The Hormuz tanker backlog eased to 385 ships from 485 and US-Iran talks continued in Doha (VP Vance: "going well"), draining the war premium. The tell of the day sits in Smart Money: not one semiconductor industry appears in the top 16 rotation ranks — the capital-flow model confirms the tape. Chips are being distributed into software, financials and healthcare. VIX at 16.59 (+0.85%) confirms reallocation, not fear.
