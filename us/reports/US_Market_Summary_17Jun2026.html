📝 Session Summary

Kevin Warsh's first meeting as Fed chair delivered the hawkish repricing the bond market had warned about for months. The Committee held at 3.50–3.75% unanimously — the easy part — but the Summary of Economic Projections did the work: nine of eighteen officials now project at least one 2026 hike, lifting the median year-end dot to 3.80% from 3.40%. Warsh abstained from a dot, stripped the easing bias from a deliberately curt statement, declined forward guidance, and hammered one phrase: price stability. FedWatch swung to 60.7% odds of an October hike.

The macro→sector→stock chain is the cleanest of the quarter: hawkish dots → higher real yields (2yr +16bp to 4.21%) + a ~1% stronger USD → long-duration cash flows discounted harder → high-multiple software and the precious-metals complex sold, while hard-asset, near-term-cash-flow plays (semis, defense, power infrastructure) were bid as the relative-value winners. When rates spike, capital does not flee equities wholesale — it migrates to where the cash flows are nearest. Today it migrated to silicon and steel.

The rotation was far more violent than the index losses imply. Software & online services were the epicentre — APP -6.93%, NOW -5.77%, ACN -5.75%, META -5.44%, RDDT -5.38%, ADBE -5.33% — alongside exchange operators NDAQ -6.72% and ICE -4.74% as the SpaceX-IPO fade pulled air out of Comm Services (XLC -2.78%, worst sector). On the other side: AVGO +4.30%, AMAT +4.35%, MRVL +3.90%, INTC +3.46%, ALAB +3.59%, WDC +4.56%, DELL +3.77% in silicon; GE Vernova +6.77% and Vertiv +6.00% in AI-power; RTX +3.11% and RKLB +3.20% in defense; HOOD +8.78% in fintech; MRNA +11.55% in biotech. The single defining pair: AVGO +4.30% vs NOW -5.77% — same sector header, opposite duration, a ~10pt spread.

The Smart Money board confirms the same pivot one layer up. The new leadership — Marine Port Services, Entertainment Production, Managed Healthcare, Personal Products, Household Products, Pension Funds, Life & Health Insurance, Restaurants — is a roll-call of low-beta, short-duration, income-bearing industries that surged 50–130 ranks over nine sessions. Semiconductor Equipment (#10, from #98 five days ago) is the lone growth survivor: the AI-capex layer is the one growth theme institutions refuse to abandon as they de-risk everything else.

The geopolitical overhang has faded to the rearview mirror. A US-Iran deal is reported reached and slated to be signed Friday, with Hormuz administration to be defined alongside Oman and Gulf states — though Israel's strike on Lebanon and Trump's warning keep tail risk live. Crucially oil held flat (WTI +0.32% to $75.04); the peace dividend was already priced over the prior week (XLE 5day -6.15%). With the energy shock fading, the inflation that remains is the sticky kind — May CPI 4.2%, hottest since April 2023 — which is exactly why Warsh can lean hawkish without an oil-spike alibi. Energy disinflation is spent, the easing bias is gone, and breadth is rolling over underneath cap-weighted indices.
