Session Summary

Monday opened the week with a chip-led decline that never became a risk-off event. The S&P slipped -0.28% and the Nasdaq 100 -0.97%, but the Dow closed green and the median S&P constituent advanced. That divergence is the whole story: capital did not leave equities, it rotated out of one crowded corner — semiconductors — and into everything else.

The reason is two sessions away. Nvidia reports after Wednesday's close, and the market is unwilling to hold max semiconductor beta into it. XSD -3.34% was the only ETF beyond ±3%; SOXX -2.67% confirmed the complex de-rated in unison. The single-stock damage reads like a pre-earnings inventory purge concentrated in the highest-torque NVDA derivatives: MU -5.83%, WDC -5.24%, SNDK -6.45%, SMCI -5.56%, COHR -4.85%, CIEN -6.02%, AAOI -13.77%. Positioning risk taken off, not a thesis abandoned.

What absorbed the flow shows a defensive reflex that is intact and selective. Consumer Staples (XLP +1.70%) and Financials (XLF +1.29%) led; payments were the standout — Visa +3.06% and Mastercard +3.31% both cleared +3% on heavy turnover. The consumer complex was broadly bid: WMT, TGT, COST, CMG, DIS, EXPE +5.44%, FIVE +4.99%. Textbook late-summer rotation — crowded growth funds a defensive-and-value barbell.

The one part of "risk" that did not sell was crypto, and that decoupling is the session's most important tell. IBIT +2.20% and ETHA +2.30% rose straight through the chip selloff; BitMine led all gainers +5.74%, MSTR +2.83%, and BTCT posted +52.63% inside the RS Top 20. Bitcoin is closing on $80,000, having beaten stocks and gold over six months. Smart Money confirms it — Blockchain & Crypto surged to rank 5 from rank 116 nine days ago.

The geopolitical overlay is Iran: a global sanctions push, Hormuz seizure warnings, and soaring shipping rates. Yet crude faded — WTI -0.82% to $84.93 — the classic "panic to pricing-in" move. The tradable signal isn't in oil, it's in shipping: Marine Port Services is rank 1 in Smart Money (ROC +123). Gold is the other beneficiary (GLD +0.79%), sitting rank 7 on the Treasury-intervention theme.
