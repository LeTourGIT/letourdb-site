04

Session summary

Two inflation relief valves opened on the same morning. September payrolls printed +29K against a consensus near 85-90K, unemployment ticked up to 4.2% and average hourly earnings rose just 0.1% on the month — and in a tightening cycle, bad news is good news: the odds of an October Fed hike collapsed to 16% from 64% a week ago. Hours later the G7 agreed to release 100M barrels of crude and diesel over four months, under US pressure and the threat of a US diesel export ban, knocking WTI futures 1.48% lower to $91.26 while Brent held $102.70. Equities took both as permission to re-rate duration: NDX +1.00% to a new all-time closing high of 30,807.93, SPX +0.73% to 7,722.72.

The bond market did not co-sign. The 10-year yield dropped to 5.18% on the print, then reversed to finish near 5.28% — higher on the day and within 6bp of this week's 24-year high of 5.34%. 'No hike in October' is not 'easing': Fed officials still call inflation too high, September CPI and PPI land before the late-October meeting, and a third US carrier group is heading to the Gulf in month eight of the Iran war. That is why the rate-sensitive groups barely moved — XLF +0.06%, XLRE +0.32%, XLU +0.38%, mortgage REITs -1.33% — while long-duration growth ran (IWF +1.07% vs RPV +0.46%). Equities traded the front end; the long end is still pricing war inflation and term premium.

Semiconductors were the engine, and the composition matters. XSD (equal-weight) +3.59% beat SOXX (cap-weight) +2.18% by 141bp, with NVDA only +1.34% despite an intraday record at $237.87. The leaders were test, analog and power — TER +8.00%, ON +6.01%, MPWR +5.79%, TXN +4.44%, SMTC +4.44% — the cyclical end of the chip complex rather than the AI-accelerator end. When analog leads on a soft-jobs day, the market is pricing a rate-pause industrial restock on top of AI capex. Smart Money agrees: Semiconductor Equipment & Testing holds rank 2 and Semiconductors rank 4.

The session's shock was storage. Nikkei reported Toshiba will invest about ¥60B to double hard-drive capacity by fiscal 2027, targeting a 30% share from just over 10%. WDC fell 10.22% on $10.29B turnover and STX 10.21%, with sympathy into NAND and memory: SNDK -3.79%, MU -2.05% two days after a record quarter. The entire AI-storage trade rests on supply discipline in a three-player oligopoly, and the smallest player just broke ranks. Citi argues the market is overstating it — Toshiba depends on outside suppliers for heads and media — but after gains of roughly 240% (STX) and 170% (WDC) this year, positioning set the size of the move. The trade inside the trade: storage buyers rallied — HPE +7.36%, NTAP +5.22%, P +4.48%, DELL +3.84%. Cheaper media is a margin transfer from component makers to system OEMs.

Beneath the index, a stealth correction is still running. SPX sits 0.98% below its record, yet only 24.75% of members are above their 50MA and T2108 is 22.95% — readings that normally accompany a 10%+ index drawdown. XLK is the only one of 11 sectors with a positive 20day return (+7.44%); the other ten are down between 2.64% and 8.66%. RSP has lost 4.37 points to the cap-weight index in 20 sessions (-4.69% vs -0.32%), and RUT and DJI remain in Bearish regimes below their 50MAs. Friday's constructive element is the washout signature: SPX new 20day lows collapsed 193 → 70, RUT 748 → 242, and T2108 has lifted off its 19.0% low of 30/09/2026 on a second straight positive Adv/Dec day (290/208).

Stock-specific catalysts filled in the rest. TSLA +4.65% on Q3 deliveries of 486,532 vs 463,000 expected made XLY (+1.13%) the top sector; SPCX +7.35% on $19.05B turnover after three launches in 13 hours added real weight to the NDX record. On the other side, NKE -3.64% (revenue miss, FY27 guided down) and ACN -6.31% (handing back part of Thursday's +15.78%) show earnings relief rallies are being sold. On the week only the NDX finished green: NDX +0.65%, RUT -0.16%, SPX -0.27%, DJI -1.26%.
