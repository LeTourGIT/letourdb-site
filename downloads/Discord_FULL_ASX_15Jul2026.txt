📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Wednesday's +0.37% grind to 8,841.1 was a large-cap resources-and-financials session dressed up by a
risk-on Wall Street lead. Cool US CPI (3.5%) and a rebound in chips set the tone; locally, the engine was
Materials (+1.70%), the only clearly-green sector, powered by an iron ore/copper bid, RIO's strongest H1
Pilbara production since 2018, and Kingsgate (KCN +15.89%) re-rating on guidance.

The most instructive move was in gold. RRL -5.16%, EVN -3.74% (soft Q4 update, classic sell-the-news),
MEK -9.09% and MLX -5.41% — gold miners were dumped almost as a cohort even as bullion rose ~2% overnight.
That is rotation OUT of a crowded trade, not a commodity-price story, and it freed capital that flowed into
energy and base metals. Under the hood, Smart Money confirms it: Oil & Gas Refining rocketed to #1 (from #28
nine sessions ago), Integrated Oil & Gas to #4, Coal to #6 — a textbook Hormuz-shock rotation.

Tech split down the middle. Data-centre and infrastructure names ran — NXT +5.66%, BVS +6.25% [H], CAT
+5.67% [H] — while software was sold (XRO -3.57%, PXA -3.18%). The AI-infrastructure thesis is intact; the
high-multiple software cohort is where the de-rating sits. Financials (+0.15%) got a late-session lift and
are quietly building as a Smart Money theme (Banks from #60 to #10), echoing the strong US bank-earnings open.

The catch is breadth. XJO advancers barely beat decliners (97/90, 1.08:1), only ~40% of XJO names sit above
their 200MA, and the Small Ords remains Bearish. This is not a broad-based advance — it is a narrow, top-heavy
push led by a handful of large-cap resource and financial names. Fresh 20day highs in CBA, MQG, QBE, ASX and
SEK show where the institutional money is; the absence of small-cap participation shows where it isn't.
