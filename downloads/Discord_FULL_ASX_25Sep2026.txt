📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
The ASX 200 slipped 0.43% to 8,665.0 on Friday — a second straight loss, a 0.76% weekly decline and the lowest close since 12 June. On the surface a quiet, thin session (Melbourne public holiday, Grand Final eve), but the internals were poor and near the month's lows: XJO breadth 43/149 (0.29:1) and only 5 new 20day highs against 50 new lows. The driver was global rates — the US 10-year through 5.1% overnight and the Australian 10-year at 5.39% — pressing directly on an RBA that is ~92% priced to hike on Tuesday.

Sector ordering was a textbook duration trade. Consumer Staples (XSJ +0.73%) and Financials (XFJ +0.27%) were the only green sectors; Info Tech (XIJ -1.66%), Consumer Discretionary (XDJ -1.36%) and Utilities (XUJ -1.25%) were the worst. Utilities are now -5.23% on the week and Tech -14.91% over 20 days. Banks catching a bid into a hike is the market telling you it expects the move — NIM expansion is being priced ahead of credit stress.

Materials (XMJ -0.84%) extended a 20day slide to -9.42%. Two engines are sputtering at once: gold is -7% over 20 days as real yields rise (the opportunity cost of holding bullion), and lithium carbonate has fallen roughly 17% since 1 September on supply restarts — GLN -17.11%, VUL -5.56%, LIN -5.56% and WC8 -4.92% sat in the losers list. Iron ore at ~US$97 is flat and not the problem; the damage is in the high-beta commodity tail.

Stock-specific news did the rest. NWL -8.15% on a class action it will defend (08:18, price sensitive); SGR -10.34% after an intraday Star Sydney regulatory update (13:51); MEK -25.93% on an institutional placement (09:26). On the positive side HLS +5.56% on the Agilex Biolabs sale to Novotech (08:49) and EOS +5.99% to a new 20day high with no announcement — pure defence rotation, Aerospace & Defense is now Smart Money rank 2. Two trading halts (PV1, GBR) are live catalysts for next week.

The setup into next week is binary around Tuesday 2:30pm. XJO RSI-15 at 31.5 and T2108 at 27% say the market is stretched to the downside, so a 'last hike' tone could spark a squeeze in the most-sold duration sectors. A hawkish hold-the-door-open statement, however, with a Bearish regime across all four indices, points to a test of the 8,600 area. Respect the regime until the 10MA reclaims the 20MA.
