📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Thursday 9 July delivered a deceptive session: the XJO fell just -0.26% to
8,762.5, yet EIGHT of eleven sectors closed green. The index was dragged
lower by the only two sectors that matter for ASX weight — Materials
(XMJ -1.48%) and Financials (XFJ -0.15%), which together are ~40% of the
benchmark. This is a cap-weight illusion, not a broad sell-off. Breadth
confirms the ambiguity: 90 advancers vs 103 decliners (0.87:1), 2 stocks
+4% against 3 stocks -4%. Nobody was in a hurry to sell; nobody bought.

The Materials story is the whole story. Iron ore is pinned near one-year
lows after Chinese port inventories hit a record ~160Mt, and the China
Mineral Resources Group / Fortescue standoff over Super Special Fines is
now an open pricing war rather than a negotiation. S32 -3.54% and the
gold complex (PNR -10.18%, CYL -4.97%, BNZ -3.56%) sold off despite spot
gold +1.10% to $4,116.80 — a classic producer-vs-metal divergence that
says equity holders are de-risking the miners, not the metal. Add China's
June CPI weakening while PPI hit a near-4-year high, and you have the worst
configuration for Australian resource exporters: Chinese producers can
raise prices at home while consumer demand stalls. XMJ -4.21% over 5day.

Energy (XEJ +1.67%) was the session's leader for the plainest reason: the
Iran ceasefire collapsed. US strikes resumed, Iran struck US bases, and
crude spiked overnight before easing back today (WTI $73.25 -1.39%,
Brent $77.71 -1.47%). NHC +5.45% and TBN +6.82% captured the war premium.
Critically, the Smart Money table shows this is not a one-day trade:
Oil & Gas E&P has surged from rank #71 to #3 in nine sessions, Oil & Gas
Refining #67→#5, Integrated Oil & Gas #69→#6. Three energy sub-industries
in the top six. Institutional capital rotated into energy BEFORE the
ceasefire broke — the rotation is nine sessions old, the headline is one day.

Real Estate (XRE -1.11%) was the second-worst sector and the tell on rates.
SGP -3.67% and CHC -3.28% fell because FOMC minutes revealed members arguing
for further hikes on energy-driven inflation. With the RBA already at 4.35%
after three consecutive 2026 hikes (Feb, Mar, May) and holding on 30 June,
a renewed oil shock is the one variable that reopens the tightening door.
The next RBA meeting is 10-11 August — a month away, so no immediate event
risk, but every REIT and consumer name is now trading the oil price by proxy.

Beneath the surface the domestic cyclical trade is quietly the strongest
thing on the board. Homebuilding is Smart Money #1 with a 14.20% 10day ROC,
and it has held the top rank for four straight sessions — this is not a
spike, it is entrenchment. JIN +10.46%, FBU +7.55% (Construction Supplies),
TPW +5.19% and ADH +4.42% (Home Furnishings), TEA +4.59% and MGH +4.55%
(Construction & Engineering). Record-high Australian rents and a housing
supply crunch are being expressed through builders and home retailers,
not through the banks. That is a durable, ASX-specific theme with no US
equivalent — and it is happening while the index goes nowhere.
