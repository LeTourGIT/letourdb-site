📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Friday was a dovish-repricing day. The July employment report unexpectedly
showed payrolls FELL 23,000 against consensus for an ~83,000 gain, with the
prior two months revised down a combined 103,000. Unemployment slipped to 4.1%,
but the internals were soft: government -53k, private +30k. The market read it
cleanly — the labor market is cracking, the Fed's hand is forced toward cuts.
Treasury yields plunged (10yr ~4.64%, 2yr 4.19% — lowest since mid-July) and
every rate-sensitive corner of the tape caught a bid.

That single macro print explains the day's three biggest structural moves.
First, GOLD AND HARD ASSETS went vertical: GDX +7.11%, SIL +6.29%, XME +4.88%,
URA +3.96%. Lower real yields + a wounded dollar + Fed-cut odds = the textbook
bullion regime, and Smart Money confirms it — Gold, Precious Metals, Integrated
Mining and Specialty Mining all SURGED up the rotation table. This is not a
one-day spike: the 5day returns (GDX +21%, SIL +20%, REMX +17%) mark genuine
institutional accumulation into the metals complex.

Second, SOFTWARE BLEW OUT on earnings — but selectively. Atlassian +35%, Twilio
+25%, Doximity +33%, Cloudflare +5.6% and Datadog +2% powered IGV +3.29% and
directly answered the 'SaaSpocalypse' bear case that AI is gutting seat-based
software. Yet the same session saw Trade Desk -22% and Figma -2.8% — proof the
re-rating is dispersion, not a blanket bid. The winners are the AI-additive
platforms; the losers are where AI (or ad-budget softness) threatens the model.

Third, SEMICONDUCTORS staged the rebound CNBC flagged: XSD +4.66%, SOXX +2.02%,
MCHP +14%, COHR +13%, GFS +7%, ONTO +15%. This is the mechanism behind NDX's
+1.19% relief bounce — but note the regime still reads Bearish. The chip complex
is repairing off oversold, not re-establishing trend leadership. Memory remains
the soft spot (WDC -3.8%, 5day -20%; SNDK -3.7%), so the rebound is fabless/
analog/optical-led, not broad-based silicon.

Energy was the mirror image: XLE -1.13%, the worst sector, capping a -7.4% week
for WTI as Iran halted strikes on Israel and Oman/Iran opened Hormuz talks. The
market has moved from 'peak fear' to 'pricing in' — the geopolitical premium is
bleeding out of crude even as headlines stay noisy ('theater diplomacy'). The
energy-vs-metals split is the cleanest expression of the day: capital is leaving
the fading war trade and crowding into the monetary-debasement / rate-cut trade.

Breadth backs the rally. SPX advancers led decliners 320/178 with T2108 at 64.4%
— a broad ATH, not a narrow cap-weighted one. RUT +1.10% (1201/723 adv/dec, 224
stocks +4%) shows small-caps, the most rate-sensitive cohort, leading — exactly
what a dovish jobs print should produce.
