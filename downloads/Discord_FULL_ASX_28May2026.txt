📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Thursday 28 May gave back Wednesday's short-covering bounce with
compound interest. XJO fell 124.8 points (-1.43%) to close at
8,592.9, with every part of the sell-off structurally meaningful:
the index broke back below 8,600, confirmed the Bearish regime
across all four indices, and produced an ADV/DEC ratio of 38:155
— the most lopsided distribution since the May-20 capitulation
session. This was not profit-taking. This was re-distribution.

The immediate catalyst was the Iran deal whiplash: Iranian state
TV's publication of a purported draft peace agreement sent oil
down 5%+ intraday, briefly providing some relief to inflation
expectations. The White House's rapid denial reversed the move,
with WTI bouncing back to settle -2.7%. For the ASX, this matters
on multiple levels simultaneously: lower oil is disinflationary
(positive for rate expectations), but "fake deal then denial" is
exactly the kind of geopolitical volatility that forces risk
managers to reduce gross exposure. Materials (XMJ -2.43%) led
all sectors lower — the biggest single-day Materials decline in
four weeks. BHP, RIO, and the major miners sold off on a
combination of AUD weakness (AUD/USD 0.7137 putting further
compression on USD revenues) and iron ore hovering at $109.

Financials (XFJ -1.64%) were the second-worst sector — the Big 4
remain structurally caught between RBA rate optionality (next
decision approaches) and deteriorating credit sentiment as
mortgage stress indicators creep higher under sustained elevated
rates. Healthcare (XHJ -1.18%) sold off sharply on what appears
to be domestic multiple compression rather than a specific
catalyst — CSL and similar names simply gave back their safe-haven
premium from the prior session's tentative recovery.

The only green sectors were Consumer Staples (+0.25%) and Consumer
Discretionary (+0.15%) — both effectively flat, representing
rotation into perceived defensive names rather than genuine buying
conviction. Energy (XEJ -0.29%) outperformed the damage given oil's
turbulence, which itself tells you something: the energy sector has
already priced considerable geopolitical risk premium; the -0.29%
is a function of WTI's volatility partially cancelling out.

Breadth statistics confirm systemic selling: XJO 200MA participation
fell to 35% (70/200 stocks), T2108 at 43%, and 20day Lows (27)
outpacing 20day Highs (14) by 2:1. The speculative tier (Small Ords
QTR-25% stocks at 45 vs QTR+25% at 33) is deteriorating faster than
the blue-chip tier — classic late-cycle distribution behaviour.
