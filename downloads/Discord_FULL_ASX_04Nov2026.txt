📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
The ASX 200 fell 0.59% to 8,834.5 (-52.3 points), handing back all of Tuesday's post-RBA relief as US midterm results landed mid-session. Democrats were projected to win the House and flip the Senate, and the blue-wave trade hit Asia hard and Asian chip stocks tumbled (CNBC). The headline loss understates the damage. XJO breadth was 56 advancers to 132 decliners (0.42:1), with 9 stocks down 4% or more against 1 up 4% or more.

Losses tracked the bottom of the Smart Money table almost exactly. Aerospace & Defense (rank 83 of 83), Semiconductors (82), Gold (81) and Steel (80) are the four weakest industries, and they supplied the day's ASX200 losers: EOS -7.77%, DRO -7.63%, ASB -4.54%, SLX -9.58% and CIA -4.85%. None of these lodged an announcement, so this is sector repricing, not stock news. Defence is being re-rated on US policy risk under divided government (on the same news cycle, Trump announced a halt to $5.7b in Israel aid), and Silex moved with the Asian semiconductor rout.

Materials (XMJ -1.46%) was the weakest sector for a second straight week: 5day -4.41%, 20day -10.8%. Gold is the engine of the unwind (industry ROC -10.8%; spot gold ~5.8% off over 20 days on the delayed 7d feed), and LME copper (-1.87%) and tin (-3.26%) also fell on 03/11. Iron ore at ~US$91/t gives BHP, RIO and FMG no support, and CIA (-4.85%) is the pure-play casualty.

The bid was defensive and USD-earning, which is the textbook post-hike, risk-off rotation. Utilities (XUJ +1.17%) topped the heatmap as Electric Utilities SURGED to rank 2 (from 25 yesterday, 73 nine days ago). ORG rose 1.89%, and Janus Electric (JNS +16.36%) rallied on its Horizon Two strategy presentation (08:57, price sensitive). Energy (XEJ +0.86%) tracked WTI (+0.88%), with refiner Viva Energy (VEA +4.07%) leading. Department stores were the contrarian winner: ABC reports David Jones is on the brink of collapse and behind on rent to Westfield, and the market is pricing share gains for the survivors (MYR +6.98%, KGN +4.75%; Department Stores rank 5).

Looking ahead: NAB reports FY results tomorrow (05/11), Macquarie on 06/11, and ANZ and Dyno Nobel on 09/11. Bank earnings week will test whether Financials (XFJ 20day +7.48%) can hold up the index while resources bleed. Tonight's US ISM Services and ADP data, then Friday's payrolls, will show whether the blue-wave selloff is a one-day shock. With XJO sitting on its 200MA, 8,800 is the line in the sand.
