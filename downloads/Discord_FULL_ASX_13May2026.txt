📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Wednesday 13 May was a tale of two markets inside the one index.
The XJO shed -0.47% to 8,630.4 — a headline concealing the most
dramatic intra-index divergence in months: Financials (XFJ -4.01%)
versus Materials (XMJ +1.97%) and Consumer Discretionary (XDJ +2.94%).

The trigger was the Federal Budget's Tuesday night announcement:
negative gearing scrapped for established housing; CGT discount
replaced with inflation indexation. Combined with CBA's Q3 quarterly
miss (profit ~$2.7B, -2% vs consensus) and a $316M impairment charge
($200M a "Middle East risk provision"), the selling was surgical. CBA
fell as much as 10% intraday — erasing ~$10B in market cap. NAB,
ANZ, WBC followed down 1.4-2.4%. This is not one-session noise: the
budget's structural changes reduce mortgage lending growth, the
investor property pool, and future loan book expansion simultaneously.

Against that, BHP closed at a new all-time high of $61.52 as COMEX
copper futures hit a record above US$6.45/lb — powered by the data
centre electrification demand thesis that now treats copper as an AI
infrastructure input. BHP is now within $2.6B of CBA as Australia's
largest listed company by market cap. If a mining company displaces
a bank at the top of the ASX, that is not a trade — that is a regime
shift in Australian equity market leadership.

Aristocrat Leisure (ALL +12%) dominated Consumer Discretionary with
H1 FY26 normalised EBITA of $1.12B (+6.2%), a $1B buyback expansion
to $2.5B total, and evidence of gaming market share gains across
land-based, online and interactive segments. In an environment where
most companies are quietly pulling guidance, that buyback commitment
is a three-line statement of management confidence.

The AUD at 0.7239 (-0.11% on the day but +0.79% in 5 days) compresses
BHP/RIO/FMG USD revenues by ~0.7% per 1 cent — at 0.72, the drag
is ~2.8%, but commodity price strength is overwhelmingly offsetting.
