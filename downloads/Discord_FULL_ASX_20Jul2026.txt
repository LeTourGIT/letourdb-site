📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Monday 20 July was a headline-flat session (XJO -0.06% to 8,791.3) that
masked a violent rotation underneath. A single macro force set the tape:
overnight US strikes near the Strait of Hormuz pushed oil and bond yields
higher and drained risk appetite from the market's most globally-exposed
sectors. Energy (XEJ +1.80%) was the sole strong performer; everything
with a US-tech or precious-metals beta was sold.

Information Technology (XIJ -1.55%) was the worst sector, an offshore
import: the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index has fallen ~20% from its June
record into a bear market, triggered by China's Moonshot releasing its
Kimi K3 model and TSMC flagging capex-cost inflation. Weebit Nano (WBT
[ASX300]) -7.36% and BrainChip (BRN) -7.14% were the local casualties —
the ASX's semiconductor-adjacent names have no shelter from a global chip
de-rating.

Materials (XMJ -0.34%) saw a base- and precious-metals wipeout: gold
-0.45%, silver -0.97%, copper -0.21% overnight, with local gold miners
off ~1.9% and Liontown (LTR [ASX100]) -4.14% and Mineral Resources (MIN
[ASX100]) -3.86% leading lithium/diversified miners lower. The offset was
energy: Yancoal (YAL [ASX200]) +5.97%, Deep Yellow (DYL [ASX200], uranium)
+6.10% and South32 (S32 [ASX50]) +4.62% rode the commodity-premium bid.
Star Entertainment (SGR) +9.09% was a stock-specific standout on casino
news, lifting consumer discretionary.

The defensive complexion was textbook risk-off: capital that left tech and
gold parked in banks (XFJ -0.08%, near flat) and consumer staples (XSJ
+0.43%). CBA's commodities desk read the oil move as the market pricing
Hormuz staying OPEN — a premium, not a closure — which is why the ASX
didn't panic. But the large-cap/small-cap divergence is the warning: the
XJO's Bullish MA stack is doing heavy lifting while XSO breaks down and
only 41% of XJO stocks hold their 200MA.
