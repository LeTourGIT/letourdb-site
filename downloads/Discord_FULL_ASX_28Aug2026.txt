📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Friday 28 August delivered a classic reporting-season split tape: the large-cap
indices finished green (XJO +0.60% to 9,092.3, XAO +0.55%, XTO +0.68%) while the Small
Ords fell -0.37%. Underneath the headline gain the market was not broad — XAO decliners
(224) edged advancers (220), XSO ran 53 up vs 90 down, and new 20day lows outnumbered
new highs on all four indices. This is index strength carried by a handful of large-cap
winners, not a genuine risk-on advance.

FY26 full-year results are the whole story. Single-name dispersion was extreme: Dicker
Data (DDR +20.66%) surged on its H1 result and FY26 guidance update, and St Barbara
(SBM +20.30%) leapt on FY26 numbers — while at the other end PEXA (PXA -17.43%), Saluda
Medical (SLD -24.41%), Cobram Estate (CBO -14.85%) and WAM Capital (WAM -18.54%) were
punished on their releases. Broken Hill Mines (BHM -10.06%) fell on a A$90m underwritten
capital raising — dilution, not operations. Every one of these moves ties to a same-day
lodgement; this is a market repricing fundamentals stock-by-stock, not trading macro.

The relative-strength board tells the cleaner structural story: it is wall-to-wall gold.
The ASX200/300 RS leaders are almost entirely gold miners — SBM, VAU, GMD, RRL, RSG, PRU,
WGX — with gold near US$4,654/oz. This is a broad institutional re-rating of Australian
gold producers, not a single-stock spike, and it is the most durable theme on the tape.
Information Technology (XIJ +2.31%) led sectors on the overnight AI read-through (Nvidia,
Salesforce), with WTC and NXT the ASX vehicles.

Real Estate (XRE -0.86%) was the weakest sector — the clean expression of rate-hike
anxiety, with the major banks now openly tipping an RBA hike (next meeting 28-29 Sept).
ASIC's warning of the 'first significant cracks' in Australian private credit, and a major
private-credit fund suspending redemptions, add a specific financial-plumbing risk to watch.
