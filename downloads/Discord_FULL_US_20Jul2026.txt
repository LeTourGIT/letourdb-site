📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Monday extended the great AI rotation — but with a twist that rewired sector leadership.
The headline story was not a chip name at all: Bitcoin miners re-rated violently as
AI-infrastructure landlords. IREN +19.57% announced a $2.8B AI-cloud contract and lifted
its AI revenue target above $4B (≈85% already contracted with Microsoft, Nvidia, Perplexity
and Figure AI); Hut 8 signed a $9.8B, 15-year data-center lease that pushed its Beacon Point
campus to a fully-contracted 1GW. APLD +7.99%, WULF +3.85% and BMNR +5.99% surged in sympathy.
The signal: the market is structurally decoupling miner equities from Bitcoin spot and
valuing them as recurring-revenue AI-capex beneficiaries — the one corner of tech still bid.

Everywhere else the AI trade cracked. The semiconductor rebound failed intraday: MRVL +3.32%,
CRDO +4.63% and TER +3.54% bounced but each still carries a 5day loss of -2% to -10%, marking
these as dead-cat repairs inside a downtrend, not fresh accumulation. Use the 5day column to
separate genuine trend from short-cover: none of the chip 'gainers' has a positive 5day return.
The pressure sources are structural — TSMC's raised capex guide, Chinese open-source models
(Moonshot/Kimi) implying less compute per unit of capability, and reports of Meta renting out
its own AI capacity (oversupply fear). ORCL -3.98%, DELL -3.65%, PANW -2.79% carried the
software/hardware de-rate; BE -8.33% (fuel-cell AI-power trade) was the single worst mover.

Capital that left tech did not leave the market — it rotated. Energy was the second-best SPDR
(XLE +0.45%) with crude at a one-month high despite an Iran ceasefire proposal: the 9th
consecutive night of US strikes plus a Houthi blockade on Saudi shipping keeps a war premium
bid even as WTI's headline print fell -1.64% on the day. Smart Money confirms it — four of the
top seven industries are energy (Refining #2, E&P #3, Integrated Oil #4, Drilling #7), and the
10day hot sector is Integrated Oil. The rest of the bid went defensive: Consumer Staples and
Managed Healthcare are the dominant 60day structural leaders, the textbook late-cycle hide.

Breadth made the risk explicit. SPX advanced just 163 vs 336 decliners — a 2:1 negative tape
on a nearly-flat index — and RUT was brutal at 593/1364 with 88 stocks down >4%. This is a
distribution session masked by cap-weighting: the few AI-infra winners and a green XLC/XLE
held the headline indices near unchanged while the median stock fell. Layer on Jamie Dimon
warning he 'wouldn't buy stocks or Treasurys' here, CIBC's Harvey pinning the fatigue on rates,
Trump's threatened 50% Canada tariffs, and rising 10yr yields, and the risk-reward has shifted
defensive. NDX's break below its 50MA into a Bearish regime is the level that matters into Tuesday.
