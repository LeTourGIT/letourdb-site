📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
The ASX 200 fell -0.43% to 8,793.2 (-38.1 pts) and slipped back below its 200-day moving average, despite a firm Wall Street lead (S&P 500 +0.58%, Nasdaq +1.05%). The selling was narrow and cap-weighted. Only three sectors closed red -- Energy (XEJ -2.42%), Materials (XMJ -1.96%) and Consumer Discretionary (XDJ -1.72%) -- and the other eight finished green. XJO breadth was flat at 96 advancers to 94 decliners, so the headline loss overstates the internal damage in the top 200.

Resources did most of the index damage. FMG -4.52% after its September-quarter report (08:56, price sensitive) warned it may mine less iron ore if talks with China's Mineral Resources Group are not finished by year-end. About 90% of FMG's ore goes to China, whose CSI 300 is now in a bear market. BHP fell -2.41% on AGM day and RIO -1.47%, with iron ore stuck near US$90.70/t (20day -6.57%). Gold producers were sold through the quarterly window: VAU -3.63%, GMD -3.19%, RRL -4.35%, EVN -2.39%, with spot gold -1.3%. Energy ignored a +2% Brent move. WDS -3.50%, VEA -4.93% on its 3Q update and STO -1.29% on its Q3 report: after a +34.95% year the sector is booking profits on quarterly prints.

The domestic story was retail. NCK -15.34% after guiding 1H profit to $28-32M (pcp $41M), about 20% below consensus. TPW (-8.95%) and WES (-3.11%) were sold on read-across, on the same day Domain reported the steepest quarterly house-price fall on record (-4.7%, Perth -7.4%). Furniture and housing-linked discretionary is now the pressure point, and RBA hike risk compounds it. BAP +13.55% on its AGM trading update (09:02, price sensitive) was the contrarian print. Auto Parts jumped from rank 28 to 11 in a single session.

Capital is hiding in pricing-power defensives. Insurers (SUN +2.46%, MPL +2.07%, IAG +1.95%), packaging (BXB +3.16%), industrials (XNJ +0.90%) and REITs (XRE +0.76%) led. Smart Money confirms it: Insurance Funds has held rank 1 for six sessions, while Life & Health Insurance (rank 39 to 4) and Professional Information Services (rank 48 to 7) are institutional SURGEs. Under the surface the broad tape is weaker. XAO decliners beat advancers 241 to 205, and XJO new 20day lows jumped from 19 to 35 in one session.

Looking ahead, the macro driver is the RBA. Deputy Governor Hauser said the inflation outlook is "materially worse" and becoming entrenched, which keeps an early-November hike a very live possibility (the decision is 12 days out, outside the 7-day RBA Watch window). Post-close catalysts are ERA's quarterly, AN1's 4C (both price sensitive) and EUR's scheme meeting results. Alkane (ALK) reports formal results tomorrow (23/10), and three trading halts are outstanding (IXC, AX8, PIM). With US futures soft, expect a cautious open.
