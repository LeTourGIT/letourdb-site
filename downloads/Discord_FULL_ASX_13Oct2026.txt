📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
The XJO closed at 8,783.9 (+0.7 points, +0.01%), a dead-flat print
that hides one of the sharpest internal rotations this quarter.
Materials (XMJ -2.03%) fell to its weakest level since 5 August
(Market Index). The lithium complex was gutted: PLS -8.61%, MIN
-6.66%, IGO -6.45%, LTR -4.67%, VUL -12.41%. Per Market Index, the
EU's move to cap Chinese hybrid imports knocked spodumene 12.4%
lower to US$1,485/t and sent Chinese carbonate futures down 8.4%.
The majors joined in: BHP -1.84%, RIO -2.40%, FMG -2.92%, with no
iron ore trigger (62% Fe US$90.83, +0.17%). That makes it de-
risking flow, not a commodity call. Rare earths (LYC -2.91%, BRE
-11.64%, IXR -18.18%) and uranium (PDN -4.86%, BOE -9.09%, LOT
-17.95%) went with it, and the whole 'critical minerals beta'
basket was liquidated together.

Information Technology (XIJ -4.37%) was the worst sector by a
distance, and its cause was imported. Samsung and SK Hynix fell
more than 8% and the Kospi was down as much as 7% intraday.
Broadcom's Hock Tan questioning AI financing added a second layer.
That landed directly on the ASX's AI-infrastructure cohort: NXT
-7.59%, MP1 -9.91%, SKS -20.53%, MAQ -7.67%, SLX -8.42%, ELS
-11.81%. Tech gave back its ~3% two-day bounce in one session. →
The ASX has no memory makers, so this was a sentiment transmission
through data-centre and AI-capex proxies. Those are the names to
watch for capitulation, not the miners.

The index held flat because capital rotated rather than left.
Financials (XFJ +1.39%) carried the tape: CBA +2.32%, NAB +1.87%,
WBC +1.53%, ANZ +1.27%. BOQ rose +2.92% into Thursday's FY result,
and QBE/IAG pushed to 20day highs. Utilities (XUJ +1.80%; AGL
+4.09%, ORG +2.76%), Staples (XSJ +1.31%) and Energy (XEJ +1.22%;
WDS +1.86%, STO +1.60%) completed a textbook defensive-plus-oil
rotation. The RBA minutes were hawkish: the September 25bp hike to
4.60% was unanimous and the board stands ready to move again. Even
so, the local 10-year slipped 4bp to 5.30%, and lower yields plus
higher rates for longer is the best mix for bank NIM sentiment.

Breadth is the warning. XJO advancers/decliners were 71/117
(0.61:1). Sixteen ASX 200 names fell more than 4% against one that
rose more than 4%, and 20day lows jumped to 43 from 25 while 20day
highs held at 23. Only 30.5% of the ASX 200 sits above its 50MA.
The Small Ords (XSO -1.87%) posted a third straight loss to
3,217.7. Its RSI-15 is 31.2, one tick from oversold, and it sits
9.17% below its 200MA. The All Ords (-0.17%) had 124 advancers
against 313 decliners. → A flat XJO on 0.6:1 breadth is a top-
heavy market: a handful of mega-cap banks are masking broad
liquidation underneath.

Regime: XJO upgraded from Bearish to Neutral Bear as the 10MA
crossed back above the 20MA, its third straight up close. XAO and
XSO stay Bearish. That XJO/XSO split is the leading signal. Either
small caps stabilise near RSI-30 and the repair broadens, or the
weakness bleeds up into the XJO at the 50MA (-1.54% away). Forward
catalysts: US bank earnings tonight, US CPI Wednesday, BOQ FY
result Thursday 15/10, and the RBA on 03/11 (~28% hike priced).
After the close, Sequoia (SEQ) resolved its morning halt with the
sale of InterPrac Financial Planning, and Forrestania (FRS)
extended its Zenith Minerals offer period for a tenth time.
