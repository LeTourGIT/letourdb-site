📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Two gravitational forces defined the session, and neither showed up in the
index close. Above the tape: Nvidia's earnings, the single most anticipated
print of the season. Below it: a macro regime hardening around sticky
inflation, a hawkish Fed, and the highest long-end Treasury yields since 2007
— the backdrop to Friday's Jackson Hole keynote from Fed Chair Kevin Warsh.

Nvidia delivered. Fiscal-Q2 revenue landed at $96.2B, up 106% year over year,
with EPS of $2.22 — more than double a year ago, and ahead of the roughly
$92B Street consensus. It was, objectively, an extraordinary quarter. And the
stock fell -1.59% into the close and another -1.07% after hours. This is the
defining paradox of late-cycle AI: when you ARE the trade, execution stops
being a catalyst and becomes a prerequisite. NVDA has now declined after the
majority of its last several reports despite serial beats — the market has
priced perfection and moved on to the next question. That "next question" got
answered emphatically after the bell, and it was not about chips.

The after-hours session was a software and cybersecurity blowout. Okta +19.1%,
Salesforce +13.5% (AI growth plus a mark-up on its Anthropic investment),
CrowdStrike +10.9%, Nutanix +9.2%, Veeva +8.0%, SailPoint +6.6%, SentinelOne
+5.2%, Tenable +5.1%, Palo Alto +5.0%, ServiceNow +4.0%. The thesis rotation
is precise: capital is migrating from the AI-INFRASTRUCTURE layer (NVDA, the
picks-and-shovels that are now a "prerequisite") to the AI-APPLICATION and
AI-SECURITY layer, where monetization is showing up in actual enterprise
software P&Ls. Identity, security, and agentic workflow platforms are printing
the beats now — AI as a revenue line, not a capex promise. The one AH crack:
HPQ -8.9% on soft guidance, a reminder that legacy hardware is not part of
this re-rating.

The intraday tape confirmed the same message from a different angle. "Chip
stock rebound fails" was the session headline — the speculative AI-power and
quantum complex was dumped: IREN -6.2%, OKLO -6.0%, IONQ -4.8%, RDDT -4.4%.
Meanwhile the market bid cyclicals and value. Industrials (XLK's rival for
leadership today) led all sectors at +1.09%, energy +0.60%, utilities +0.46%,
materials +0.17% — while long-duration defensives and growth-adjacent names
sagged: Healthcare -1.00% (worst), Consumer Discretionary -0.67%, Real Estate
-0.60%, Communication Services -0.50%. This is the textbook signature of a
rates-up, inflation-sticky regime: the market pays for near-term cash flows
and cyclical leverage, and de-rates long-duration multiples.

The macro is the engine. July PCE printed +0.2% month over month, leaving the
headline at 3.7% (hotter than the 3.6% expected); core held at 3.3%, above the
2% target for a 65th consecutive month. The July FOMC held at 3.50-3.75% but
with three regional presidents dissenting FOR a hike — the most hawkish-side
dissents since 2016. The 30year yield sits near 5.17%, a level unseen since
2007, and the 10year near 4.64%. Layered on top is a genuine Fed-independence
drama: Treasury Secretary Bessent has doubled long-bond buybacks (dubbed a
"weak form of Operation Twist") to suppress yields, even as the national debt
crosses $40T — putting visible pressure on Warsh ahead of his Jackson Hole
podium Friday. Sticky inflation + hawkish Fed + record long yields is exactly
why growth stalled and cyclicals led today.

The third force is geopolitical, and it is why Smart Money is pouring into
shipping. The US-Iran conflict is in its sixth month; both sides missed the
60day ceasefire window. Bessent has promised an "economic D-Day" of sanctions,
while Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority is threatening seizure, fines, and
"toll" demands on vessels transiting Hormuz — the chokepoint for roughly a
quarter of seaborne crude. Global tanker rates and war-risk insurance are
spiking. Counter-current: sanctions landed "less severe than anticipated," and
Iran and Oman are discussing a joint safe-transit route — hence WTI's path was
two-sided, finishing +1.94% at $81.83 on the data-file close (intraday
headlines whipsawed both ways; number preserved as ground truth). The trade
the market is expressing is not the oil price — it is the SHIPPING DISRUPTION.
