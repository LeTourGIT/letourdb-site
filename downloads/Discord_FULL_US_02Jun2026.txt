📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Tuesday's session had a single, defining catalyst: Jensen Huang took the
stage at Computex in Taipei alongside Marvell CEO Matt Murphy and delivered
four words that sent a $191B company surging 32.52% in a single session —
"the next trillion-dollar company." The remark, underscoring Marvell's
critical role in AI data centre connectivity and NVLink Fusion integration,
wasn't just a compliment — it was an institutional re-rating trigger. MRVL's
move is its biggest single-day gain in company history, and it landed with
full context: Nvidia had already invested $2 billion in Marvell, and MRVL's
custom chip business is tracking $10B+ in annual revenue by fiscal 2029.

The HPE earnings beat, reported after close on 1 June, was the second
structural event. Revenue of $10.68B (+40% YoY) versus $9.79B consensus —
a $900M beat — combined with EPS of $0.79 vs $0.53 expected, and guidance
raised by a full dollar, constitutes the most significant AI infrastructure
earnings validation since Dell's results the prior week. HPE's AI server
order backlog hit $5.9B; cumulative AI bookings reached $16.4B. AI server
orders more than doubled in the quarter. The ProLiant DL394 Gen12, built
on NVIDIA's Vera CPU and designed for agentic AI workloads, further
confirms that the hyperscaler capex cycle has migrated structurally into
enterprise hardware. Computer Hardware now ranks #3 in Smart Money rotation.

Alphabet's announced $80B equity raise — the largest ECM transaction in
history — was the day's negative counterweight. The mechanism matters:
$10B direct to Berkshire Hathaway, $30B in underwritten offerings, and $40B
via at-the-market sales. The ATM component — which systematically dilutes
as shares are sold into the secondary market — is what hit GOOGL -4%. This
is not a bear signal for AI; it's a dilution event masquerading as a
bullish capex announcement. Buffett's $10B investment simultaneously
validates Google's AI platform and signals Berkshire's first major tech
commitment in years — a structural vote of confidence that the market is
parsing correctly.

On geopolitics: Iran-related headlines remain volatile. Lebanon fighting
intensified Tuesday, with Iran suspending US talks as Lebanon operations
re-escalated. WTI bounced +1.57% to $97.47 — giving back some of May's
ceasefire optimism — but remained well below the $100+ levels of April's
escalation peak. The oil market is now a binary volatility machine: every
Lebanon headline triggers 1-2% swings. The broader Iran resolution thesis
is intact (WTI has declined 20% from 2026 peak) but the path is non-linear.

JOLTS data (April) showed job openings rising 4.6% to 7.6M — the highest
in nearly two years — which prints as labour market resilience but also
keeps the Fed's June decision complex. A strong labour print at this point
in the cycle is a dual-edged signal: it confirms the economy is absorbing
Hormuz disruption, but it reduces the urgency of near-term rate cuts.

SPX breadth improved materially from prior sessions: 257 advancers vs 242
decliners — essentially flat, but a major upgrade from Monday's 207/293.
RUT +0.90% with 979 advancers vs 872 decliners confirms genuine breadth
expansion. NDX remained split: 44 advancers vs 56 decliners — index gains
driven by semiconductor weight concentration (MRVL +32.5%).
