📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Wednesday was a textbook short-covering rally in the AI supply chain —
and then a textbook reminder of where the concentration risk sits.
SOXX +6.01% (⚠ EXTREME) led a semiconductor rebound that carried NDX
+2.28% and SPX +1.31%, with VIX crushed 10% to 17.49. The leaders were
the capex RECIPIENTS: memory (MU +11.89%, SNDK +13.69%), optical
interconnect (AAOI +15.60%, LITE +10.67%, COHR +9.25%, CIEN +8.80%),
servers (DELL +11.75%, SMCI +9.50%) and neocloud CRWV +24.71% on
earnings. Fourteen of the top 15 gainers were Technology.

The capex PAYERS went the other way: MSFT -2.26%, META -1.97%, AMZN
-1.74% all sat in the bottom 15. That split is the institutional read
of the session — money is paying for the AI build-out's suppliers while
penalising the balance sheets funding it. Morgan Stanley's call to stick
with "high-quality AI bonds as debt worries persist" is the same
message from the credit side: the market is questioning who pays, not
whether demand exists.

The macro backdrop should have been a headwind — Goldman now pencils in
December AND March hikes. Equities rallied through it because Brent
briefly broke $90 on Iran de-escalation. Falling oil lowers the
inflation impulse forcing the Fed's hand, which is why the market can
absorb hawkish rate talk without breaking.

Then the after-hours tape changed the conversation. The WSJ reported
Nvidia's board is preparing to remove Jensen Huang, and NVDA fell 8.61%
to $158.78. The critical detail: NVDA lagged its own sector all day
(+2.87% vs SOXX +6.01%). Smart traders were already rotating out of
the name and into the supply chain — AMD +7.32% in session and +4.32%
AH says that rotation continues tonight. This is a governance shock,
not a demand shock, and the market is treating it that way.

The breadth picture is the most important and least obvious signal.
SPX advancers beat decliners ~2:1 (329/168), 26 stocks rose 4%+ with
zero falling 4%, 43 new 20day highs vs 1 low, and T2108 climbed to
73.4%. Those are bull-market internals. Yet the SPX regime prints
Bearish. When the average stock is healthy and the index regime is
broken, the problem is the handful of trillion-dollar weights — and
tonight one of them dropped 8.6%.
