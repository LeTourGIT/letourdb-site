📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Tuesday 14/07/2026 was a study in a quiet index masking a loud rotation.
XJO finished dead flat at 8,808.5 (+0.00%) and the All Ords a hair
lower at 9,001.3 (-0.02%) — yet beneath the surface capital moved
hard along a single fault line: the Strait of Hormuz.

Trump's weekend move to reinstate the Iranian blockade and impose a
20% cargo toll on Hormuz traffic sent Brent up double-digits into the
session and drove a clean energy bid. Energy (XEJ +1.98%) led all 11
sectors — Santos, Woodside and Karoon (KAR +3.81%, 5day +10.7%) the
large-cap expressions, Stanmore (SMR +8.13%, coal) the standout. The
Smart Money rotation table confirms this is institutional, not noise:
Oil & Gas Refining SURGED to #1 (from #49 nine days ago), Oil & Gas
Exploration to #3, Integrated Oil & Gas to #5, and Coal to #10 — an
entire energy complex re-rating in unison.

The mirror image was the rate-sensitive and uranium complex. Real
Estate (XRE -1.62%) was the worst sector as bond yields backed up on
revived inflation fear — the oil spike directly threatens the CPI path
and pushes any RBA-cut hope further out. Uranium/nuclear-fuel names
were routed independently of oil: Paladin -5.99%, Deep Yellow -5.38%,
Bannerman -4.00%, Silex -9.52%, Alligator -7.55% — a sharp, specific
de-rating of the nuclear-fuel trade even as fossil energy ran. Kingsgate
(KCN -16.09%, gold) was the single worst large mover.

Breadth tells the cautionary tale the index hides. XJO advancers
trailed decliners 90/103 (0.87:1), the All Ords a weak 184/253, and
new 20day lows outnumbered highs at every index (XJO 22 vs 13, XAO 64
vs 25). Only 35% of XJO stocks sit above their 20MA and 40.5% above
their 200MA. This was a flat tape carried by a handful of energy heavyweights,
not a broad advance.

RBA context: the Bank held at 4.35% in June and next meets 10-11 August
— outside the immediate window, so no rate event today. But the oil shock
matters precisely because it reloads the inflation risk that has kept the
RBA hawkish (three hikes already in 2026). Westpac still flags Aug/Sep hikes.
