📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
The ASX 200 fell 1.03% to 8,819.4, its worst session since June, erasing about
$32bn after being down as much as $50bn intraday — a third straight decline that
leaves the index ~5.7% below its early-August record of 9,296. This was a macro
selloff, not a stock story: every one of 11 sectors closed red.

The trigger was offshore. Brent settled above US$101 for the first time since July
after the US destroyed five Iranian tankers and Iran pledged to intensify attacks,
reviving the stagflation narrative. Simultaneously the US 10yr yield pushed to its
highest since 2023 (~4.85%) and the Australian 3yr broke out to levels last seen in
2011. Higher oil + higher yields is the textbook long-duration-equity headwind.

Materials (XMJ -1.63%) was the second-worst sector: BHP -2.9% and RIO -3.0% fell
even as COMEX copper hit a record US$6.86/lb and iron ore held ~US$99/t. The move
in equity risk premium simply overwhelmed the commodity tailwind — a signal that
this is a discount-rate event, not a demand-destruction one. The lone bright spot
in resources was gold equities: SBM +16.44% on agreeing to sell its Simberi stake
to Lingbao, FML +9.17% on a maiden dividend, OBM +4.93% on first Waihi ore.

The defensive fingerprints were everywhere. Utilities (-0.33%) and Comm. Services
(-0.13%) were the least-bad sectors, while IT (-1.74%) was crushed. Smart Money
rotation confirmed it: Electric Utilities entrenched at rank 1, and the entire
energy/coal complex plus REITs and insurance surged up the table — capital hiding
in real-asset and inflation-hedged cashflows exactly as US energy led Wall Street.

The read-through: with the RBA decision on 29 Sept (NAB tipping a hike to 4.60%),
an oil-driven inflation scare is the worst possible pre-meeting backdrop. Watch the
XJO 200MA (~8,816) — a decisive break opens the door to the 8,700 area.
