📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
This was a geopolitics-first session. Trump declared the Iran ceasefire "over"
and "a waste of time" from the NATO summit in Ankara, hours after CENTCOM struck
80+ Iranian targets — air-defense, command-and-control, coastal radar and 60+
IRGC fast-boats — in retaliation for Iranian attacks on three commercial vessels
(including Saudi and Qatari tankers) in the Strait of Hormuz. Washington also
reimposed the oil sanctions it had lifted under June's Islamabad Memorandum,
stripping out the central concession of the deal. This is the mirror image of
the 1 May peace-proposal session: the same chokepoint, the opposite vector.

Oil repriced the entire tape. WTI +3.32% to $74.66 and Brent +4.08% to $79.14
dragged the 10year Treasury yield up to 4.57% as the market re-underwrote the
inflation path. That single rate move is the transmission belt for the day's
worst damage: homebuilders were the session's ugliest cohort (ITB -3.97%,
XHB -3.08%, both down ~8% over 5day), and consumer-credit lenders cracked hard —
Synchrony -9.61%, Capital One -5.39%, Citizens -4.49%. With no company-specific
catalyst (SYF doesn't report until 21 July), that financials air-pocket is a pure
macro repricing: higher-for-longer yields plus oil-driven recession/credit-loss
anxiety compressing consumer-finance multiples. Trace the chain cleanly —
Hormuz strike → oil +3-4% → 10yr 4.57% → housing and consumer credit gutted.

The offsetting bid was narrow and mechanical. Energy was the only sector clearly
working (XLE +1.76%; VLO +6.26%, BKR +5.71%, MPC +5.39%, PSX +5.02% — refiners
and services capturing the crack-spread/price shock), and OIH +3.15% was the
lone ETF above +3%. Technology (+1.24%) was green only because a mega-cap chip
bounce (ANET +8.76%, SMCI +7.31%, AKAM +10.67%, SNDK +6.77%, CIEN +5.27%) and a
crypto-miner squeeze (WULF +12.8%, IREN +8.01%, CRWV +7.75%) papered over broad
weakness — and CNBC's tape read ("chip rebound fails") is corroborated by the
NDX closing up just +0.27% on 40 advancers vs 62 decliners. This is not
leadership; it's a handful of index-weight names masking a distribution day.

Breadth is the tell. SPX advancers 112 vs decliners 388 — a 1:3.5 ratio on a
day the index fell only -0.28%. RUT 601/1375, DJI 6/24. Only 16 SPX names made
new 20day highs against 25 new lows, and across the entire RS Top 20 only ONE
name (PENG) carries an [H] flag. Participation has collapsed even as the headline
indices hold — the definition of a narrowing, top-heavy market that is one
catalyst away from a broader repricing.

Precious metals offered no shelter, which is itself a signal. Gold -1.15% and
Silver -3.48% fell on the day of the geopolitical shock — Morgan Stanley is
openly questioning gold's portfolio role — because the dominant macro force was
the yield spike (a rising real-rate, stronger-dollar impulse), not a flight to
safety. Materials (XLB -2.62%) was the worst SPDR, dragged by the metals complex
(SIL -3.28%, Coeur -4.99%). When both risk assets AND gold sell while yields rise,
the market is pricing an inflation/rate problem, not a growth-panic — a crucial
distinction for positioning into next week.
