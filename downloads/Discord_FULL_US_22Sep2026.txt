Tuesday was a two-speed market wearing a flat SPX mask. SPX closed at 7,764.64 — six
cents below Monday — while NDX rallied +0.82% to 30,732 and the Nasdaq Composite posted
a record close. Beneath that, XLF fell -1.97%, the S&P bank index lost around 3% and
Adv/Dec on the SPX was a coin flip at 249/250. Two powerful sector trades cancelled each
other out at index level: long AI-hardware, short AI-disrupted incumbents.

The geopolitical input drove the macro tape. Trump described a 'very good' meeting with
Iranian envoys in New York, and CNBC reported Iran says it can reopen Hormuz within seven
days if US pressure eases — though Trump also told the UN a deal would come after the
November midterms. WTI fell -3.44% to $89.77, XLE -1.09% (5day -6.29%), XOP -1.70% (5day

three days, E&P from rank 6 (5days ago) to 124. That is an institutional EXIT, not a dip.

The chip trade carried the tape. SOXX +2.40% for a sixth straight gain (5day +14.82%, 20day
+13.16%) with memory in front — SNDK +6.82% on $24.2B turnover, MU +5.00% on $31.5B — and
power/interconnect names MPWR +8.06%, ALAB +6.67%, VICR +19.85%, TER +4.49%. CNBC framed

hence SCHW -6.11%, LPLA -7.45%, AMP -4.36% and INTU -3.87%, ADBE -4.52% in software.
AI is simultaneously the market's biggest buyer and its biggest seller.

long end lower — Reuters cited curve flattening as a direct bank-margin threat. The mirror
image: ITB +3.21%, XHB +2.53%, LEN +6.38%, and Homebuilding vaulting to Smart Money rank

market is trading the 10-year, not the Fed funds rate.
