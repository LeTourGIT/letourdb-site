The ASX 200 added 1.25% to close at 8,914 — its biggest single-
session gain since the first ceasefire announcement — as the

triggered the most decisive sector rotation of 2026. Materials
surged 4.06%, gold stocks exploded to the upside, and energy
was obliterated -5.58% as the geopolitical war premium melted out
of oil in real time.

This session followed a playbook that traders have been rehearsing
since April 8 (the initial ceasefire) but today the context is
different: this is a permanent deal, not a two-week pause. WTI's
decline from $88.62 on 12 June (7d delayed data) to $79.89 at
report time is a structural repricing of the global energy risk
premium, not a tactical bounce. For the ASX, the transmission
mechanism is immediate: energy companies that had benefited from
war-inflated oil prices (WDS, STO, BPT, ALD, VEA) are being
repriced to reflect a post-conflict supply-normalisation world.

The gold cohort's outperformance deserves its own note. OBM
+16.29%, VAU +14.71%, RRL +13.33%, BGL +13.01% — this is not
the typical "risk-off gold" trade. Ceasefire-driven dollar
softness, falling real yields, and rotation out of energy into
inflation-sensitive stores of value drove bullion. Gold futures
in the data file sit at $4,334, having pulled back from $4,690+
intraday highs earlier in the week; the gold equity move today
suggests the physical price rebound is being front-run by miners.

32 stocks up +4% against only 9 down -4%. XSO breadth was even
stronger at 3.0:1 (115/38), with 32 small caps gaining more than
4% — the speculative tier fully engaged with the ceasefire thesis.
T2108 at 65% for XJO is the highest in months, pushing firmly
above the neutral 50% zone and approaching overbought territory.

Tomorrow's RBA decision is the key risk to the nascent recovery.
The consensus is a hold at 4.35%, and the ceasefire's oil-price
impact materially reduces the inflation justification for a fourth
hike. But a hawkish hold statement that explicitly keeps August
live would pressure REITs, consumer names, and utilities — the
same sectors that moved today on "peak rate" assumptions.
