📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Monday 1 June opened the new month almost exactly where Friday left it
— XJO 8,729.4, a fraction lower — but the flat tape masked the most
concentrated single-sector move in weeks. INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY
(XIJ +5.43%) did virtually all the lifting, tracking Friday's record
Nasdaq close and the Dell-led AI-hardware rally. WiseTech (WTC) was
the standout, with support stepping back in around its AI/CargoWise
pivot narrative after a brutal twelve months; Xero and NextDC joined.
This was bargain-hunting in the most beaten-down corner of the index,
not a broad risk-on surge.

Underneath IT, the session was defensive-to-soft. HEALTHCARE
(XHJ -1.68%) was the worst sector, CSL (-2.3%) still carrying its
impairment/guidance overhang. Real Estate (-0.76%) and Financials
(-0.33%) drifted, the banks mixed. Materials (+0.49%) was a quiet
positive: BHP went ex-dividend (-0.9%, $1.03 ff) yet RIO bucked the
ex-div rule (+1.2-1.7% after going ex $3.60), and the GOLD MINERS were
the real strength — Evolution (+4.3%) and Northern Star (+3.4%) firm
as bullion held near records.

The LITHIUM/critical-minerals cohort had a strong day inside an
otherwise muted Materials sector — IGO (+4.7%), Pilbara (+4.33%) and
Liontown (+3.31%) all advanced, consistent with lithium carbonate's
continued recovery (NE Asia ~US$21/kg, +17% in May). The institutional
lithium re-rating thesis stays alive even with iron ore down 4.15%.

The most important development isn't on the screen at all: WTI is down
~16% over 20 days as the Iran/Hormuz risk premium drains, and AUS
monthly CPI just slipped 0.3% m/m — first decline since February. The
RBA's May hike leaned explicitly on Middle-East fuel costs; both props
are now reversing into 16 June. Against that, the caution is real:
only ~39% of XJO names sit above their 200MA, XAO breadth was actually
NEGATIVE on the day (226 adv / 231 dec), and every index remains below
its 200MA. A narrow tech-driven flat session is not a healthy market —
it's a market leaning on a few large names ahead of Q1 GDP this week.
