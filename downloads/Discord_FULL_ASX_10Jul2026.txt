📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Friday 10 July closed the week with XJO +0.50% to 8,806.0, and
the headline number is the least interesting thing about it. The
session was a violent sector rotation dressed up as a quiet
half-percent advance: Materials +2.32% and Health Care -1.96% —
a 4.3-point spread between the best and worst sector on a day
the index moved 50bp. Capital did not enter the market today. It
moved across it.

Materials is the mandated lead. XMJ +2.32% arrives after roughly
a 14% drawdown from 17 June, and the composition of the bounce
matters more than its size. This was NOT an iron ore rally —
iron ore CFR China 62% sits at $98.57/t, -0.29%, and has been
stuck in a $95-105 band for a month on Chinese port inventory
build. The leadership was uranium (CXU +27.78%, DYL +7.43%,
BMN +7.25%, BOE +6.72%, SLX +9.40%), copper (CSC +6.45%, MLX
+7.69%) and gold (BC8 +5.56%). LME copper +2.04% and zinc
+2.64% overnight gave the base metals complex a hard bid, and
UBS's note on the pending US Section 232 copper tariff decision
— arguing any US import moderation is only a modest headwind —
gave the copper names permission to run. BHP and RIO are not in
today's top 15. This is a second-tier resources bounce with
first-tier index consequences.

Health Care -1.96% is the funding source, and it is the cleanest
mean-reversion trade on the board. XHJ rallied roughly 25% from
3 June; it is still +11.68% over 20days even after today. PME
-6.34% (and -6.00% over 5days) is the marquee unwind — a stock
that sits at RS 93.29 and ASX100 constituency, so this is
institutional profit-taking, not retail capitulation. TLX -3.96%
and 4DX -2.46% confirm the sector, not the stock, is being sold.

The quietest and most important line in the whole dashboard is
the 20day High list: CBA, NAB, WBC, ANZ, MQG, CGF, ASX, NHF,
REA, EDV. That is the entire banking and financial infrastructure
complex printing fresh 20day highs on a day XFJ moved just
+0.55%. Banks do not make new highs quietly by accident. With
~75% hold priced for the 11 August RBA meeting and three hikes
already delivered in 2026 (cash rate 4.35%), the market is
beginning to price the end of the tightening cycle — and the
first expression of that thesis is always the banks, then REITs
(XRE +0.58%), then Homebuilding (Smart Money #3, entrenched).

Breadth says be careful. XJO advancers beat decliners 112/78
(1.44:1) — respectable, not emphatic. But only 37.2% of XJO
constituents sit above their 20day MA, 41.7% above their 200MA,
and quarterly -25% names (34) still outnumber +25% names (26).
On the All Ords the damage is starker: 163 above 200MA vs 325
below — barely one in three. A market where two-thirds of stocks
are in long-term downtrends and the index is 0.29% above its
200MA is an index held up by a handful of names.
