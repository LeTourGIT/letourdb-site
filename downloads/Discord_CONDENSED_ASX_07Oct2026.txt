📰 KEY HEADLINES
---------------------------------------------------
🇦🇺 ASX struggles for direction, Wall Street hits record highs (ABC
   News Business)
   The lead headline is the session in one line: a record S&P 500
   delivered XJO -0.09%. The gap is composition — the US record
   was built on AI semis and nuclear-linked utilities, and the ASX
   has neither in size. Until banks or iron ore lead, US records
   are a sentiment input for the ASX, not an earnings one.

💵 The RBA isn't trying to stop inflation. So don't expect prices
   to fall (ABC News Business)
   One of three ABC rates-and-recession pieces today (alongside
   CBA chief Matt Comyn on interest rates and "Recession strikes
   fear into many") — repetition across the feed marks this as the
   domestic priority story. Cash rate 4.60% after four 2026 hikes,
   next decision 03/11/2026, and Westpac expects another move.
   Banks were the worst sector today for exactly this reason.

🛢️ Oil rises as concerns over Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia
   eclipse supply recovery (CNBC Markets)
   Corroborated by a second CNBC item on Iranian tanker attacks
   threatening rebounding Hormuz exports and an ABC World report
   of a Black Sea tanker strike — three energy-security stories in
   one feed. It also validates the Section 1 quote: WTI +0.26%
   at US$90.33, Brent US$101.53. For Australia the chain is oil →
   CPI → RBA → rate-sensitives.

🇦🇺 Woolworths freight fuel levy surcharge almost triples since
   Iran war (ABC News Business)
   This is the oil shock arriving in the supermarket supply chain.
   Either suppliers absorb it (margin pressure upstream), WOW
   absorbs it (WOW +0.26% today, 5day -2.50%), or shelf prices
   rise and the RBA sees it in CPI. None of the three outcomes is
   benign for Consumer Staples (XSJ 5day -1.89%).

💵 India's central bank hikes rates for the first time since 2023
   as inflation risks build (CNBC Markets)
   The RBA is not an outlier — energy-importing Asia-Pacific
   central banks are tightening together. Synchronised tightening
   erodes the AUD's rate-differential support, one reason the
   currency is -3.20% over 20 days despite four domestic hikes.
