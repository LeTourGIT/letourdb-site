📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Thursday 25 June was a textbook risk-off rotation, not a broad capitulation. The ASX 200
fell 0.68% to 8,748.7 — but the index-level number badly understates the dispersion beneath
it. Capital didn't leave the market; it stampeded out of resources, energy and gold and into
defensives. Healthcare (XHJ +2.56%) and Consumer Discretionary (XDJ +2.11%) led every sector,
while Materials (XMJ -2.28%) and Energy (XEJ -2.48%) were dumped. When the two largest
cyclical sectors are red and the defensives are green, that is the market repricing the
growth/rate outlook, not fleeing equities wholesale.

The session's defining shock was JDO (Judo Capital) -40.39% to A$0.91 — a near-A$800m market-
cap wipe-out. The SME lender slashed FY26 profit-before-tax guidance to A$163-169m (from
A$180-190m) after three specific loan exposures across different sectors deteriorated,
lifting its expected FY26 cost of risk to A$116-122m. 90-days-past-due and impaired loans
are now seen at ~3% of gross loans. This is a single-name credit event, not a systemic bank
read — the Big 4 are far better provisioned — but it is a pointed reminder that the lagged
effect of 4.35% cash rates is now showing up in business-loan books. It dragged Financials
(XFJ -1.22%) into the red despite IAG +4.32% holding up the insurers.

The commodity complex took a triple hit. Gold futures slipped below US$4,000/oz (the 7d sheet
has the gold ETF -2.22% on the session), gutting the gold miners — PDI -13.25%, SMI -10.38%,
PNR -7.84%. Iron ore sits near US$100.5/t under the structural weight of Simandou's supply
ramp and softer Chinese steel demand. And lithium remains in oversupply (China carbonate
~CNY 157-169k/t) with Bald Hill and Finniss restarts adding tonnes — ELV -8.77%, GLN -7.69%,
INR -9.68% all cratered. IPX -9.46% and PNR -7.84% rounded out a brutal day for spec miners.
WOR -9.70% (Worley) was the large-cap industrials casualty, caught in the energy-services
downdraft as the oil premium evaporated.

The bright spots were genuinely fundamental. EIQ +30.24% surged on news Pro Medicus will make
an initial A$10m (up to A$20m) convertible-note investment and become a reseller of EchoIQ's
EchoSolv cardiac-AI platform — a blue-chip validation of a micro-cap medtech story. GYG
+8.89%, LOV +5.05%, MYR +5.17% and PMV +4.23% show the consumer-discretionary bid has legs:
with NAB economists now flagging the RBA's next move as a cut rather than a hike, the
rate-pain trade for spending-sensitive names is unwinding. LLC +8.93% led Real Estate on the
same duration logic. The macro backdrop shifted this week: stronger-than-feared jobs data
('some cracks' but unemployment still dipped) trimmed near-term rate-cut bets, while underlying
inflation ran at its highest since 2024 — a mixed bag that keeps the RBA firmly on hold.

The structural caution stands: breadth was poor (XAO advancers/decliners 175/284), 'Bullish'
regime labels mask a sub-MA tape, and only 42.5% of the All Ords sit above their 50MA. The
offset is Micron's after-hours blowout flipping US futures green and oil's collapse easing
the inflation pulse — both constructive for Friday's open. Watch whether XJO can hold 8,700.
