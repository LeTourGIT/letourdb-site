📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
This was a textbook geopolitical risk-off session — and the near-perfect mirror
image of the Iran PEACE-proposal session earlier this cycle. US forces struck Iran
and Tehran retaliated, raising the risk of a wider war. The market's reaction was
mechanical and immediate: WTI crude +4.53% to $90.68, Brent +6.97% to $95.17, the
US 10-year Treasury yield spiked, and VIX jumped +9.52% to 16.34 — just shy of the
+10% 'elevated volatility' threshold. Every major index closed red (SPX -0.71%,
NDX -1.29%, RUT -1.23%, DJI -0.79%) and, crucially, breadth confirmed the weakness
rather than diverging from it.

The rotation was the cleanest risk-off signature you will see. Trace the chain:
oil shock → inflation/rate impulse → yield spike → multiple compression on long-
duration equities. Energy (XLE +1.27%) and the defensives — Utilities +0.78%,
Health Care +0.66%, Staples +0.32% — were the only green sectors. Everything with
a high multiple or high beta was sold: Technology XLK -1.53%, Consumer Discretionary
XLY -1.72%, Industrials XLI -1.37%. CNBC's tape read captured it exactly — 'S&P 500
and Nasdaq close lower as chip stock rebound fails.' The semiconductor bounce that
had been building simply could not hold against a rising-rate, rising-oil backdrop.

The single most important cross-asset tell was gold. On a genuine geopolitical
risk-off day, gold FELL -2.49% to $4,375 and silver dropped -4.08% — the opposite
of the textbook safe-haven bid. This is a rate-and-dollar story: the same yield
spike that compressed tech multiples raised the opportunity cost of holding non-
yielding metal, and Morgan Stanley publicly questioned gold's portfolio role into
the move. The precious-metals miners bore the brunt — GDX -3.90%, SIL -3.74%,
SLV -3.68%, COPX -3.90% — a full-complex de-rate. When the haven asset sells with
the risk assets, the driver is liquidity and rates, not fear alone.

Beneath the index prints, the leadership rotation is the real story. RS momentum
leadership has swung violently OUT of semiconductors and software and INTO biotech
and healthcare: roughly 12 of the RS Top 20 are now biotech/pharma/med-tech names.
This is defensive, idiosyncratic-catalyst leadership taking over as the cyclical
growth complex de-rates. Only two software names survive in the top 20 (TEAM rank 9,
APPS rank 15) and both fell hard today. Simultaneously, Smart Money is tilting into
hard assets: Oil & Gas E&P, Drilling and Refining are all climbing the rotation
ranks, alongside Coal (rank 7), Agricultural Chemicals (rank 8) and a Steel surge —
the classic inflation-beneficiary basket that an oil shock pulls into favour.

The earnings tape added a sharp bifurcation. DELL is the session's key callout: it
fell -6.80% into its print with the broad tech tape, then surged +7.89% after hours
after lifting its FY2027 forecast on AI-server strength — a clean 'sell the sector,
buy the specific AI-infrastructure winner' setup, echoed by HPE +4.22% AH. Palo Alto
beat on AI-driven cybersecurity demand yet still closed -5.24% — proof that in a
multiple-compression tape, even a beat does not save a high-multiple name intraday.
MongoDB -11.34% AH and Credo -8.65% show the downside dispersion; GitLab +18.24% AH
shows the reward for a clean beat still exists after the bell.
