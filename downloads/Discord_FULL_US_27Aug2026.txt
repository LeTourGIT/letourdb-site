📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Thursday was a mega-cap engine running against a stalled chassis. The S&P closed
+0.72% and the Nasdaq 100 +1.43%, but the advance was manufactured by a handful
of software and semiconductor names reporting earnings — beneath them, decliners
beat advancers 347 to 151 on the S&P. This is one of the widest gaps between
index direction and internal breadth of the cycle, and it is the single most
important thing to understand about today's tape.

The catalyst chain is clean. NVIDIA's beat ($68B in turnover, +8.74%) removed the
"AI capex isn't paying off" overhang and short-covered the entire infrastructure
layer. Simultaneously Salesforce (+22.58%) and CrowdStrike (+20.50%) detonated the
"AI disrupts software" thesis — the market had positioned these as losers, and the
quarters forced a violent re-rate. The result: IGV (software) +7.74%, CIBR
(cybersecurity) +7.61%, and XLK +3.16% were the ONLY three ETFs in the entire
dashboard above +3%, and XLK was the ONLY green sector on the board. Every other
sector closed red, tightly clustered between -0.2% and -1.4%.

That configuration — one sector vertical, ten red, indices up — is textbook
funding rotation. Capital was sold out of defensives and consumer names to buy the
AI-software complex. The heatmap confirms it: Staples XLP -1.38%, Healthcare XLV
-1.13%, Discretionary XLY -1.09%, Comm Services XLC -1.07% led the downside, and
MarketWatch's own sector roundups flagged utilities and comms falling specifically
"on rotation into tech." The losers list is the mirror image of the gainers: pure
software up top, retail (BURL -7.64%, BBY -4.44%, DLTR -3.92%, ROST -2.71%) and
travel (EXPE -4.35%, BKNG -3.03%) at the bottom. Nothing was added to the market;
it was rearranged into technology.

The geopolitical cross-current is a Hormuz RE-escalation — the mirror image of the
peace-proposal cycle earlier this year. A tanker attack in the Strait, Iranian
warnings of ship seizures ahead of Bessent's sanctions push, and soaring global
shipping rates pushed WTI +2.15% to $83.60 and Brent +2.45% to $88.63. Energy the
sector (XLE -0.22%) barely moved because the equity complex is transfixed by
earnings, but the Smart Money rotation tables tell the truer story: shipping and
marine industries have surged to the very top of the institutional flow rankings
(see below). The freight-rate trade is being expressed through capital rotation
before it shows up in sector ETF prices.

Overlaying everything is Jackson Hole. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh delivers his keynote
Friday, and the setup is unusually tense: Treasury's Bessent has been intervening
in the bond market, which — per multiple Fed watchers quoted today — "complicates
things" for Warsh and leaves the dollar and long end "on edge." The market's answer
was to crush volatility (VIX -4.60% to 14.51, a five-session drop of 9.4%) and lean
into the mega-cap winners. That is a complacent posture into a binary macro event
with breadth already this thin. The asymmetry for Friday is not symmetrical.
