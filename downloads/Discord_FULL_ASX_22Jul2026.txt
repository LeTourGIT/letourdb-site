📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Wednesday's +0.34% lift to 8,823 was a narrow, resources-led grind rather
than a broad advance. Only three of eleven sectors closed green - Materials
(XMJ +2.30%), Energy (XEJ +1.67%) and a barely-positive Financials (+0.19%) -
while the other eight, led by Healthcare (-1.93%), Real Estate (-1.46%) and
Consumer Discretionary (-1.44%), were sold. XJO advancers beat decliners just
100 to 94 (1.06:1): a green index masking a split tape.

The engine was the commodity complex. Copper leapt ~3% overnight on tightening
Chinese supply, gold bounced 1.7%, and Brent settled +3.1% on the Hormuz
standstill - a trifecta that drove the big miners: South32 +3.5% (strong
quarterly, FY26 volumes +15%), BHP +3.0%, RIO +2.5% and Fortescue +2.1%.
This is the XMJ >1.5% resources-rally signal, and it's institutional in weight -
S32 [H] and the majors, not just speculative juniors, doing the lifting.

Beneath the majors, the juniors expressed the theme with leverage: gold
(TCG +11.2% [H], KCN +7.6%, OBM +7.3%), silver (SS1 +9.8%, USL +7.8%) and
uranium (CXU +15.4%). But uranium was bipolar - Paladin (PDN) +6.7% on a Q4
beat and FY27 guidance ~11% ahead faded to +2.7% as brokers cut targets, and
Peninsula (PEN) cratered -26% after withdrawing CY2026 guidance. Selective,
not a blanket bid.

The other side of the ledger was defensive and growth destruction. Healthcare
was gutted - CSL -2.8%, COH -4.9%, PME -3.7%, TLX -2.9% - and tech tracked the
global chip wobble (WBT -7.3%, XRO -2.2%). Lynas (LYC) was the standout single
loser, off ~8% intraday on a Q4 NdPr miss (15% below consensus on Mt Weld ore
quality) with HRE costs blowing out to ~$294m. Rate-sensitives (REITs,
utilities, discretionary) sagged into Thursday's jobs print.

Net: a commodity-driven tape riding the Hormuz oil premium, China copper
tightness and gold's safe-haven bid - powerful at the top end but thin
underneath. Sustaining above 8,800 requires the resource bid to broaden or
the defensives to stop bleeding. Thursday's labour-force data is the circuit.
