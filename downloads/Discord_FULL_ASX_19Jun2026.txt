📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
The ASX 200 fell -0.92% to 8,828.70 and the All Ords -0.87% to 9,047.30,
but the headline badly understates the day. This was a single-sector
earthquake: Materials (XMJ) collapsed -4.03% while ten of eleven sectors
finished within +/-1.3% of flat and five were green. Strip out the
miners and this was a quietly constructive, rotational session.

The catalyst was offshore and overnight. The US-Iran 14-point memorandum
removed a large geopolitical risk premium, and Kevin Warsh's debut as Fed
Chair delivered a hawkish hold -- a dot plot tilting toward a 2026 hike
and a firmer USD (dollar at a 1-year high). That combination is kryptonite
for precious metals: gold -3.78% to ~US$4,175 and silver -6.25% overnight.
ASX gold and silver miners were marked down at the open and never recovered.

The casualty list is unambiguous -- the bottom of the market was wall-to-
wall gold, silver, uranium and lithium: DPM -7.92%, MEK -8.70%, BTR -7.14%,
LGF -8.08% (gold); USL -7.69% (silver); DYL -9.88%, AGE -29.52% (uranium);
WC8 -10.32% (lithium). Alcoa (AAI -6.88%) extended base-metals weakness.
Iron ore is quietly soft at ~US$101/t (-8.1% over 20 days) -- no offset
for BHP/RIO.

Against that, capital rotated cleanly into defensives and domestic
cyclicals. Health Care (XHJ +3.51%) led on CSL +7.62% -- a sharp bounce in
a sector still down ~40% over 12 months. Consumer Staples (+1.27%) followed
on A2M +9.82%. Smaller winners told the same story: AD8 +27.43%, 4DX +17.62%,
EOS +14.13% (defence), IEL +6.67%, 360 +6.19%. The Smart Money table
corroborates emphatically -- Multiline Insurance surged to #1.

The tell is breadth vs the tape: XJO advancers trailed decliners 84 to 111
(0.76:1), yet new 20day highs (33) crushed new lows (9) and 65% of XJO names
held above their 20MA. That is an index dragged by a handful of heavyweight
miners while the median stock holds firm. With the RBA having held at 4.35%
on 16 Jun (inflation "still too high"), the macro overhang is steady rather
than acute. The real question: does the crowded, overbought gold complex
stabilise, or is there more unwind to come?
