📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
The session's defining catalyst was geopolitical: Iran halted its strikes on
Israel, and CNBC's lead line said it plainly — "S&P 500 closes higher as chips
stage a comeback, Iran stops strikes on Israel." The market reaction was a
textbook de-risking unwind. VIX collapsed -9.10% to 17.68 (5day -17.81%), its
sharpest single-session vol crush of the cycle, while WTI crude fell -3.23%
to $88.62 (5day -6.04%, 20day -15.33%) as the war-risk premium that has been
embedded in oil since the Hormuz escalation began to deflate. This is a
direct mirror of the early-May "Iran peace proposal" session — but this time
the catalyst is an actual cessation of fire, not just a diplomatic gesture,
and the market's response was broader and cleaner: all four major indices
green, RUT leading at +0.79%.

The truce remains fragile — Trump is publicly disputing Iran's account of the
deal terms and calling out a "new drone attack" as the work of "dishonorable
people," and Netanyahu has said the war with Iran and Hezbollah "isn't over."
Treasury yields are not playing along with the equity rally: the 10year sits
near 4.45% and Cleveland Fed tracking has May YoY inflation pushing toward 5%,
largely energy-driven from the Iran conflict. New Fed chair Kevin Warsh's
posture is "silence may be the point" — staying out of the political crossfire
while the data forces his hand toward "higher for longer." Stocks up + yields
up on the same day is an unusual combination; today's rally is being driven
by the removal of tail-risk, not by any dovish repricing.

Semiconductors were the other half of the story. The Smart Money rotation
shows Semiconductor Equipment & Testing surging from #87 (9days ago) to #4
today — and the price action confirms it: AMKR +8.71%, ONTO +6.70%, TER
+5.72%, KLAC +5.55%, INTC +6.51%, SNDK +5.24%, AMD +4.73% all feature in the
top-15 movers. Eleven of today's top fifteen gainers are Technology Equipment
names. But software told the opposite story: ADBE -6.76% despite a genuine
beat-and-raise (record $6.62B revenue, +13% YoY, non-GAAP EPS $5.96 vs $5.81
consensus, FY26 guide raised to $24.35-24.45 EPS) — the headline risk was
CFO Dan Durn's surprise departure (to MARVELL, of all places) on June 15,
leaving Adobe with no permanent CEO AND no permanent CFO mid-"AI eats
software" debate. SNOW -3.17%, ADSK -3.47%, RDDT -6.44%, PLTR -2.36% and
DDOG -1.85% rounded out a broadly red software/SaaS complex. The bifurcation
from the May1 session has flipped: then, capital rotated FROM semi-equipment
INTO software; today it is rotating back the other way, hardware over
application layer, as the AI-capex thesis re-asserts itself on chip names.

The day's biggest single market-structure event was SpaceX's (SPCX) IPO — the
largest in history at ~$75B raised, closing +19% at $161 and making Elon Musk
the world's first trillionaire. Former Nasdaq chief Robert Greifeld said this
"opens the window" for OpenAI and Anthropic to follow this year. The
sympathetic casualty: existing small-cap space names ASTS -15.53% and RKLB
-10.79% — both among today's largest losers, suggesting a "SpaceX vacuum
effect" where speculative capital rotated out of the incumbents and into the
new mega-listing on day one.

Breadth was healthy and unremarkable in the best sense: SPX Adv/Dec 396/104
(~3.8:1 positive), T2108 at 63.4% (green zone, >60%), and 121 stocks +25% in
the quarter vs only 25 down -25%. This is NOT a narrow, cap-weight-distorted
advance — today's gain is broad-based, in sharp contrast to the May1 ATH
session's 172/326 negative breadth divergence.
