  "second-round effects" and keeping "options open" for June was
  deliberately non-committal — but with Westpac forecasting two

  market is treating this as probable cycle-end, not confirmed.

  For sector allocation: rate-sensitive names (REITs, consumer
  discretionary, utilities) face a structurally higher discount
  rate. Resources, energy, and USD-revenue names are the hedge.
  Westpac's 1H26 result (statutory NPAT $3.4B, +3%) was buried
  by the macro — solid delivery into a headwind that will only
  intensify as mortgage resets flow through over 6–12 months.

  The Hormuz-oil channel is now the RBA's core headache: WTI
  above $100 is not a temporary supply shock — it's becoming
  embedded in services inflation via transport, logistics, and
  energy bills. Until there is a credible de-escalation pathway,
  the RBA has no off-ramp.

📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Tuesday 5 May delivered the session the market had been preparing
for since March CPI printed 4.6%. The RBA hiked 25bp to 4.35% in
an 8-1 vote at 2:30pm AEST, completing the full unwinding of 2025's
75bp cut cycle. The XJO closed -0.19% at 8,680.5 — a remarkably
contained reaction given the magnitude of the policy shift — but
beneath the index surface the sectoral divergence was dramatic and
instructive: Energy led on surging oil (WTI +2.26% Monday, Brent
+5.45%) and Financials bore the brunt of the policy reality.

The market's contained headline reaction masks what was effectively
a 10th loss in 11 sessions. The XJO has shed approximately 350
points from the April highs. The 200-day moving average at 8,803
(per IG) now looms as a recovery target, not a floor. The RBA's
upgraded inflation forecasts (4.8% headline for June quarter vs
4.2% prior) and downgraded GDP (1.3% from 1.8%) lock in the
stagflation-lite narrative — Australia faces higher rates AND slower
growth simultaneously. This is the most hostile macro combination
for rate-sensitive equity.

The session's winner was energy, and the logic is ironic: WTI
above $100 is exactly what forced the RBA to hike, yet the same
oil price rewards ASX energy names (BPT, STO) with higher revenues.
Resources navigated a conflicted day — materials were softer as the
stronger AUD (0.7169 against USD) compresses dollar-denominated
revenues, and the RBA hike signals slower domestic consumption
growth (pressure on BHP's domestic sales outlook), yet commodity
prices themselves remain elevated.

Westpac's 1H26 result was solid on the numbers — $3.4B statutory
NPAT, up 3%, mortgages +7%, business lending +16% — but delivered
into a macro that makes the next 12 months structurally harder.
Rising rates improve NIMs at the margin but Westpac (and peers) are
now staring at a mortgage stress clock: Roy Morgan projects 30.3%
of borrowers in stress if the May hike fully flows through. The
market sold financials regardless of the beat.

Regime analysis offers a key forward read: XAO downgraded from
Neutral Bull to Neutral Bear today — both 10MA and 20MA have now
crossed below the 50MA. This confirms the April recovery failed to
repair structural damage. The XSO remaining Neutral Bull is the one
bright spot — small caps with domestic earnings and less rate
sensitivity to large-cap financials may outperform on a relative
basis, but in absolute terms the path of least resistance remains
lower until oil stabilises and/or the RBA signals a genuine pause.
