📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Thursday was a rotation, not a liquidation. The S&P 500 fell -1.21% — its
worst session since July — yet nearly two of every three constituents rose
(334 advancers / 165 decliners). That is an INVERSE breadth divergence: the
index was dragged lower by a narrow AI-megacap cohort while the broad market
quietly rallied. Meta's decision to create a non-voting share class to fund
AI spending, Lam Research's slightly-light guide, and CoreWeave's debt
worries (-11%) combined into one message: the AI build is now being funded
by equity issuance and leverage, and the market is repricing the cost of
that capital.

The semiconductor complex took the full hit. SOXX -6.79% and XSD -6.15%
were EXTREME moves; the Philadelphia SOX lost roughly $616B in value and
closed at its lowest level since 29/07. The damage tracked the capex
supply chain with surgical precision — equipment (LRCX -11.76%, ENTG
-13.12%), optical (LITE -11.54%, COHR -12.51%, AAOI -11.96%, CIEN -10.71%),
connectivity (ALAB -11.11%, MTSI -15.87%), PCB (TTMI -27.67%), and the
power/electrical layer (NVT -12.63%, FPS -21.54%). After-hours, WDC, BE and
FIX extended the pattern. This was the market selling the AI picks and
shovels, and it hit the layers furthest from end-demand hardest.

The money did not leave equities — it rotated into rate-sensitives and
defensives. The 10-year yield collapsed from 5.13% to 4.96%, its biggest
one-day drop in over two years, and that bid flowed straight into housing
(ITB +5.39%, XHB +3.77%, LEN +8.69%), regional banks (KRE +2.53%), REITs,
insurers and staples (XLP +3.31%, led by PG's earnings beat). XLP up 3.3%
on the same day XLK fell 3.3% is a near-perfect mirror — the cleanest
growth-to-defensive rotation of 2026.

Geopolitics added the second leg. Brent hit $112.80 — a five-month high —
after reported attacks on Saudi infrastructure (Dammam airport, an Aramco
field), the US pulling diplomats from Saudi Arabia, and reports of renewed
US/Israel strike plans post-midterms. Washington also invoked the Defense
Production Act for oil production and refining. Energy equities did NOT
follow crude (XLE -0.87%, OIH -4.21%, CRAK -2.25%): when government
intervention enters refining, margin upside gets capped, and the market
priced that immediately.

The forward read: a falling yield curve, strong labour data (claims 194K)
and improving breadth (SPX T2108 51.6% → 65.8% across five sessions) are
NOT a bear-market backdrop. This is a leadership change. The risk is
concentrated in the AI-capex cohort that has carried index returns all year
— which means the INDEX can keep falling while the AVERAGE stock rises.
Position sizing should reflect that divergence, not the headline print.
