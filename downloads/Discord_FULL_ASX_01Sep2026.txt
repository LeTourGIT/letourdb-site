📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Tuesday 1 September was a quiet-looking headline (XJO -0.10% to 9066.7) hiding a sharp
rotation underneath. The catalyst was rates, not equities: a heavy Australian government
bond sell-off — described in the press as 'once-in-a-generation' — backed yields up and
detonated the rate-duration corners of the market. The clearest expression was a
fund-manager rout: Pinnacle (PNI) -10.1%, MA Financial (MAF) -5.5%, Regal (RPL) -4.3%,
with credit fund Metrics (MXT) -5.0% alongside. Financials as a sector only fell -0.37%,
so this was NOT a broad bank de-rating — it was a targeted hit on asset-gatherers and
credit vehicles whose earnings and NAVs are most exposed to a yield shock.

The second leg was long-duration growth and housing. With '93% of capital-city suburbs'
recording price declines and banks openly forecasting further hikes, REA -4.2%, Car Group
(CAR) -4.2% and Nextdc (NXT) -4.2% led Consumer Discretionary (-1.81%) and IT lower. This
is a textbook bond-up / duration-down day — and it mirrored the US overnight almost exactly,
where mega-cap growth and credit names fell on the same yield back-up.

Against that, the tape's other half was firmly risk-ON in resources and cyclicals. Oil
spiked (Brent >US$91) on renewed US-Iran strikes, lifting Energy (XEJ +1.18%) to sole
sector leadership. Metallurgical coal (CRN +8.9%, SMR +6.6%), high-purity alumina (A4N
+17.1% on its FY26 result) and lithium (LTR +6.5% on a Centenario brine earn-in) drove a
spec/small-cap resources rally that is exactly why XSO (+0.41%) outran the large-cap
indices. Smart Money confirmed the reflation tilt: Homebuilding vaulted to rank 1, with
Industrial Machinery, Auto Parts, Steel, Coal and Gas Utilities all surging.

The internals carry a warning the flat close hides: new 20day LOWS outnumbered new 20day
HIGHS on every one of the four indices (XJO 8 highs vs 35 lows; XAO 36 vs 73). Advance/
decline was near-even (XJO 105/90). This is a market splitting hard along a single factor
line — own the yield-up winners (energy, coal, cyclicals), avoid the yield-up losers (fundies,
REITs, long-duration growth) — rather than trending as a whole. The RBA (29 Sep, 4.35%,
~44% hike-priced) is the event that resolves it.
