Monday was a governance-driven short squeeze of historic scale. Nvidia's board
unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO eleven days after his 12/11
resignation and six days after saying it would not; CNBC reports the White
House sealed the deal. NVDA +31.48% on $129.2B turnover — its biggest one-day
gain ever — repriced the AI complex's largest single risk in one print. SPX
+4.61% and NDX +6.71% had their best day since April 2025; VIX fell 25.83%.

Breadth was a real thrust — SPX 416/83, RUT 1,678/251, 196 of 236 large-
turnover names up — but cap-weight still led: IVV +4.58% vs RSP +2.59%, and
IWF +7.97% vs RPV +1.12% is a 6.85pt growth-value spread. Money went where
last week's pain was: SOXX +13.63%, MU +19.04% on $35.4B, CRWV +22.32%.

200MA (~7,352), new 20day lows beat highs 34 to 2, and only 31.2% of SPX is
above its 20MA. That is a relief rally from RSI-15 sub-30. A trend change
needs the 200MA reclaimed and 20MA participation above 50%.

Rates are the binding constraint. NY Fed's Williams sees higher rates likely
'over time' and the 2-year jumped, which is why XLRE (+1.04%), XLU (+1.48%)
and XLP (-0.09%) trailed. Deutsche Bank's 'AI dollar' note adds a twist: an
AI drawdown is now also a USD risk.

Oil was the geopolitical tailwind: despite Houthi strikes on a Saudi refinery,
WTI fell 4.12% to $80.03 as Tehran signalled talks. XLE (-1.06%) was the worst
sector; JETS +5.16% and ULCC +11.86% collected the dividend. Smart Money's
10day board is still a defensive bunker (7 of 137 industries positive), and
semis sit at rank 78 — the 3-5 session test is whether they reach the top 30.
