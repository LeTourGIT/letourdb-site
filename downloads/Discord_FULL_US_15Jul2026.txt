📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Wednesday was a rotation, not a selloff — and the distinction is the whole story.
Three of four indices closed green with genuinely broad breadth (RUT advancers
1,219 vs 747; 113 stocks +4%), yet the Nasdaq 100 finished red because the entire
AI-hardware complex was liquidated in a single session. Micron -8.0% on $49B of
turnover, Sandisk -8.1%, Western Digital -8.8%, Dell -9.8%, Marvell -7.3%, and the
optical/networking cohort (AAOI -13.0%, Lumentum -7.7%, Corning -7.1%, Ciena -6.4%,
Arista -5.8%) all capitulated together. XLK -1.11% was the worst SPDR sector, and
the semiconductor ETFs (SOXX -2.23%, XSD -1.84%) confirm this was the source of
funds, not idiosyncratic. When a group this large moves together on no single stock's
news, it is a factor unwind — the memory/hardware leg that carried Q2 is being rung.

Where did the money go? Straight into the bank earnings kickoff and capital markets.
JPMorgan +1.2%, Wells Fargo +2.6%, Goldman +1.1%, Citi +1.2%, Morgan Stanley +0.4%
all reported and held gains, but the real fireworks were in asset managers and
exchanges: BlackRock +6.6%, BNY +5.1%, KKR +3.8%, Nasdaq +3.8%. XLF +0.68% understates
it — the capital-markets sub-complex (KCE +2.50%, IAI +1.47%) was the cleaner signal.
Layered on top: PayPal +17.2% on a reported $53B takeover bid, and Apple +4.0% on a
China AI breakthrough that also dragged Alphabet +3.2% and the whole XLC +1.73% (top
sector). Mega-cap platform tech and financials absorbed every dollar semis shed.

The geopolitical wildcard is that the Hormuz ceasefire collapsed overnight — the US
and Iran exchanged fresh strikes, Trump says Tehran called to deal after the attacks.
Yet WTI rose just +0.34% to $80.20 and VIX FELL 5% to 15.67. The market is refusing
to re-price the fear premium it paid in the spring, when crude traded above $100.
Under the surface, though, Smart Money disagrees: energy sub-industries are surging
back up the rotation table (Refining #3, Oil Transport #11, Drilling #12, Integrated
Oil #14 — the last two both SURGE entries). Price says 'past peak fear'; institutional
rank-flow says 'quietly re-accumulating energy.' That divergence is the week's tell.

Two earnings landmines detonated away from the rotation: Progressive -9.4% (combined-
ratio miss) took the insurance group down (IAK -3.11%, the day's worst clean ETF), and
Elevance -8.5% reopened the managed-care wound — notable because MCOs are the 60day
structural RS leader. Pentair -15% on an 'alarming update' was the industrial shock of
the day. None are contagion; all are reminders that under a benign index print, single-
name dispersion is violent. With Netflix reporting Thursday, XLC's bid may be positioning.
