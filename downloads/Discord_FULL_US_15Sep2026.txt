📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Tuesday delivered a broad, orderly decline across all four US indices —
SPX -0.45% to 7,585.73, NDX -0.65%, DJI -0.63%, RUT -0.76% — as two macro
forces collided: a Middle East supply shock driving oil higher and a
10-year Treasury yield at a 19-year high on the eve of an expected Fed
rate HIKE. The regime tape is deteriorating beneath the surface. SPX and
NDX cling to Neutral Bull, the Dow has slipped to Neutral Bear, and the
Russell 2000 is now outright Bearish (15day ROC -4.17, RSI 35.48). Small
caps breaking first is the classic tell — rate-sensitive, unprofitable and
levered names get repriced ahead of the index.

Energy was the unambiguous rotation winner. With the Hormuz conflict
intensifying (a vessel struck in the Strait, a US drone reportedly
downed), WTI rose +2.56% to $105.56 and Brent +1.31% to $108.46. XOP
+3.22% was the ONLY ETF above +3%, and XLE +2.17% was the sole green
sector on the board. Critically, the entire value chain bid at once — E&P
(EOG +3.50%, COP +3.33%, DVN +3.22%) and refiners (VLO +3.68%, MPC +3.63%,
PSX +3.06%) together. Smart Money confirms this is structural, not a
headline pop: four energy sub-industries sit in the top 10 rotation ranks
— E&P rank 6, Integrated Oil rank 7, Refining rank 8, Transportation rank
10. Capital is layering into energy as a Hormuz hedge across the whole
complex.

Crypto was the mirror image — the day's most violent complex and the
purest rate-shock casualty. COIN -10.10%, CRCL -11.41%, MSTR -5.36%, BMNR
-8.39% and IREN -3.68% led the equity carnage, while IBIT -3.64%, ETHA
-5.06% and EETH -4.98% all breached -3%. Two catalysts stacked: a 10-year
yield at a 19-year high crushes the highest-duration risk assets first,
and the Senate cloture vote on the Clarity Act failed, pulling the near-
term regulatory catalyst. Note the Smart Money divergence to interpret
carefully — Blockchain & Cryptocurrency prints ↑↑ SURGE on the 3-day
rotation window (ranks 71-82 → 3 → 14), but that rank is a rear-view
mirror: today's -10% rout is the surge reversing in real time. When
rotation rank and price diverge this hard, price wins the forward read.

The defensive bid concentrated in healthcare and life-sciences. XLV -0.05%
was the best relative sector, and the RS Top 20 has now tilted 13-of-20
into biotech, medical and pharma (TEVA rank 2, MRNA rank 5, plus a wall of
diagnostics and tools names). Cash-flow-positive healthcare tools led the
actual tape: TMO +4.53%, RVTY +9.11%, ILMN +6.76% and Tempus AI +10.67%.
This is textbook late-cycle behaviour — when yields spike and growth
multiples compress, capital hides in profitable, non-cyclical healthcare
rather than leaving the market.

Semiconductors split straight down the duration line. SWKS +13.55% (the
day's top large-cap mover) and QCOM +4.25% powered an Electronic Equipment
& Parts ↑↑ SURGE in the rotation model (rank 94 → 15), yet the chip
rebound 'failed' at the index level as WDC -3.51% and the long-duration
software/EDA complex sagged (SNPS -3.60%, TTWO -4.93%, MSTR -5.36%). The
split is the whole story: profitable handset/hardware silicon gets bid
while long-duration software is sold — a rate-driven bifurcation inside
the same sector, not a semiconductor thesis change.

Breadth is the risk flag that matters most into the Fed. SPX
advancers/decliners ran 157/342 — a 1:2.2 negative ratio — with only 23%
of SPX names above their 20-day MA, T2108 at 31%, and 124 fresh 20-day
lows against just 30 new highs. This is broad internal deterioration, not
a mega-cap-masked wobble; if anything the -0.45% headline understates the
damage underneath. With the rate decision tomorrow and Hormuz unresolved,
the risk-reward has tilted defensive — energy and profitable healthcare
over duration and leverage.
