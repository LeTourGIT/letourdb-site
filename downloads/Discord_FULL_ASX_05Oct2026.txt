📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
A holiday-thinned fade. With NSW out for Labour Day, the XJO
opened on a +0.29% futures lead, reached +0.62% and bled back to
close +0.05% at 8,686.4 (+4.3 points). The index print flatters
the tape: 58 advancers against 134 decliners (0.43:1) on a green
close means the median ASX 200 stock fell while a handful of
heavyweights - BHP +1.11%, CSL +0.94%, MQG +0.97%, ANZ +0.75%, RIO
+0.70% - held the number up. On thin volume that is narrow
leadership rather than proof of distribution, but it is not the
follow-through Friday's +0.8% bounce needed, and Friday's 135/62
breadth has already fully inverted.

Healthcare (XHJ +0.74%) led, and both drivers are stock-specific
rather than sector rotation. CSL announced an exclusive agreement
with Alentis Therapeutics at 08:22 (price sensitive) - US$355m
upfront for a rare kidney disease asset - and the market gave it
+0.94%, a measured vote for a company buying pipeline rather than
buying back stock. COH +4.20% had no announcement at all: it was
-9.97% over 5day and simply mean-reverted. Materials (XMJ +0.36%)
faded from +0.99% as the iron ore majors rose but gold miners were
sold despite firmer gold futures - PDI -2.94% on an in-line
September quarter update is the tell that gold equities are in
sell-the-news mode, consistent with Gold sitting at rank 42 of 74
in the 10day rotation table.

The only real momentum on the board is AI-capex engineering. SXE
+9.69% was the largest ASX300 move and it was broker-driven, not
announcement-driven: Macquarie initiated at Outperform with a
$5.60 target on the data-centre construction pipeline. GNP +5.40%
rose in sympathy and MGH +2.94% re-rated on its 3.2% Firmus stake
as that IPO priced at $11 a share (about $43.7bn). Add CDA at a
record high on conflict-driven communications demand, EOS +3.92%
and the semiconductor names WBT +3.97% and SLX +4.32%, and the
leadership profile is clear: earnings streams that do not depend
on the Australian consumer or the Australian housing market.

Everything that does depend on them stayed weak. Consumer
Discretionary (XDJ -0.88%) was the worst sector and Real Estate
(XRE -0.35%) saw WPR -3.81%, GOZ -3.14% and LLC -3.03%, while
Homebuilding is dead last in the rotation table (rank 74, 10day
ROC -14.03%). The headline tape explains why: auction clearance
under 50%, warnings of the biggest property downturn in 40 years,
and UBS estimating a roughly $30bn hit to state revenues. Private
credit is now part of the same story - three Metrics trusts (MXT,
MOT, MRE) were reinstated to quotation before the open after a
halt since 28/09, and MOT fell -7.41% on its return. Market Index
reports sister trust MXT's audited accounts carried a $47.1m
fair-value loss and a halt to redemptions in its underlying loan
funds. The 4.60% cash rate is doing exactly what it was designed
to do.

Forward look. T2108 at 22% and only 29.5% of the XJO above its
20MA is the washed-out zone where tradeable bounces begin, but new
20day lows (35) still outnumber highs (12) almost 3:1 and all four
regimes are Bearish. The bounce needs breadth before it deserves
trust: an Adv/Dec day above 2:1 with the percentage above the 20MA
reclaiming 40% would be the first real evidence. Post-close
filings not in today's prices: VAU (16:06, price sensitive) court
approval for its scheme meeting, and FBR (17:24) on its Hadrian
demonstration build. Three trading halts (DAL, EGR, AQX) resolve
this week. The macro calendar is light until FOMC minutes on
Thursday; US futures are flat.
