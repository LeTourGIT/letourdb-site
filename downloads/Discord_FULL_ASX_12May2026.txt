📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Tuesday 12 May delivered a softish -0.36% decline on the XJO to
8,670.7 — the 15th fall in 19 sessions and a five-week low print of
8,619 was reached intraday before a partial recovery. The session had
two dominant headwinds: Federal Budget-night anxiety over proposed
negative gearing and capital gains tax reform (which structurally
pressures Financials and Real Estate), and renewed Hormuz geopolitical
pessimism after Trump's Sunday rejection of Iran's peace terms.

The session also carried the lingering tail-risk of tomorrow's CBA Q3
trading update. CBA is the ASX's single largest constituent, and its
guidance will set the tone for the entire banking sector's second half.
The prospect of negative gearing reform overnight created a double
whammy for financials: mortgage book repricing risk AND structural
demand destruction in the residential property market.

Against that backdrop, the outperformers told a clear story: Materials
(XMJ +2.43%) powered by NEM +4.32%, RIO +3.09%, BHP +2.50%, and
Alcoa +3.81% — a global commodity complex rerating on Hormuz supply
anxiety and the Trump-Xi summit's rare earths dimension. Semiconductors
erupted into the #2 Smart Money position (from #51 yesterday) as WBT
+1.44% and AXE +12.07% capitalised on the US NVDA/chip AI frenzy
flow-through. The IT sector's -3.42% sector performance reflects heavy
selling in high-multiple names, contrasting sharply with the discrete
semiconductor theme within it.

The most important laggard was DroneShield (DRO) -14.76% on an ASIC
investigation notice — the probe into market disclosures and insider
trading around a botched contract announcement in November is a
material governance shock. This is a reminder that single-stock risk
in the defence-tech complex remains acute, particularly in names that
have rerated dramatically on sentiment rather than recurring revenue.
