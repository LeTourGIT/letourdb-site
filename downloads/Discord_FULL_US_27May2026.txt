📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Wednesday was a story of two geopolitical shock absorbers — one completing (Micron's
memory supercycle passing $1 trillion) and one potentially beginning (Iran's reported
commitment to restore Hormuz traffic within 30 days of a deal). Those two catalysts
collided to produce the most divergent intraday sectoral action of the week: energy
crashed -1.49% as oil fell through $92, while semiconductors paradoxically sold off
(-1.07% SOXX) as profit-taking followed Micron's +19% session on Tuesday. The Dow
printed a fresh ATH on the strength of industrials and transports absorbing the peace
dividend; the NDX slipped as chip names cooled and cybersecurity (Zscaler -20.78% AH)
added to the post-close anxiety.

The Iran framework is the macro event of the week. Iranian state television reported
Tehran has committed to restoring Hormuz traffic to pre-war levels within one month
of a deal — WTI traded to $88.53 intraday (-5.7%) before the White House denied a
formal MOU existed. Trump restated the key condition: "The strait is going to be open
to everybody — it's international waters, nobody's going to control it." The gap between
what Iran is offering (managed traffic with Oman cooperation, implying tolls) and what
the US will accept (uncontrolled international waters) remains unbridged. WTI settled
at $92.35 after the denial — down 5.41% but off the lows. This is directional risk
reduction, not a resolution. Energy sector capital positioned for a prolonged Hormuz
premium is being gradually unwound; the pace of that unwind is the key variable for
E&P and oil services names through June.

Micron's $1 trillion milestone (26/05 session) is the structural event contextualising
the memory-chip side of the RS list. UBS tripled their price target to $1,625, citing
new long-term memory supply agreements with partially fixed pricing — a structural
shift that converts MU from cyclical commodity chip maker to strategic AI infrastructure
supplier. SNDK (SanDisk) received a Barclays upgrade to Overweight with a near-doubled
PT to $2,300. TSMC is reportedly planning 15% price hikes on 3nm chips in H2 2026.
These three data points form a coherent thesis: the AI memory supercycle is extending
contracts, repricing at the supply level, and pulling the entire semiconductor substrate
complex higher. SOXX's -1.07% session is consolidation within a trend, not reversal.

After-hours the baton passed to software. Snowflake +29% AH on a revenue beat and a
$6 billion five-year AWS Graviton deal signals that enterprise AI workload migration
is accelerating beyond experimentation. Marvell +3% AH on thin headline beats. Salesforce
flat to slightly negative AH — $11.13B revenue beat vs $11.05B expected, but FY27 guidance
(organic growth in high single digits after stripping Informatica) disappointed a market
that had priced in an Agentforce inflection. CRM -33% YTD means it started tonight cheap
enough that flat AH is almost a relief. The divergence between SNOW (platform benefiting
from AI migration) and CRM (legacy SaaS threatened by AI agents) is the sharpest enterprise
software split of the earnings cycle.

SPX breadth: 234 advancers vs 262 decliners on a flat session — structurally negative
but much healthier than 172/326 on 01/05. T2108 at 52.4%, 50MA participation at 54.8%.
The market is not deteriorating internally; it is consolidating after the ATH push.
