📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Tuesday 26 May delivered a modest -0.40% decline on the XJO
(8,657.8), against a session that had no US overnight lead —
Memorial Day kept Wall Street dark — and was instead entirely
driven by the Hormuz oil whipsaw and domestic corporate news.

The macro narrative reversed in real-time. Asian and European
markets opened Monday on peace-deal euphoria after WTI
collapsed -6.9% overnight to $90.30 — the cheapest oil since
the Hormuz closure began in February. By Tuesday's open,
however, US "self-defence strikes" in southern Iran targeting
IRGC mine-laying operations had shattered that optimism. WTI
rebounded back above $92 in Asia trade, Brent gained 2% to
$98.26, and Energy (XEJ -0.88%) gave back its peace-dividend
trade while Oil & Gas E&P names saw volatile intraday swings.
For the RBA's inflation calculus, this oil whipsaw is almost
noise — what matters is the regime, not the daily move. WTI
still sits in the $90s, well above pre-conflict levels.

The session's most consequential piece of domestic news was
ASX Limited (ASX.ax) crashing -11.83% to $51.84. The exchange
operator raised its FY27 capex forecast to $180–200M (from
$160–180M) and flagged higher expense growth, stoking fears
of margin compression. In a high-rate, high-cost environment,
capex surprises in infrastructure companies are punished
severely. This is the exchange-operator itself — the
meta-signal is that tech cost inflation is structural, not
transitory, even for the ASX's own systems.

Goodman Group (GMG) was the session's institutional anchor,
delivering a solid Q3 update: FY26 EPS growth of ≥9% reaffirmed,
data centre pipeline of ~6GW (~10-11 year runway), and targeted
work-in-progress of $18B by June 2026. The 400–500MW per year
data centre production run-rate is the key statistic — this
translates directly into industrial lease demand, construction
contracts, and grid infrastructure requirements. Morgan Stanley
had flagged scope for a guidance upgrade; the lack thereof
(reaffirm at 9% vs consensus 10%) was mildly disappointing.
NRW Holdings (NWH) subsidiary Fredon secured an additional $69M
electrical package for a Victorian data centre project, taking
total site value to ~$155M — another data-centre-linked revenue
thread for the construction sector thesis.

Breadth was modestly negative: XJO 61 advancers vs 128 decliners
(2.1:1 decline dominance). Only 3 stocks rose ≥4%, and 5 fell
≥4%. The advance/decline ratio confirms a soft but not
catastrophic session — more distribution than outright selling.
