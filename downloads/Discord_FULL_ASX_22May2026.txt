📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Friday's session delivered the ASX 200's second consecutive advance as
two macro catalysts aligned: the US–Iran ceasefire report (oil -1.47%
overnight; WTI sub-$97) and Thursday's April unemployment shock (4.5%,
highest since Nov 2021) which has fundamentally repriced the RBA forward
path. XJO gained +0.41% to 8,657.0, a five-session high, with Materials
leading for the second consecutive session.

ELV (Elevra Lithium) +12.36% was the session's most significant
institutional move — a 20day high break driven by GFEX lithium carbonate
futures surging +6.7% to August 2023 levels. This is not isolated
to ELV: the entire lithium complex participated (IGO 20D high +1.54%,
LTR +3.11%, PLS +2.92%). Critical minerals' institutional RS leadership
is consolidating into a durable cluster, with EV demand timelines being
pulled forward by the Hormuz energy shock narrative.

GYG (Guzman y Gomez) +9.57% was the session's consumer story: exiting
the US market (Chicago restaurants), upgrading FY26 Australian guidance,
and lifting the projected dividend. In a 4.35% rate environment, capital
discipline — walking away from a loss-making expansion — is exactly what
the market rewards. Short interest at 13.82% amplified the move as
shorts were caught onside by both the announcement and the broader
market bid.

Communications (XTJ -1.86%) was the weakest sector — domestic telcos
hit simultaneously by consumer spending headwinds under elevated rates
and their own duration characteristics. Real Estate (XRE -0.93%) and
Utilities (XUJ -1.09%) also underperformed — but critically, these
sectors are the primary beneficiaries of the emerging rate pivot thesis.
Watch for structural re-rating in these three sectors as the Hormuz
peace deal and unemployment data reshape RBA expectations.
