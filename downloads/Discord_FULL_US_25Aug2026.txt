📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Tuesday delivered a clean sweep of green index closes — SPX +0.32%, NDX +0.64%, Dow
+0.30%, Russell +0.50% — but the session's real information sat beneath the tape, in
two macro dislocations pulling capital in opposite directions: a collapsing crude
complex and a surging nuclear-power/shipping bid.

The geopolitical driver was de-escalation. With Iran halting strikes on Israel, the
war premium bled out of oil violently: WTI -5.02% to $81.14 and Brent -7.00% to
$86.00. Yet the equity energy complex barely flinched — XLE fell just -1.66% and XOP
-1.88%, with OIH down a mere -0.74% and ZERO energy ETFs breaching the -3% flag line.
That gap — a 5-7% crude crash versus a ~1.7% energy-equity decline — is the day's
cleanest divergence. The market is repricing the geopolitical premium out of the
commodity while treating producer earnings power as intact. Crude fell on peace, not
on demand; equity energy is priced for normalisation, not recession.

But de-escalation on missiles is NOT de-escalation on logistics. Even as crude fell,
Hormuz shipping risk and a Bessent sanctions push kept global freight rates elevated —
and that shows up unmistakably in Smart Money. The three highest-ranked institutional
industries are Passenger Transportation Ground & Sea (rank 1), Marine Port Services
(rank 2) and Marine Freight & Logistics (rank 10), with Marine Freight climbing from
rank 49 nine days ago. Capital is paying for the shipping-rate spike while selling the
crude — a coherent trade only if you separate the oil price (down on peace) from
shipping capacity (tight on sanctions/seizure risk). This is the session's subtlest
gold nugget: the Iran story bifurcated into a bearish-crude / bullish-freight pair.

The dominant equity theme was NUCLEAR POWER FOR AI. URA +5.36% (an EXTREME >5% move)
and URNM +4.10% were the ONLY two ETFs above +3% on the entire board. This was
corroborated at every layer: OKLO +11.54% and BE +6.58% (Bloom Energy) led the R1000
gainers; Uranium surged in Smart Money from rank 44 to rank 16; and after-hours,
Standard Nuclear (+16.75%) and Critical Metals (+21.44%) extended the bid. The chain
is macro → sector → stock in textbook form: hyperscaler power demand + NVDA-eve
positioning → nuclear/SMR + uranium fuel-cycle → OKLO/BE/URA/URNM. With software
stumbling, capital rotated toward the physical-infrastructure layer of the AI build.

Under the surface the semiconductor complex staged a comeback (AMD +4.91%, MRVL
+4.84%, SMCI +9.35%, CDNS +5.06%, LITE +6.67%, AAOI +5.13%) while software was sold
(PANW, CRWD, WDAY, ZM, TEAM all lower; INTU crushed -8.80% AH). That is a clean
semis-over-software rotation — the market pre-positioning into hardware ahead of NVDA
and away from high-multiple SaaS after Intuit's guide. Breadth, meanwhile, was quietly
negative: SPX advancers 205 vs decliners 293 on a green index day — cap-weight
distortion again, with a narrow leadership cohort lifting the tape while the median
stock fell. T2108 at 56.4% and 58.2% of SPX names above their 50MA keep the structural
floor intact, but the green close overstates participation.
