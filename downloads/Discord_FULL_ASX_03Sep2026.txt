📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Thursday 3 September saw the ASX 200 add +0.46% to 9,020.1, with all four indices clustered in a
+0.41% to +0.46% band. On the surface a broad green day; underneath, a narrow one. Advancers beat
decliners 125/66 on the XJO, but new 20day LOWS (21) still outnumbered new 20day HIGHS (6), only
37% of XJO names are above their 20MA, and T2108 sits at 45%. Large-caps did the lifting; the
median stock did not. That is the single most important internal in today's tape.

Financials (XFJ +0.86%) led — the fingerprint of the day's macro catalyst. Australia's Q2 GDP
printed stronger than expected, and with July trimmed-mean CPI stuck at 3.6% the market has
nudged the 29 September RBA hike probability back to ~54%. Higher-for-longer helps bank net
interest margins, so banks and insurers were bid; Property & Casualty Insurance is simultaneously
SURGING in Smart Money (+38 ranks). The mirror image: Info Tech (XIJ −0.78%) was the worst sector
and Energy (XEJ −1.24%) the outright laggard as WTI eased overnight.

The day's dominant single-stock story was mechanical, not fundamental. Corporate Travel Management
(CTD −85.56% to A$2.32) resumed trading after a year-long suspension tied to a UK accounting and
customer-overcharging scandal (>A$250m). Against its last pre-suspension price near A$16, the open
was a brutal repricing — but it is a relisting event and says nothing about broad market health.
Regis Healthcare (REG −26.99%) is the more meaningful red flag: the government's AN-ACC aged-care
pricing announcement (lodged 09:12, price-sensitive) is a genuine sector funding shock.

Beneath the sector print, the real rotation is thematic. Gold miners own the RS leaderboard —
VAU, GMD, RSG, SBM, ALK, RRL, PRU are 7 of the top institutional names — while lithium (LTR
−4.03%, ELV −5.40%) is being sold as spodumene softens. Uranium provided the day's cyclical spice
(DYL +8.44%, BMN +7.79%). Smart Money confirms a reflation tilt: Industrial Machinery, Homebuilding,
Steel, Ground Freight and Auto Parts are all climbing the rankings — a domestic-cyclical bid that
squares neatly with a hotter GDP number.
