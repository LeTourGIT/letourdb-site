📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Monday 25 May delivered a third consecutive session of gains for the ASX,
with XJO adding +0.40% to 8,692 — a quiet, conviction-lite advance on a day
US markets were shuttered for Memorial Day. The driver was not domestic: it
was geopolitical. Trump's Saturday social media post declaring the US-Iran
deal "largely negotiated" sent US equity futures surging in overnight trade,
with the ASX following suit at the open. The Hormuz peace trade is now the
dominant cross-market variable, with oil's behaviour the transmission mechanism
directly to the RBA and Australian inflation trajectory.

Coal was the standout sector narrative of the session. WHC surged +8.70%,
NHC +4.73%, and YAL +7.48% — an extraordinary triple-header for ASX thermal
coal in a single session. This is not momentum chasing; it is a fundamental
re-pricing. The Hormuz crisis drove oil above $130 in March before easing,
pulling thermal coal higher in sympathy as LNG substitution compressed
alternative energy supply. A Hormuz reopening scenario introduces a structural
paradox for coal: lower oil removes the LNG substitution premium that has
propped coal prices, but markets are simultaneously pricing in Hormuz-resolution
as a risk-off release. Coal was positioned as a "Hormuz trade" on the way up,
and is now surging again on peace speculation — because if the deal collapses
(as Trump cautioned, "won't rush"), coal holds its premium. Smart capital is
playing the optionality of the outcome, not a single direction.

MIN (Mineral Resources) +2.68% and SRL (Sunrise Energy) +2.93% reflected
the ongoing institutional bid for critical minerals and integrated mining. The
materials sector outperformance (XMJ +1.84%) was broad and weight-confirmed.
BHP and RIO contributed but it was the mid-tier names carrying the velocity.

The critical macro event overhanging the week is Wednesday's April CPI (due
27/05). March CPI at 4.6% was the shock that prompted the RBA's May hike to
4.35%. April CPI consensus is ~4.2-4.5%, with risks on both sides: oil spiked
in March/April (Hormuz effect) so energy transport costs will be elevated; but
base effects from 2025 begin to turn more favourable. A surprise print above
4.6% would reignite June hike pricing. A print closer to 4.0% gives the RBA
cover to hold at 4.35% through year-end as the CBA now projects.
