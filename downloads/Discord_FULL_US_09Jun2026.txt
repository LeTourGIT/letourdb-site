📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Tuesday opened with tentative optimism: chip stocks staged a modest rebound from Friday's rout,
Trump restated that an Iran deal was "two to three days away," and the National Association of
Realtors reported May existing home sales ahead of forecasts, lifting homebuilders. What looked
like a continuation of Monday's stabilisation unravelled at midday. Trump announced the Iranian
military had shot down a US Apache helicopter patrolling the Strait of Hormuz and declared the
US "must respond." The WWDC catalyst layered on top: AAPL -3.64% as Apple's Siri AI launch
disappointed — the new "AFM Cloud Pro" model runs on Nvidia GPUs in Google's cloud, but the
AI features were not integrated into Siri itself. The classic WWDC sell-the-news dynamic, but
this time with a harder edge given AAPL's elevated post-ATH valuation.

The optics/photonics complex absorbed its heaviest single-day drawdown of the month: COHR
-11.44%, LITE -8.22%, GLW -7.25%, AAOI -17.17%. This is correlated basket selling, not
fundamental deterioration. AAOI guided Q2 2026 revenue to $180-198M with a sequential ramp
in Q3; Coherent's datacenter segment grew 41% YoY in its most recent quarter. These moves
are priced-for-perfection mean-reversion in names up 87-900%+ YTD, not thesis breaks. The
tell: Corning (GLW) and COHR declining in tandem without company-specific news confirms the
profit-taking thesis over fundamental deterioration.

The defensive rotation is real and deliberate. Nine of 11 SPDR sectors closed green. Healthcare
names dominated the top of the gainers list: TMO +5.20%, ZTS +4.29%, SYK +4.14%, HCA +3.76%.
Managed Healthcare (HUM +2.02%, UNH +1.58%, ELV +1.50%) consolidating at the top of the
Smart Money rotation. The homebuilder surge (ITB +4.02%, XHB +3.61%) was catalysed directly
by the existing home sales beat and connects to the rate-cut optionality thesis: Kevin Warsh's
Fed signalling potential data flexibility gave housing bulls a reason to reload. Airlines
(UAL +4.09%, DAL +3.78%, AAL +3.60%) surged simultaneously — a coherent old-economy bid
as institutional capital exited the AI infrastructure trade.

The Iran dynamic remains the overriding macro overlay. WTI fell to $88.66 on early peace
signals, then reversed toward $91 intraday on the helicopter news before settling -1.63%.
The gap between WTI at $88-89 and Brent at $91.80 (vs WTI above $100 in May) confirms
meaningful Hormuz premium has already been unwound. Every further escalation/de-escalation
news cycle will produce these intraday swings; CPI tomorrow morning is the next scheduled
macro tripwire with Fed rate-hike odds at 43.3% vs 13.6% a month ago.
