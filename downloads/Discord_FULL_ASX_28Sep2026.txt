→ A hike is fully priced — the tradeable variable is the statement. A ‘hawkish hike’ (explicit November bias) validates money-market pricing of two more moves and extends pressure on REITs, discretionary and small caps; a ‘one-and-done’ tone would be the first dovish surprise of 2026 and is the only realistic trigger for a relief rally in the rate-sensitive complex. Today’s tape shows positioning: banks and insurers (NIM and float-yield beneficiaries) were bought, small caps were sold. Resources and energy with USD revenues remain structurally insulated, but today proved insulation from rates is not insulation from commodity prices.

📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
The ASX 200 added +0.17% to 8,679.7, snapping a two-session losing streak off Friday’s lowest close since 12 June — but the headline hides the session. Advancers lost to decliners 85/111, the Small Ords fell -1.30% and the ASX 100 outperformed (+0.30%). This was a top-heavy, defensive bid into Tuesday’s RBA decision: a narrow group of large caps held the index up while the broad market kept liquidating.

The bid had a clear logic. Financials (XFJ +1.16%) were bought as hike beneficiaries — MQG +2.28%, NAB +1.50%, and insurers SUN +2.69% / IAG +1.79%, whose float earns more as yields climb. Healthcare (XHJ +1.49%) led on CSL +2.80% and COH +1.99% (fresh 20day high): USD earners with no mortgage-book exposure. Utilities (+1.17%) and Staples (+0.83%) completed a textbook pre-hike rotation — 6 of 11 sectors green, and every one of the top four is a defensive or a rate beneficiary.

Materials (XMJ -1.34%) was the drag. Spot gold slid ~2% to its lowest since 5 August (10% off the 25 August peak), copper extended its pullback from last Tuesday’s record, and CISA urged Chinese mills to curb output just as August industrial profits slowed to 4.2%. BHP -1.52%, RIO -1.10%, FMG -0.67%, and the gold complex bled (OBM -6.15%, MEK -7.00%, NMG -12.66%). The lone exception was Northern Star +6.15% to $23.47 after rejecting Gold Fields’ A$38.7bn proposal — a stock-specific bid that masked how weak the rest of the sector was.

Two stress signals deserve more attention than the index print. First, three Metrics private-credit trusts (~A$3bn) were suspended after KPMG’s audit forced NTA cuts of up to 12% — the retail yield trade of this rate cycle is being marked down on the eve of a hike (PNI, ~35% owner of Metrics’ holdco, traded as low as -6.9% before closing -1.97%). Second, Karoon -12.89% on a ~10% production guidance cut is the kind of idiosyncratic blow-up that punishes crowded small-cap energy longs.

Structurally nothing is repaired. All four indices print ‘Bearish’, XJO sits 3.29% below its 50MA with RSI-15 at 33.4, only 36.5% of the ASX 200 is above its 200MA and T2108 is 23% — deeply washed out. New 20day lows (49) outnumbered highs (9) more than 5:1, and XAO lows expanded to 122 even on an up day. Oversold conditions set up a bounce, but the trigger is the RBA statement at 14:30 tomorrow and CPI on Wednesday — not today’s tape.
