📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Thursday was a violent one-day relief rally stacked on a still-broken trend. Two
catalysts did the heavy lifting: Microsoft's cloud/AI earnings blowout (+15.5%, a
$3.4T name) and a snap-back in the semiconductor complex that had been the epicentre
of the recent drawdown. SOXX +8.50% and XSD +7.48% were the day's standout ETFs, yet
both remain deeply negative on 5day (-8.5%/-7.7%) and 20day (-15.9%/-19.0%) — you are
watching an oversold bounce in real time, not a base breakout.

The mechanics matter. NDX ripped +3.36% while its own advance/decline split was a
coin-flip 51/50, and SPX rose +1.66% on NEGATIVE breadth (197 advancers vs 301
decliners). That is a cap-weight melt-up: MSFT plus the mega-cap semis (MU +18.4%,
LRCX +18.0%, SNDK +26.0%, ALAB +20.0%) mathematically carried the index while the
median stock went nowhere or fell. META -7.95% proves the leadership is selective even
inside big tech — the bid is for AI-infrastructure earnings, not the whole complex.

The most important tell is the VIX: down -17.2% from 20.63 to 17.09. Volatility above
20 into this session means the prior tape was genuinely fearful — an Iran escalation
scare and a contentious Fed hold had lifted the fear premium. Thursday was the unwind
of that premium as the US-Iran conflict paused for peace talks and WTI sat calm at
$83.89 (+0.29%). Falling oil + falling VIX + rising mega-cap tech is the textbook
relief-rally signature. The question for next week is whether real money follows.

Underneath the tech headline, a genuine hard-asset rotation broadened. Gold +1.11%,
Silver +2.50%, and the miners (GDX +4.36%, SIL +4.46%, XME +4.39%, COPX +4.41%) all
bid, and Smart Money confirms it: Gold jumped to rank #9 (from #133 nine days ago),
Precious Metals to #10 (from #138), Integrated Mining to #14, Uranium to #15. Alongside
Renewable Energy surging to #2 and FinTech/crypto to #1 (IREN +30.5% led all gainers),
the capital-flow story is a rotation into real assets and energy transition — a
different, and arguably more durable, signal than the one-day semiconductor squeeze.
