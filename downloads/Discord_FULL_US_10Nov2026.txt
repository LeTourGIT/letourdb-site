📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Tuesday ran on two shocks that pulled in opposite directions for the index and the same
direction for leadership. October CPI came in a tenth hot on both headline (+0.4%) and core
(+0.3%), the third straight 0.3% core print. With Waller having said overnight that a 0.3%
core puts 50bp on the table, the 2Y jumped 18bp to 4.75% and the 10Y briefly traded above 5%.

The second shock was NVDA. The Huang / suspected Chinese intelligence story added a
governance discount and a fresh China-export policy risk (Congress may tighten China sales).
NVDA fell -5.47% on $72.5B of turnover — by far the largest dollar flow in the market —
and is now -18.29% over 5day. XLK -1.15% (20day -13.94%) did the index damage on its own.

Underneath, the market was close to flat. SPX Adv/Dec was 246/248 and RSP (equal weight)
finished -0.03% while SPY lost -0.48%. More striking: 69.0% of S&P 500 stocks sit above
their 50MA and T2108 is 71.0%, yet the cap-weighted SPX is -2.02% BELOW its 50MA and in a
Bearish regime — a reverse cap-weight divergence. Over 20day RPV (value) is +3.71% and IWF
(growth) -10.31%: a 14-point leadership swap in four weeks.

Where the money went: insurers (IAK/KIE +1.51%) and healthcare providers (IHF +1.41%) led
the ETF board. Higher short rates lift insurers' float income, and managed care is the market's
preferred defensive growth. Smart Money confirms it — Reinsurance SURGED from rank 88 to 16
in three days, and the 10day leadership is travel and consumer (Airlines rank 1, Hotels &
Cruise rank 6, Airport Operators rank 8). Lower oil is a fuel-cost tailwind for travel.

Where it left: every inflation and war hedge. GDX and SIL -5.43% (⚠ EXTREME), gold -2.02%.
Gold falling on a hot CPI reads as real yields up plus the war premium fading. TAN, ITB and
ETHA (all -3%+) were sold for the same discount-rate reason.

After the bell CoreWeave beat and grew its backlog to ~$132.5B (+7.90% AH), which says AI
demand is intact. The setup for Wednesday is whether NVDA can separate a governance problem
from a demand story that CRWV just reconfirmed.
