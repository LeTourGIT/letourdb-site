📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Wednesday delivered a fifth straight SPX loss and a second consecutive
close below the 200-day MA (-0.31%), but the internals improved sharply
beneath a mixed tape. Three separate forces collided: a second day of
regulatory panic in managed care, a hawkish set of Fed minutes, and a
powerful idiosyncratic bid in cybersecurity — all into the evening
pricing of Anthropic's record IPO.

Healthcare was the epicentre. CMS's 16 November proposal to codify and
widen Medicare Advantage audits — with payment clawbacks reaching back to
2018 — turned from a one-day shock into a liability repricing. UNH
-10.21% and HUM -12.98% took two-day losses to roughly 14% each; ELV
-7.63%, CVS -5.20% and HCA -4.96% show the selling spreading from payors
into providers. IHF -5.56% (EXTREME) and XLV -2.20% were the day's worst
sector prints, and Smart Money registers Managed Healthcare as an EXIT
(-66 ranks to rank 71). This is a retroactive-liability story: until
insurers can size the clawback, analysts can't model 2027 EPS, and that
uncertainty — not the earnings run-rate — is what's being sold.

The Fed minutes added a macro layer. Nearly all officials see further
hikes as possibly necessary and explicitly link AI-capex demand to
inflation. Precious and industrial metals took the hit hardest: gold
-3.22%, silver SLV -4.55%, copper -3.60%, and the miners amplified it —
COPX -6.31%, GDX -5.97%, SIL -5.89%, FCX -6.96%, NEM -6.05%. Gold's
Smart Money rank collapsed 97 places in three days to rank 115 — the
hedge trade is being unwound as real-rate expectations reprice higher.

The bid came from cyber. DHS warnings on AI-powered attacks and a CISA
emergency patch order for "Java4Shell" drove CIBR +4.72% — the ONLY ETF
above +3% — with OKTA +12.07%, PANW +9.90%, AKAM +8.34%, CRWD +7.95% and
FTNT +5.87%. PANW then beat and raised after the bell (+9.08% AH).
Retail earnings added a second pocket of strength: TJX +8.05%, TGT
+4.01%, XRT +2.28%, making Consumer Discretionary (XLY +1.79%) the top
SPDR sector.

Breadth was the quiet positive. SPX Adv/Dec improved to 235/264 from
75/424 on Tuesday, and the Russell 2000 printed a positive 984/926 —
its first green breadth day this week. But structure remains damaged:
SPX new 20-day lows outnumber highs 63 to 11, only 40.4% of SPX members
sit above their 20MA, and RUT has 430 new lows vs 75 highs. Today was
selling exhaustion in the broad tape, not accumulation.
