📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Friday delivered the week's most structurally significant catalyst: Apple and
Intel have reached a preliminary agreement for Intel to manufacture some chips
powering Apple devices, per the Wall Street Journal. This is not a product
launch — it is a geopolitical and industrial strategy event. The US government,
now Intel's largest shareholder after the CHIPS Act deal with CEO Lip-Bu Tan,
played a direct role. President Trump personally lobbied Tim Cook at the White
House. The deal simultaneously validates Intel's foundry revival, diversifies
Apple's TSMC dependence, and anchors advanced semiconductor manufacturing on
US soil. INTC +13.96%, AAPL +2.05%.

The companion macro catalyst was April NFP: 115K jobs added vs a 62K estimate,
nearly double consensus, and the second consecutive positive monthly print —
a combination unseen in nearly a year. Unemployment held at 4.3%. The strong
beat reinforces hawkish Fed rhetoric from FOMC members, but markets read it as
"growth justifies premium" given the Hormuz war backdrop. Computer Hardware's
Smart Money ROC shot to #1 at +15.32% — the direct Apple/Intel beneficiary
chain — while Semiconductors dropped from #1 to #3, signalling capital moving
UP the value stack from fabrication toward design and hardware integration.

The semiconductor complex dominated the session completely. XSD +5.80% and
SOXX +5.67% are the ONLY ±3% flag ETFs. AMD +11.44% (Rackspace AI cloud MOU
for regulated industries — the natural next TAM after hyperscalers), MU +15.49%,
MXL +21.20% (RS #1), INTC +13.96%. RXT +55.97% was the session's most explosive
single move. The Apple/Intel deal is a direct order pipeline event for Intel's
18A process node, using the same ASML EUV equipment stack that activates the
entire equipment and testing supply chain.

The Iran situation deteriorated: US forces fired on and disabled two more
Iran-flagged tankers attempting to breach the naval blockade. The May 1 peace
proposal has faded. WTI rebounded to $98.87 (+0.50%), but energy equities
(XLE -0.45%) failed to participate — the market is pricing a structural
resolution scenario rather than an escalation premium. Goldman's 4–6 month
Hormuz normalisation timeline is back in full effect.

SPX breadth at the ATH is deeply concerning. 205 advancers vs 294 decliners.
Only 41.88% of SPX components above their 20MA. RSP +0.28% vs SPX +0.84% =
a 56bps gap quantifying cap-weight distortion. The NDX RSI-15 at 81.63 is
entering territory that has preceded consolidations in every prior cycle. The
index performance is exhilarating; the underlying stock-level setup is fragile.
