📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Wednesday was the cleanest sector rotation of the cycle. Three catalysts fired at
once — a historic biotech readout, a Treasury intervention in the bond market, and
a crypto regulatory proposal — and every one of them pulled capital in the same
direction: OUT of the crowded AI-semiconductor trade and INTO healthcare, hard
assets and digital assets. The result was a market where the index level barely
moved but the internals were violently reallocated.

Catalyst one — and the day's defining event — was Moderna. MRNA closed +176.97%,
very nearly tripling, after Merck and Moderna announced that intismeran autogene
(mRNA-4157/V940) combined with Keytruda succeeded in the Phase 3 INTerpath-001
trial in resected high-risk melanoma. This is the first-ever positive late-stage
result for an mRNA / individualised neoantigen cancer vaccine — a category that was
science fiction a decade ago. The prior Phase 2b readout had cut recurrence-or-death
risk 49% and distant-metastasis-or-death risk 59% versus Keytruda alone; the Phase 3
confirmation validates the entire personalised-vaccine platform. Moderna added
roughly 40 billion dollars in market cap in a session (from a ~25 billion base),
Merck rose +12.60%, and the Nasdaq Biotechnology Index printed a record. This is a
platform-validation event, not a one-drug pop — which is why it lit the whole complex.

Catalyst two was the macro engine under everything else: the Treasury. On the same
day US public debt crossed 40 trillion dollars for the first time, Secretary Bessent
announced Treasury would "at least double" its long-end buybacks (10/20/30yr),
lifting individual operations from 2bn to at least 4bn and doubling the number of
quarterly operations, effective 9 September through 4 November. The 30-year yield —
which had hit a 19-year high earlier in the week — fell from 5.26% to as low as
5.18%; the 10-year eased from 4.68% to 4.63%. Suppressed long yields plus a softer
dollar is rocket fuel for gold, silver and rate-sensitive equity. It is also a
direct complication for Kevin Warsh's Fed, which prefers market-set rates and whose
July minutes flagged a willingness to HIKE if inflation does not cool. That
policy tension lands next week at Jackson Hole, where Warsh delivers the keynote.

Catalyst three was crypto. The SEC's proposed "Regulation Crypto Assets" framework
(two capital-raising exemptions plus a safe harbour that lets mature networks exit
securities classification) combined with the Treasury-driven risk-on to send Bitcoin
back toward 68-69k and Ether up double digits. IBIT +5.96% and ETHA +9.97% led the
ETF tape; MSTR +12.68%, BMNR +10.72%, COIN +9.55% and CRCL +9.56% led the equities.
The honest read: these names are still down 35-39% year-to-date. This is a short-
covering rebound off summer lows riding a regulatory tailwind — a sentiment reset,
not yet a confirmed trend change. Trump also hosted crypto executives at the White
House during the session, adding a policy-momentum narrative on top.

The funding source for all of it was the AI-semiconductor / tech-hardware complex.
XLK -1.07% was the worst SPDR sector and NDX the only red index. The optical,
networking and semi-equipment names that led the tape all spring — AAOI -7.02%,
LITE -5.23%, COHR -6.19%, LRCX -6.33%, TER -6.09% — were among the worst losers,
alongside hardware (WDC -6.87%, DELL -6.64%, NTAP -5.00%) and even steel (STLD
-7.53%, NUE -5.85%). Crucially, breadth was POSITIVE on every index (SPX 283/216,
RUT 1106/812) with VIX collapsing -6% to 14.89. This is the signature of healthy
rotation — money leaving one crowded factor and finding a home elsewhere — rather
than de-risking. The one name bucking the tech rout was MRVL +9.85%, which reportedly
won custom-silicon share at Google from Broadcom: idiosyncratic, not a sector reprieve.
