📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
The Fed delivered the session — and the market did not take it well. Chair
Kevin Warsh's committee approved the FIRST rate hike since 2023 and signaled
one more increase before year-end, framing it against still-stubborn inflation.
The Dow bore the brunt, dropping ~600 points (-1.21%) as Warsh's hawkish
inflation commentary repriced the front end. SPX -0.45% and RUT -0.40% followed;
NDX (+0.02%) escaped only via a fading chip bounce. This was a textbook
rate-shock tape: long-duration and rate-sensitive cohorts (small caps, REITs,
banks on curve/credit fear) leading lower, defensives (healthcare) the only
green sector.

Beneath a modest -0.45% SPX headline sits a genuine breadth washout that is the
real story. Advancers 159 vs decliners 339 (0.47:1). New 20day lows exploded to
149 against just 17 new highs — a 8.8:1 skew. T2108 collapsed to 25.6% (from the
high-50s that defined the summer), only 18.4% of the SPX holds above its 20day MA
and 30.6% above the 50day. This is the internal damage of a multi-week distribution
finally showing up in the averages: the index is being held aloft by its largest
weights while the median stock is already in a correction. When T2108 leaves the
50-60% band and prints mid-20s, the tape has shifted from 'buy-the-dip' to
'sell-the-rip' until proven otherwise.

Sector leadership confirms the rate-shock read. Energy XLE -2.88% was the worst
SPDR, but the damage was concentrated in exploration & production: XOP -3.96% and
IEO -3.54% were the ONLY two ETFs to breach -3%, and the E&P losers table is a
bloodbath — FANG -8.03%, OXY -6.55%, COP -6.15%, EOG -5.73%, DVN -5.63%. Crucially
this is NOT a demand-collapse signal: WTI only fell -2.62% to $102.04, pulling back
from FOUR-MONTH HIGHS as the market moves 'from panic to pricing in' the Iran/Hormuz
premium (Saudi pipeline damage, a downed US drone over Hormuz). E&P equities are
giving back an outsized share of a crowded, over-owned rally — profit-taking layered
on top of a rate hit, not a thesis break. Financials XLF -1.62% (HBAN -5.55%,
KEY -4.52%, HBAN/KEY regional banks) is the more worrying tell: banks falling INTO
a hike flags curve-flattening and credit-cost anxiety, not rate-margin optimism.

Where is the money going? Two directions. First, DEFENSE via healthcare — XLV
+0.07% was the lone positive defensive sector and the RS Spotlight has been
overrun by biotech/pharma: 10 of the top 20 RS names are Biotechnology & Medical
Research, Pharmaceuticals or Medical Equipment (TEVA, CYPH, MRNA, IOVA, CDNA, ARCT,
TWST, WGS, PSNL, REPL). This is capital seeking earnings that don't depend on the
rate cycle. Second, POWER/ELECTRIFICATION — Heavy Electrical Equipment rocketed to
Smart Money rank 2 (from rank 99 nine days ago) and produced two of the day's top
gainers, FPS +11.10% and BE +4.11%, alongside GEV +4.79%. The AI-datacenter power
build-out is the one cyclical theme still attracting institutional flow into a
hawkish Fed. Semis were a mixed two-way tape — SMTC +11.03%, CRDO +7.38%, COHR
+6.92%, ALAB +6.59%, INTC +4.03% up hard, but ON -9.02% and SWKS -4.90% down hard —
dispersion, not a clean bid.

Bottom line: a hawkish policy pivot has cracked the regime beneath a still-orderly
index. The zero ETFs above +3% and only two below -3% say the selling was contained,
not a liquidation — but 149 new lows, RUT Bearish and DJI Neutral Bear say the trend
has turned. Watch T2108 for a reflex bounce off the mid-20s; watch whether banks
stabilize; and respect that 'one more hike this year' plus a brewing Trump-Warsh
independence fight is a headwind, not a tailwind, for multiple expansion.
