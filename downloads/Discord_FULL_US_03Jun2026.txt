📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Wednesday's session erased Tuesday's ATH-adjacent optimism in a single morning
shock: Iran launched ballistic missiles at Kuwait's international airport and
Bahrain, killing at least one at the airport terminal, and threatening to expand
targeting to the Bab Al-Mandeb Strait. The market had been riding back-to-back
record closes; the geopolitical flare-up hit risk assets immediately, driving
WTI back above $95 (+1.81%), Treasury yields higher on inflation expectations,
and sector rotation hard into Energy (+1.29%) and defensive Healthcare (+0.79%)
while hammering Technology (-1.00%), Financials (-1.15%), and Communications
(-1.31%). CENTCOM confirmed most missiles failed to reach targets — missiles
aimed at Bahrain were intercepted, two aimed at Kuwait fell short — but the
market wasn't waiting for a damage assessment. The attack reinforced that the
Iran ceasefire path is not linear, and the May 1 peace proposal optimism is now
directly contested by renewed hostilities.

The breadth story is where the session becomes structurally alarming. The RUT
breadth print is a regime-level signal: 422 advancers vs 1,451 decliners, with
200 individual stocks dropping 4% or more in a single session. That is not
index-level noise — that is systematic small-cap liquidation. SPX breadth
deteriorated materially too: 200 advancers vs 297 decliners with new 20day
lows (62) exceeding new 20day highs (53). The SPX 50MA participation
has now dropped to 49.4%, crossing below the neutral 50% threshold. T2108 at
45.6% is in bearish territory. Three consecutive deteriorating breadth readings
are flashing a condition where the bull market's internal scaffolding is being
quietly removed, even as index levels remain technically above key MAs.

After the close, the megacap semiconductor earnings wave arrived. Broadcom
(AVGO) delivered Q2 FY2026 revenue of $22.19B (+48% YoY) — a technical beat
on EPS ($2.44 vs $2.40 est) but a slight miss on top-line revenue vs the
whisper numbers — with Q3 guidance of $29.4B well above the $28.53B consensus.
AI revenue was the catalyst for the forward outlook. Yet the stock fell 6.5%
after hours: when 48% growth is priced in and the street needs a blowout, in-line
is disappointing. CrowdStrike beat Q1 FY2027 across all metrics — EPS $1.10 vs
$0.88 est (25% beat), record net new ARR of $255.8M, and a stunning 4-for-1
stock split — but also fell 7% AH, the victim of a 98% pre-earnings run that
left no margin for anything less than spectacular. The split itself is a
confidence signal; the reaction is purely a positioning flush. Veeva beat and
raised guidance to $9.05 FY27 EPS but fell 2.6% AH, with the life sciences
software franchise demonstrating AI integration is proceeding but investors
remain cautious on valuation compression risk.

The intelligent read here is that Wednesday's AH earnings reaction is NOT a
fundamental negative. AVGO guided $29.4B for Q3, CRWD announced a split, VEEV
raised. The negative AH price action reflects elevated expectations, not
deteriorating fundamentals. The real macro risk Thursday is whether the Iran
missile strikes catalyse a broader geopolitical feedback loop into Friday NFP.
