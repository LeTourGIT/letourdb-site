📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Thursday delivered the session's defining catalyst before the open: Cisco's
blowout fiscal Q3 report (revenue $15.84B vs $15.56B est; EPS $1.06 vs $1.04;
Q4 guide $16.7–16.9B, over $1B above consensus). The networking giant surged
+13.41% to $115.53, triggering a 189% volume day vs the three-month average and
catalysing broad re-rating across the AI infrastructure complex. CSCO is now the
third consecutive session where a non-NVDA name has provided the AI thesis's
primary validation — the investment universe is broadening.

The macro backdrop came from two simultaneous signals. First, the Trump-Xi summit
in Beijing produced a joint statement agreeing that the Strait of Hormuz "must
remain open" and that Iran cannot have nuclear weapons. Xi pledged China would not
supply military equipment to Tehran, expressed interest in purchasing US oil, and
Trump invited Xi to the White House in September. Crucially, the statement added
no concrete resolution mechanism — it aligned with China's prior stated position —
and the market correctly read this as directional but not definitive. WTI held above
$100, largely unmoved. Second, April retail sales (+0.5% MoM) confirmed consumer
resilience despite the Iran-driven energy price spike, though they provided no relief
from the inflationary signal embedded in Tuesday's CPI (3.8% YoY, highest since
May 2023) and Wednesday's PPI (+1.4% MoM, the largest gain since March 2022).

The data mosaic is unambiguous: the economy is running hot, inflation is re-accelerating
driven by Hormuz disruption feeding into transport and goods costs, and the Fed has no
cut cycle in sight. Kevin Warsh is expected to be confirmed as Fed Chair (Powell's term
ends Friday 15 May), and markets are not pricing accommodation from him. This inflation-
resilient rally is being carried by earnings power and Hormuz resolution optionality, not
by rate cut expectations — an important structural distinction from prior bull phases.

IYZ (Telecom ETF) led all ETFs at +3.68%, driven by network infrastructure re-rating
as AI demand for bandwidth and compute interconnects accelerates. CIBR (cybersecurity)
+3.31% confirms the security layer of AI infrastructure continues accumulating. CSCO
cements its transformation from legacy networking to AI infrastructure backbone.
Boeing -3.8% was the session's defining laggard — Trump's China trip generated ~100
aircraft orders vs analyst estimates of 500, a massive shortfall that the market punished.
