Thursday 7 May produced the ASX's best session in weeks — XJO +0.96% to
8,878.1, XAO +1.01% to 9,107. The catalyst was not a single event but a
confluence: the Axios report of a 14-point US-Iran peace MOU framework
sent Brent down 7.5% to ~$101 overnight, a sharp reversal of the
primary macro headwind that has dominated Australian markets since
February. When oil falls that sharply — and on a credible peace catalyst
rather than demand destruction — the RBA's forward guidance flips. Three
consecutive hikes at 4.35% look much more like a peak when the inflation
impulse from energy begins unwinding.

Materials (XMJ +3.67%) was the overwhelming session leader, with the
broadest and most institutionally weighted rally since the Iran conflict
began. This was not speculative noise — PMT (+10.7%), ELV (+7.6%), IGO
(+6.6%), LTR (+6.3%), and PLS (+3.3%) all participated in a GFEX lithium
carbonate surge of 6.7% to a three-year high. Separately, copper's return
to US$6.12/lb delivered BHP (+~3.2%) its sixth consecutive session of
institutional accumulation. The XMJ 5day return of +7.53% is now the
strongest sector 5day run of 2026 — the kind of momentum that typically
attracts further institutional positioning in the following sessions.

The RBA's 8-1 vote to hike to 4.35% was delivered Tuesday (5 May) but
its market implications are crystallising on Thursday: the dovish post-
decision tone from Governor Bullock, combined with oil's sharp reversal,
has shifted the dominant rate narrative from "one more hike after this"
to "this might be the peak." That shift is visible in today's sector
ordering — Financials (XFJ +0.37%) participated for the first time in
multiple sessions. When banks go from being the only red sector (as on
1/5) to participating on the green side, it signals the market is
genuinely repricing the rate trajectory. NIM tailwinds remain; the bad
debt stress from three hikes is manageable if rates pause here.

XSO +1.53% outperforming XJO +0.96% is the most technically significant
signal of the session. Small-cap outperformance during a risk-on day is
the market's most granular expression of genuine appetite for the rally
— institutions don't size into small-caps if they expect the move to
reverse quickly. The breadth confirms it: XJO Adv/Dec 136:55 (2.5:1)
with 28 stocks +4% versus only 8 stocks -4%. That is clean upside skew.

The structural caution remains: XJO is back above its MAs but the Neutral
Bear regime requires more than two consecutive up sessions to flip.
15day ROC is still negative at -0.86 across all indices. The real test is
the first pullback — does XJO hold 8,800 (now the converted 50MA/200MA
level) as support? If it does, the regime narrative changes materially.
