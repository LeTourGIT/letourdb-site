📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Friday delivered the most brutal single session of 2026 for technology investors. Two independent catalysts converged with destructive precision: Broadcom's Q2 earnings exposed the gap between AI hype and AI revenue reality, and the May NFP print of +172K — more than double the consensus of 80K — killed the Fed rate-cut narrative for the rest of the year, sending the 10-year Treasury yield to 4.55%. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) fell 10.44% in a single session. XLK -6.66%, NDX -4.77%, SOXX -10.44%. VIX surged 39.68% to 21.51. This is not a correction whisper — this is an institutional repricing event.

The Broadcom mechanism is critical to understand. AVGO Q2 results were operationally strong: $22.2B revenue (+48% YoY), AI semiconductor revenue $10.8B (+143% YoY), Non-GAAP EPS $2.44 vs est. $2.32. But CEO Hock Tan did not raise the company's full-year AI semiconductor guidance above the $100B target previously communicated. In a market where the semiconductor complex has been priced for $120-130B+ AI outcomes, the refusal to raise the ceiling triggered a full-scale re-rating. AVGO fell ~13%, wiping ~$80B in market cap. The XPU custom chip model — the structural justification for the Marvell (MRVL -16.74%), Broadcom, and ARM premium — came under direct scrutiny. Hock Tan attributed slower ramp to two unnamed hyperscaler clients and confirmed a shift to "chips only" rather than complete integrated AI systems. That sentence alone repriced the entire custom silicon supply chain.

The NFP data added a second layer of pressure. May payrolls +172K (consensus +80K) — the largest upside surprise since January 2025 — with unemployment unchanged at 4.3% and average hourly earnings at +3.4% YoY. The 2-year Treasury yield hit 4.17%, its highest since February 2025. Markets now price an 85% probability of a Fed rate hike by year-end. This is the most hawkish single-session repricing of 2026. Higher discount rates are the mathematical enemy of high-multiple growth stocks — and after a May in which NDX added 15.3%, the combined Broadcom + NFP shock is a textbook mean-reversion catalyst.

The rotation into defensives was textbook and immediate. XLP +1.71%, XLU +0.93%, XLV +0.61%, XLF +0.21%. The sole ETF above +3% was IAK (insurance) at +3.19% — itself a direct beneficiary of higher-yield environments. COO +8.58%, KMB +4.84%, PGR +4.42%, PG/CL +4.09% represent institutional capital rotating into quality defensive names with pricing power and dividend yield now competitive against government bonds. Smart Money #1 (Educational Services, ROC +25.05%), #3 (Personal Services), and #7 (Computers & Electronics Retailers) signal retail-consumer defensiveness, not expansion.

Bitcoin -5.22%, Ethereum -11.35% in a session that saw VIX +39.68% confirms that digital assets remain a high-beta risk-on instrument. Bitcoin has now fallen ~22% from its May 14 peak near $82K, with $4.4B in IBIT/FBTC ETF outflows over 13 sessions. Gold -3.24% — unusual for a risk-off day — points to forced deleveraging of commodity/precious metals positions alongside tech. The structural bull regime is intact: SPX above both MAs, RSI reset, breadth recoverable. But a -4.77% NDX single-session with VIX +39% leaves nothing ambiguous about who was margin-called on Friday.
