📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
The ASX 200 ended a 3-session recovery with the smallest of losses
— down 8.0 points (-0.09%) to 8,727.7 — after failing to follow
Wall Street's record close. The headline number understates what
happened underneath: 117 XJO stocks rose against 77 fallers and 6
of 11 sectors finished higher. The index fell because its two
heaviest sectors did: Financials (XFJ -0.59%) and Materials (XMJ
-0.20%).

The bank sell-down is the session's macro message. CBA -1.32%, WBC
-1.21%, BOQ -2.15% and NAB -0.78% came one day after
Westpac-Melbourne Institute consumer sentiment fell 4.7% to 80.4,
with respondents surveyed after the RBA's 29/09/2026 hike to 4.60%
printing 67.2 against 86.9 beforehand — the widest
pre/post-decision gap since daily tracking began in 2019. With the
Australian 10-year at 5.37% and Westpac calling a follow-up hike
at the 03/11/2026 decision, the read-through is credit quality
rather than margins. Insurers confirm it by going the other way:
QBE +1.31% to a new 20day high, SUN +0.91%, IAG +0.90%.
Higher-for-longer yields lift insurers' investment income while
they threaten bank bad-debt lines — same macro input, opposite P&L
effect.

Materials (XMJ -0.20%) was nowhere near the 1.5% threshold that
would make resources the lead story, but its internal split is
instructive. The iron ore majors fell — FMG -2.44%, BHP -0.65%,
RIO -0.61% — with 62% Fe at US$91.18/t (-0.30%; 5day -5.74%, 20day
-8.84%) and mainland China shut for the National Day holiday until
tomorrow, so there has been no physical-demand signal for a week.
Gold producers rose (EVN +2.49%, NST +1.92%, PNR +4.55%, CMM
+2.57%) even with gold futures -0.31% at US$4,155.70 — a relief
bounce in an industry that ranks 66 of 71 on 10day ROC (-6.59).
Lithium was sold again (CXO -6.56%, WC8 -5.36%, VUL -3.27%) after
carbonate fell 7.8% last week.

Uranium was the one part of the US lead that travelled. Google's
nuclear offtake with Constellation lifted BMN +8.58%, BOE +4.64%,
DYL +4.61%, NXG +4.25% and PDN +2.54%. Context matters: Uranium
ranks 69 of 71 in the Smart Money table with a 10day ROC of -10.4
— this is a bounce off the bottom of the rotation ladder, not
leadership. Stock-specific news did the rest. ARF +12.98% after
its 11:46 Edge Early Learning administration update (Goodstart is
to take over 31 Edge centres, 20 of them in Arena-owned
properties) removed a tenant-default overhang. SKS -12.26% on
$119.8M of turnover — four times its average — after its founders
sold a block of roughly $106M at $10.60. PYC -7.84% on its PKD
data presentation: a sell-the-news response from a top-decile
relative-strength name.

Structurally nothing has changed: all four indices remain Bearish,
only 25% of XJO stocks sit above their 50day MA and T2108 is 25%.
The direction of travel inside the bounce is better — new 20day
lows have fallen from 46 on 01/10 to 19 — and five of the top
eight Smart Money industries are now consumer discretionary,
bought at the moment sentiment is at recession-type lows. That is
either early contrarian accumulation or short covering; XDJ is
still -21.72% over one year, so the burden of proof sits with the
buyers. Forward catalysts: FOMC minutes tonight; mainland China
reopens tomorrow, restarting iron ore and lithium price discovery;
SEN's post-close ATO class ruling on its capital reduction (16:53)
is not in today's price; four trading halts (TZL, RKT, PRX, TOR)
are pending resolution.
