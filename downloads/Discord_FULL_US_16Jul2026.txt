📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Thursday was a textbook rotation session dressed up as a down day. The headline
— SPX -0.51%, NDX -1.62% — hides the most important fact on the tape: 368 S&P
advancers against just 131 decliners, a 2.8-to-1 POSITIVE breadth reading on a
red index. This is the mirror image of a cap-weight distortion: the mega-cap
growth cohort (semis, AI-infrastructure, high-beta tech) fell hard enough to pull
the cap-weighted index down while the median stock ROSE. Small-caps (RUT -0.06%)
and the Dow (-0.20%) barely moved. When the average stock outperforms the index
by this much, the correct read is redistribution of capital, not liquidation.

Where did it go? Into defensives, energy and value. Consumer Staples (XLP +2.80%)
was the day's single best sector — PM +4.95%, MDLZ +4.60%, Colgate +2.84% — with
Health Care (XLV +2.22%) close behind on an ABT +10.71% earnings blowout, MCK
+5.65% and SYK +4.66%. Real Estate (XLRE +2.02%) and a clutch of REITs (PLD
+4.63%, REZ/INDS both >+3%) caught a duration bid. This is the classic late-cycle
defensive playbook, and Smart Money confirms it: Tobacco surged to #4, Tires &
Rubber and Appliances/Tools both posted 80-plus-rank SURGES into the top 15.

The other side of the barbell was brutal. The semiconductor and AI-hardware
complex was not merely soft — it was routed. XSD (equal-weight semis) fell -5.63%,
an EXTREME single-session move, and is down -11.01% over five days. The single-
stock damage is where the story lives: MRVL -8.71% (-22.6% week), ALAB -8.81%
(-23.4% week), CRDO -8.28% (-21.7% week), WDC -9.15%, SNDK -12.63%, GLW -9.19%,
COHR -7.49%. This is a coordinated de-rating of the AI-infrastructure premium,
not idiosyncratic weakness. Three catalysts converged: TSMC's higher capex guide
(read as margin pressure across the supply chain), a report that Alphabet's Gemini
3.5 Pro is delayed (a crack in the AI-demand narrative), and a hawkish Dallas Fed
(Logan calling for 'modestly higher' rates) that punishes long-duration growth.

Financials told the rotation in miniature. Goldman and Morgan Stanley reported
record quarters earlier in the week — Goldman's $20.98 EPS and Morgan Stanley's
$6.3B equities-trading line were both powered by the AI-driven trading boom. That
is precisely why GS -4.91% and MS -4.45% LED financials lower on the 16th: as the
AI trade unwound, the banks whose beats were AI-flow-levered were sold as sell-the-
news. Regional banks geared to net-interest-margin and Main-Street lending — CFG
+4.61%, USB +1.59%, MTB +2.22% — were bid instead. Same sector, opposite trades,
split along the AI-exposure line. The RS leaderboard has already re-sorted around
this: biotech and healthcare now hold 9 of the top 20 RS slots; the two remaining
semiconductor names (MXL, PENG) were down -16% and -9% on the day.

The tell for tomorrow is after-hours. Netflix fell -7.88% AH on narrowed guidance
and a decision to disclose fewer engagement metrics — a second mega-cap-growth
crack after Alphabet. Intuitive Surgical dropped -10.88% AH despite a strong
session, a reminder that even the day's leadership (medtech) is priced for
perfection. VIX +6.76% to 16.73 is doing exactly what it should in a rotation:
rising modestly on repositioning, not spiking on fear.
