📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Materials (XMJ +4.06%) drove the ASX 200's sharpest single-session
gain in months, lifting the benchmark +1.98% to 8,804.0 and erasing
Thursday's slide to a four-month low. The catalyst was unambiguous:
Trump's overnight decision to call off planned Iran strikes and signal
an imminent peace deal sparked a broad Wall Street risk-on bounce, and
crucially collapsed the oil-driven inflation premium that has weighed
on risk assets all week. The flow-through into ASX resources was
immediate and broad: LTR +9.82%, PLS +9.76%, ELV +12.49% [H], DVP
+10.37% [H] and MI6 +14.67% all surged as the overnight Lithium &
Battery Tech (+5.5%) and Copper Miners (+7.4%) ETF moves found their
ASX equivalents.

Gold told a more nuanced story. Bullion itself was little-changed to
slightly lower (Gold -0.64% to $4,193.70, near a six-month low per
overnight commentary as SpaceX's record $75bn IPO drains liquidity
from defensive assets) — yet ASX gold miners were sharply two-way:
GMD +10.83%, PNR +9.57% and BC8 +9.77% rode the broader Gold Miners
ETF bounce (+5.3% overnight), while DTR -11.54% and FRS -5% reversed
hard. This is a sector being driven by overnight momentum and
stock-specific newsflow rather than the gold price itself — don't read
gold-miner strength today as a pure bullion signal.

Breadth confirmed the rally's width: XJO advancers led decliners 160
to 36 (4.4:1), with 126/200 constituents above their 20MA and 59 new
20day highs against just 4 new 20day lows. Across the All Ordinaries,
advancers beat decliners 345 to 110 (3.1:1). Financials (XFJ +1.61%),
Real Estate (XRE +1.82%) and Consumer Discretionary (XDJ +2.05%) all
participated strongly — rate-sensitive sectors rallying into next
Tuesday's RBA decision is best read as these names simply being
high-beta participants in a broad global risk-on day rather than an
RBA-specific repricing.

The only soft spots were Energy (XEJ -0.46%) and Communication
Services (XTJ -0.29%) — Energy's underperformance directly mirrors the
overnight oil collapse, with VEA -3.43% the standout laggard. Smart
Money's 10day rotation table tells a different story to today's tape:
the top-ranked industries by 10day flow are still domestically-
defensive — Multiline Insurance & Brokers (#1), Specialty Chemicals
(#2) and Distillers & Wineries (#4) — suggesting today's resources/
tech-led bounce is a sharp one-day catch-up trade layered on a
medium-term rotation that has, until now, quietly favoured insurance,
consumer staples and domestic services names (XSJ's 5day +8.84% is
the heatmap's standout medium-term trend).
