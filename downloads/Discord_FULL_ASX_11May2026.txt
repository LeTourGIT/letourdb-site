📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Monday 11 May was defined by a single event: CSL's fourth major guidance
reset in two years triggered its largest ever single-session decline — down
approximately 16-20% depending on the time of measurement — as the company
announced a US$15.2 billion FY26 revenue target and NPATA of approximately
US$3.1 billion, both well short of consensus, alongside US$5 billion in
non-cash impairments across FY26/FY27 (primarily CSL Vifor intangibles).
The healthcare index (XHJ) lost 6.47% — a sector-index level crash driven
by one company's weight and the broader contagion into adjacent names.

The session unfolded against a backdrop that should have been constructive:
SPX printed its sixth consecutive ATH on Friday night, April NFP beat at
115,000, and Nasdaq surged 1.71%. Instead, the ASX decoupled in real time
as the CSL announcement processed, with the index spending the entire day
below Friday's close. The divergence is a classic case of stock-specific
idiosyncratic risk overriding macro signal — and for a blue-chip held by
virtually every managed fund in Australia, the forced selling was systemic.

The secondary story is oOh!media (OML), which bucked the trend — rising
+7.14% on a second competing takeover bid from I Squared Capital at A$1.45/
share, following PEP's earlier A$1.40 proposal. The board rejected both as
inadequate while permitting due diligence, signalling a bidding war. OML's
10day Smart Money rank of #1 (Advertising & Marketing at ROC +13.79%) now
has a fundamental catalyst to match the technical momentum. This is the most
active M&A situation on the ASX right now.

The macro calendar looms large: the Federal Budget is tomorrow (12 May),
delivered one week after the RBA hiked to 4.35% in an 8-1 vote — the most
hawkish split of the current tightening cycle. A budget that adds stimulus
will directly complicate the RBA's June meeting calculus and could push
Westpac's call for 4.60% in August toward consensus. Markets are threading
a very tight needle.

Small caps (XSO +0.25%) outperformed the large-cap indices — confirming the
regime divergence flagged in the Macro Regime section. Small-cap RS complex
in lithium, mining services, and IT services continues to accumulate.
