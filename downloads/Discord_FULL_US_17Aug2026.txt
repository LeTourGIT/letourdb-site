📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Monday was a risk-off session driven by two macro shocks hitting simultaneously.
First, oil re-priced sharply higher — WTI +3.27% to $84.93, Brent +2.68% to
$91.08 — as Iran tensions re-escalated: Trump refused to extend the ceasefire and
threatened Oman, Iran rebuffed his Strait of Hormuz claim, and a ship strike was
reported. Second, the 30-year Treasury yield topped 5.31% — its highest in 19
years. Higher oil (an inflation impulse) plus a higher long-end yield (a discount-
rate shock) is the textbook combination that compresses long-duration equity
multiples — which is exactly what the tape delivered.

The session's defining feature was an internal rotation, not an index move. CNBC's
read — 'chip stock rebound fails' — captures it precisely: semiconductors and
hardware/memory led every gainer board (SMTC +9.88%, SNDK +8.88%, CRDO +8.82%,
COHR +7.79%, TER +5.81%, AMAT +5.55%, MRVL +5.54%, WDC +5.35%), yet NDX still
closed -0.17% and SPX -0.52%. The chips rallied intraday on Nvidia's re-awakening
(Huang: OpenAI data centre could generate $600B revenue) but could not carry the
broad tape. Their fuel came straight out of software: NOW -5.08%, MDB -4.70%,
TWLO -4.03%, ADBE -3.78%, and the IGV software ETF -2.01%. Capital rotated from
the software/applications layer into the silicon/hardware layer — a mirror image
of the software-led leadership seen earlier in the cycle.

Beneath the rotation, the damage was breadth-wide. SPX advancers 131 vs 366
decliners (roughly 1:2.8) and RUT 658 vs 1268 — but this is coherent weak breadth
on a down tape, NOT a cap-weight divergence (the index fell WITH its internals, it
was not propped up by a handful of megacaps). Losers spanned software, online
services (RDDT -7.63%, SPOT -3.97%), consumer cyclicals (CVNA -7.28%, ROST -4.11%,
NKE -4.03%, EXPE -4.27%), alternative-asset financials (KKR -4.54%, APO -4.42%),
and defense — LHX -4.61%, cleanly explained by L3Harris ousting CEO Kubasik over
'conduct.' A stock-specific governance shock, not a defense-sector signal.

Where did the money go? Energy (XLE +1.08%) was the only meaningfully green sector,
a direct read-through from the oil spike. Metals and miners bid (GDX +2.13%, COPX
+1.91%, SLV +1.86%), shipping surged (BOAT +2.69%, the day's top ETF), and Bitcoin
firmed (IBIT +2.20%) even as VIX jumped +6.60% to 15.19 — a rare risk-on/risk-off
split that says this was a rotation with a fear overlay, not a wholesale de-risking.
The losers of the day — airlines (JETS -2.25%, oil-cost victims), solar (TAN -2.02%),
software (IGV) and online retail (IBUY -1.93%) — trace the same macro chain cleanly.
