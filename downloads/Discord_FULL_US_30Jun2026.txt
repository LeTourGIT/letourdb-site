📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
The final session of Q2 belonged entirely to semiconductors. XSD (equal-weight
semis) ripped +5.24% and SOXX +4.30% — the only two broad chip ETFs through +3%,
but the breadth underneath was real: AMD +7.68% (Wells Fargo PT to $615 on AI-CPU
dominance), KLAC +8.38%, ONTO +7.68%, MRVL +7.25%, CRDO +10.69%, SNDK +10.89%. The
equal-weight ETF leading the cap-weight one is the signal — this is broad supply-
chain accumulation, not a single mega-cap melt-up. It caps the chip sector's best
quarter ever (+$2 trillion combined value across Micron, Intel and AMD in Q2).

The cruel irony of the quarter: NVDA mostly sat it out. The cycle's former bellwether
was the chip LOSER of Q2 while the equal-weight complex, semi-equipment (KLAC/ONTO/
MKSI), memory (SNDK), and AI-CPU (AMD) carried leadership. Capital has rotated DOWN
the supply chain and ACROSS to AMD — a leadership-broadening that is healthier for
the cycle than a NVDA-only tape, even as it punishes index-weighting concentration.

The second story is a wholesale rotation WITHIN the AI trade. As chips melted up,
the AI-power/infrastructure layer was dumped: data-center REITs DLR -5.77% and EQIX
-3.94% were the worst large-caps, dragging XLRE to -1.98% (worst sector). The nuclear/
power-AI pair CEG -4.22% and TLN -3.78% sold off in lockstep, and XLU -1.48%. The
market is repricing the 'AI needs power and real estate' thesis lower while bidding
the chips themselves — a clean infrastructure→silicon rotation. Michael Burry shorting
Caterpillar (CNBC) is the same trade expressed short: fade the AI-capex 2nd derivatives.

Crypto-fintech was the day's outright casualty. CRCL (Circle) -17.55%, MSTR -6.20%,
COIN -3.60% as IBIT -2.40% and ETHA -2.53% — a coordinated de-risking of the crypto
complex even as equity risk-on raged (VIX -6.80% to 16.45). Telecom bond-proxies got
hit on higher yields (T -5.13%, VZ -3.99%, TMUS -3.59%) after June's jobs data lifted
Treasuries, and staples (MDLZ -3.84%) sold on the risk-on rotation.

Macro backdrop turned constructive: oil posted its largest quarterly drop in six years,
Brent its worst month since March 2020, as Iran de-escalated — US-Iran talks move to
Qatar Tuesday and a peace framework reportedly grants UN access to nuclear sites. The
Hormuz premium is bleeding out (WTI $70.07). The lone hawkish note: Cleveland Fed's
Hammack warned AI could fuel inflation and that rate hikes may be necessary — the new
tail risk for a market pricing AI as pure productivity tailwind.
