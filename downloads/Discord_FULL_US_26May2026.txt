📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Tuesday capped the post-Memorial Day return with a clean ATH print on the SPX
(7,519.12) and a broad rally that, for once, had genuine breadth — the Russell 2000
leading at +1.77% is the structural tell. The session had one overwhelmingly dominant
catalyst: Micron Technology surging ~19% intraday to breach the $1 trillion market cap
threshold for the first time in its history. UBS analyst Timothy Arcuri tripled his
price target from $535 to $1,625 — the most aggressive single analyst revision of
the semiconductor cycle — citing Micron's entire 2026 HBM4 capacity being sold out
under long-term supply agreements and DRAM prices up 58-63%. This is not a momentum
trade rerating; it is a structural repricing of AI memory from commodity to
essential infrastructure. The corollary was immediate: SNDK +9.3%, WDC and STX
surging in sympathy. The SOXX ETF closed +6.10%, its largest one-day gain in recent
weeks, and XSD +4.69% confirmed the breadth of the semi move.

The macro backdrop aided the rally. Treasury yields retreated materially — the 10-year
dropped ~8 basis points to 4.485% — as bond markets reopened after the long weekend
with bolstered optimism around Iran peace negotiations. Secretary Rubio acknowledged
talks were progressing but required "several more days" on wording. This dual signal
— yield compression + peace probability rising — was the perfect conditions for a
growth-and-rate-sensitive session. WTI fell ~2.37% to $94.31 as a result, hitting
a five-week low, with the implied Hormuz resolution premium continuing to erode.

Sector dispersion told the real story. Technology (XLK +2.63%) was the only sector
above +2%, driven exclusively by the semi/memory complex. Energy (XLE -2.76%) was
the worst performer as oil dropped. The Dow -0.23% reflects this energy/value drag
— the cap-weight composition of DJI contains far more cyclical exposure than NDX.

AutoZone's BMO Q3 report added a clean, if understated, consumer read: domestic SSS
+4.1%, EPS $38.07 beat estimates, revenue $4.84B slightly missed. Not a catalyst for
the session but confirms the auto parts aftermarket is absorbing inflation without
demand destruction — a healthy real-economy read in a high-oil environment.

⚠ BREADTH DIVERGENCE: SPX hit ATH with only 245 advancers vs 254 decliners — a 
  near 1:1 Adv/Dec on an ATH day. The MU +19% move on a ~5% SPX index weight
  is mathematically lifting the composite. T2108 at 53.4% and 50MA participation
  at 55.2% show modest but still-fragile breadth improvement from last week.
