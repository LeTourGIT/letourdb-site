📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Friday 29 May delivered the ASX's strongest session in weeks —
XJO +1.61% to 8,731.7, XAO +1.65% to 8,965.0, XSO outpacing at
+2.27% — as two catalysts converged: the US-Iran 60-day ceasefire
MOU driving WTI toward $87 in Asian hours, and the S&P 500 closing
at a fresh ATH overnight despite PCE inflation printing at a 3-year
high. The market's refusal to sell on hot US inflation tells you
everything about current sentiment: rate risk is priced, tail risk
has been removed, and capital is looking for reasons to buy.

EOS (Electro Optic Systems, ASX300) +16.18% was the session
standout — driven by appointment of two high-profile defence board
directors (Air Vice-Marshal (Ret.) Catherine Roberts, Major General
(Ret.) Kathryn Toohey) carrying momentum from last week's $150M
institutional placement. EOS holds a $459M order book, 63% gross
margin, and is the ASX's clearest counter-drone (C-UAS) play. The
board appointments signal the company is institutionalising its
defence relationships ahead of major program awards (HELW, C2,
Slinger). This is not a governance event — it's a contract pipeline
signal. Smart Money Aerospace & Defense moved from #78 to #11 over
9 sessions; EOS's move validates the institutional positioning.

Materials (XMJ +2.89%) led large sectors for the second consecutive
week. MIN (Mineral Resources, +3.76%) recovered after POSCO lithium
transaction completion ($765M deal accelerates balance sheet
deleveraging). SGM (Sims, +3.59%) hit a 20day high on steel scrap
recovery — the ceasefire narrative reduces supply-chain risk
premiums embedded in industrial metals globally. The XMJ +2.89%
adds to last week's +2.09%, making Materials the strongest sector
on a 5day and 20day basis by some margin.

Rate-sensitive sectors staged their expected relief rally on the
April CPI undershoot: XRE +1.84%, XDJ +1.59%, XIJ +1.68%. But
these are tactical trades within a structural headwind — the fuel
excise reversal in July and the trimmed mean CPI at 3.4% (rising)
keep the August hike scenario live. Financials (XFJ +1.24%) bounced
after the Big 4's -1.4% to -2.2% hammering on Thursday, but the
emerging credit quality signals (household spending falling, 4.5%
unemployment) are a medium-term overhang.

Breadth was the cleanest read: XJO 160/31 advance/decline (5.2:1)
is the strongest ratio in the current correction cycle. 30 new
20day highs vs 13 lows is the first net-positive session on the
high/low metric this month. The structure is improving but not yet
confirmed as a trend reversal — Bearish regime across XJO/XAO/XTO
means the macro trend context is still down. The 200MA test at XJO
8,795 (approx) will determine if this bounce has legs.
