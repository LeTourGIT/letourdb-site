📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Friday's tape was a textbook monetary-debasement rotation. The macro trigger sits in
the bond market: Treasury Secretary Bessent's increasingly activist debt-management
push (buybacks, front-end issuance) is stoking inflation worries even as it tries to
calm yields, and Ray Dalio publicly framed the move as a signal a debt crisis is
drawing closer — explicitly recommending gold and bitcoin. The market traded exactly
that script: hard assets and crypto bid together, the dollar softer (AUD/USD +0.80%
to 0.7175), and rate-sensitive defensives sold.

Metals and miners were the dominant institutional story. Uranium miners URNM +7.66%,
rare earths REMX +6.69% (with MP Materials +9.10%), copper COPX +5.18% (Freeport
+7.64%), and broad metals & mining XME +4.05% drove Materials XLB to the top sector
spot at +2.14%. Nine ETFs closed above +3% and NOT ONE closed below -3% — the widest
positive breadth of ETF flags in weeks, and every one of them a hard-asset or
capital-markets vehicle. This is not a defensive gold hedge in isolation; it is a
full cyclical-commodity repricing spanning precious AND industrial metals.

Crypto ran alongside in lockstep. IBIT +5.51% (5day +22.59%) and ETHA +3.70% (5day
+28.63%) capped a ferocious week for digital assets, and the equity proxies were the
day's top gainers: Robinhood +13.70%, Coinbase +8.20%, Strategy (MSTR) +6.10%,
BitMine +5.84%. Smart Money confirmed it — Blockchain & Cryptocurrency SURGED to rank
9 from rank 128 just three sessions ago. Gold, silver, uranium, copper and bitcoin
rising as one set is the signature of a debasement trade, not a growth trade.

The mirror image was the electric-utility complex and the AI-power derivative trade,
which were liquidated. Utilities XLU -2.28% was the only red sector, and the losers
list was a wall of grid names: AEP -3.79%, XEL -3.06%, SO -2.72%, DUK -2.31%, VST
-1.96%, alongside data-center and power-infrastructure plays Quanta PWR -3.49%,
CoreWeave -2.13% and crypto-miner/power hybrid TeraWulf -4.92%. Capital rotated out of
the SECOND-DERIVATIVE AI-electricity theme and into DIRECT hard assets and crypto —
a meaningful signal that the "AI needs power" consensus trade is being trimmed.

Semiconductors remained the drag that kept the Nasdaq pinned. Marvell -5.57% led the
losers, with Intel -2.24%, Western Digital -2.05% and Teradyne -1.93% following; the
chip-rebound attempt failed intraday. That single-sector weakness explains the entire
NDX-vs-Dow spread and the Neutral Bull regime. Retail earnings, by contrast, were a
quiet positive: Target +4.54%, Ross +4.39%, BJ's +5.61%, Estée Lauder +6.02% and
Deere +4.27% all beat, and homebuilder Hovnanian +11.24% — the consumer is not the
weak link here.
