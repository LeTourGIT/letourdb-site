📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Monday 14 Sep closed fractionally green (XJO +0.10% to 8,749.90; XAO +0.04%)
but the number flatters a soft session. Wall Street surged on Friday (SPX
+0.86%, DJI +0.98%) and SPI futures pointed the ASX up ~0.2%, yet the index
gave back an early +0.31% to finish barely positive. Heavyweights BHP, RIO,
NAB, Goodman and Fortescue all dragged — this was a defensive, narrow tape.

The rotation was textbook late-cycle risk-off: Health Care (+1.52%), Consumer
Staples (+0.61%), Financials (+0.43%), Energy (+0.38%) and Utilities (+0.33%)
led, while Materials (-0.55%) and IT (-1.14%) lagged. UBS captured it —
equities are selling off as yields rise, the inverse pattern that forms when
inflation, not deflation, is the fear. With the Fed ~80-90% to hike on 16 Sep
and the 10yr nearing 5%, rate-sensitive growth is being repriced in real time.

Health Care led on a clean catalyst: Telix (TLX +4.28%) won FDA approval for
its brain-cancer imaging drug Pixclara — a genuine institutional re-rating, not
flow. Corporate activity was the other bright spot: FleetPartners (FPR +12.11%)
on revised non-binding takeover offers and Cleanaway (CWY +4.67%) on the EQT
Infrastructure proposal. Materials was the drag — iron ore has slipped below
US$100/t (~$98) and the sector is now -6% over three sessions.

Beneath the surface the tape was heavy. Uranium was hit hard (PDN -6.91%, DYL
-8.83%, both extending 5day losses beyond -18%), IT growth names de-rated (MP1
-8.30%), and Core Lithium (CXO +13.04%) spiked to a two-year high on NO news —
short-covering, not fundamentals, with lithium carbonate still soft. Breadth
told the real story: XJO advancers 93 vs decliners 94, and 40 new 20day lows
against just 4 new highs. This is an oversold market with no confirmed buyers.
