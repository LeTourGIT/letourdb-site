→ This is not a pivot — it's the arrival at the plateau. The 8-1
  vote signals broader board consensus than the 5-4 March split,
  but the language was simultaneously more hawkish ("second-round
  effects", "risks tilted upside") and more dovish ("well placed
  to respond"). Translation: they hiked, they're watching, but
  they're not done if energy persists. Bullock explicitly flagged
  the Iran-Hormuz conflict as having "complicated things immensely."
  The market reaction on 6 May reflects post-decision relief —
  the hike was priced in at 75-85%, removing uncertainty.
  Rate-sensitive sectors (REITs, consumer, banks) face structural
  recalibration: the question shifts from "will they hike?" to
  "how long at 4.35%?" Resources and USD-revenue names remain
  the structurally insulated pocket.

📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Wednesday 6 May delivered the market's second straight advance
(+1.30% to 8,793.6) on what was always going to be one of the
most consequential domestic sessions of the year. The RBA's 8-1
vote to raise the cash rate by 25bp to 4.35% landed as widely
expected, and with that uncertainty now resolved, the market
exhaled. Risk-on sentiment flooded in from two directions: the
pre-positioned post-decision relief trade and the powerful US
overnight backdrop — SPX at record highs, WTI -3.55%, and
Hormuz ceasefire signals compressing the energy risk premium.

Materials (XMJ +2.48%) again led all sectors, with Liontown
(LTR +6.28%) extending its extraordinary April re-rating
into a consecutive institutional confirmation session. But the
genuine surprise was Financials (XFJ +2.39%) — the sector most
directly exposed to the RBA decision turned into the second-best
performer on the day. This reflects the post-certainty trade:
the banks had been pricing in hike anxiety for weeks and, with
the decision delivered and language signalling possible pause
ahead, traders covered shorts and rotated back in. CBA +2.96%
and ANZ +3.12% led, with Westpac the drag after its half-year
earnings landed broadly in-line but with margin concerns.

The WTI -3.55% overnight was arguably more important for the
RBA's next move than today's decision itself: if oil stabilises
below $110, the second-round fuel inflation impulse that Bullock
flagged weakens, increasing the probability of a genuine pause
in June. The AUD at 0.7238 (+0.77%) confirms risk-on and
rate-differential attractiveness post-hike — but at these
levels the miner revenue compression math intensifies: every
1 cent AUD rise = ~0.7% revenue headwind for BHP/RIO/FMG in
AUD terms. Today's AUD move alone absorbed roughly half that.

Critically, the session finally delivered XJO above its 50MA
(now +0.27%). That's a technical milestone after weeks of
sub-50MA closes. The test is whether 8,793 holds — the 200MA
sits at 8,803 (per IG). A close above 8,803 clears the
structural damage and repositions for a retest toward 9,000.
