📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
The ASX 200 fell 137.8 points (-1.58%) to 8,592.6, its seventh straight loss,
with every one of the 11 sectors closing red. The trigger was offshore: Wall
Street's 2% slide on Anthropic's listing day turned the world's largest IPO
into a liquidity drain on the rest of the AI complex, and Asia followed
(Nikkei -4.8%, SoftBank -16%). The All Ords lost 1.63% and the Small Ords
2.71% -- the further down the cap curve, the harder the selling.

Information Technology (XIJ -4.70%) took the full force. NextDC (NXT -7.11%)
is the session's most telling print: it lodged a FY27 GUIDANCE UPGRADE at
08:24 and still fell 7% -- when good news is sold this hard, positioning, not
fundamentals, is driving price. Megaport (MP1 -12.89%), Xero (XRO -5.65%),
WiseTech (WTC -3.37%) and Macquarie Technology (MAQ -3.52%) completed a
de-rating of anything priced off AI/data-centre demand. None of MP1, XRO or
WTC had an announcement -- this was pure sector flow.

Materials (XMJ -2.15%) extended a brutal week to -12.32% over 5 days -- the
worst sector drawdown on the board. The damage is in gold and copper, not iron
ore: the Global X Physical Gold ETF (GOLD -5.29%) caught down to the overnight
bullion slump and gold miners fell for an eighth straight day (NST -3.73%, EVN
-4.78%, NEM -5.58%), while copper-exposed names and mining services (PRN
-11.23%) remained under pressure. Yet the iron ore and lithium end held: FMG
+2.13%, PLS +2.34%, ELV +2.10%, RIO flat -- iron ore steady near US$91/t keeps
the bulk miners as the relative safe harbour inside the resources complex.

Domestic macro is compounding the offshore shock. Thursday's labour data
(43,500 jobs lost; unemployment 4.9%) has the ABC carrying three separate
recession-warning headlines, yet with the cash rate at 4.85% and inflation
still above band, the market is not getting the usual 'bad news = rate cuts'
cushion. Banks slipped (WBC -2.23%, NAB -1.31%, CBA -1.00%) and consumer
credit was punished -- Zip (ZIP -9.41%) is the purest expression of rising
unemployment meeting high rates. Only Utilities (XUJ -0.53%) and Staples (XSJ
-0.84%) held under -1%.

Breadth confirms capitulation-grade selling: 19 advancers against 175
decliners in the ASX 200 (9.8% up), zero stocks up 4% versus 29 down 4%, 71
new 20day lows against 3 highs, and T2108 cut from 50.2% to 39.2% in one
session. Readings this lopsided often mark the late stage of a flush rather
than the start of one -- but a durable low needs a catalyst, and the next two
are Friday night's US session (ES flat after hours) and Tuesday's October CPI.
