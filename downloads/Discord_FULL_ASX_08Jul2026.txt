📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Wednesday 8 July was a risk-off session masked by a shallow headline
loss. The XJO fell just 0.21% to 8,785.1, but that number hides a
violent sector rotation driven by one catalyst: President Trump
declaring the Iran ceasefire 'over' and the US Treasury revoking
Iran's oil-sales waiver. Brent jumped >5%, WTI +2.55% to $74.10, and
the Hormuz war premium came flooding back into energy names.

Energy (XEJ +3.26%) was the standout, and the leadership was broad:
STO +5.78%, KAR +6.64%, WDS +3.22%, VEA +3.26%, YAL +4.01%. This is
the ASX's cleanest expression of the oil-shock trade — integrated
producers and refiners with direct Brent leverage. Coal names (YAL,
SMR +3.51%) rode the same energy-security bid.

The mirror image was Materials (XMJ -2.00%). Two forces hit at once:
lithium carbonate slid to a 10-week low (~CNY 151,750) on speculation
CATL's Jianxiawo mine restarts — CXO -12.07%, and gold -1.78% pressured
the precious names. MI6 -16.45% and IPX -14.14% (both ASX200) led the
losers board. With iron ore soft near US$98/t, the big diversified
miners had no offset. Info Tech (XIJ -1.88%) imported the US chip rout
(WTC -7.28% led), and Comm Services (XTJ -2.10%) was dragged by the
nationwide Telstra outage — an operational and reputational hit.

Beneath the surface, breadth was clearly negative: XJO advancers 83 vs
decliners 113, and 43 new 20day lows against just 8 new highs. The
defensive rotation showed in the winners too — Real Estate (SGP +5.1%,
LLC +4.0%, MGR +3.3%) and Staples (+1.04%) caught a yield/safety bid
as bond proxies. This was not broad selling; it was surgical rotation
out of growth/resources and into energy and defensives.
