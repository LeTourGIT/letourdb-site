📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
June opened on a single dominant catalyst: at Computex in Taipei, Nvidia CEO Jensen
Huang unveiled the RTX Spark Superchip (N1X) — Nvidia's first dedicated PC processor,
an Arm-based system-on-chip pairing a Blackwell GPU with a CPU co-developed with
Microsoft and MediaTek, 128GB unified memory, ~1 petaflop FP4, capable of running
LLMs fully on-device. Six OEMs (MSFT, DELL, HP, ASUS, Lenovo, MSI) ship it this fall.
NVDA closed ~+6%, MSFT +3%, ARM +15.73% — while the incumbents it threatens were
gutted: INTC -4%, AMD -5%, QCOM -8.78%. Huang also confirmed the Vera data-center CPU
is in full production (early customers: Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI, Dell, Oracle, CoreWeave).

This is why the tape looks the way it does. The catalyst was structurally bullish for
the AI-hardware complex but narrow: XLK +2.48% was the only sector up meaningfully,
software (IGV +5.94%) and cybersecurity (CIBR +5.74%) caught the AI-platform bid, and
Computer Hardware ripped (DELL +10.75%, HPE +9.20%) on the OEM halo. Everything outside
the AI trade — and outside energy — was sold. SPX printed a record with only 207
advancers vs 293 decliners; the Dow's internals were 8 up / 22 down. Mega-cap
concentration is doing the heavy lifting: Evercore's Julian Emanuel notes MU, NVDA and
GOOGL alone account for 40%+ of the YTD revision to 2026 S&P EPS. Jamie Dimon used the
Reagan forum (29/05) to warn the tape is "exuberant" and risks underpriced.

The second engine was energy — the only non-tech sector in the green (XLE +1.79%,
XOP +2.37%, CRAK +1.81%). This is the macro story that has fully inverted since 01/05.
The Iran "peace proposal" of early May collapsed into active conflict: the Strait of
Hormuz has been de-facto closed since the war began 28/02, US forces struck Iranian
radar/drone sites, Tehran fired ballistic missiles at Kuwait and sent drones at the
Strait. WTI rallied off ~$87.36 to ~$92 (+~5.8%) intraday; Brent ~$95 (+4.5%). Trump
"cited a rapid pace" in talks yet said he is "not worried about oil prices if Iran
blocks Hormuz," and Washington declared deals for safe Hormuz transit prohibited. UBS:
Gulf crude loadings remain "extremely low." Oil-up + tech-up + everything-else-down is
a barbell tape, not a broad advance.

Macro reinforced the rotation. ISM Manufacturing (released 10:00 ET) came in at 54.0,
the strongest expansion since May 2022, with new orders 56.8 — but the prices paid
component stayed elevated at 82.1. Growth-resilient AND inflationary. That combination
sent yields up and safe-havens down: gold -1.63% to ~$4,518 (GLD -1.40%), gold miners
GDX -3.14%, silver SLV -0.97%. Rate-sensitives followed gold lower — Utilities XLU
-2.97% (worst sector), Real Estate XLRE -1.64%, Staples XLP -1.06%. The session was a
clean macro→sector→stock chain: strong ISM + sticky prices → yields up → defensives
and gold out, AI growth and geopolitically-bid energy in.
