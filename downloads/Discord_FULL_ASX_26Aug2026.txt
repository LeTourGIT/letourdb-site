📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
The ASX 200 slipped -0.40% to 9,127.8, but the headline number badly
undersells the day. This was August reporting season at full velocity —
the index barely moved while individual names swung 10-20%. Decliners beat
advancers 123 to 73 on the XJO, yet 20day highs (46) still outnumbered lows (14):
a rotational shake-out inside an uptrend, not distribution.

The damage was concentrated in tech/growth. WiseTech (WTC -10.07%) was the
session's paradox — it printed record revenue up 79% and beat on EBITDA, then
was sold hard on valuation, dragging Info Tech (XIJ -3.47%) to worst sector and
pulling Xero (XRO -5.67%) and SiteMinder (SDR -8.53%) down in sympathy. Worley
(WOR -10.81%) and Neuren (NEU -10.56%) added to the reporting-season wreckage.

Against that, the defensives and the miners held the line. Woolworths (WOW
+3.42%) delivered a bumper FY profit and carried Consumer Staples (XSJ +1.83%)
to the only strong sector — though the fact the index still fell on a 'good'
WOW is itself the story ABC led with. Lovisa (LOV +12.73%) and HMC Capital
(HMC +11.26%) were the results winners; gold names — Perseus (PRU +9.46% on
record shareholder returns + a buy-back) and a swarm of drillers led by Torque
(TOR +20.45% on multi-ounce intercepts) — kept Materials flat despite the risk-off.

Macro sat underneath it all. A hot Australian CPI print (ABC: 'unfortunate kick
in the guts') has re-loaded RBA hike risk into the 28-29 September meeting
(cash rate 4.35%), pressuring Financials (XFJ -0.58%) and keeping a lid on
rate-sensitives. Offsetting that at the margin, oil fell again — Brent -2.67%
to ~$89 on Iran-Hormuz de-escalation — which is disinflationary and trims the
imported-fuel side of the CPI problem, even as domestic services inflation stays hot.
