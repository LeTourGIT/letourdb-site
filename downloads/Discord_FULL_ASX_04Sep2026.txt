📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Friday 4 September was a mild risk-off drift at the index level (XJO -0.16% to
9,005.9) that masked a sharp rotation underneath. The heavyweight resources and
energy complex dragged — Materials -0.80% (BHP -2.40%, 5day -7.5%) and Energy
-1.22% (ALD -6.01%, VEA -3.65%) — while a US-led tech bid pulled Info Technology
+1.00% and Communication Services +0.98% to the top of the board.

The overnight cue was strong: SPX +1.06%, Nasdaq +1.4%, Dow +1.18% (its best day
since 4 August) after Fed Governor Waller flagged 'disinflation' and eased US
hike bets. But that relief is US-specific. Australia is travelling the opposite
direction on rates — NAB is now the first big-four to call a 29 Sep RBA hike to
4.60% — so the tailwind lifted ASX tech but not the rate-sensitive or resources
blocks, leaving the index slightly red.

Stock-level, the story was M&A and gold. Stanmore (SMR +3.06%, [H] 20day high)
agreed to acquire Moranbah South, and Loyal Metals (LLM) moved its scheme of
arrangement to implementation — corporate action was the session's spine. FRS
+18.31% was the standout, confirmed by a 14:52 price-sensitive filing completing
the Edna May gold acquisition — an evidenced catalyst, not momentum. Gold miners
continue to own the RS table (VAU, RSG [H], GMD, PRU [H], OBM) even as spot gold
paused -0.31%; the relative-strength regime is unambiguously gold-led.

The clearest institutional loser was Nine Entertainment (NEC -8.50%, ASX200),
tied to an 08:35 price-sensitive share-acquisition disclosure. Breadth was
deceptively positive — XJO advancers led decliners 124:65 — but only 37.5% of
XJO names sit above their 20MA, so the bounce was broad yet shallow. The real
read: a gold/energy pause and rate anxiety are capping an otherwise firm tape.
