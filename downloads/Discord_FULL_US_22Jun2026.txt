📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Monday was the day the market stopped buying the AI SPENDERS and started buying the AI
SUPPLIERS. The trigger was talent, not earnings: Alphabet sank ~5% — its worst session
in over a year — after a second marquee researcher (Nobel laureate John Jumper, days
behind transformer co-author Noam Shazeer) left for a rival lab. The exits crystallised a
fear building since Alphabet flagged ~$190B of 2026 AI capex + an $80B equity raise:
the hyperscalers are pouring capital into a buildout whose returns — and now talent — are
in question.

The response was surgically clean. SELL the capex payers: GOOGL -4.99%, AMZN -4.75%,
ORCL -5.00%, AVGO -4.67%, PLTR -6.98%, NFLX -5.82%. BUY the capex receivers: MU +6.82%,
SMCI +15.66%, INTC +5.19%, LRCX +5.27%, ON +8.16%, ALAB +5.42% — plus the optical/
electrical picks-and-shovels COHR +9.22%, GLW +7.65%, CIEN +7.50%, VRT +7.48%. DA
Davidson framed it exactly: sell the firms SPENDING on AI compute, buy the ones TAKING
that money. Micron — up ~6% and reporting Wednesday — sits dead centre.

This is why XLK closed GREEN (+0.37%) while XLC was the WORST sector (-2.37%): GOOGL,
NFLX and FOXA live in Communication Services; the rallying semis live in Technology. The
sector tape isn't contradictory — it's the rotation rendered in GICS. Industrials (+0.49%)
caught the same updraft via the PHYSICAL AI build: Vertiv (cooling/power) +7.48%, Quanta
(grid) +5.40%. The AI trade didn't break — it re-allocated from the model layer to the metal.

Smart Money confirms it without ambiguity: Semiconductor Equipment & Testing is the #1
industry (from #51 nine days ago); Semiconductors surged to #7 from #84 in ONE day;
Computer Hardware sits #6. The RS Top 20 is a semiconductor roll-call (MXL, AEHR, MRVL,
PENG, VSH, MU) with software (BAND, RTB) clinging to the bottom. Equipment → fabless →
memory: accumulation moving THROUGH the supply chain, not out of it.

The geopolitical overlay was a sideshow. Iran declared Hormuz 'closed again' over the
weekend, but tankers kept transiting, CENTCOM disputed it, and Switzerland produced a
60-day road map + Hormuz coordination mechanism. Market read posturing over rupture:
WTI -2.23% to $74. Energy equities held on a shipping-disruption premium crude refused to pay.
