📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Tuesday was a textbook late-cycle rotation on the eve of the Fed decision. The
Dow surged +1.03% (~500 points) to a fresh high while the Nasdaq 100 fell -0.98%
— a 2%+ divergence that captures the day's single dominant theme: capital is
leaving the AI/semiconductor complex and rotating into healthcare, staples,
materials and value. Under the headline, breadth was broadly POSITIVE — SPX
advancers led decliners 354/145 (2.4:1) — so this was the median stock rising
even as the index-level tech names dragged the cap-weighted averages.

The semiconductor unwind was violent and institutional. SOXX -4.80% (5day
-11.08%) and XSD -4.33% (5day -10.81%) confirm this is a multi-session
distribution, not a one-day wobble. Micron -8.85% on $48B turnover, AMD -8.15%
on $16B, Sandisk -14.25% on $28B — the tape shows heavy, deliberate selling in
the highest-liquidity chip names. Amkor -24.74% (advanced-packaging guide cut)
led the rout, with the whole optical/AI-hardware chain (COHR -10.3%, AAOI -9.9%,
LITE -8.4%, GLW -12.1%) marked down in sympathy. AH offered no relief: KLAC
-8.3%, NXPI -5.6%, SWKS -5.7% — chip-equipment guidance is the next shoe.

The receiving end of the rotation was healthcare and defensive value. IQVIA
+13.94% (earnings) was the marquee beat; HCA +7.33%, Incyte +9.30% and a
healthcare-led XLV +2.36% topped the sector table. Staples (XLP +1.99%, KO
+5% beat) and materials (XLB +1.85%, SHW +8.25%, NUE +7.17%) confirm the
late-cycle destination trade. The RS leaderboard has already re-populated with
healthcare/biotech/pharma at the top (AGL, FBRX, CRNX, CORT, ETON) — structural
leadership is changing hands, not just daily prices.

Macro tailwinds reinforced the risk-off-into-quality tone. WTI -3.07% to $79.06
(3rd straight decline) as the US-Iran pause held and Iran-Oman-Saudi talks over
reopening the Strait of Hormuz progressed — Goldman now sees Brent to $80 by
year-end if Hormuz fully reopens. Falling oil pressured energy (XLE -1.35%, OIH
-3.72%) but eases the inflation impulse into the Fed. Apple briefly touched a $5
trillion market cap. With no SEP/dot-plot at this meeting and the funds rate at
3.50-3.75% under Chair Warsh, Wednesday's statement tone is the swing factor.
