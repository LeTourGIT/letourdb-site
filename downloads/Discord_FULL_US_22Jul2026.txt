📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Wednesday was a geopolitical risk-off session dressed up as a quiet index day.
SPX slipped just -0.14% and the Dow was flat, but beneath the cap-weighted
surface the tape bifurcated violently. The trigger: the US struck Iran, the
Houthis threatened to close the Red Sea, and Tehran claimed an attack on Amazon
infrastructure in Bahrain. WTI jumped +1.35% to $86.46 and Brent +1.79% — an
escalation-driven oil bid that is the precise mirror image of the spring's
peace-signal repricing. Energy is re-installed as the dominant sector theme.

The oil impulse fed straight into rates. The 10-year yield rose as traders
revived Fed rate-hike odds — an inflationary energy shock landing just as growth
wobbles. That macro cocktail (higher yields + higher crude) is textbook poison
for long-duration software and rocket fuel for hard assets. It is the single
chain that explains the whole session: policy/oil shock → yields up → software
de-rates → capital rotates to energy, metals, utilities and defensives.

The equity expression was a clean split. AI HARDWARE surged — SMCI +19.84%,
DELL +9.32%, CRWV +3.85%, CEG +4.84% — on undiminished hyperscaler and power
demand. AI SOFTWARE was gutted: IGV -3.05%, with PATH -11.13%, NOW -6.47%,
WDAY -6.20%, PLTR -6.10%, CRM -4.15% and ADBE -3.87%. The catalyst was a return
of AI-disruption fear — an OpenAI cyber-model reportedly broke its training
sandbox, and IBM plus Pegasystems issued AI warnings. Where May's Five9 print
said 'AI is additive to software,' Wednesday's tape said the opposite. This is a
layer call: infrastructure accumulates, applications de-rate.

Smart Money confirmed the rotation is structural, not a one-day flinch. The
Industry Rotation top 16 is now dominated by energy (Refining #5, Integrated #7,
E&P #9) and a hard-asset reflation surge — Steel rocketed from #48 to #13, Paper
Packaging from #65 to #12, Consumer Goods Conglomerates from #54 to #16. There
is not a single semiconductor or software industry in the top 16 — a wholesale
shift from the semi/software leadership that defined the spring. The 60day
structural leaders (Managed Healthcare +41% ROC, Household Products +8.3%) show
this defensive/reflation tilt has been building for weeks and just accelerated.

The real breadth tell was the Russell 2000: Advancers 601 vs Decliners 1,323, a
~1:2.2 ratio, with 117 stocks down >4%. Small caps sold hard while the S&P held
on defensive and energy cap-weight. Yet VIX FELL -2.40% to 16.64 — no panic bid
in vol, which means this is orderly rotation, not a liquidation. That is the
setup into Thursday, when the after-hours GOOGL/TSLA/IBM prints get digested.
