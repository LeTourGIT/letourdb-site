📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Two shocks landed on the same session and reinforced each other. Houthi forces struck
two Saudi tankers in the Red Sea after declaring a maritime blockade; Trump threatened
major military punishment on Iran and told Axios he was considering a massive attack.
Brent settled +7% at $100.69, WTI +6% at $92.19 — both back to pre-ceasefire levels.
The structural point that matters: Bab el-Mandeb was the alternative route while Hormuz
traffic sits near a standstill. With both chokepoints impaired there is no reroute left,
so the market priced this as an inflation event, not an energy-sector opportunity.

That distinction is the whole session. The 10year yield topped 4.70%, its highest since
January 2025, the 2year cleared 4.35%, and fed funds futures moved to roughly an 82%
chance of a September HIKE. A discount-rate shock of that size lands hardest on the
longest-duration assets in the market — which is exactly where the earnings news hit.

Alphabet beat on every line: revenue $119.8B, Cloud +82% to $24.8B, Search +17%. The
stock fell 7.13% because management lifted 2026 capex guidance to $195-205B and free
cash flow swung to roughly -$5.8B. Tesla beat on revenue at $28.24B but missed EPS at
$0.33 against ~$0.53, with operating margin collapsing to 1.4% from 4.1% and capex up
142% year over year — its first cash burn in two years — and fell 14.52% to an 11-month
low. The mechanism is identical in both names, and it is not an earnings problem. The
market has stopped treating AI capex as an automatic growth signal and started
underwriting it as cash burn against a rising discount rate. Every hyperscaler reporting
into August now faces a bar where the beat must arrive with a spending ceiling attached.

Where the money went is more instructive than where it left. ITA +3.08% was the day's
only ETF above +3%, powered by three defence primes reporting into an escalating
conflict: LMT +10.54%, RTX +7.33%, LHX +5.09%. Health care ran a completely separate
bid — TMO +8.71%, DGX +8.61%, DHR +7.46%, MEDP +14.71% — and extended after hours with
THC +15.57% and EW +6.48%. Both destinations share one property: near-term realised
cash flow with no AI capex line to defend. That is the organising principle of the
rotation, and it is a cleaner read than any sector label.

Then Intel reported. Revenue $16.13B against $14.42B expected, adjusted EPS $0.42
against $0.21, the fastest revenue growth since 2011 on a 59% jump in Data Center and
AI, with Q3 guidance above consensus. The stock rose 8.73% after hours. Note that SOXX
-0.77% held up far better than IGV -2.16% during the session — the tape was already
separating hardware with realised AI revenue from software still spending toward it, and
Intel's print validates that split. This is the single most important read for Friday.

⚠ BREADTH DIVERGENCE — in the opposite direction to usual. Indices fell hard but the
internals fell harder in small caps: RUT advancers/decliners 610/1315, with 343 new
20day lows against just 90 highs and 134 stocks down more than 4%. NDX percent-above-
20MA has collapsed to 30.4% and T2108 to 33.33%. SPX is comparatively orderly at
213/287 with T2108 at 55.0% and 63.4% still above the 200MA. The damage is concentrated
in high-beta and long-duration, not distributed — which is what a rotation looks like
before it becomes a de-rating.
