📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
The ASX 200 added 49.3 points (+0.57%) to 8,735.7, a third straight
gain and +1.41% from the 01/10/2026 close of 8,614.4. It was a
top-heavy advance. The ASX 100 (XTO +0.64%) advanced 63/29; the
Small Ords (XSO -0.27%) split 72/72 and logged 9 stocks down 4% or
more against 1 up. BHP (+1.57%), RIO (+1.09%), FMG (+1.36%), CSL
(+0.95%) and the big four banks (+0.56% to +0.78%) did the lifting.
When the index rises on its largest weights while the small-cap
index falls, the buying is index-level and defensive in character
(fund flow and short-covering) rather than a broadening appetite for
risk.

Leadership was the rate-peak trade. Real Estate (XRE +1.11%) led
from 34-month lows, with VCX +3.15%, SCG +2.72%, SGP +2.46%, GPT
+2.29% and ARF +4.79%, followed by Utilities (XUJ +0.90%) and
Financials (XFJ +0.73%). The sector print understates it: Goodman
(GMG -0.65%), by far the largest weight, fell, and the typical REIT
outside it gained 2% to 3%. The fuel was Comyn's "probably finished"
call on RBA hikes and a Melbourne Institute inflation gauge up just
0.3% in September, the smallest rise in three months. Note what did
not move: the Australian 10-year held 5.41%, and auction clearance
rates are under 50%. XRE is still -22.56% over a year and only
+0.22% over 5 days. Equity is front-running a pivot the bond market
has not priced. That is what short-covering off a multi-year low
looks like, and it needs follow-through in yields to become a trend.

Technology (XIJ -2.90%) was the only meaningful loser, and it fell
on the day the Nasdaq set a record. WTC -2.54%, NXT -2.16%, TNE
-1.76% and XRO -1.42% set the index tone; the damage underneath was
heavier: WBT -12.98%, ELS -11.55%, MP1 -5.70%, CAT -5.14%, CDA
-4.32%. None of them lodged an announcement in Section 2b, so this
is flow, not news. Two tells. Digico (DGT -6.94%) and Goodman (GMG
-0.65%), the REITs with data-centre exposure, fell while SCG, SGP,
GPT and VCX rallied: the market prices them as AI-capex beta, not
property. And the defence cohort unwound together (DRO -4.16%, ASB
-3.46%, EOS -2.85%), with Aerospace & Defense dropping from rank 5
to rank 26 in the Smart Money table in one session. XIJ is -43.55%
over a year. The US rally is megacap semiconductors plus M&A; the
ASX has neither, only long-duration software and data-centre capex
discounted at a 5.4% bond yield. The usual hyperscaler read-through
to NXT failed today.

Materials (XMJ +0.91%) rose despite iron ore, not because of it. The
62% Fe benchmark is about US$91.05/t, -5.64% over 5 days and -8.16%
over 20 days (sheet 7d, 05/10/2026), with mainland China shut for
the National Day holiday week. The majors are being carried by the
currency and by base metals: AUD/USD at 0.6969 is -3.41% over 20
days, roughly a 1.7% AUD-revenue cushion on the 0.7%-per-cent rule,
and aluminium snapped a five-session losing run (AAI +4.14%, S32
+1.21%). Lithium names bounced (LTR +3.11%, PLS +2.42%, MIN +2.45%,
CXO +3.39%), but LTR is still -12.63% over 5 days and carbonate last
printed CNY 122,800/t on 30/09/2026, -22.5% on the month: a relief
bid in a broken tape. Gold developers were sold hard (TCG -10.87%,
PDI -6.67%, DTR -5.71%, BTR -4.35%) with gold only -0.20% at
US$4,147. The Gold industry has slid from rank 16 to rank 55 in five
sessions. Juniors are leading the metal lower.

Company news explains two of the sharpest moves. EQ Resources (EQR
-11.39%) lodged "Record Cash Generation on Higher Production" at
10:09 [PS]; Market Index's live blog had it up about 6% early before
it reversed to the third-worst close in the 6d list. Record results
being sold in a stock already -12.50% over 5 days is distribution.
Stakk (SKK +4.35%, +33.33% over 5 days) ran on its 09:39 [PS] IBM
deal. Looking forward: Dicker Data (DDR) was halted at 09:50 and
announced the acquisition of Sektor Group at 17:01, after the close,
so price discovery is tomorrow's. Halts in PMM, ALV, KZR, ZMI, SCP
and GLS (entitlement offer) are pending resolutions. Breadth is the
caution: T2108 lifted to 25.5% from 22.0%, and oversold bounces
start from readings like these, but new 20day lows still outnumber
highs 24 to 7 in the XJO.
