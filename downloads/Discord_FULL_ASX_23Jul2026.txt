📝 SESSION SUMMARY
-----------------------------------------------------
The ASX 200 closed +0.18% at 8,839.0 — a one-month high, but a hollow one.
The index was up 1.17% intraday and gave back roughly two-thirds of that,
leaving a close that flatters what was actually a violently two-sided
session underneath. Advancers led decliners just 98/92 (1.07:1) on an up
day, and six ASX 200 stocks fell more than 4% against only four that rose
more than 4%. That is not what a genuine +0.18% session looks like.

The 11:30am labour force print set the tone. Employment rose +76.3k against
a +15k consensus — a five-fold beat — with the unemployment rate steady at
4.4% only because participation jumped to 67.0%. Full-time added +29.3k.
For the RBA this removes the one thing that could have justified a hold at
11 August: a softening labour market. With the cash rate at 4.35% after three
hikes in 2026 and Q2 CPI landing 29 July, the market is now pricing the CPI
print as the binary event. Westpac already forecasts an August hike.

The transmission was textbook. Info Technology (XIJ -3.32%) was the worst
sector on the board by more than 1.8 percentage points, with WTC -6.97%,
360 -5.45% and XRO -5.01% — none on company news. Healthcare (XHJ -1.43%)
fell for a fifth straight session with PME -6.53% and now -16.35% over five
days. Discretionary (XDJ -1.52%) made it three straight. Every one of those
is a long-duration cash-flow structure being repriced against a US 30-year
yield that has now held above 5% for twelve consecutive sessions.

The other side was energy and resources. Materials (XMJ +1.37%) posted a
third consecutive gain after a ~15% multi-week drawdown, led by JHX +6.14%
on a Q1 guidance upgrade. Energy (XEJ +0.98%) is the real structural move:
+6.66% over five sessions with Brent up 34% since 1 July as US forces struck
Iran for an 11th consecutive night and Trump threatened Iranian power plants
and bridges over Hormuz shipping. PDN +11.61% carried uranium along with it.

Two idiosyncratic moves dominated the tape. OFX +69.47% on an agreed A$1.00
all-cash bid from UK fintech Equals — a 108% premium to its undisturbed
February close, valuing equity at $247m on 9.2x FY26 EV/EBITDA. And GDG
+37.13% on FY26 group FUM up 36%. GDG matters beyond its own move: it is
single-handedly responsible for Insurance Funds ranking #1 in Smart Money.
