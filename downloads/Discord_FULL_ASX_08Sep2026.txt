📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
The ASX 200 fell 1.00% to 8,920.8, its lowest close in six weeks, as a
domestic confidence shock met hardening rate-hike expectations. The Westpac-
Melbourne Institute consumer sentiment index dropped 5.2% to 84.4 and NAB
business confidence slid to -8 — and Westpac became the last of the big four
to forecast an RBA hike, now tipping +25bp to 4.60% in November. With the
29 September meeting 21 days out and market-implied Sep odds ~54%, the tape
repriced duration aggressively.

This was a textbook defensive rotation, not a broad capitulation. Only three
sectors held green — Utilities (+0.59%), Health Care (+0.45%) and Energy
(+0.22%) — the two rate-agnostic defensives plus the oil-leveraged name. The
damage concentrated where duration and rate-sensitivity live: Consumer Disc
(-1.88%), IT (-1.76%), Financials (-1.63%) and Real Estate (-1.47%). Banks
did the heavy lifting to the downside (ANZ -2.9%, CBA -1.8%) — the direct
expression of hike + mortgage-stress fears.

Stock-level catalysts cut against the gloom. SRL (Sunrise Energy Metals)
+21.19% was the standout on its FY26 results and annual report. AEL
(Amplitude Energy) +12.31% [ASX300] hit high-quality gas at its Juliet-1
well, and CXO (Core Lithium) +6.06% produced first spodumene concentrate at
the restarted Finniss operation — a genuine lithium milestone. Gold names
(TCG +7.23%, plus RS leaders RSG, VAU, GMD) provided the Materials cushion
that kept XMJ to just -0.57% despite BSL -5.84% on US-Canada steel tariffs.

Oil is the macro wildcard: WTI +2.85% to US$94 and Brent to a six-week high
near US$98 after fresh strikes on Saudi Aramco facilities. Higher fuel feeds
straight into Australian headline CPI — the exact channel that hardens the
RBA's hand. Housing stress deepened too (property developer Bathla's near-
collapse), compounding the banks' bad-debt overhang and REIT weakness.
