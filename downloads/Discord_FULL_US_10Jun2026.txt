📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Wednesday delivered a triple shock that unravelled April's optimism in a single
session. First: CPI printed at a 3-year high, confirming that Iran-driven energy
inflation has migrated fully into the broader price complex. Second: Trump
abandoned the peace-signal framing, threatening more strikes on Iran and pledging
the U.S. will be "attacking them very hard." Every market that had been pricing a
Hormuz resolution — gold, semis, construction, industrials — violently reversed.
Third: Oracle reported earnings, beat on revenue, then announced plans to raise
another $20 billion in debt for AI infrastructure — a signal of AI capex
confidence, but the leverage appetite spooked equity holders. ORCL -5.97% AH.

The sector rotation was surgical and brutal. Industrials (XLI -3.38%) was the
worst sector ETF, with CAT -6.4% and Quanta Services -5.93% leading the
Construction & Engineering liquidation — the Smart Money sector that had surged to
#1 nine days ago is now being dismantled. Semis followed hard: SOXX -3.67%, XSD
-3.55%, QCOM -6.92%, ON Semiconductor -5.84%, TER -5.86%. These were the April
darlings; they are now the crowded-exit problem. SMCI -27.98% was the single most
dramatic unwind — $5.5B in turnover on a name down 38% in just 5 days.

The defensive repositioning is also transparent. Consumer Staples (XLP +1.65%)
and Energy (XLE +1.50%) were the session's only SPDR winners. Gainers were
dominated by food retail (CASY +20.29%, KR +2.33%), tobacco (PM +2.50%), energy
(DVN +5.74%, COP +2.68%), and telecom (TMUS +3.39%, VZ +2.56%). These are
inflation-protection rotations, not momentum chases. The Smart Money rotation
out of Construction, Industrials, and Semis and into Fishing & Farming, Marine
Port Services, and Consumer Non-Cyclicals tells the story precisely.

One signal stands apart from the noise: RUT (-1.10%) materially outperformed SPX
(-1.62%) and NDX (-1.98%). Small-caps tend to be more domestically exposed and
less sensitive to semiconductor supply chain volatility — the relative resilience
here is a tell that the selloff is concentrated in megacap tech, not a broad
credit event. Russell breadth (883 advancers vs 969 decliners) was far better
than SPX (173 advancers vs 326 decliners, a 1:2 negative ratio).

Iran is the fulcrum of everything. Gold's -2.74% collapse to $4,092 looks
paradoxical on a risk-off day until you read the CPI headline and the Trump
"more strikes" framing simultaneously: if Trump's posture is offensive not
defensive, the flight-to-safety bid in gold gets replaced by dollar strength
and oil premium. WTI +3.60% to $91.80 confirms energy markets are pricing
active escalation, not imminent ceasefire. The geopolitical risk premium
hasn't collapsed — it has rotated from gold to crude.
