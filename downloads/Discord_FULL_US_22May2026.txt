📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Friday delivered a nuanced session: the Dow broke through to a fresh ATH while the Russell
2000 led the charge, signalling a rotation into cyclicals, small-caps, and value — precisely
the sectors that had lagged the April/early-May mega-cap tech surge. The character of this
advance is different from 01/05/26: that session was AAPL + software-narrowly-led; today
was broad-based with 1,008 of the Russell's constituents advancing vs 841 declining. That is
breadth expansion, not compression.

The session's dominant stock story was Dell. DELL +15% on no earnings release — the trigger
was the confluence of Dell Technologies World 2026 (Las Vegas, 19-21 May), multiple analyst
upgrades (Evercore ISI, JPMorgan, BofA all raising price targets), and the clean read-through
from HPE's strong server numbers earlier in the week. The market is pre-positioning for Dell's
28/05/26 earnings with the highest conviction in the stock since 2024. HPE +9% and SMCI +5%
both surged in sympathy, confirming this is a sector-wide re-rating of AI infrastructure
hardware, not a single-name event. The AI capex supercycle now has three simultaneous
confirmation pillars running: GPU (NVDA's $81.6B quarter), networking (CIBR, VIAV, CSCO),
and servers (DELL, HPE, SMCI).

The geopolitical backdrop is shifting — incrementally, not conclusively. Secretary of State
Rubio said "there's been a little bit of movement" and called the pace "good" while attending
a NATO summit in Sweden. Qatar has joined Pakistan and Oman as active mediators. Iran is
reviewing the latest US 15-point proposal. The sticking points remain structurally unchanged:
Iran's enriched uranium stockpile and Hormuz tolls. WTI's muted response (+0.15%, $100.35)
reflects this ambiguity — the market is not yet pricing a resolution, but it is pricing reduced
tail risk. Critically, WTI remains trapped below $102, which Goldman views as the threshold
above which energy-sector earnings guidance becomes materially impacted.

Thursday's quantum catalyst — the DOC's $2B grant programme with equity-stake provisions
for RGTI, QBTS, QUBT, IBM, INFQ and four others — continued its momentum on Friday. None of
the quantum names appear in the RS MOMO Top 20 from the Excel (their ADR/float profiles
make them thin-float names), but QUCY (Quantum Cyber NV) at RS #2 with a 3month return of
+351.95% reflects the sector's speculative intensity. The government's decision to take
minority equity stakes is structurally significant: it aligns federal technology validation
with shareholder upside, a new form of sovereign AI/quantum endorsement.

The semiconductor complex reasserted leadership — XSD +4.75% was the ONLY ETF above +3%.
AXTI +16.37% (3month +279%), NVTS +19.98%, AMBQ +6.67%, MXL -0.51% (consolidating) — the
mix confirms differentiated accumulation within semis. The 60day ROC leader remains
Communications & Networking (+38.78%), with the 10day leader Renewable Energy (+11.35%).
The sector profile is not a single-theme rally — it is multi-layer institutional accumulation.
