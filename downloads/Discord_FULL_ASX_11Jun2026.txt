📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Thursday 11 June delivered a measured -0.23% retreat to 8,633.2
for the XJO — a surface-calm number that belies the severity of
cross-currents. The night before, the US launched fresh strikes
on Iran, US CPI printed at a 3-year high of 4.2% YoY, and the
VIX spiked 12% to 22.2. That the ASX managed to hold within
20 points of flat is a structural story, not an accidental one:
the XJO's heavy weighting toward resources and energy — sectors
that benefit from a Hormuz premium — cushioned the blow that
hammered US tech.

The session's dominant feature was a sharp bifurcation. Energy
(XEJ +1.46%) and defensives (Consumer Staples XSJ +1.29%,
Healthcare XHJ +1.02%) absorbed the geopolitical bid, while
Financials (XFJ -1.45%) and Technology (XIJ -2.24%) bore the
brunt of the global risk-off. XFJ's decline is not purely
contagion — it reflects the specific RBA dynamic: three hikes
in 2026 have tightened bank lending margins at the edges
while mortgage stress provisions are rising in the Big 4's
forward guidance. Short sellers are active in ANZ, CBA,
NAB, and WBC according to market intelligence.

NXT (Nextdc) -4.23% to $14.50 is the session's clearest
US-derivative move. The SOX index is down 12% in five sessions,
and ASX data-centre names — which have tracked hyperscaler
capex enthusiasm — are unwinding in sympathy. The hyperscaler
capex-to-operating-cash-flow ratio is now projected to reach
100% (versus ~40% in 2023): that's positive long-term for
NXT demand, but the short-term risk-off is overwhelming the
structural thesis.

AAI (Alcoa CDI) -8.34% is the largest institutional loser in
the session, confirming the commodity/metals rout: aluminium
down 3.3%, copper down 2.5%, zinc down 2.5% in the prior
session (7d sheet data). The construction and infrastructure
metals complex is being double-hit — US CPI hawkishness
suppresses industrial demand expectations, while the
geopolitical risk premium is channelled entirely into energy.

On the positive side, MEI (Meteoric Resources) +10.34% and
CXO (Core Lithium) +8.33% confirm that the lithium recovery
thesis is resilient even in a risk-off day. CXO's 5day return
of -13.33% shows this is a volatile bounce rather than a
trend reversal, but the directional signal is intact.
