📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Wednesday was a broad, orderly risk-off session driven by a single macro
pivot: oil is back. WTI jumped +2.61% to $96.64 and Brent reclaimed $100
(+2.32% to $101.60) after Houthi rebels struck a Saudi Aramco refinery and
the US Navy attacked three Iranian oil tankers near Kharg Island in
retaliation for missiles aimed at US warships. This is the exact mirror image
of the spring "Iran peace proposal" trade — the geopolitical premium that was
being priced OUT four months ago is now being priced back IN, hard. OPEC+
left October output unchanged, removing the supply-side release valve.

The second-order consequence is what actually moved equities. Oil back above
$100 "fuels fresh inflation fears at a crucial time for interest rates"
(MarketWatch) — and it lands 48 hours before two inflation prints that the
Fed's next decision hinges on. That fed a bond sell-off; rising yields fall
heaviest on the longest-duration, most capital-hungry equity. Hence the day's
defining divergence sat INSIDE technology, not across sectors.

XLK closed flat (0.00%) — a number that conceals a violent internal rotation.
The software and application layer ripped: Cloudflare +10.51%, Datadog +7.15%,
Meta +6.55%, Fair Isaac +5.35%, HPE +5.12%, Akamai +4.87%, DigitalOcean
+4.72%, IBM +3.38%, Okta +3.07%. Simultaneously the AI-infrastructure and
data-centre build-out names were taken apart: Vertiv -9.61%, CoreWeave -4.90%,
IonQ -5.76%, AST SpaceMobile -5.60%, Rocket Lab -4.25%. This is not "AI down."
It is a quality rotation WITHIN the AI trade — capital exiting the cash-hungry,
long-duration physical layer (power, cooling, neocloud, quantum, space) and
concentrating into profitable, cash-generative software. When yields rise, the
market stops paying for capex promises and starts paying for free cash flow.
Cramer's "be a little more reserved on data-centre stocks" comment on the tape
is the narrative catching up to the flow. Note the semis split the difference —
Marvell +4.26%, Astera Labs +4.05%, MACOM +4.12% and Teradyne +3.13% held the
green side, so this is a capex-intensity sort, not a semiconductor exit.

Breadth confirms the risk-off was broad, not surgical. SPX advancers 98 vs
decliners 402 — a 1:4.1 ratio on a day the index only fell half a percent, with
177 new 20day lows against just 20 new highs (roughly 9:1). Stocks above the
20MA collapsed to 21%, T2108 slid to 31.2%, and RUT printed 1,518 decliners.
This is the kind of internal deterioration that a -0.48% headline badly
understates — the median stock had a far worse day than the cap-weighted index.

Earnings were a consumer/retail-heavy grab-bag with violent single-name
dispersion: ServiceTitan -29.98%, Casey's General -14.24%, Braze -21.73% and
Chewy -10.83% blew up, while Signet +23.96%, Oddity +26.53% and Academy Sports
+14.40% ripped. The read is bifurcation, not direction — the consumer is
rewarding specific execution and punishing any miss with no mercy in a
higher-rate tape. Apple held its 2026 product event (folding iPhone Duo,
iPhone 18 Pro, added AI) — a slow-burn catalyst, not a session driver.
