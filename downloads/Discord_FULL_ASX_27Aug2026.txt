📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Thursday 27 August was a reporting-season reality check: the ASX 200 fell
-0.98% to 9,038.2 with breadth deeply negative (43 advancers vs
149 decliners, a 0.29:1 ratio) and only two of eleven sectors green. This
was not a macro sell-off — the US closed flat overnight and volatility is
low — it was the market re-pricing a heavy load of FY26 results.

The clearest theme was a fund-manager de-rating. Magellan (MFG -14.03%),
Generation Development (GDG -15.36%) and Centuria (CNI -10.53%) all missed
on results, dragging Qualitas (QAL -7.30%) in sympathy. Crucially, this hit
the investment-management SUB-industry, not the banks: XFJ fell only -0.68%
because the Big 4 held firm. The read-through is idiosyncratic earnings
disappointment in the funds complex, not a broad financials de-rating.

The bright spot was Bapcor (BAP +41.76%), the session's standout. A
beaten-down auto-aftermarket turnaround, its FY26 result (Appendix 4E,
08:51) showed H2 segment-sales traction that cleared a very depressed bar
despite a large statutory impairment — enough to trigger a violent re-rate
in a heavily-sold-down name (Jefferies upgraded to Buy). This flowed
straight into the Smart Money table, lifting Auto Parts to rank 3 (+37).
Ramsay (RHC +13.72%) and Qantas (QAN +4.77%, new business-class seats)
were the large-cap results winners.

Consumer Discretionary (XDJ -3.18%) was the worst sector as Wesfarmers
(WES -4.58%), ARB (-4.86%) and Eagers (APE -5.08%) all sold on results
— solid numbers priced for perfection. Info Tech (XIJ -2.38%) was anchored
by WiseTech (WTC -3.28%). Gold names gave back recent gains as bullion
eased (-0.53%): VAU -3.00%, GMD -2.91% — a pause in a still-intact
3-month leadership theme, not a trend break.
