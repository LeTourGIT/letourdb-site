Thursday 30 July delivered an orderly -0.78% pullback to 8,967.7, the ASX absorbing an
ugly Wall Street session (SPX -1.52%, Dow -2.19%, NQ-100 into correction) after the US
struck Iran and the Fed held rates with three dissenting hike votes. The calm response was
earned: US futures had already stabilised by the ASX close, so the local tape repriced
without panic. But beneath a benign index number, breadth was poor — 36 advancers to 160
decliners on the XJO, 16 stocks down >4% versus one up.

Materials (XMJ -1.59%) was the clear laggard, and the internals matter more than the sector
print: this was small-cap mining and uranium carnage — LOT -26.09%, LTR -10.86%, ARU -7.89%,
RSG -7.22% — swamping GREEN prints from the liquid large-caps RIO +1.83%, MIN +3.90% and
PLS +2.68%. That divergence is the tell. With iron ore drifting toward one-year lows (~US$98/t
on the Simandou ramp and China property weakness), the big miners did NOT rise on fundamentals;
they rose on defensive/oversold rotation into liquidity. The pain was in the speculative tier,
amplified by the late-July quarterly reporting window (352 announcements, 293 price-sensitive).

The two green sectors told the macro story cleanly. Info Tech (XIJ +0.91%) tracked the offshore
capex read-through (Microsoft +8%, Samsung AI-chip beat), and Energy (XEJ +0.32%) rode the
Hormuz crude spike — VEA +2.20% [H] and Ampol's 'Resilient Supply Chain Underpins Earnings
Growth' note (09:12, PS) are the ASX's direct refiner-margin windfall, echoing the ABC report
of Ampol margins jumping 250% on the Strait closure.

The single loudest capital-flow signal came from Smart Money: Insurance Funds rocketed to #1
from #79 nine sessions ago (ROC +17.24) — a genuine institutional surge with GDG the listed
ASX proxy. Oil & Gas Refining sits entrenched at #2, confirming the energy-margin theme is a
flow, not a one-day headline. Consumer names bucked the tape idiosyncratically: DMP +9.08%,
SGR +8.00%, GYG +2.64% [H] — Restaurants & Bars is #3 in Smart Money.
