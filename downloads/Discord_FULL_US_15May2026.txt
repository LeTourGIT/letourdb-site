📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Friday delivered a broad institutional risk-off flush — all four indices closed lower,
VIX +6.78% to 18.43, and Treasury yields hit their highest levels in a year as two
structural macro events collided: Kevin Warsh officially assumed the Fed chair on the same
day Trump publicly declared he is "losing patience" with Iran following his Beijing summit
with Xi Jinping. WTI surged +4.14% to $108.99. The 10-year yield spiked to 4.595%, its
highest since early 2025; the 30-year breached 5.127%, a level not seen since 2007.

The macro read: Thursday's ATH at SPX 7,501.24 was the cycle's reward for optimism about
Trump-Xi progress on Iran. Friday's selloff was the market adjusting when that optimism
failed to materialize as a concrete resolution. Trump told Fox that Xi would like Hormuz
reopened and agreed not to supply Iran with military equipment — but that is geopolitical
alignment on direction, not a ceasefire deal. Energy markets, properly, did not accept
"aligned intentions" as resolution premium. WTI breaking higher negates the April 30 peace
proposal bounce and forces the market back into the Iran-inflation-yields chain: oil up →
CPI up → rate hike odds up → growth multiples down.

The Warsh transition adds a policy uncertainty layer. At the CME FedWatch, hike probability
climbed from 1% a month ago to ~45% for somewhere in 2026. CPI already at a three-year
high of 3.8% YoY; PPI running hotter. Warsh's first FOMC meeting is June 16-17, and
markets have now fully priced out any cut this year, with a non-trivial hike probability
entering the forward curve. This is the most consequential change in Fed leadership in a
generation, arriving into the hottest inflation environment in three years. The yield signal
at 30Y above 5.12% is not noise — bond vigilantes are repricing the Warsh era.

The metals rout was the cleanest expression of the rate-hike narrative. Gold down 3.19%,
silver down 8.57%, platinum down 6.23%, copper down 3.09%, gold miners GDX down 7.03% —
the entire precious metals complex is repricing to a higher-for-longer world where the
safe-haven real-return calculation inverts. This is not geopolitical selling (gold normally
rises in conflict); it is rate-driven selling, confirming the market's primary fear is not
war but inflation.

The session's sole structural positive was solar energy. SEDG +22.93% and ENPH +10.16%
against a -1.24% SPX represents the largest positive divergence of any macro-linked theme
this week. Section 25D residential solar tax credit expiration in 2027 is pulling forward
installation demand, creating an earnings acceleration story independent of the Iran
narrative. Renewable Fuels as #1 Smart Money industry and Renewable Energy Equipment & Services
at #6 confirm the capital flow: the energy transition trade is being bought as a hedge
against the fossil-fuel-inflation cycle, not just as an ESG play. This is a structural re-rating.
