📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Two macro shocks hit simultaneously and pulled the market in opposite
directions along the growth-value axis. First, the June Iran ceasefire
collapsed into open conflict over the weekend — Iran's Persian Gulf Strait
Authority declared the Strait of Hormuz 'unnavigable' while Washington
insisted it stays open, and Trump floated a 20% toll on Hormuz cargo and
restarted the blockade. WTI ripped +5.03% to $78.03, Brent +5.25% to $83.24,
and USO +8.79%. Roughly 20% of seaborne crude moves through that chokepoint —
this is a genuine supply-side repricing, not a headline blip.

Second, the multi-week AI-capex valuation reset deepened. TSMC's record
revenue failed to revive the AI trade (MarketWatch), SK Hynix HBM-expansion
doubts and a hawkish Fed under Warsh — with July rate-hike odds rising as he
heads to Capitol Hill — combined to gut semiconductors. XLK -2.42% was the
worst sector by a wide margin; SOXX -4.77%, XSD -4.62%. Single-name damage
was severe: APP -12.65%, SNDK -12.63%, ALAB -12.33%, MRVL -7.75%, INTC -6.12%,
ORCL -6.47%, CRWV -6.27%. This is a valuation/rate story, not a demand story —
HBM remains sold out through 2027 and Q2 semi earnings are set to grow ~131%.

The rotation was textbook. Capital fled long-duration AI infrastructure and
bought the oil complex and defensives. Energy was the ONLY green sector. The
four ±3% up-ETFs were ALL energy (XOP, IEO, CRAK, XLE); every down-ETF was
semis, uranium, silver, rare earth, solar or EV — the entire clean-energy /
long-duration-growth basket. Refiners led equities (VLO/PSX/MPC +5%) as diesel
crack spreads hit records on 5m bbl/day of global refining outages.

The breadth signal is the day's most important nugget. SPX closed -0.79% yet
advancers BEAT decliners 271/229 — an INVERSE cap-weight distortion. The
mega-cap tech that dominates index weight fell hardest and dragged the tape
red, but the median S&P name rose. T2108 at 65.2%, 66% above 200MA, 92 stocks
+25% on the quarter. Beneath a scary-looking index print, the broad market is
healthy and rotating — not deleveraging. The tell to watch: whether energy
strength can offset tech weight, or the AI unwind broadens past chips.
