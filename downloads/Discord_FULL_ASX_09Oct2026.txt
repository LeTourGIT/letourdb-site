📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
The ASX 200 recovered most of Thursday's loss, adding 0.64% (+55.7
points) to 8,716.6, and the rally was broad: 140 advancers to 48
decliners (2.9:1), with 10 of 11 sectors green. What matters is who
led. Technology (XIJ +2.23%), Consumer Discretionary (+2.16%), Real
Estate (+1.84%) and Utilities (+1.75%) carried the index, while
Materials finished flat. That is a domestic-growth and yield
rotation, and it came on a night when the Nasdaq fell 1.25%. The ASX
took its cue from the US equal-weight index (+0.60%) and the 5bp
drop in the US 10-year, not from Nvidia.

Resources split three ways. Gold producers rallied with the metal at
US$4,219 (DPM +6.23% on Q3 production, WAF +5.43%, NEM +2.71%, EVN
+2.26%). Energy-transition metals were liquidated: lithium carbonate
futures fell 4% to their lowest since December 2025 (PLS -5.41%, SRL
-11.05%, CXO -7.55%, VUL -6.89%), and uranium fell hard with no
company news (BOE -9.93%, BMN -7.37%, DYL -7.11%, PDN -3.67%). Iron
ore near US$91 weighed on FMG (-2.85%). The XMJ's flat close
averages three very different trades.

The stock-level message is about expectations. EOS opened +15% (and
traded as high as +22%) on a A$700m counter-drone contract, then
closed -4.25%. That is a bearish outside reversal on a genuinely
large contract, and the conditions (including a £74m bank guarantee)
gave holders a reason to sell into the spike. GYG reported comps of
+7.1% (vs +4.0% a year ago) and extended its buyback, yet closed
-2.25%; the stock had already run +5% in 5 days and sits in the
hottest 60day industry. MGH (-6.65%, -32% over 5 days) was halted,
then released with a Firmus update after the AI data-centre group
withdrew its A$43.7bn-valuation float.

Structurally, nothing has been repaired yet. All four indices still
print a Bearish regime with 5day and 20day trends Down. Only 26.5%
of XJO stocks are above their 50MA and 37% above their 200MA, and
T2108 is just 30%. New 20day lows (27) still outnumber new highs
(17), even on a 2.9:1 up day. XJO has reclaimed its 10day and 20day
MAs but sits 2.46% below the 50MA (~8,936) and 1.25% below the 200MA
(~8,827). 8,827 is the first real test, and a close above it would
be the first higher-timeframe repair signal. Small caps are the weak
link: XSO is 7.19% below its 200MA with an RSI of 37.

Into next week: the MGH/Firmus fallout, BOE's September quarterly
(call notice lodged after the close), the gold quarterlies arriving
early, and BOQ results on 15/10. With Brent at US$103 and the RBA on
03/11 (~27% hike odds), the rate-sensitive leadership needs yields
to cooperate. A rebound in the US 10-year is the main risk to
today's rotation.
