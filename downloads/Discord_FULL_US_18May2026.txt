📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Monday's session was defined by two structural repricing events colliding
simultaneously: the NextEra/Dominion mega-merger and the deteriorating
Iran/Hormuz macro backdrop. Neither was in Friday's close. Both matter
for the week ahead.

The NextEra-Dominion deal — $66.8B all-stock, largest utility tie-up in
history — is fundamentally a declaration that power, not chips, is now
the binding constraint on AI buildout. PJM grid capacity prices for
2026–27 were approved at $329/MW-day, up from $29/MW-day the prior year.
That 11x repricing is the structural signal: AI hyperscalers need
guaranteed electrons before they need guaranteed bandwidth. The deal gives
NextEra dominant control over Virginia's data center corridor — the
single most AI-dense power market in the world. Dominion +11.5%, the
correct reaction. NextEra -4.0%, also correct: dilutive all-stock deal,
regulatory risk extends to 2028, $6.5B termination fee exposure.
The market read the deal intelligently. Watch Bloom Energy (BE +2.5%
in RS list, #12) and GEV (Industrial power) as secondary beneficiaries
of the AI-power thesis repricing.

On the macro side, conditions deteriorated materially from the May 1
peace-signal session. Trump's "Clock is Ticking" warning Sunday and the
UAE nuclear plant drone strike created a weekend risk premium that opened
futures -0.49% for Dow and -0.85% SPX. Iran's latest proposal to
Pakistani mediators was framed as excluding nuclear matters — directly
contradicting the US demand for enrichment moratorium. The IEA's Fatih
Birol told the G7 Paris meeting that commercial oil inventories are weeks
away from depletion; strategic reserve releases are running at 2.5M
bbl/day but "are not endless." That is the clearest official warning of
a structural supply crisis yet. Yet WTI eased intraday on the new Iranian
proposal. The contradiction — peace signals vs depleting inventory — is
the core tension running into next week. OIH +3.04% as the market prices
continued supply constraint regardless of diplomatic outcome.

The NVDA earnings setup (Wednesday) is the week's primary market-wide
catalyst. KeyBanc raised its price target to $300 on 150-200k
Blackwell GPU quarterly shipments. The NDX -0.45% vs SPX -0.07%
gap is almost entirely semi-complex de-risking pre-earnings. Positions
are being lightened, not exited — VIX -3.31% to 17.82 confirms
orderly hedging, not panic. If NVDA beats and guides up, the 5day
downtrend in tech breaks immediately. If it misses, the -6.92% 5day
in SOXX has further to run.

Healthcare absorbed two company-specific shocks independently:
REGN -11.4% (Phase 3 melanoma failure for fianlimab) and UNH -4.8%
(continued de-rating from prior-week Medicare/CMS concerns). These
are not sector-wide signals — REGN is clinical-stage risk crystallisation,
UNH is regulatory pressure thesis. XLV +0.43% confirms the Street is
correctly treating these as idiosyncratic, not systemic.
