📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Monday 29 June opened the week with the ASX 200 +0.68% to 8,823.4 and the All Ords
+0.70% to 9,026.9 — but this was a narrow, catalyst-led advance rather than a broad
risk-on day. Healthcare and Technology did the heavy lifting while Real Estate and
Utilities fell, the latter two on mechanical ex-dividend rather than any fundamental
de-rating (a large block of REITs and infrastructure names traded ex-div today).

Neuren Pharmaceuticals (NEU) +36.07% to A$16.60 was the session's defining move — a
European regulatory approval for trofinetide (DAYBUE) re-rated the stock and pulled the
entire Healthcare index (XHJ +2.12%) higher. This is a fundamental binary event, not
technical momentum: a binary regulatory de-risking that opens an ex-North-America
commercial pathway Neuren had retained. Clarity Pharmaceuticals (CU6 +15.70%), 4DMedical
(4DX +10.34%) and Pro Medicus (PME +4.54%, new 20day high) rode the sector's coattails.

Technology (XIJ +4.04%) was the top sector outright, led by Wisetech (WTC +7.19%),
Life360 (360 +11.64%, new 20day high) and Nuix (NXL +7.11%). The cross-market read is
notable: this strength came DESPITE a soft US tech tape (Nasdaq -0.24% Friday, an AI/chip
selloff and a reported OpenAI IPO delay), so ASX tech is trading on its own SaaS/
logistics-software thesis rather than importing US semiconductor sentiment.

The losers' board is dominated by ex-dividend names: APA Group (-5.20%), Transurban
(-4.42%), Dexus (-4.17%), Growthpoint (-5.63%), Digico (-5.06%), Centuria Office
(-4.30%) and Region Group (-3.64%) all traded ex-div Monday. Read these as price
adjustments, not selling pressure. The genuine weakness sits in beaten-up small-cap
resources — Core Lithium (-3.92%, 5day -18.33%), Minerals 260 (-4.12%, 5day -17.26%)
and Dateline (-3.57%, 5day -22.86%) — confirming the small-cap/XSO structural softness.

The structural read: XTO (large-caps) sits above all four MAs while XSO sits below all
four. The index is being carried by mega-caps and a handful of catalyst stocks; T2108
on the XAO is just 44.6% and only 169 of ~490 All-Ords names are above their 200MA.
The 'Bullish' regime label is real on short-term MAs but the breadth underneath does not
yet confirm it. With the RBA on hold (next meeting 28-29 July) and the Hormuz oil-risk
premium unwinding, the near-term macro backdrop is benign; the test is breadth, not macro.
