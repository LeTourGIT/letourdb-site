📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Wednesday 02/09/2026 was an imported risk-off session: the ASX 200 fell −0.97% to 8,978.4,
tracking a global bond rout that drove developed-market yields to multi-decade highs and a fresh
US-Iran escalation that pushed Brent above US$95. This was not a domestic story in origin — it
was Sydney wearing Wall Street's Tuesday (SPX −0.71%, Nasdaq −1.03%) with a local twist.

That twist was the Q2 GDP print: +2.1% y/y, beating expectations and reviving the rate-hike
thread into the 29 September RBA. The combination is toxic for long-duration equity — higher
global yields plus a domestic growth beat that hardens the case for another RBA move. Info
Technology (XIJ −3.35%) was the clearest expression: SLX −8.57%, ELS −8.72% as discount rates rose.

Materials (XMJ −3.08%) was the heaviest single drag and the day's real damage. It was broad
within the sector: uranium (LOT −9.62%, BMN −9.34%), copper (CSC −8.89%, FFM −7.69%), lithium
(CXO −9.21%) and a gold-miner pullback all at once. Critically, gold's structural RS leadership
did NOT break — 7 of the top-10 institutional RS names remain gold (VAU, GMD, RSG, ALK, RRL, PRU)
— today was profit-taking inside an intact uptrend, not a top. EQR −9.41% even fell on RECORD
A$55m revenue: a classic sell-the-news tell in a risk-off tape.

The green was defensive and idiosyncratic. Consumer Staples (+0.81%) and Financials (+0.25%,
the only cyclical green) held, carried by IAG +5.46% and CGF +5.16% — insurers/diversifieds that
read higher-for-longer as a tailwind. GNC +11.02% (a new 20day high) and SHV +6.17% gave the
agriculture complex a genuine bid, and KAO +151.7% on a copper discovery led a thin spec board.

Breadth is the warning: XJO advancers/decliners 50/143 (1:2.9), 42 new 20day lows against just
7 highs, and only 45% of XJO names above their 200MA. The medium-term structure holds for now,
but small-caps sit below both key MAs and the tape has clearly rolled short-term. Real test:
whether 8,900 holds as US yields and oil settle, or whether this is the first leg of a deeper
mean-reversion into the RBA.
