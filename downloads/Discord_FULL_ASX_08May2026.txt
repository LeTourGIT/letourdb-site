📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Friday 8 May delivered the ASX 200's biggest single-session decline in seven
weeks — a −1.51% fall to 8,744.4 — erasing Thursday's +0.96% rally and
most of the week's gains in a single afternoon. The catalyst was dual: an
overnight Wall Street retreat driven by Hormuz ceasefire doubts, and the
continued digestion of Tuesday's RBA hike. Tehran's signal that the Hormuz
reopening plan was "unrealistic" broke the peace-deal narrative that had
supported ASX financials and consumer names through Monday to Thursday.
US forces struck Qeshm Port and Bandar Abbas in self-defence strikes,
re-injecting geopolitical risk premium that the market had briefly priced out.

The breadth collapse was the defining feature: XJO Adv/Dec came in at
35/158 (0.22:1) — the mirror image of last Friday's bounce. All 11 sectors
finished in the red. Financials (−2.25%) and Real Estate (−2.23%) were the
worst performers — the direct expression of RBA's decision still percolating
through mortgage-linked balance sheets and income-sensitive property trusts.
The Big 4 banks shed 1.2%–3.8% individually. March trade data compounded the
gloom: the first deficit since December 2017 as imports hit record peaks
(energy import bill surging) while exports fell.

Earnings-day stories created pockets of resilience. SKS Technologies (+6.49%)
secured a $22m Buildcorp contract and revealed a tender pipeline of $1.25bn
(+120% since February), with data centre tenders now $1bn — validating the
IT Services Smart Money rotation. QBE reiterated FY26 guidance with premium
growth ahead of targets. REA Group Q3 missed near-term but lowered cost
guidance and April listings +19% YoY provided forward comfort. MQG hit a
20D High on full-year results day.

The week's net result is still constructive in context: XJO is up ~0.5% for
the week, its first positive weekly close in over a month. But the RBA's
triple-hike cycle has embedded a structural headwind for rate-sensitives,
and Hormuz ceasefire uncertainty makes each day's session hostage to the
next Iran headline.
