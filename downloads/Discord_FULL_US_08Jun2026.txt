📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Monday's session was a semiconductor rehabilitation story. Three sessions after
Intel hit its deepest drawdown of 2026 and the chip complex suffered its worst day
since April 2025, the sector mounted a coordinated reversal: INTC +11.19%, MU +9.87%,
MRVL +9.63%, KLAC +9.27%, ALAB +9.23%, AMAT +8.64%. SOXX gained +5.87% and XSD
+4.33% — the two broadest semiconductor ETFs both above +3% — confirming institutional
re-entry, not speculative short-covering. The catalyst chain: Iran ceasing strikes on
Israel over the weekend removed the geopolitical tail risk that had compressed
risk appetite since Thursday's NFP shock, and the VIX collapsed -12% in response.

The breadth picture remains structurally challenged, however. SPX closed +0.30% on
180 advancers vs 319 decliners — a 1.77:1 negative ratio on an up day. This is
cap-weight distortion, not broad recovery. Technology and its constituent mega-caps
are doing the mathematical heavy lifting while the average SPX member finished red.
Five of the eleven SPDR sector ETFs were negative on the day. This is not a market
breadth recovery — it is a market-cap recovery, and the distinction matters for
assessing whether Thursday's damage has been genuinely repaired.

Iran halting strikes is the event unlocking risk appetite, but it is not a peace
settlement. The geopolitical overlay remains active: Netanyahu confirmed the war
isn't over, Trump says negotiations are continuing, and Iraq/UAE are racing to build
alternative pipelines around Hormuz. Goldman's 4–6 month normalisation framework
still applies. WTI at $95 (+0.72%) vs the May highs above $102 reflects partial
relief, not resolution. Energy ETFs (XLE +1.14%, XOP +1.45%) are recovering but
remain structurally below the Hormuz-risk premium peak.

The INTC +11.19% move demands context. Intel pre-market on Tuesday was +11.93%
(data file), meaning institutional accumulation continued overnight. This is not
a mean-reversion trade in the conventional sense — Intel at 105% gain in 1 year
and now re-testing Semiconductor Equipment territory reflects a genuine AI datacenter
re-rating driven by foundry capacity and x86 server demand. The $554B market cap
at $110 gives scale; INTC's +11% adds roughly $54B in market cap in a single session.
For context, that's a Full Marvell ($230B) added in one day to the Intel balance sheet.

Software bifurcation was the session's underbelly. TWLO -5.95%, AKAM -4.99%,
ADBE -2.57%, CIEN -4.41% — enterprise software names continued Thursday's selling
even as infrastructure chips rebounded. CIEN specifically is a sell-the-news post
its guidance cycle, and TWLO's -5.95% on no fresh catalyst confirms that the
software re-rating that carried WCLD +6.38% on May 1st is now being unwound in
the higher-interest-rate, no-rate-cut-narrative environment post-NFP.
