📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
No large-cap earnings anchored Monday, so the session was pure macro-flow —
and three catalysts fired the same direction: DE-RISK DURATION. The 10-year
Treasury yield touched 5.00% (first since 2023) with markets pricing ~90% odds
of a 25bp Fed hike Wednesday under Chair Warsh, defending inflation-fighting
credibility after a hot August CPI. A higher discount rate is lethal to the
longest-duration equities — and nothing is longer-duration than a parabolic
AI-hardware complex priced on 2027 cash flows.

The result was the cleanest intra-tech rotation of the cycle. The AI-INFRA
trade was violently de-rated — SOXX -5.63%, XSD -6.12% (both EXTREME) — with
optical/connectivity leaders GLW -13.7%, TER -13.3%, COHR -12.7%, MTSI -12.8%
and ALAB -11.7% leading the whole tape lower. But capital did NOT leave tech;
it rotated DOWN THE STACK into asset-light software/cyber, where the names
monetise AI rather than fund its capex: CIBR +5.99%, IGV +5.04% were the ONLY
two ETFs above +3%, and the gainers board was a clean sweep — ZS +16.5%,
RBRK +15.6%, CRWD +13.9%, PANW +13.1%, OKTA +12.0%. The 'SaaSpocalypse' fear
inverted: for two years software was the AI victim; Monday it became the hedge.

The defensive complement was textbook. XLV +1.45% and XLP +1.25% led the SPDR
board, and the RS leaderboard has swung hard to defensive-growth — 13 of the
top 20 RS names are Healthcare, eight of them biotech. Smart Money confirms:
Managed Healthcare vaulted to rank 6 (from rank 58 nine days ago). This is
capital buying earnings resilience and low duration into a hawkish Fed.

The geopolitical leg hardened the stagflation read. Saudi Arabia shut its
East-West pipeline — the workaround for the Strait of Hormuz — after attacks,
and a tanker was struck in the Strait itself. Crude popped toward $103 before
settling near $102 (USO +1.32%), and the energy-rotation signal broadened to
five industries in Smart Money's top 15. This is the MIRROR IMAGE of 1 May:
that day oil FELL on an Iran peace proposal; Monday oil ROSE on Hormuz
escalation. Same machinery, opposite driver — resolution flipped to supply shock.

One genuinely constructive point: daily breadth was POSITIVE (SPX 280 adv vs
219 dec) even as the index fell — a mega-cap-led decline where the median stock
held. But do not over-read it: only 25% of SPX names are above their 20-day MA,
and new 20-day lows swamp highs 95 to 31. The orderly Monday close sits on a
deteriorating foundation, and Wednesday's FOMC is the binary catalyst.
