📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Thursday 18 June closed -0.62% to 8,911.1 in a textbook risk-off session — but
the print flatters a broadly red tape. XJO advancers trailed decliners 56 to 133
(137/310 on the All Ords); large-cap defensives (Staples +0.73%, Health +0.35%)
cushioned the headline. This is a market rotating defensively, not capitulating.

The catalyst was offshore: Kevin Warsh's hawkish Fed debut lifted the US yield
path and repriced ASX rate-sensitives — IT (XIJ -1.36%) the weakest sector,
Real Estate (XRE -1.15%) close behind. The local read: ASX fell as a US rate
rise this year looks increasingly likely.

The commodity complex was the other story. The signed US-Iran deal (Hormuz
reopening) + IEA 2027 glut call knocked WTI to ~$74 (Energy 5day -8.21%). Gold
consolidated (-0.31%) and the gold-mining cohort was dumped: OBM (ASX200) -7.53%,
PNR (ASX200) -6.56%, plus a tail of specs (SVL -13.8%, AEE -13.9%). Materials
(XMJ -1.27%) was the worst major — but +7.93% over 5days, so profit-taking off
strength, not a broken thesis.

Uranium bucked the whole tape on the Western nuclear-fuel supply-chain theme:
CXU +33.33%, PEN +19.32%, AEU +12.5%. Speculative tier, read through [Spec] filter,
but the cluster is the signal. Macro: RBA held 4.35% Tuesday (unanimous), CPI
eased to 4.2%, Bullock kept the door open — Financials' +3.42% 5day shows banks
still treated as the higher-rate-plateau winner. Falling oil is the quiet
game-changer: sub-$80 WTI weakens the inflation case behind the third hike.
