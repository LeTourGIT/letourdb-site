📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Wednesday 10 June's ASX session produced a +0.57% advance to 8,653.3 on
two dominant themes: a $7.7 billion takeover bid for Steadfast Group (SDF)
and a violent sector rotation out of resources and technology into consumer
defensives, with the Consumer Staples (XSJ +3.87%) and Consumer Disc.
(XDJ +3.58%) sectors posting their strongest single-session performances
in weeks. The rotation is the macro story: as Iran-US military hostilities
flared overnight, market participants re-assessed risk positioning and sold
the commodity/growth complex while buying defensives and yield plays.

SDF +36.2% was the session's undisputed standout. Dragoneer Investment Group
(US private equity) mounted a $6/share indicative offer valuing the insurance
broker at $7.7B, a premium of ~52% to SDF's pre-halt price of ~$3.95. The
board recommends acceptance. The institutional-grade Smart Money signal here
is the Insurance sector surge in today's rotation data — SDF's deal lit up
the entire Multiline Insurance & Brokers industry, taking it to #2 in the
rotation table from #41 yesterday in a single session.

Materials (XMJ -1.14%) and IT (XIJ -2.34%) were the session's drag anchors.
Gold fell into an official bear market during the period, down more than 20%
from January's record peak as the strong USD suppresses metals broadly. The
gold stock casualties (Northern Star -3.5%, Evolution Mining in prior session)
are a direct read-through to Australia's gold miner cohort. The XIJ print
mirrors the US tech rotation — when NDX drops 2%, ASX tech reprices.

The domestic macro backdrop adds a layer of complexity. Consumer confidence
fell 3.5% in June per the Westpac-MI survey, logging its fourth decline this
year, with inflation and borrowing costs still the primary drag. Yet the same
consumers are sending Consumer Staples to the top of today's sector table —
defensive necessity spending (Coles, Woolworths, Metcash) benefits when
confidence falls because the market reprices income reliability. COL
(+4.95%), WOW (+3.15%), and WES (+4.25%) powered XSJ and XDJ higher.

Employment data is the key context for the RBA: April unemployment hit 4.5%,
providing the board with a clear rationale for June's near-certain hold.
