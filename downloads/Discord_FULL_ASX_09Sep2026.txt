📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Wednesday 9 Sept was a soft, orderly down day — XJO -0.11% to 8,911.4, XAO -0.13%
to 9,102.9 — that masked a decisive rotation beneath the index line. This was a
resources-and-energy session: Energy (XEJ +1.73%) and Materials (XMJ +1.51%) were
the only two sectors with real green, while every defensive and rate-sensitive
group bled. The trigger was offshore — Houthi strikes on Saudi energy infrastructure
drove WTI to a three-month high — and the ASX chose to trade the commodity, not the
equity weakness the US Dow (-1.18%) had just printed.

The institutional standout was Austal (ASB +7.13%). A brief pre-lunch trading pause
(09:56) resolved into a price-sensitive announcement (10:42): a Wildcat-led syndicate
lodged a non-binding US$1.25-1.35bn bid for Austal USA — cash-free, debt-free, subject
to four weeks' due diligence — topping Korea's Hanwha (which already owns 19.9%). With
the US arm alone valued near Austal's entire ~A$1.95bn market cap, this is a genuine
value-unlock contest, not a rumour bounce. It is the clearest announcement-driven move
of the session.

At the other extreme, Echo IQ (EIQ) fell ~50% after the US FDA issued a
Not-Substantially-Equivalent determination on its EchoSolv HF heart-failure software —
a binary regulatory knockback (its EchoSolv AS product remains cleared). That single
name did much of the damage to Health Care (XHJ -1.50%), the session's worst sector.
Elsewhere the red was rotational: SEK -5.6% and APE -5.0% (both ASX100) carried
Comm Services and Consumer Discretionary lower, the cleanest read on a household under
rate and cost-of-living strain.

Breadth confirmed the caution behind the shallow index move: XJO advancers/decliners
ran 75/123 (0.6:1), new 20-day lows (38) swamped new highs (5), and only 43.5% of XJO
names hold their 200MA. The rate backdrop underwrites the defensiveness — the RBA meets
28-29 Sept (20 days out) with ASX futures pricing ~66% odds of a +25bp hike to 4.60%
from 4.35%, and the RBA deputy governor reiterated inflation work isn't done.
