📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Kevin Warsh's first meeting as Fed chair delivered the hawkish repricing the
bond market had been warning about for months. The Committee held the funds
rate at 3.50-3.75% unanimously — the easy part, fully expected — but the
Summary of Economic Projections did the work: nine of eighteen officials now
project at least one 2026 HIKE, lifting the median year-end dot to 3.80% from
3.40% in March. Warsh himself abstained from submitting a dot, stripped the
"easing bias" from a deliberately "curt," truncated statement, declined to
offer forward guidance, and hammered one word repeatedly — "price stability."
CME FedWatch swung to a 60.7% probability of an October hike.

The transmission was textbook and immediate: the 2-year yield ripped +16bp to
4.21% (a one-year high), the dollar index gained ~1% for its best session in
nearly a year, and every duration-sensitive asset took the hit. This is the
single most important macro→sector→stock chain of the quarter: hawkish dots →
higher real yields + stronger USD → long-duration cash flows discounted harder
→ high-multiple software and the precious-metals complex sold, while
hard-asset, near-term-cash-flow plays (semis, defense, power infrastructure)
were bid as the relative-value winners.

The rotation inside the tape was far more violent than the index losses imply.
The Software & Online Services cohort was the epicentre: APP -6.93%, NOW
-5.77%, ACN -5.75%, META -5.44%, RDDT -5.38%, ADBE -5.33%, plus the exchange
operators NDAQ -6.72% and ICE -4.74% as the SpaceX-IPO-fade pulled the air out
of Comm Services (XLC -2.78%, worst sector). On the other side of the trade,
the semiconductor and AI-hardware complex was the ONLY green pocket of size:
SOXX +1.44%, AMAT +4.35%, AVGO +4.30%, MRVL +3.90%, INTC +3.46%, ALAB +3.59%,
WDC +4.56%, DELL +3.77% — joined by aerospace/defense (RTX +3.11%, RKLB
+3.20%, ITA +1.37%), power/AI-infra (GE Vernova +6.77%, VRT +6.00%), fintech
(HOOD +8.78%) and biotech (MRNA +11.55%, IBB +1.17%). When rates spike, capital
does not flee equities wholesale — it migrates to where the cash flows are
nearest. Today it migrated to silicon and steel.

The Smart Money rotation board confirms the same defensive value pivot one
layer up: the new top of the leaderboard is Marine Port Services, Entertainment
Production, Managed Healthcare, Personal Products, Household Products, Pension
Funds, Life & Health Insurance and Restaurants — a roll-call of low-beta,
short-duration, dividend-bearing industries that have surged 50-130 ranks over
nine sessions. This is institutions pre-positioning for a higher-for-longer
regime, not chasing momentum. Semiconductor Equipment & Testing (#10, up from
#98 five days ago) is the lone growth name holding rank — the AI capex cycle
is being treated as a hard-asset, not a multiple-expansion, story.

The geopolitical overhang that dominated April-May has faded to, in CNBC's
framing, the "rearview mirror." A US-Iran deal is reported reached and set to
be signed Friday, with Hormuz administration to be defined with Oman and Gulf
states — though Israel's strike on Lebanon and Trump's warning "not to blow it"
keep tail risk live. Crucially, oil did NOT fall further: WTI +0.32% to $75.04,
because the peace dividend was already priced (XLE 5day -6.15%). With the energy
shock fading, the inflation that remains is the sticky, broad-based kind — May
CPI 4.2%, the hottest since April 2023 — which is precisely why Warsh can lean
hawkish without the cover of an oil spike. That is the uncomfortable setup into
week's end: disinflation help from energy is gone, the Fed has removed its
easing bias, and breadth is rolling over underneath cap-weighted indices.
