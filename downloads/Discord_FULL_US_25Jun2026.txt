📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Thursday was a single-catalyst session that fractured the technology sector along
the one fault line that matters in 2026: who SELLS memory versus who BUYS it.

Micron reported the largest quarter in its history after Wednesday's close, and the
numbers redefined the AI-memory supercycle. Fiscal Q3 revenue more than QUADRUPLED
year-over-year to $41.46B (from $9.3B), gross margin held an historically absurd 81%,
and — the figure that actually moved the tape — Q4 guidance landed near $50B versus a
~$44B Street estimate, a >$6B beat. CEO Mehrotra confirmed all of calendar-2026 HBM
output is already sold under price-and-volume contracts, with supply tightness extending
beyond 2026 and new fabs not delivering meaningful bits until fiscal 2028. MU closed
+15.74%. This is no longer a cyclical memory bounce; it is a structural shortage economy.

The second-order consequence was the day's real story. If memory is sold out and
contract DRAM prices are up 90-95% q/q (TrendForce's "RAMageddon"), then every company
that CONSUMES memory faces margin compression — and Apple proved it in real time. AAPL
raised prices across the entire MacBook, iPad, HomePod and Vision Pro lineup ($100-$300
per unit, ~17% weighted Mac increase), citing "unsustainable" memory costs (Cook, WSJ).
The stock fell -6.12% — its worst single day since April 2025 — erasing ~$275B of market
cap. The mechanism is exact: the same shortage minting Micron's 81% margins is now a tax
on the hardware OEMs. Dell -5.67%, HPE -4.16% confirmed the read across computer hardware.
"Micron's gain is the hyperscalers' (and OEMs') pain" was the literal CNBC framing.

The memory tax also detonated the semiconductor-EQUIPMENT complex, which is the picks-
and-shovels beneficiary of the $58B+ memory-fab capex arms race: AMAT +13.42%, TER +10.48%,
KLAC +7.62%, LRCX +7.21%, ONTO +6.83%, MKSI +7.52%. SanDisk (SNDK +21.97%) led the entire
S&P 500 as the purest NAND/storage pricing-power play. Note the internal nuance: ON Semi
fell -7.21% in the after-hours — analog/auto silicon is NOT in the memory trade and got
sold as funds concentrated capital into the memory-levered names. This is surgical
rotation, not a blanket "chips up" day.

Beneath the tech civil war, the market broadened impressively. Industrials led all
sectors (XLI +2.17%) on CAT +6.29% and a machinery surge; healthcare (XLV +1.49%), and
materials (XLB +1.33%) were green; Russell 2000 closed +0.71% with 990 advancers. SPX
internal breadth was POSITIVE (305 advancers / 195 decliners) even as the cap-weighted
index finished flat — the inverse of a classic narrow-ATH day. The weak spots were
high-multiple software (IGV -1.64%; Palantir -5.49%, ServiceNow -4.56%), consumer
discretionary (XLY -1.49%; TJX -6.04%, ROST -5.89%), capital-markets financials (ICE
-4.25%, MSCI -5.67%, Jefferies -9.15% on earnings) and the entire crypto-beta complex
(MSTR -9.35%, COIN -5.06%, IBIT -3%) as speculative leverage was bled off.

Geopolitics re-armed the energy bid late. After crude briefly dipped below $70 — its
closest to the pre-war level in four months as ~35M barrels of stranded Hormuz cargo
finally transited — Iran's IRGC struck the Singapore-flagged container ship Ever Lovely
off Dahit, Oman, damaging its bridge. Oil REVERSED to close higher (WTI +3.06% $71.45;
Brent +3.43% $75.00). The signal: de-escalation is the base case (Citi sees Brent $60-65
over 6-12 months and says "fade summer rallies"), but the tail risk is not dead, and any
dip toward $70 is being defended. OIH +2.49% and MOO +2.76% rode the bid.
