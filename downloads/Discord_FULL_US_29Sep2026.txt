📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
The defining feature of Tuesday was a decoupling. Crude fell hard — WTI futures -5.47% to $88.97, Brent -3.94% to $95.69 — as Saudi Arabia resumed flows through its East-West pipeline, Washington ordered another strategic reserve release, and US and Iranian officials held separate mediator talks. Yet long bonds sold off anyway: the 10-year printed its highest level since 2007 and the 30-year its highest since 2002. That combination matters more than either move alone. The bond market stopped listening to oil and started pricing term premium — a harder headwind for multiples than any energy spike, because it does not reverse on a ceasefire headline.

The index print hides the damage. SPX -0.17% is almost entirely Apple (-2.66%). Underneath: Adv/Dec 197/298, 152 new 20day lows vs 33 highs, 24.8% above 50MA, T2108 21.4% — washout-grade internals under an index still tagged Bullish. SPX breadth has sat below 31% above-50MA for ten straight sessions while the index held 7,550–7,765: distribution beneath a stable cap-weighted shell.

The AI capex supply chain took the flows: Applied Materials +5.19%, KLA +3.89%, Marvell +4.51%, Lumentum +5.66%, Corning +4.70%, Coherent +3.46%, AAOI +3.98%. SOXX +1.19% against a flat XLK (-0.02%) is the tell: the market is paying for picks-and-shovels with order books, not the platform megacaps. Bloom Energy +10.80% (Jefferies target lift, Oracle reaffirming Project Jupiter) and Oracle +3.91% extend that chain into AI power — which is why Utilities (+1.17%) led the sector board on a day the 30-year hit a 24-year high. Utilities are trading as AI infrastructure, not bond proxies.

The oil drop fed straight into fuel-sensitive travel. Carnival +13.41% on a record Q3 and a guidance raise despite a ~$150M fuel headwind, Royal Caribbean +7.45% in sympathy; Hotels, Motels & Cruise Lines SURGED to Smart Money rank 13 (from 62 five days ago) and Airlines sit at rank 3 (from 73 nine days ago). Cheaper crude → fuel-cost relief → cruise/airline re-rating.

On the other side, Fair Isaac -26.52% — its worst day since 1989 — after the FHFA moved Fannie and Freddie to a single pricing grid that puts VantageScore on par with FICO. Regulated 'toll-booth' pricing power is now an explicit policy target. Financials (-0.33%) remain the weakest large sector into month-end, with bank ETFs KBE -1.04% and KRE -1.02%. Energy (-0.90%) followed crude, led lower by services (OIH -2.44%, SLB -3.15%). Net read: the Bullish SPX regime is a cap-weight artefact, the median stock is in a correction, and the long bond sets the pace. PCE (Wed) is the next binary for yields; Micron (Wed AH) for the semi-equipment leadership.
