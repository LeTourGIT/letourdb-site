📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Tuesday was a single-theme session: the memory-chip complex broke, and it
dragged every silicon-adjacent name with it. The catalyst was offshore and
structural, not fundamental. South Korea's KOSPI closed ~10% lower after
tripping circuit breakers twice in one session, as regulators publicly warned
that leveraged chip ETFs had become dangerously overheated. SK Hynix and
Samsung each fell more than 12%; the CSOP SK Hynix Daily 2x leveraged product
collapsed -23.8%. Oppenheimer framed the SK Hynix leveraged ETF as equivalent,
relative to its home market, to a $750B single-stock leveraged product in the
US — when that unwinds, the whole tape feels it. The forced-selling wave
crossed the Pacific into every US memory and semi name.

Micron was the epicentre: MU -13.18%, with SanDisk -13.64% right alongside it.
The cruel irony is timing — Micron reports earnings AFTER the close on
Wednesday 24 June. Tuesday's collapse is pre-print positioning into a leveraged
unwind, meaning the single most important catalyst for the entire memory cycle
(HBM demand commentary, data-center order guidance) lands into an already
fragile tape. If MU reassures, it stabilises the whole complex; if guidance
softens, the deleveraging loop reloads. Every chip desk is now hostage to one
earnings call.

The breadth picture is the session's most important non-obvious signal. The
SPX fell -1.44% yet advancers BEAT decliners 284 to 215. This is reverse
cap-weight distortion: the index headline is being written by a handful of
mega-cap semiconductors (XLK -4.14%, IYW -3.91%) while the median S&P name
actually rose. T2108 held a healthy 56.8% and 58.8% of SPX names sit above
their 50MA. Beneath the red screen, this was a rotation day far more than a
liquidation day — capital didn't leave, it relocated.

And it relocated into defensives with conviction. Staples XLP +1.87% and
Healthcare XLV +1.41% led all sectors; the day's gainer board reads like a
bond-proxy roll-call — IBM +5.04%, Merck +3.57%, J&J +3.37%, Philip Morris
+3.19%, Colgate +3.11%, Verizon +3.02%, AT&T +3.21%. When IBM and Merck are
your tape leaders on a -3% Nasdaq day, the message is unambiguous: institutions
are de-risking growth and parking in cash-flow durability, not exiting equities.

The deleveraging bled well beyond chips. Precious metals and miners were hit
hard — Silver -2.69% (SLV -5.40%), GDX -4.64%, SIL -5.47% — alongside copper
(COPX -6.37%) and rare earths (REMX -5.62%). That breadth of liquidation
across uncorrelated hard assets is the signature of margin-driven, not
thesis-driven, selling: when leverage unwinds, everything liquid gets sold to
raise cash. Crucially, oil did NOT spike despite a fresh CNBC headline on Iran
declaring Hormuz "closed" — WTI slipped -0.79% to $73.06, confirming the market
treats the post-April-ceasefire energy premium as fully unwound and is not
pricing geopolitical escalation.
