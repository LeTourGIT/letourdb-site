📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Friday closed the week with a deceptively quiet index tape hiding a violent
rotation beneath the surface. The SPX slipped just 0.25% and the Dow was flat,
yet the Russell fell 1.39% and the real action was a coordinated liquidation of
gold, silver, uranium and the miners — thirteen ETFs below -3% and ZERO above.

The catalyst was Jackson Hole. Chair Warsh's tough talk on inflation convinced
bond traders he is serious about holding — or hinting at hiking — into September,
and Treasury yields rose. That single move rippled through the risk surface:
gold futures -2.75% ($4,504), silver -3.56%, GDX -3.90%, and the uranium complex
cratered (URNM -7.19%, URA -5.79%, both EXTREME). Rising real yields are kryptonite
for zero-carry hedges; this was the great unwind of the year-long hedge trade.

Technology was the worst sector (-1.55%). NVIDIA reported and the tape could not
hold it — down 4.57% with a near-flat after-hours print — while Marvell was
pole-axed -10.28% on AI-accelerator disappointment. The chip rebound failed,
dragging SOXX -3.20% and XSD -3.68%. The second-derivative AI-infra names were
punished hardest: crypto/bitcoin miners (IREN -12.53%, APLD -7.65%, MSTR -7.34%,
COIN -6.33%) and quantum/optical (IONQ -7.68%, LITE -6.39%). When yields rise, the
longest-duration, lowest-cashflow AI proxies are sold first.

The offset — and the reason the index only fell a quarter percent — was an
enterprise-software earnings bid. Elastic detonated +19.31%, with Workday +5.76%,
ServiceNow +4.54% and Atlassian +2.58% confirming a cohort move. Consumer risk-on
held too: Amazon +3.97%, Nike +3.02%, DraftKings +4.29% lifted Discretionary and
Comm Services to the only green large-sector prints. Capital is rotating out of
rate-sensitive hard assets and AI-hardware into cash-generative software and
defensive-growth healthcare — visible directly in the RS leaderboard.

Breadth quietly deteriorated. SPX printed 227 advancers vs 272 decliners — mild —
but new 20day lows (61) outnumbered new 20day highs (31) for the first time in
weeks, and the Russell's internals were ugly: 699/1,200 adv/dec, 160 stocks -4%.
With VIX pinned at a year-to-date low of 14.43, the equity market is calm on the
surface while its rate-sensitive underbelly is repriced — a divergence to watch.
