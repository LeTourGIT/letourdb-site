📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Q3 opened with a violent, single-theme unwind: the semiconductor complex — thebiggest
winner of Q2 — was liquidated hard. SOXX -6.41% and XSD -4.43% led, with Micron (MU -10.57%)
giving back the least of a stock that more than tripled last quarter. This is textbook
calendar-turn profit-taking: books closed on the year's best trade, capital rotated out on
day one of the new quarter. The losers list is almost pure silicon — MU, KLAC -11.77%,
TER -11.68%, ALAB -10.80%, AMAT -9.97%, LRCX -9.71%, INTC -9.03%, MRVL -8.67% — plus
AI-infrastructure adjacents CoreWeave -13.92% and Corning -13.62%. Michael Burry disclosed
a short on the Dow's hottest AI name, adding narrative fuel to the de-rating.

Crucially, the money did not leave the market — it rotated. Software (IGV +3.02%),
broker-dealers (IAI +3.35%, with HOOD +8.35%, COIN +8.93%, IBKR +7.13%), healthcare
providers (IHF +3.18%, ELV +7.62%) and internet (OGIG +3.39%) all caught the outflow.
META +8.81% was the mega-cap standout after confirming plans to launch a cloud business —
monetising the $700B+ the hyperscalers have poured into AI this year and easing the single
biggest overhang on the stock. RDDT +13.93% and APP +9.58% show the application layer,
not the chip layer, is where today's bid landed. This is the classic late-cycle handoff:
from those who BUILD AI infrastructure to those who USE it.

The macro backdrop turned actively hostile to high-multiple silicon. New Fed Chair Kevin
Warsh, speaking at the ECB's Sintra forum, said flatly that 'prices are too high' and framed
inflation as his primary objective despite AI-productivity optimism. Cleveland Fed's Hammack
went further — AI could FUEL inflation and rate hikes 'may be necessary.' The 10-year yield
rose to 4.46%, the 30-year to 4.97%. Higher yields compress the long-duration cash flows that
justify semiconductor multiples — a direct mechanical headwind on top of the profit-taking.
ADP printed a light 98k (vs 110k est), throwing Thursday's June payrolls into sharp relief.

Energy kept falling: WTI -2.42% to $68.12, Brent -2.80% to $71.19 — Brent's steepest monthly
decline since March 2020. The Strait of Hormuz tanker backlog eased to 385 ships from 485, and
US-Iran technical talks continued in Doha (VP Vance: 'going well'), draining the war premium
further. Oil services (OIH -3.21%) bore the brunt. The tell of the day sits in Smart Money:
NOT ONE semiconductor industry appears in the top 16 rotation ranks — the capital-flow model
confirms the tape. Chips are being distributed, and the rotation is going to software,
financials, healthcare and — at the fringe — real-asset and defensive names.
