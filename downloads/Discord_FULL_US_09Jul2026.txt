📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Thursday was a geopolitical repricing session that resolved in favour of risk.
Wednesday's session had priced an escalating Hormuz conflict: Trump declared the
Iran ceasefire over, WTI settled +4.4% at $73.52, NYSE decliners beat advancers
3.5-to-1, and the SOX had drawn down 16% from its intraday high. Thursday reversed
the commodity leg of that trade without reversing the conflict. WTI fell -3.35% to
$71.79 and Brent -3.60% to $76.03 as traders concluded that strikes on Iranian air
defence and missile sites — roughly 90 targets — do not, by themselves, close the
strait. Oil is no longer trading the headline; it is trading the tanker.

The consequence was mechanical and immediate. Falling crude relieves the second-round
inflation channel that had pushed CME rate-hike-by-year-end odds toward 87% after
Wednesday's FOMC minutes revealed a genuinely split committee. Yields eased, the VIX
collapsed -6.27% to 15.84, and long-duration growth was repriced upward. But the
capital did not flow to the hyperscalers. It flowed to silicon.

This was a semiconductor session, and specifically a semiconductor-versus-megacap
session. XSD +3.80% and SOXX +3.50% led every equity ETF on the board; XLK managed
only +2.18% and the Magnificent Seven complex was flat-to-lower. Micron's plan to
lift US fab spending toward $250B and SK Hynix's US listing running more than seven
times oversubscribed gave the market two independent, non-narrative confirmations
that AI demand is being met with physical capacity commitments rather than slideware.
LRCX +6.01%, ONTO +8.77% and AMD +5.67% show the bid extending from memory through
equipment into logic. HPE +9.94%, SNDK +7.59% and LITE +11.13% extend it further into
hardware and optical. Capex is the trade; the compute-consumers are the funding source.

Underneath, the day's most instructive event had nothing to do with oil or chips.
Ionis fell -23.90% — its worst session since March 2021 — after the Phase 3
CARDIO-TTRansform trial of eplontersen missed its primary endpoint in ATTR-CM.
BridgeBio rose +15.12% on 1.83B turnover, the single largest gainer in the S&P/R1000
universe. This is a clean, zero-sum transfer of a multibillion-dollar cardiomyopathy
franchise from one balance sheet to another, and it explains why Biotechnology &
Medical Research holds Smart Money rank #16 with ROC +5.71% while the biotech ETFs
(IBB +0.51%) barely moved. The money is not buying the theme; it is buying the winner.

Breadth was broad and genuine. SPX advancers 299 vs decliners 201, RUT 1,341 vs 621,
T2108 at 63.6% on SPX and 64.4% on RUT. The one dissenter is the Dow, where decliners
(17) beat advancers (13) despite a green close — PEP -3.26%, COST -4.21%, MCK -3.16%
and CL -2.18% dragging the staples-and-healthcare block. That divergence is not a
warning; it is the rotation itself, visible in the only index cap-weighted toward the
sectors being sold.
