📝 SESSION SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------
Tuesday delivered the third S&P 500 all-time high in five sessions, with the rally broadening materially
from Friday's narrow AAPL-driven close. The day's character was unambiguously semiconductor-led:
XSD +4.45% was the session's dominant ETF, driven by MU +11% (HBM demand, sold out through 2026),
INTC +13% (post-Intel ATH momentum continuing), SNDK +12%, and SIMO +5%. The semi capex cycle is
no longer a theme — it is the structural regime, confirmed by the breadth within the sector. Eight of the
RS top 20 are semiconductor or semi-adjacent names; the accumulation is reaching mid-cap fabless
and substrate plays, not just NVDA and the large-cap infrastructure layer.

The Iran/Hormuz backdrop shifted tone on Tuesday. Defense Secretary Hegseth confirmed the ceasefire
"certainly holds" while simultaneously stating the Strait is "far more crucial to the rest of the world" —
language that positions the U.S. for burden-sharing negotiation rather than unilateral resolution.
WTI -3.9% to $102.27 is a second consecutive day of oil compression, but Goldman's four to six
months-to-normalise thesis remains the operative framework. The directionality of the signal is clear;
the duration of the trade is not.

SPX breadth on Tuesday was constructively positive — a meaningful improvement from Friday's
anomalous 172/326 Adv/Dec on an ATH day. Tuesday printed 319 advancers vs 181 decliners (1.76:1),
with 20 stocks gaining more than 4% and only 10 losing more than 4%. T2108 at 57.0% and 50MA
participation at 52.4% are structurally supportive. The RUT +1.16% outperformance is the most
telling data point: small-cap strength at SPX ATH is a healthy breadth confirmation, the polar opposite
of Friday's cap-weight distortion. The market is buying risk broadly.

The after-hours earnings cluster is the session's centrepiece narrative. AMD AH +5% on $10.25B Q1
revenue (beat $9.89B) and $11.2B Q2 guide (beat $10.8B est) is architecturally significant — Lisa Su's
commentary on Helios rack systems (OpenAI and Meta signed up), x86 AI Compute Extensions with Intel,
and Data Center growing 57% YoY to $5.8B all confirm that the AI silicon market has room for multiple
winners. This de-risks NDX's semi concentration; the thesis isn't dependent on one name.

PayPal -10% on a beats-across-the-board Q1 report is the session's most instructive divergence: revenue
$8.35B (+7% YoY) beat $8.05B est, EPS $1.34 beat $1.27 est — yet Q2 EPS guidance implied a -9% YoY
decline, sending fintech investors fleeing. This is the 2026 market's core dynamic: beat the quarter, miss
the forward guide, get punished. Palantir -3% on an extraordinary beat (EPS 33c vs 28c est, revenue
$1.63B vs $1.54B est, 85% YoY growth) follows the same pattern — "not wow enough" in a high-valuation
cycle. The Pinterest exception (+15%/+18% AH) underscores the rule: beats need to include forward
re-rating to move stocks higher.
